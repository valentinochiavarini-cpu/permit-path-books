# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

A {{STATE}} license comes in a fixed order of steps, and most delays come from arriving without one piece of paper. The {{STATE}} Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV) runs licensing under Chapter 322 of the {{STATE}} Statutes. Many county tax collector offices handle licenses for FLHSMV. Rules and fees change, so check flhsmv.gov before each visit.

> **On the test**
>
> - The age and seat of the person supervising a learner.
> - Learner hours: daylight only for three months, then until 10 p.m.
> - Curfews for licensed drivers aged 16 and 17.
> - The 50 supervised hours, 10 of them at night.
> - Points rules for drivers under 18.

## 1.1 The Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses **graduated licensing**: you gain freedom in stages, because new drivers are most likely to crash in their first year.

| Stage | Earliest age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Learner's license | 15 | Drive with a licensed adult 21 or older beside you |
| Class E license | 16 | Drive alone, with a curfew until 18 |
| Full privileges | 18 | No age-based curfew |

{{STATE}} calls the permit a **learner's license**. The car license is **Class E**, which covers any non-commercial vehicle with a gross vehicle weight rating under 26,001 pounds. A motorcycle with an engine over 50 cc needs a motorcycle endorsement or a motorcycle-only license.

## 1.2 Courses and Paperwork

### The Course You Need First

- **Under 18:** before your learner's license, finish a **driver education course** approved by FLHSMV. It replaced the old requirement for teens to take the Traffic Law and Substance Abuse Education course.
- **18 or older:** finish the **Traffic Law and Substance Abuse Education (TLSAE)** course, at least four hours long, before you apply. You skip it if you have held a license in another state or country, or if you passed a Department of Education driver education course.

Both courses are sold by approved providers, many online; FLHSMV posts the lists. If you already completed TLSAE under the old rule, ask your service center whether the certificate is still accepted.

### Documents

Every applicant brings **originals or certified copies**; photocopies and notarized copies are refused:

- **Primary identification**, such as a certified U.S. birth certificate from a government office (not a hospital) or a valid U.S. passport.
- **Proof of your Social Security number**, such as the card, a W-2 or a 1099.
- **Two documents showing your {{STATE}} home address**, such as a lease, utility bill, bank statement or school transcript.
- A marriage certificate, divorce decree or court order if your name changed.

If you live with someone else, bring two of their address documents and a letter from them.

**Under 18:** a parent or legal guardian signs the **Parental Consent Form** in front of the license agent or a notary. A stepparent may sign only after a legal adoption. The signer becomes responsible for your driving and may cancel your license later. You must also be in compliance with school attendance.

**REAL ID.** Every credential issued in a {{STATE}} office carries the gold star, so the document list above is the REAL ID list. You do not choose; your license will be REAL ID compliant.

> **Road tip:** Check that your name is spelled the same on every document. One different letter can send you home.

## 1.3 Fees

| Item | FLHSMV fee |
|---|---|
| Original Class E (includes the learner's license) | $48 |
| Knowledge exam retest | $10 |
| Driving skills retest | $20 |
| Replacement license | $25 |
| Tax collector service fee, where charged | $6.25 |

You pay the $48 once, for the learner's license. Trading it for a Class E license costs nothing extra if your name and address are unchanged. Course fees are set by the provider.

## 1.4 The Knowledge Exam

The **Class E Knowledge Exam** covers {{STATE}} traffic laws, safe driving practices and traffic controls. Sign questions are mixed in, not a separate section.

| Feature | The {{STATE}} rule |
|---|---|
| Questions | 50, multiple choice |
| To pass | 40 correct (80 percent; 10 misses allowed) |
| Language | English only |
| If you fail | Retest fee of $10 each time |

{{STATE}} now gives all license exams **in English only**, and translation devices are not allowed.

**Where to take it.** You have four choices:

- A driver license service center (state or tax collector office).
- An FLHSMV-approved **third-party administrator**, in person.
- **Online**, through an approved third-party administrator, if you are **under 18**. A parent must complete the **Parent Proctoring Form**, notarized or signed in front of an examiner.
- A high school driver education class in the **DELAP** program.

Third-party and DELAP scores reach FLHSMV electronically. FLHSMV may pick anyone who passed with a third party for a free, unannounced retest at an office. Pass it and you get your license. Fail it, and the next attempt costs the retest fee.

> **Test trap:** 50 questions is long. Many students fail on numbers they skimmed: curfew hours, school zone speeds, points and alcohol limits. Learn every number in this chapter and in the {{STATE}} rule boxes.

## 1.5 The Vision and Hearing Screening

Before your exam you take a **vision and hearing screening**. The standards:

- **20/40 or better** in each eye, with or without glasses or contacts, passes outright.
- **20/50 or worse** in either eye means a referral to an eye specialist. Vision of 20/70 may pass after that referral, as long as your weaker eye is better than 20/200.
- If one eye is blind or 20/200 or worse, the other eye needs 20/40 or better.
- Your field of vision must be at least **130 degrees**.

If you need lenses to pass, your license carries **restriction code B**, and you must wear them every time you drive. Telescopic lenses do not count.

## 1.6 Driving on a Learner's License

**Who supervises you.** A licensed driver **21 or older**, licensed for the vehicle you are driving, in the **front seat closest to your right**. That rule never relaxes, even on the way to school.

**When you may drive.**

| Time on learner's license | Hours you may drive |
|---|---|
| First 3 months | Daylight only |
| After 3 months | Until 10 p.m. |

Breaking either rule is a moving violation.

### Before You Can Test for Class E

Under 18, you need all of these:

- **12 months** on the learner's license, or reaching 18, whichever comes first.
- **No moving violation convictions** in the 12 months after the learner's license was issued. One violation is allowed if adjudication was withheld. A conviction pushes your license back one year from the conviction date.
- **50 hours** of supervised driving, at least **10 at night**. A parent, legal guardian or responsible adult 21 or older certifies them on the **Certification of Minor Driving Experience** form, signed in front of a notary or examiner.

> **Remember it:** 15 for the learner's license, 12 months, then 50 hours with 10 at night. Record each drive in the log at the back of this book.

## 1.7 Your First Class E License

**Curfews.** Until 18, these hours apply:

| Age | You may not drive alone | Exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | 11 p.m. to 6 a.m. | Driving directly to or from work, or with a licensed driver 21+ |
| 17 | 1 a.m. to 5 a.m. | Same exceptions |
| 18 | No curfew | |

A curfew violation is a moving violation and adds 3 points. {{STATE}} sets no limit on the number of teen passengers, but every passenger under 18 must be belted, and the driver gets the ticket if one is not.

**Points.** If you are under 18 and collect **6 or more points within 12 months**, your license is restricted to **Business Purposes Only** for one year. Each extra point adds 90 days to that restriction.

**Phones.** {{STATE}} bans texting for every driver and bans holding a phone in school zones, school crossings and active work zones (see Chapter 11).

> **Test trap:** A 16-year-old's curfew starts at 11 p.m. and ends at 6 a.m. A 17-year-old's starts at 1 a.m. and ends at 5 a.m. A friend who is 19 does not lift either one; the companion must be 21 or older.

## 1.8 The Driving Skills Test

### Booking and What to Bring

You may take the **Class E Driving Skills Test** at a service center, with a third-party administrator or through DELAP, after 12 months on your learner's license or at 18. Book ahead; many offices take visits by reservation only. Bring:

- Your learner's license and, if under 18, the signed driving experience certification.
- A car with a **valid plate and registration** and **proof of insurance**.
- Any identity documents you did not show before.

### The Car Check

The examiner inspects the car first. There is no test if the horn, mirror, turn signals, steering, brakes, tires, brake lights or tail lights fail; if there is no driver's side wiper; if glass is cracked enough to block your view; if the tag is expired; or if a front door will not open from inside and outside. A low-speed vehicle is not accepted. No passengers ride along, and interpreters and translation devices are no longer allowed.

### What the Examiner Scores

You will be asked to:

- **Back up 50 feet**, slowly, looking over your shoulder, without using mirrors or the backup camera.
- Make a **three-point turn** in a space 20 to 40 feet wide.
- Do **straight-in parking**, centered in the space.
- **Park on a grade**, uphill and downhill, with and without a curb.
- Make a **quick stop** when told.
- Signal for the **last 100 feet** before a turn and keep **four seconds** behind the car ahead.
- Obey stop signs and signals, yield the right of way, stay in your lane, and keep both hands on the wheel.

FLHSMV does not publish a list of automatic fails. Afterward the examiner goes over your mistakes. If you fail, practice and come back; each retest costs $20. Pass, and you trade your learner's license for a Class E license.

## 1.9 Adults Getting a First License

At 18 or older, you do **not** need a learner's license first. You complete TLSAE (unless licensed before), pass the vision screening and the knowledge exam at an office or with an in-person third-party administrator, then take the driving skills test. There is no 12-month wait, no certified 50 hours and no curfew. Practice with a licensed adult anyway; the skills test is the same for everyone.

## 1.10 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

Once you become a {{STATE}} resident, or take a job here or enroll a child in a public school, you have **30 days** to get a {{STATE}} license.

| Your current license | Knowledge and skills tests |
|---|---|
| Any U.S. state, territory or the U.S. Armed Forces | Waived; license surrendered |
| Canada | Waived only if you surrender the Canadian license |
| France, South Korea or Taiwan | Waived |
| Germany | Skills test waived; knowledge exam required |
| Any other country | Both required |

Everyone takes the vision screening. A visitor 16 or older who is not a resident may drive on a valid license from home; from another country, carry an International Driving Permit with it. A valid foreign license you hold also exempts you from TLSAE.

## 1.11 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **Official {{STATE}} Driver License Handbook**, the source of the exam questions, is free at flhsmv.gov, along with a practice test, document checklists and office locations. The current handbook does not yet show every recent law change, such as the driver education course, so where this book and current law differ, the law wins. Check the site the week before each exam.

## Quick Review

- Learner's license at 15 after an approved driver education course; adults take TLSAE instead.
- The knowledge exam has 50 questions in English; you need 40 correct.
- Under 18, you may take the knowledge exam online with a third-party administrator and a Parent Proctoring Form.
- Learners drive daylight only for 3 months, then until 10 p.m., with a licensed adult 21+ in the front right seat.
- Before Class E: 12 months on the learner's license and 50 hours (10 at night). Curfew: 11 p.m. to 6 a.m. at 16, 1 a.m. to 5 a.m. at 17.
