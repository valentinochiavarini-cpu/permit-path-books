# Illinois inserts

## school_zone

In a marked {{STATE}} school zone, on school days from **6:30 a.m. to 4 p.m.** when children are present, slow to **20 mph**, stop and yield to anyone in the crosswalk, and put down your phone (hands-free use is allowed only for drivers 19 and older). Passing is not allowed in any posted school zone. On school days, come to a full stop when children are close to a school zone crosswalk.

## work_zone

In a {{STATE}} construction or maintenance zone, obey the posted work zone speed limit **24 hours a day, 7 days a week**, whether or not workers are present; it may drop further when they are. Slow down, stop using a handheld phone, yield to workers and work vehicles, and change to a lane away from them when you can. Every construction zone is a **no passing zone**. A second speeding conviction within two years with workers present can suspend your license.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, after a complete stop, you may turn **right on a steady red light** unless a sign forbids it, yielding to pedestrians and to traffic in or near the intersection. You may turn **left on red** only from a one-way street onto a one-way street whose traffic moves to the left. The same two turns are allowed on a **steady red arrow**, after the same full stop and yield, unless a sign prohibits them.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Move Over law (Scott's Law) covers any stationary emergency or maintenance vehicle showing flashing red, blue, amber, white or green lights. On a road with at least two lanes in your direction, move to a lane **not next to** the vehicle if it is safe. If you cannot, slow down and pass with caution. Do the same for a disabled vehicle with hazard lights on a four-lane highway. First offense: fine of **$250 to $10,000**; a crash adds a license suspension.

## school_bus

In {{STATE}}, stop for a school bus with red lights flashing and stop arm out, from **either direction**, on any road, private road or school parking lot. Stay stopped until the arm folds and the lights stop, or the driver waves you on. Exception: on a highway with **four or more lanes** (two each way), oncoming traffic need not stop. The law sets no stopping distance; the State Board of Education says stop **at least 20 feet** away. Passing: **3-month suspension** and a **$300** fine.

## u_turn

In {{STATE}}, never turn around on a curve or near the top of a hill unless you can see at least **500 feet** in every direction. Cities may ban U-turns on some streets, so watch for No U-Turn signs. Never make a U-turn on a controlled-access highway: you may not cross the median or back up on the shoulder.

## speed_limits

Unless signs show otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Interstates and tollways | 70 mph |
| Four-lane highways | 65 mph |
| Other highways, rural areas | 55 mph |
| Cities and towns | 30 mph |
| Alleys | 15 mph |
| School zones (when in effect) | 20 mph |

Local governments may post different limits. Slow below the limit at intersections, curves, hilltops and on narrow, winding roads.

## following_distance

{{STATE}} law bans following another vehicle more closely than is **reasonable and prudent** for your speed, the traffic and the road. The law sets no number of seconds. The official manual asks for at least **three to four seconds** behind a motorcycle, and extra space around trucks, buses and other large vehicles.

## hov

{{STATE}} has no carpool (HOV) lane rules in general use. In the Chicago area you may meet **reversible express lanes**, such as those on the Kennedy Expressway, which carry traffic one way at a time: enter only where signs show they are open in your direction. On some expressways, Pace buses may drive on the shoulder when traffic is slow. Never use a shoulder as a lane yourself.

## parking_rules

{{STATE}} bans parking within:

| Place | Feet |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant | 15 |
| Fire station driveway or crosswalk at an intersection | 20 |
| Stop sign, yield sign or traffic signal | 30 |
| Nearest rail of a railroad crossing | 50 |

Never stop on a sidewalk, crosswalk, bridge, railroad track, bike lane or controlled-access highway, or double park. On a two-way street, park with your right wheels within **12 inches** of the curb.

## bicycle_passing

In {{STATE}}, pass a bicyclist slowly with at least **3 feet** between the bike and your side mirror, including a rider on the shoulder or in a bike lane. You may pass a bicycle in a no-passing zone only if it is going less than half the speed limit, you stay within the limit and the left side is clear. Never drive, park or stop in a bike lane. Open your door with your right hand (the Dutch Reach).

## crash_reporting

After a {{STATE}} crash, stop in a safe place, help the injured and call 911. Report the crash to police right away (the official manual says within **30 minutes**) if anyone is hurt or killed, or damage is over **$1,500** (over **$500** if any vehicle is uninsured). Failing to report can bring a fine up to **$2,500** and up to one year in jail. If you hit an unattended car, leave your name, address, phone and plate number.

## bac_limits

{{STATE}} limits:

| Driver | Limit |
|---|---|
| 21 and older | 0.08 BAC |
| Under 21 | Any trace of alcohol |
| Cannabis (THC) | 5 ng per mL of whole blood |

Under 21 with any alcohol in your system: at least a **3-month** suspension. Any trace of an illegal drug is also a violation. You can be convicted of DUI below 0.08 if your driving is impaired.

## implied_consent

By driving in {{STATE}}, you consent to breath, blood, urine or other tests for alcohol or drugs. Failing a test (0.08 or more) suspends your license for **6 months**; refusing suspends it for **1 year**. As a repeat offender within five years: **1 year** for failing, **3 years** for refusing. The officer takes your license and gives you a 45-day receipt; the suspension starts on day 46.

## dui_penalties

A first {{STATE}} DUI is a **Class A misdemeanor**, and the conviction revokes your license for at least **1 year** (under 21: at least **2 years**). A second DUI within 20 years: at least **5 years**. At 0.16 or more: minimum **$500** fine and **100 hours** of community service. With a passenger under 16: an extra **$1,000** fine minimum.

**Open container:** no one may drink in a vehicle, and no opened alcohol may be in the passenger area. Passengers in a limousine, chartered bus or motor home are exempt.

## phone_laws

{{STATE}} bans using a handheld phone while driving: no calls, texting, video, video calls or social media. Drivers **19 and older** may use hands-free or voice mode or one earpiece; drivers **under 19** may not use a phone at all, except for emergencies. You may use a handheld phone to report an emergency, while parked on the shoulder, or when stopped by blocked traffic in neutral or park. Fines: up to **$75** first offense, rising to **$150**.

## seat_belts

{{STATE}} requires every driver and passenger **8 or older**, front and back seats, to wear a seat belt, even with airbags. The driver must make sure every passenger obeys the belt and child seat laws and must buckle any passenger who cannot do it alone. Wear the lap belt across your hip bones and the shoulder strap across your chest, never behind your back or under your arm.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} requires:

| Child | Required |
|---|---|
| Under 2 | Rear-facing seat, unless over 40 pounds or 40 inches |
| Under 8 | Child safety seat or booster seat |
| 8 and older | Seat belt in every seat |

A booster seat needs a lap and shoulder belt. If the back seat has only lap belts, a child over 40 pounds may ride there with a lap belt. Children 12 and under are safest in the back seat.

## vehicle_rules

{{STATE}} drivers must carry liability insurance of at least **$25,000** per person and **$50,000** per crash for injury, plus **$20,000** for property damage, and show proof when stopped. Driving uninsured costs at least a **3-month** suspension. Use headlights from sunset to sunrise, whenever you need wipers for rain, snow or fog, and when you cannot see **1,000 feet** ahead. A sound system heard **75 feet** away is illegal. Report a new address within **10 days**.

## road_test

The {{STATE}} driving exam scores: checking controls and adjusting seat, belts and mirrors before moving; backing about **50 feet** straight and slowly, looking back over your right shoulder; a **turnabout** using an alley on the left; parking uphill and downhill and starting from each; and obeying signs, signals, right of way and lane markings. Only the examiner rides along. You may lose up to **36 points**. Any traffic violation or dangerous action is an **automatic fail**.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers under 18 need before a license:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total supervised practice | 50 |
| At night | At least 10 |
| Driver education behind the wheel | 6 (separate) |

Night hours count toward the 50. Your supervisor is a parent, guardian or responsible adult **21 or older** with a valid license and at least one year of driving experience, in the front passenger seat. A parent or guardian signs your log. You must also hold your permit **9 months**.

## signal_distance

In a {{STATE}} business or residential district, give a continuous turn signal for at least the last **100 feet** before you turn. Elsewhere, signal at least **200 feet** ahead. Signal every lane change. Flashing your turn signals to tell another driver it is safe to pass is illegal.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, switch to low beams when an oncoming vehicle comes within **500 feet**, and when you follow another vehicle within **300 feet**.
