# Illinois inserts

## school_zone

In a marked {{STATE}} school zone, the limit is **20 mph** on school days from **6:30 a.m. to 4 p.m.** when children are present. Passing is not allowed in any posted school zone.

On school days, come to a **full stop** when children are close to a school zone crosswalk, and yield to anyone in it. Put your phone down: only drivers 19 and older may use one, and only hands-free.

## work_zone

In a {{STATE}} construction or maintenance zone, the posted work zone limit applies **24 hours a day, 7 days a week**, even when no one is working. It may drop lower when workers are present.

Every construction zone is a **no passing zone**. Stop using a handheld phone, yield to workers and work vehicles, and move to a lane away from them when you can.

A second speeding conviction within **two years** in a zone with workers present can suspend your license.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, you may turn **right on a steady red light** unless a sign forbids it. Stop completely first, then yield to pedestrians and to traffic in or near the intersection.

You may turn **left on red** only from a one-way street onto a one-way street whose traffic moves to the left.

A **steady red arrow** allows the same two turns, after the same full stop and yield, unless a sign says no.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Move Over law is called **Scott's Law**. It covers any stopped emergency or maintenance vehicle with flashing red, blue, amber, white or green lights.

On a road with at least two lanes your way, move to a lane **not next to** the vehicle if it is safe. If you cannot, slow down and pass with care. Do the same for a disabled vehicle with hazard lights on a four-lane highway.

A first offense costs **$250 to $10,000**. Damage or injury also brings a license suspension.

## emergency_vehicles

{{STATE}} law names the **right-hand edge or curb**, clear of any intersection, with no exception for one-way streets. Stop if that is needed to let the vehicle pass. If you are already stopped at an intersection with two-way traffic, stay stopped until the emergency vehicle has gone through it.

Never follow a fire truck answering an alarm closer than **500 feet**, or stop within 500 feet of one at a fire. Never drive over a fire hose.

## school_bus

In {{STATE}}, stop for a school bus with its **red lights flashing** and **stop arm out**, from **either direction**. This applies on any road, private road or school parking lot. Go again when the bus moves, the lights stop, or the bus driver signals you on.

The one exception: on a road with **four or more lanes**, two each way, oncoming traffic does not stop, with or without a median.

The law sets no distance, but the State Board of Education says stop **at least 20 feet** away. Passing a stopped bus: **3-month suspension** and a **$300** fine.

## u_turn

In {{STATE}}, never turn around on a curve or near the top of a hill unless you can see at least **500 feet** in every direction. Cities may ban U-turns on some streets, so watch for No U-Turn signs. On a controlled-access highway, never cross the median or back up on the shoulder.

## speed_limits

{{STATE}} limits when no sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Interstates and tollways | 70 mph |
| Four-lane highways | 65 mph |
| Other highways, rural areas | 55 mph |
| Cities and towns | 30 mph |
| Alleys | 15 mph |
| School zones, when in effect | 20 mph |

Cities and counties may post different limits.

## following_distance

{{STATE}} law sets no number of seconds. It says you may not follow more closely than is **reasonable and prudent** for your speed, the traffic and the road.

The official manual asks for at least **three to four seconds** behind a motorcycle. Leave extra space around trucks, buses and other large vehicles too.

## hov

{{STATE}} has no carpool (HOV) lanes in general use. Near Chicago you may meet **reversible express lanes**, which carry traffic one way at a time: enter only where signs show they are open in your direction. On some expressways, Pace buses may use the shoulder when traffic is slow. You may never drive or pass on the shoulder.

## parking_rules

{{STATE}} bans parking within:

| Place | Feet |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant | 15 |
| Crosswalk at an intersection | 20 |
| Fire station driveway | 20 |
| Stop sign, yield sign or traffic signal | 30 |
| Nearest rail of a railroad crossing | 50 |

On a two-way street, park with your right wheels within **12 inches** of the curb.

## bicycle_passing

In {{STATE}}, pass a bicyclist slowly, with at least **3 feet** between the bike and your side mirror. This applies even when the rider is on the shoulder or in a bike lane.

In a no-passing zone, you may pass a bicycle only if it is going **less than half** the speed limit, you stay within the limit, and the left side is clear.

Never drive, park or stop in a bike lane. Open your car door with your right hand (the **Dutch Reach**) so you look back for bikes.

## crash_reporting

In {{STATE}}, report a crash to the police if anyone is hurt or killed, or if damage is over **$1,500** (over **$500** if any vehicle has no insurance). The manual gives you **30 minutes** to report it.

Within **10 days**, also send a written crash report to the {{STATE}} Department of Transportation. Failing to report can bring a fine of up to **$2,500** and up to a year in jail.

## bac_limits

{{STATE}} limits:

| Driver | Illegal at |
|---|---|
| 21 and older | 0.08 BAC |
| Under 21 | Any trace of alcohol |
| Any driver, cannabis | 5 ng of THC per mL of whole blood |

Any trace of an illegal drug is also a violation.

## implied_consent

By driving in {{STATE}}, you agree to breath, blood, urine or other tests for alcohol or drugs.

Fail a test (**0.08** or more) and your license is suspended for **6 months**. Refuse and it is suspended for **1 year**. For a repeat offender within five years, it is **1 year** for failing and **3 years** for refusing.

The officer takes your license and gives you a receipt that lets you drive for 45 days. The suspension starts on **day 46**.

## dui_penalties

In {{STATE}}, a first DUI is a **Class A misdemeanor**. It **revokes** your license for at least **1 year**. A second DUI within 20 years: at least **5 years**.

With a BAC of **0.16** or more, a first DUI also brings a fine of at least **$500** and **100 hours** of community service. With a passenger under 16 in the car, add a fine of at least **$1,000**.

**Open container:** no one may drink in a vehicle, and no opened alcohol may be in the passenger area. Passengers in a limousine, chartered bus or motor home are the exception.

## underage_alcohol

In {{STATE}}, a driver **under 21** with **any trace** of alcohol loses the license for at least **3 months**. A DUI conviction under 21 revokes it for at least **2 years**.

Off the road, buying, accepting, holding or drinking alcohol under 21 is a **Class A misdemeanor**. So is carrying a **fake ID** or using someone else's. If you were in a vehicle at the time, court supervision means a **3-month** suspension, and a conviction at least **6 months**.

Adults who give alcohol to anyone under 21 can be fined, jailed and suspended.

## phone_laws

{{STATE}} bans using a **handheld phone** while you drive: no calls, texts, video, video calls or social media.

Drivers **19 and older** may use hands-free or voice mode, or a single earpiece. Drivers **under 19** may not use a phone at all, except in an emergency.

You may hold a phone to report an emergency, while parked on the shoulder, or when stopped by blocked traffic in neutral or park. Fines: up to **$75** the first time, rising to **$150**.

## seat_belts

{{STATE}} requires every driver and passenger **8 or older** to wear a seat belt, in the front and back seats, even with airbags.

The driver must make sure every passenger follows the belt and child seat laws. If a passenger cannot buckle up alone, the driver must do it.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} requires:

| Child | Required |
|---|---|
| Under 2 | Rear-facing seat, unless over 40 pounds or 40 inches tall |
| Under 8 | Child safety seat or booster seat |
| 8 and older | Seat belt in every seat |

A booster seat needs a lap and shoulder belt. If the back seat has only lap belts, a child over 40 pounds may ride there with a lap belt. Children 12 and under are safest in the back.

## vehicle_rules

{{STATE}} drivers must carry liability insurance of at least **$25,000** per person and **$50,000** per crash for injuries, plus **$20,000** for property damage. Show proof when police ask.

Driving uninsured costs your license for at least **3 months**, plus a $100 reinstatement fee.

A sound system that can be heard **75 feet** away is illegal. Report a new address within **10 days**.

## police_stop

The {{STATE}} manual adds a few points. Do not stop on a bridge, a curve or next to a wall or guardrail; find the next safe spot. The officer may come to either side of the car.

If your license or insurance card is in the glove box or on your phone, tell the officer before you reach for it.

If you cannot tell whether an unmarked car is the police, drive slowly to a well lit, busy place or a police station, or call 911. Turn on your hazard lights to show you will stop.

Sign a ticket if you get one: signing does not admit guilt.

## headlight_use

Besides sunset to sunrise, {{STATE}} law requires headlights:

- whenever rain, snow or fog makes you use your **wipers**;
- any time you cannot clearly see people and vehicles **1,000 feet** ahead.

## driver_record

{{STATE}} gives each moving violation **points**: for example, **5** for speeding 1 to 10 mph over, **15** for 11 to 14 over, **20** for 15 to 25 over, **50** for 26 or more over, and **20** for running a red light or stop sign.

**Three or more** convictions within **12 months** bring a suspension sized by your points: **15 to 44** points means 2 months, and **110** or more means revocation.

Drivers under 21 face a suspension after just two convictions in 24 months (Chapter 1).

## road_test

The {{STATE}} driving exam scores these tasks:

- checking controls, and adjusting your seat, belt and mirrors before you move;
- backing about **50 feet** straight and slowly, looking back over your right shoulder;
- a **turnabout** using an alley on the left;
- parking uphill and downhill, and starting from each.

You are also scored on signs, signals, right of way and lane markings. You can lose up to **36 points** and still pass. Any traffic violation or dangerous action is an **automatic fail**.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers under 18 need before a license:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total supervised practice | 50 |
| At night | At least 10 |
| Driver education behind the wheel | 6, separate |

Night hours count toward the 50. Your supervisor sits in the front passenger seat. It must be a parent, guardian or responsible adult **21 or older** with a valid license and at least one year of driving experience. A parent or guardian signs your log. You must also hold your permit **9 months**.

## signal_distance

In a {{STATE}} business or residential district, signal for at least the last **100 feet** before you turn. Elsewhere, signal at least **200 feet** ahead. Signal every lane change too. Flashing your turn signal to tell another driver it is safe to pass is illegal.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, switch to low beams when an oncoming vehicle comes within **500 feet**. When you follow another vehicle, switch to low beams within **300 feet** of it.
