# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

The {{STATE}} Secretary of State issues licenses and permits at its DMV facilities, under the {{STATE}} Vehicle Code (625 ILCS 5). Most wasted trips come from a missing document or an unfinished course. Rules and fees change, so check ilsos.gov before each visit.

> **On the test**
>
> - The age, seat and experience of the adult who supervises a permit holder.
> - Night driving limits for drivers under 18.
> - The passenger limit in your first 12 months of licensed driving.
> - The 50 supervised hours, 10 of them at night, and the 9-month permit hold.
> - The phone ban for drivers under 19, hands-free included.

## 1.1 The Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses **Graduated Driver Licensing (GDL)**: drivers aged 15 to 20 earn freedom in three phases, and a clean record is what moves you from one phase to the next.

| Phase | Usual age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Permit | 15 to 17 | Drive only with a qualified supervisor |
| Initial licensing | 16 and 17 | Drive alone, with night and passenger limits |
| Full licensing | 18 to 20 | Only the phone ban, until 19, if your record is clean |

The basic car license is **Class D**. A license issued before 21 expires three months after your 21st birthday.

## 1.2 Driver Education and Paperwork

### The Course Comes First

To get a license at 16 or 17, you must pass a state-approved **driver education course**: at least 30 hours in the classroom and 6 hours behind the wheel. Most teens take it in high school or at a licensed commercial driving school.

- **15 to 17:** you can get an **instruction permit** once your school or driving school shows you are enrolled in an approved course, or will start one within 30 days.
- **17 years and 3 months or older:** you may get a permit without enrolling in driver education. A license before 18 still requires the completed course.
- **18, 19 or 20, never licensed and no approved course:** you must finish a 6-hour **Adult Driver Education Course** from a provider certified by the Secretary of State before you can be licensed. Some teach it online.

The course provider reports your completion to the Secretary of State, and it must show on your driving record before a license is issued.

### Documents

Every first-time applicant proves name, date of birth, Social Security number, residency and signature. The Acceptable Identification Documents chart in the official manual lists what counts, and one document can cover more than one item. {{STATE}} gives you a choice of two cards:

| Item to prove | Standard license | REAL ID license |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and lawful status | Not required | Required |
| Date of birth, SSN, signature | Required | Required |
| {{STATE}} residency | 1 document | 2 documents |

You now need a REAL ID (or a U.S. passport or passport card) to board a domestic flight or enter a military base or secure federal building. Both cards are equally valid for driving.

**Applicants without lawful status.** The **Temporary Visitor Driver's License (TVDL)** is no longer issued. People who would have qualified for one can now apply for a **standard license** instead, which is marked "Federal Limits Apply" and is not a REAL ID. If you are not eligible for a Social Security number, you sign a statement saying so. The vision, written and driving exams are the same for every applicant.

**Under 18:** a parent or legal guardian must give written consent. If neither can come to the facility, a legal guardian or a responsible adult over 21 can sign the **Affidavit/Consent for Minor to Drive**. The parent or guardian who consents can cancel your license for any reason until you turn 18.

> **Road tip:** Make sure your name is spelled the same way on every document. If it changed, bring the marriage certificate, court order or other record that links the names.

## 1.3 Fees

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Instruction permit | $20 |
| Driver's license, ages 18 to 20 | $5 |
| Driver's license, ages 21 to 68 | $30 |
| Duplicate or corrected license or permit | $5 |

Under 18, if you get your license before your permit expires, you pay no extra fee for it. Course fees are set by the provider.

## 1.4 The Written Exam

You take the written exam at a DMV facility before you get your permit. Under the Secretary of State's rules, the Class D exam has **at least 35 questions**, and you need **80 percent** correct to pass. You identify signs by shape, color or symbol, read signals and pavement markings, and answer multiple-choice and true-or-false questions on traffic laws, safety, crash prevention and equipment. The sign questions are part of the same exam, not a separately scored test. State-licensed driving schools describe a 35-question exam on which about 15 questions are signs, so plan on needing **28 correct answers**. The state does not publish its question mix, so learn every sign in Chapter 2.

| Feature | The {{STATE}} rule |
|---|---|
| Questions | At least 35 |
| To pass | 80 percent (28 of 35) |

**Languages and help.** The exam is given in English, in Spanish and in other languages the Secretary of State chooses to offer. An oral exam can be requested in person, Monday through Thursday, for applicants with a language barrier or a reading or learning disability. Applicants who are deaf or hard of hearing can arrange an interpreter in advance.

**Retakes.** You get three attempts within one year of paying, and a same-day retest is sometimes possible. Cheating fails the exam and bars you from retaking it for 30 days. An open book in view or a look at your phone draws a warning, so put both away. Your passing result stays valid until your permit expires.

> **Test trap:** Many students lose points on numbers: the 9-month permit hold, the night hours, the 20 mph school zone and the 100 feet for a turn signal in town. Learn every number in this chapter and in the {{STATE}} rule boxes.

## 1.5 The Vision Screening

Every applicant has a vision screening, or brings a **Vision Specialist Report** from an eye doctor or physician dated within six months of the visit.

- **20/40 or better** with or without glasses or contacts meets the standard.
- **20/41 to 20/70** gets a license limited to **daylight driving only**.
- You need at least **140 degrees** of side vision.

If you need lenses to pass, your license notes the restriction, and you must wear them whenever you drive.

## 1.6 Driving on an Instruction Permit

**Who rides with you.** Outside your driver education lessons, your supervisor is a parent, legal guardian or responsible adult who is **21 or older**, holds a valid license and has at least **one year** of driving experience. That person sits in the **front passenger seat** beside you.

**Permit phase limits:**

- No driving Sunday through Thursday from **10 p.m. to 6 a.m.**, or Friday and Saturday from **11 p.m. to 6 a.m.** Some towns set stricter curfews.
- Only **one passenger** in the front seat, and no more people in the back than there are seat belts.
- No phone use at all, hands-free included, except to call police, a health care provider or emergency services.

The permit is valid for **24 months**.

### Before You Can Get a License

Under 18, you need all of these:

- At least **16 years** of age.
- The permit held for at least **9 consecutive months**.
- No driving offenses, underage alcohol convictions or court supervisions during those 9 months.
- **50 hours** of supervised practice, at least **10 at night**, on top of driver education. Track them in the log at the back of this book; your parent or guardian signs the official log you bring to the facility.
- Driver education completion on your driving record.

> **Remember it:** 15 for the permit, 9 months of holding it, 50 hours with 10 at night, then the license at 16 at the earliest.

## 1.7 The Initial Licensing Phase

Until you turn 18, these rules apply:

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| Night limits | Same hours as the permit phase |
| Passengers, first 12 months or until 18 | Only one passenger under 20, unless siblings, stepsiblings, children or stepchildren |
| Passengers after that | One in front; back seat limited to its belts |
| Phone | No use while driving, even hands-free, until 19 |

**Keeping a clean record.** You must have **six months** with no traffic convictions before you turn 18, or the limits can last past your 18th birthday. Breaking the night limits during the permit or initial phase can suspend your driving privileges.

**Penalties for drivers under 21.** Two moving violation convictions within 24 months bring at least a **30-day suspension**. You are allowed only one court supervision for a serious traffic offense, and you must attend traffic safety school to get it. Under 18, you must appear in court with a parent or guardian. Under 21, any trace of alcohol while driving suspends your privileges for at least three months (Chapter 11).

> **Test trap:** The passenger limit in your first 12 months counts passengers under 20. A friend who is 19 counts; your 15-year-old brother does not.

## 1.8 The Driving Exam

### Who Takes It and Where

Every new driver takes a driving exam. If your high school takes part in the **Cooperative Driver Testing Program** and you finish driver education with a combined grade of A or B, your instructor's road test can replace the facility exam. You then bring the **Cooperative Driver Testing Certificate**, signed by your instructor and parent, which expires on the same day as your permit. Students whose birthdays fall on certain spot-check dates must still pass a facility driving exam.

To apply for your license under 18, bring your permit, identity documents, the consent form or certificate if they apply, and your signed 50-hour log.

### The Car

The car must have valid front and rear plates, a current registration sticker, the required equipment and insurance, with proof shown at the exam. It must match the weight class on your application, and someone with a valid license or permit must drive it there. Only the examiner rides with you: no friends, children or pets in the car.

### What the Examiner Scores

- Adjusting the seat, belts and mirrors and checking the controls **before** the car moves.
- **Backing** about 50 feet, slowly and straight, while you turn your head right and watch through the rear window.
- A **turnabout** using an alley on the left side of the street: signal left, pull in, back out and stay on your side of the road.
- **Parking uphill and downhill** at the side of the road, and starting from each.
- Obeying signs, signals, right of way and lane markings, and signaling correctly.

Errors cost points, and you are allowed up to 36. Any traffic violation, dangerous action or refusal to do a task is an **automatic fail**. Both you and the examiner must wear seat belts.

## 1.9 Adults Getting a First License

At 18 or older, no permit holding period, supervised hours or GDL limits apply. You pass the vision screening, the written exam and the driving exam at a facility. If you are 18 to 20 and never licensed, finish the Adult Driver Education Course first. At 21 or older, no course is required, though the Secretary of State suggests a driving school. You may get an instruction permit to practice, with the same supervisor rules: a licensed adult 21 or older beside you.

## 1.10 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

If you move to {{STATE}} with a valid license from another state or country, you may drive on it until you get an {{STATE}} license, which you must do within **90 days** or before your license expires, whichever comes first. Everyone passes the vision screening, and you should plan on the written exam too. A crash or moving violation on your record can also require the driving exam.

Visitors may drive on a valid license from home. If you hold a license from another country and settle here, expect to take the vision, written and driving exams, the same path as a new driver. Ask your facility whether any exam can be waived for your country.

## 1.11 Where to Find the Official Rules

The Secretary of State's **Rules of the Road**, the source of the written exam, is free at ilsos.gov and at every facility, along with the Parent-Teen Driving Guide and document lists. Where this book and current law differ, the law wins.

## Quick Review

- Instruction permit at 15 with driver education enrollment; written exam of at least 35 questions, 80 percent to pass.
- Hold the permit 9 months and log 50 hours, 10 at night; the license comes at 16 at the earliest.
- Night limits until 18: 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. Sunday to Thursday, 11 p.m. to 6 a.m. Friday and Saturday.
- First 12 months licensed: one passenger under 20, family excepted. No phone at all until 19.
- First-time drivers aged 18 to 20 complete the 6-hour Adult Driver Education Course.
