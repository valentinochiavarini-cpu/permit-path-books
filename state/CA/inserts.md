# California inserts

## school_zone

The {{STATE}} school zone limit is **25 mph** within **500 feet** of a school while children are present, unless signs show a lower limit. Cities and counties may now post **20 mph** school zones, with a sign that says when it applies (when flashing, when children are present, or listed hours). Some residential streets near schools are posted at **15 mph**. Always obey crossing guards and school safety patrols.

## work_zone

In a {{STATE}} highway construction or maintenance zone, traffic fines are **doubled** when workers are present, and fines there can reach $1,000 or more. Some roads are posted as **Safety Enhanced Double Fine Zones**, where fines are doubled too. Assaulting a highway worker brings fines up to $2,000 and up to one year in jail. Merge early without crossing the cones or drums, and obey flaggers.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, after a full stop at the limit line, you may turn **right on a steady red light** unless a NO TURN ON RED sign is posted, yielding to pedestrians and close traffic. You may turn **left on red** only from a one-way street onto a one-way street, under the same conditions.

A **red arrow** means no turn at all. Stay stopped until a green light or green arrow appears.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Move Over law covers stopped emergency vehicles with emergency lights, stopped tow trucks and road maintenance vehicles with flashing amber lights, and **any stopped vehicle** showing hazard lights or warning devices such as flares or cones.

- If you can, move to a lane **not next to** the stopped vehicle.
- If changing lanes is unsafe or impossible, **slow to a safe speed**.

Never follow within **300 feet** of an emergency vehicle using its siren or flashing lights.

## school_bus

When a {{STATE}} school bus flashes its **red lights**, drivers coming from **either direction** must stop before reaching it and stay stopped until the lights stop flashing. On a **divided highway** or a road with **two or more lanes each way**, you need not stop if the bus is on the other side. {{STATE}} law sets no minimum stopping distance. Flashing **yellow** lights mean slow down and prepare to stop. Passing a stopped bus: fines up to **$1,000** and a possible **one-year** suspension.

## u_turn

{{STATE}} allows a U-turn across a double yellow line, at an intersection on a green light unless a sign forbids it, in a residential district when no vehicle is within **200 feet**, and through a divided highway opening. Never make one where you cannot see **200 feet** each way, on a one-way street, at a railroad crossing, in front of a fire station, across a divider, or in a business district except at an intersection or median opening.

## speed_limits
Unless posted otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Alley, blind intersection, railroad crossing without a clear view | 15 mph |
| Business or residential district | 25 mph |
| Two-lane undivided highway, or towing a trailer | 55 mph |
| Most highways | 65 mph |

Some freeways are posted at **70 mph**. {{STATE}}'s **Basic Speed Law** forbids driving faster than is safe for conditions, whatever the sign says.

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} handbook teaches the **three-second rule**. Pick a fixed marker, count from when the car ahead passes it, and slow down if you reach it before three. Leave more room behind a motorcycle, when a tailgater is behind you, and on wet or icy roads. When merging onto a highway, leave three seconds ahead. Never follow within **300 feet** of an emergency vehicle using its siren or lights.

## hov

{{STATE}} **HOV (carpool) lanes** are marked with a **diamond** and the words CARPOOL LANE. Signs list the minimum number of people and the hours the rule applies. Motorcycles may use them unless signs say otherwise. Enter and leave only at marked openings, never across **double solid white lines**. Clean air vehicle decals for solo drivers are **no longer valid**; every vehicle must meet the posted occupancy rule.

## parking_rules

{{STATE}} bans parking:

| Place | Distance |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant or fire station driveway | 15 feet |
| Approach side of a crosswalk | 20 feet (15 with a curb extension) |
| Sidewalk ramp for disabled persons | 3 feet |
| Nearest railroad rail | 7 and a half feet |

Park within **18 inches** of the curb. Curb colors: red, no stopping; yellow, loading; white, passenger pickup; green, time limit; blue, disabled placard or plate only.

## bicycle_passing

In {{STATE}}, if another lane in your direction is open, **change lanes** to pass a bicyclist when it is safe. If you stay in the same lane, give at least **3 feet** of space. If you cannot give 3 feet, slow down and wait. Before a right turn, you may enter a bike lane no more than **200 feet** before the corner, after checking for cyclists.

## crash_reporting

After a {{STATE}} collision, stop and exchange information. If anyone is hurt or killed, the driver reports it in writing to the **California Highway Patrol** or city police within **24 hours**. Every driver must report the crash to **DMV** on the **SR 1** form within **10 days** if anyone was injured or killed or if damage to any one person's property is over **$1,000**. Leaving an injury crash without stopping can cost you your license.

## bac_limits

{{STATE}} sets these limits:

| Driver | Limit (BAC) |
|---|---|
| Age 21 and older | 0.08 percent |
| Under 21 | 0.01 percent |
| On DUI probation | 0.01 percent |
| Commercial vehicle or passengers for hire | 0.04 percent |

Under 21 at **0.01 or higher**: license suspended **one year**. You can be convicted of DUI below these limits if alcohol or drugs impair you.

## implied_consent

By driving in {{STATE}}, you have agreed to a breath, blood or urine test if arrested for DUI. Refusal: **one-year** suspension; **two-year** revocation for a second within 10 years; **three years** for a third. At **0.08 or higher**, a first offense brings an immediate **four-month** suspension. The officer may take your license and give you a **30-day** temporary license; you have **10 days** to request a DMV hearing. Under 21, refusing a preliminary screening test: one-year suspension.

## dui_penalties
First {{STATE}} DUI: **96 hours to 6 months** in jail (at least 48 hours in a row), a **$390 to $1,000** fine, a **6-month** suspension, a DUI program and proof of insurance (SR 22). A court may order an ignition interlock device. The conviction stays on your record **10 years**.

**Open container:** no driver or passenger may drink or hold an opened alcohol container, or use cannabis. Keep it in the trunk, never the glove box. Bus, taxi, camper and motorhome passengers are exempt.

## phone_laws

In {{STATE}}, adults may not hold a phone while driving. A phone may be **mounted** on the windshield, dashboard or center console and used with a **single swipe or tap**; otherwise use it hands-free only. Drivers **under 18** may not use a phone at all, even hands-free, except for emergency calls. Base fines: **$20** first offense, **$50** after. A second conviction within 36 months adds **one point**.

## seat_belts

{{STATE}} requires a seat belt for the **driver** and every passenger **16 or older**, in every seat. The driver gets the ticket for any unbelted passenger **under 16**. Base fine: $20 first offense, $50 after. Wear the shoulder belt across your chest and the lap belt low across your hips, never under your arm or behind your back.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} requires:

| Child | Required |
|---|---|
| Under 2 | Rear-facing seat, unless 40 pounds or 40 inches |
| Under 8 | Car seat or booster in a rear seat |
| 8 or older, or 4 feet 9 inches | Safety belt, only if it fits as described above; if not, a booster |

Never put a rear-facing seat in front of an active airbag. Never leave a child **6 or younger** alone in a car without someone **12 or older**.

## vehicle_rules

{{STATE}} owners must carry liability insurance of at least **$30,000** for one person's injury or death, **$60,000** per crash, and **$15,000** for property damage, and show proof when stopped. Turn on headlights in darkness, when you cannot see **1,000 feet**, and whenever wipers run continuously. Report an address change within **10 days**. Never wear earplugs or a headset in both ears, or smoke with a minor in the car.

## road_test

The {{STATE}} drive test starts with a pre-drive check of the car and controls. Only the examiner rides with you. The examiner scores parking lot driving, backing, intersections, turns, lane changes and street driving. **Critical driving errors** fail you at once: examiner intervention, hitting an object, running a sign or signal, unsafe moves, or 10 mph over or under the limit. Driver assistance features must be off. Minors wait **14 days** to retest; each retest costs **$9**.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers under 18 must complete before the drive test:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total supervised practice | 50 |
| At night | At least 10 |
| Driver training with a licensed instructor | 6 |

Night hours count toward the 50. The supervisor must be a {{STATE}}-licensed driver **25 or older**. A parent or guardian certifies the hours. You must also hold the permit **6 months**.

## signal_distance

{{STATE}} drivers signal at least **100 feet** before a turn. On a freeway, signal at least **five seconds** before changing lanes, and about five seconds (roughly **400 feet**) before your exit. Signal before every lane change and before pulling to or away from the curb, even when no one seems to be around.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, dim to low beams within **500 feet** of an oncoming vehicle and within **300 feet** of a vehicle you are following.
