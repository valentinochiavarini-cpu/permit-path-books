# California inserts

## school_zone

In {{STATE}}, the speed limit is **25 mph** within **500 feet** of a school while children are present, unless signs show a lower limit. A city or county may post a **20 mph** school zone. Its sign tells you when the limit applies: when flashing, when children are present, or during listed hours. Some streets near schools are posted at **15 mph**. Always obey crossing guards and school safety patrols.

## work_zone

In a {{STATE}} highway construction or maintenance zone, traffic fines **double** when workers are present, and they can reach **$1,000** or more. Some roads are posted as **Safety Enhanced Double Fine Zones**, where fines double too. Hitting or attacking a highway worker brings a fine up to **$2,000** and up to **one year** in jail. When a lane closes, merge early. Never drive across the cones or drums, and obey flaggers.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, you may turn **right on a steady red light** after a full stop at the limit line, unless a NO TURN ON RED sign is posted. Yield first to people in the crosswalk and to traffic close enough to be a danger. You may turn **left on red** only from a one-way street onto another one-way street, under the same rules.

A **red arrow** means no turn at all. Stay stopped until a green light or green arrow appears.

## emergency_vehicles

When a police car, fire engine or ambulance comes with its siren and red lights on, {{STATE}} law says pull to the **right-hand edge or curb**, clear of any intersection, and stop until it passes. One-way streets get no different edge. If you are in a carpool lane, leave it as soon as it is safe. Never follow within **300 feet** of an emergency vehicle using its siren or flashing lights. Driving to a fire or crash scene to watch can get you arrested.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Move Over law covers stopped emergency vehicles with their lights on and stopped tow trucks and road maintenance vehicles with flashing amber lights. It also covers **any stopped vehicle** showing hazard lights, flares or cones.

- If you can, move to a lane **not next to** the stopped vehicle.
- If changing lanes is unsafe or impossible, **slow to a safe speed**.

## school_bus

When a {{STATE}} school bus flashes its **red lights**, drivers from **both directions** must stop before reaching it. Stay stopped until the lights stop flashing. On a **divided highway**, or a road with **two or more lanes each way**, you need not stop if the bus is on the other side. {{STATE}} law sets no minimum stopping distance. Flashing **yellow** lights mean slow down and get ready to stop. Passing a stopped bus costs up to **$1,000** and can bring a **one-year** suspension.

## u_turn

In {{STATE}}, a U-turn is legal across a double yellow line, at an intersection on a green light unless a sign forbids it, and through an opening in a divided highway. In a residential district, make one only when no vehicle is within **200 feet**. Never make a U-turn:

- where you cannot see **200 feet** each way
- on a one-way street or at a railroad crossing
- in front of a fire station, or across a divider
- in a business district, except at an intersection or a median opening

## speed_limits

{{STATE}} speed limits unless a sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Alley, blind intersection, railroad crossing without a clear view | 15 mph |
| Business or residential district | 25 mph |
| Two-lane undivided highway, or towing a trailer | 55 mph |
| Most highways | 65 mph |

Some freeways are posted at **70 mph**. The **Basic Speed Law** still forbids driving faster than is safe for conditions, whatever the sign says.

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} handbook teaches the **three-second rule**. Pick a fixed marker. Start counting when the car ahead passes it, and slow down if you reach it before you count three. When you merge onto a highway, leave **three seconds** ahead too. Leave more room behind a motorcycle, when a tailgater is behind you, and on wet or icy roads.

## hov

{{STATE}} **HOV (carpool) lanes** are marked with a **diamond** and the words CARPOOL LANE. Signs list how many people you need and the hours the rule applies. Motorcycles may use them unless signs say otherwise. Enter and leave only at marked openings, never across **double solid white lines**. Clean air vehicle decals for solo drivers are **no longer valid**, so every vehicle must meet the posted rider rule.

## parking_rules

In {{STATE}}, do not park within these distances:

| Place | Distance |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant or fire station driveway | 15 feet |
| Approach side of a crosswalk | 20 feet (15 with a curb extension) |
| Sidewalk ramp for disabled persons | 3 feet |
| Nearest railroad rail | 7 and a half feet |

Park within **18 inches** of the curb. Curb colors: **red**, no stopping; **yellow**, loading; **white**, passenger pickup; **green**, time limit; **blue**, disabled placard or plate only.

## bicycle_passing

In {{STATE}}, if another lane in your direction is open, **change lanes** to pass a bicyclist when it is safe. If you stay in the same lane, give at least **3 feet**. If you cannot give 3 feet, slow down and wait. Before a right turn, you may enter a bike lane no more than **200 feet** before the corner, after checking for cyclists. Motorcyclists may legally ride between lanes of traffic (**lane splitting**), so check your mirrors and never drift over to block one.

## crash_reporting

After a {{STATE}} collision, stop and exchange information. If anyone is hurt or killed, the driver must report it to the **California Highway Patrol** or city police within **24 hours**. Every driver must also send DMV the **SR 1** form within **10 days** if anyone was hurt or killed, or if damage to any one person's property is over **$1,000**. Leaving an injury crash without stopping can cost you your license.

## bac_limits

{{STATE}} sets these limits:

| Driver | Illegal at a BAC of |
|---|---|
| Age 21 and older | 0.08 percent |
| Under 21 | 0.01 percent |
| On DUI probation | 0.01 percent |
| Commercial vehicle or passengers for hire | 0.04 percent |

Under 21, a BAC of **0.01 or higher** means a **one-year** suspension. You can be convicted of DUI below these limits if alcohol or drugs impair you.

## implied_consent

By driving in {{STATE}}, you agree to a breath, blood or urine test if arrested for DUI. A first **refusal** brings a **one-year** suspension. A second within 10 years brings a **two-year** revocation, and a third, **three years**. A first test at **0.08 or higher** brings an immediate **four-month** suspension. The officer may take your license and give you a **30-day** temporary one. You have **10 days** to ask DMV for a hearing. Under 21, refusing a roadside screening test also means a one-year suspension.

## dui_penalties

A first {{STATE}} DUI brings:

- **96 hours to 6 months** in jail, at least 48 hours in a row
- a fine of **$390 to $1,000**
- a **6-month** license suspension, a DUI program and proof of insurance (SR 22)

A court may also order an ignition interlock device. The conviction stays on your record **10 years**.

**Open container:** no driver or passenger may drink or hold an opened alcohol container, or use cannabis. Keep it in the trunk, never the glove box. Bus, taxi, camper and motorhome passengers are exempt.

## underage_alcohol

In {{STATE}}, a driver under 21 may not carry alcohol unless a parent, guardian, adult relative or an adult the parent named is along. The penalty is up to **$1,000** and **6 months** in jail, and your own car may be impounded up to **30 days**. Having alcohol in public, or using a **fake ID** to buy it, costs at least **$250** or **24 to 32 hours** of community service. At 13 to 20, a DUI conviction, even on a **bicycle**, means a **one-year** license suspension or delay.

## phone_laws

In {{STATE}}, adults may not hold a phone while driving. A phone may be **mounted** on the windshield, dashboard or center console and used with a **single swipe or tap**. Otherwise, use it hands-free only. Drivers **under 18** may not use a phone at all, even hands-free, except for emergency calls. Base fines are **$20** the first time and **$50** after that. A second conviction within 36 months also adds **one point** to your record.

## seat_belts

{{STATE}} requires a seat belt for the **driver** and for every passenger **16 or older**, in every seat. The driver gets the ticket for any unbelted passenger **under 16**. The base fine is **$20** the first time and **$50** after that.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} requires:

| Child | Required |
|---|---|
| Under 2 | Rear-facing seat, unless 40 pounds or 40 inches |
| Under 8 | Car seat or booster in a rear seat |
| 8 or older, or 4 feet 9 inches | Safety belt, only if it fits as described above; if not, a booster |

Never put a rear-facing seat in front of an active airbag. Never leave a child **6 or younger** alone in a car without someone **12 or older**.

## vehicle_rules

In {{STATE}}, tell DMV about a new address within **10 days**. Never drive wearing earplugs or a headset in **both ears**. Smoking in a car with a minor inside is against the law and can bring a fine.

## road_test

The {{STATE}} drive test starts with a check of the car and its controls. Only the examiner rides with you, and driver assistance features must be off. The examiner scores parking lot driving, backing, intersections, turns, lane changes and street driving. A **critical driving error** fails you at once: the examiner has to step in, you hit something, you run a sign or signal, you make an unsafe move, or you drive **10 mph** over or under the limit. Minors wait **14 days** to retest, and each retest costs **$9**.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers under 18 must complete this before the drive test:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total supervised practice | 50 |
| At night (part of the 50) | 10 |
| Driver training with a licensed instructor | 6 |

The supervisor must be a {{STATE}}-licensed driver **25 or older**. A parent or guardian certifies the hours. You must also hold the permit **6 months**.

## signal_distance

In {{STATE}}, signal at least **100 feet** before a turn. On a freeway, signal at least **five seconds** before you change lanes, and about five seconds (roughly **400 feet**) before your exit. Signal before every lane change and before pulling to or away from the curb, even when no one seems to be around.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, dim to low beams within **500 feet** of an oncoming vehicle and within **300 feet** of a vehicle you are following.

## headlight_use

In {{STATE}}, headlights must be on from **30 minutes after sunset** to **30 minutes before sunrise**. They must also be on when you cannot see a person or vehicle **1,000 feet** ahead. If rain, fog or snow makes you run your **wipers** nonstop, turn on your low beams too. Driving with only parking lights is illegal. Also turn on headlights on mountain roads, in tunnels, and wherever a sign says so.

## police_stop

The {{STATE}} handbook adds a few steps. Move completely onto the right shoulder, even if you were in the carpool lane, and stop in a well-lit area if you can. Roll your window down before the officer reaches you. You and every passenger keep your hands in clear view. The officer must tell you why you were stopped before asking questions. You must show your **license, proof of insurance and registration**. You may say no to a search, but never resist.

## driver_record

{{STATE}} counts points. Most moving violations and at-fault crashes count **1 point**. A DUI, hit-and-run or reckless driving counts **2**. DMV can suspend an adult as a **negligent operator** at **4 points in 12 months**, **6 in 24 months** or **8 in 36 months**. Convictions stay on your record **3 years** or longer. For a 1-point ticket, a judge may offer **traffic violator school**, once every **18 months**, to keep it from your insurer. Minimum insurance: **$30,000** per person, **$60,000** per crash, **$15,000** property. Teen limits are in Chapter 1.
