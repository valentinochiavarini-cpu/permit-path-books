# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

Getting a {{STATE}} license is a sequence: paperwork, a vision check, a knowledge test, practice and a drive test. Teens add a course, lessons and a waiting period. The {{STATE}} Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) runs every step under the {{STATE}} Vehicle Code. Fees and procedures change, so check dmv.ca.gov before each office visit.

> **On the test**
>
> - The minimum age for an instruction permit: 15 and a half.
> - Who may sit beside a permit holder: a licensed driver 25 or older for minors.
> - The 50 practice hours, 10 of them at night.
> - Provisional limits in the first 12 months: no driving from 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. and no passengers under 20 without a qualified adult.
> - The phone rule for drivers under 18.

## 1.1 The Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses **graduated licensing**. You earn independence in steps because new drivers crash most often in their first year alone at the wheel.

| Stage | Earliest age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Instruction permit | 15 and a half | Drive only with a qualified adult beside you, after lessons begin |
| Provisional license | 16 | Drive alone, with night and passenger limits for 12 months |
| Full privileges | 18 | Provisional status ends |

The ordinary car license is **Class C**. It covers passenger cars, pickups and most noncommercial vehicles. Motorcycles need a Class M license, and commercial vehicles need a commercial license.

## 1.2 Before You Apply

### Driver Education and Driver Training

{{STATE}} separates the two:

- **Driver education** is the classroom part, taken in person or online from a DMV-licensed school or a high school. The standard program is **30 hours**. You need a certificate of completion, or of enrollment, before you apply for a permit between 15 and a half and 17 and a half.
- **Driver training** is **6 hours** behind the wheel with a licensed instructor. You may start it only after driver education. Your permit is not valid until the instructor signs it at your first lesson.

If you are 17 and a half or older, you may get a permit without these certificates, but you cannot take the drive test until you turn 18.

### Documents

Start the **Driver's License and ID Card Application** online, then bring to your office appointment:

- **Proof of identity**, such as a U.S. birth certificate or valid passport (original or certified copy).
- **Two proofs of {{STATE}} residency**, such as a utility bill, lease or school record.
- **Your Social Security number** (exceptions apply).
- A legal name document if the name on your identity document differs from your application.

**Under 18:** a parent or guardian signs the application and, by signing, accepts financial responsibility for your driving. If parents share joint custody, both sign. A parent may later cancel a teen's license with a DMV form.

**REAL ID.** In {{STATE}}, REAL ID is your choice. You need a REAL ID card, or a passport, to board a domestic flight or enter most federal buildings; the document list is at dmv.ca.gov/realid. A standard license works fine for driving.

## 1.3 Fees

| Item | DMV fee |
|---|---|
| Original Class C license application (any age) | $46 |
| Drive test retest | $9 |
| Replacement license | $37 |

The $46 application fee covers the instruction permit, up to three attempts at the knowledge test and up to three drive tests. It stays valid for **12 months**. After that, or after three failures of the same test, you reapply and pay again. Driving schools set their own prices.

## 1.4 The Knowledge Test

The **knowledge test** is a multiple-choice exam built entirely from the {{STATE}} Driver's Handbook. Sign questions are mixed in with the rest; there is no separate sign section.

| Feature | The {{STATE}} rule |
|---|---|
| Questions | DMV has reported 46 under 18 and 36 for adults; current pages give no count |
| To pass | 80 percent; under 18, miss no more than 8 |
| Languages | More than 40 |
| Attempts | 3 per application |
| Retest wait, under 18 | 7 days, not counting the day you failed |

**Where to take it.** First-time applicants test at a DMV office, and the test must start before 4:30 p.m. DMV's online option, **eLearning**, is a course for license renewals only; it does not replace the permit test. No handbook, notes or phone may be used during the test.

The mock exams in this book have **46 questions** each, the count DMV has reported for applicants under 18. To pass, miss no more than 8: 38 of 46 correct.

> **Test trap:** {{STATE}} tests numbers you might skim: 25 for the supervising adult, 20 for the passenger age, 11 p.m. to 5 a.m., 50 and 10 practice hours, 100 feet for signals, 15 and 25 mph limits. Learn each one.

## 1.5 The Vision Test

The DMV screening standard is **20/40 with both eyes together**, or 20/40 in one eye and at least 20/70 in the other, with or without glasses or contacts. If you pass only with lenses, your license carries a **corrective lenses** restriction, and you must wear them whenever you drive.

If you do not pass, DMV asks your eye doctor to complete a **Report of Vision Examination (DL 62)**. DMV reviews it and may give you a drive test. Applicants who see 20/200 or worse in the better eye are not scheduled for a drive test.

## 1.6 Driving on an Instruction Permit

**Who rides with you.** Under 18, your supervisor must be a {{STATE}}-licensed driver **25 or older**: a parent, guardian, spouse, other adult or a licensed instructor. The person must sit close enough to take control of the car. At 18 or older, the supervisor needs to be at least 18.

**Lessons first.** A teen permit works only after your instructor validates it at the first training lesson. Until then, you may not practice on public roads.

### Before You Can Take the Drive Test

Under 18, you need all of these:

- At least **16** years old.
- An instruction permit held for at least **6 months**. Time on an earlier permit counts if you had to reapply, so bring both permits.
- Proof that you finished **driver education and driver training**.
- **50 hours** of supervised practice, at least **10 at night**. A parent or guardian certifies the hours and that you are ready.

> **Remember it:** 15 and a half, 6 hours of training, 6 months on the permit, 50 hours with 10 at night, and 25 years old for the person beside you. Record every practice drive in the log at the back of this book.

## 1.7 Your Provisional License

Until 18, your license says **provisional**. For the **first 12 months**, you may not:

| Restriction | Unless |
|---|---|
| Drive between 11 p.m. and 5 a.m. | A parent, guardian, licensed driver 25+ or instructor rides with you |
| Carry passengers under 20 | The same qualified adult rides with you |

You also may not drive for pay or drive any vehicle that needs a commercial license.

**Exceptions.** You may drive alone at night or carry a family member when there is a real need and no reasonable alternative transportation. Carry a signed note that explains it:

- **Medical:** a note from your doctor stating the condition and expected recovery date.
- **School or a school activity:** a note from your principal, dean or designee.
- **Work:** a note from your employer confirming your job.
- **Driving a family member:** a note from your parent or guardian giving the reason, the person and when the need ends.

**Phones.** Under 18, you may not use a phone or other wireless device while driving, even hands-free. Only emergency calls are allowed (see Chapter 11).

**Collisions and tickets.** In your first 12 months, DMV watches closely:

- One at-fault collision or conviction: DMV may take action on your record.
- Two: **30 days** of driving only with a licensed adult 25 or older.
- Three: **6-month suspension** and **1 year of probation**.

An alcohol or drug conviction between ages 13 and 20 brings a one-year suspension or a one-year delay in getting a license. Turning 18 does not erase a restriction already in place.

> **Test trap:** The passenger limit counts riders under 20, not under 18, and the qualified companion must be 25 or older. A 21-year-old friend does not lift either restriction.

## 1.8 The Drive Test

### Booking and What to Bring

Schedule the **behind-the-wheel drive test** online or by phone at 1-800-777-0133. Bring:

- Your instruction permit.
- A licensed driver (25 or older for minors) to ride with you to and from the office.
- A car that is safe to drive, with **valid proof of insurance and registration**. In a rental car, your name must be on the contract, and the contract must not exclude drive tests.

### The Pre-Drive Check

Before you leave, the examiner has you show the controls and checks the car: the driver's window opens, the windshield gives a clear view, there are at least two mirrors with one on the left, both brake lights work, the tires have tread and none is a spare donut, the horn works, the foot brake has room under it, and every seat belt works. You also show the parking brake, wipers, turn signals and the hand signals for left, right and stop. A car that fails gets the test rescheduled.

Only the examiner rides along. Self-parking, lane keeping and adaptive cruise control must be off. A backup camera may be used, but you still look over your shoulder.

### What the Examiner Scores

The examiner scores parking lot driving, backing straight along a curb for about three car lengths, intersections, turns, lane changes, and driving in business and residential areas. Expect two instructions at once, such as two turns in a row.

**Critical driving errors** fail the test on the spot:

- The examiner has to step in, by word or by action.
- Hitting an object, or driving onto a curb or sidewalk.
- Disobeying a sign or signal, such as rolling a stop sign faster than a brisk walk.
- Passing a stopped school bus with red lights flashing.
- A dangerous move that makes someone else swerve or brake.
- Driving 10 mph over or under the limit without a reason.
- Driving over 200 feet in a bike lane or center left turn lane.
- Skipping wipers, defroster or headlights when conditions require them.

If you fail, minors wait **14 days**, not counting the test day. Each retest costs **$9**.

## 1.9 Adults Getting a First License

At 18 or older, you skip driver education, driver training, the 6-month wait and the 50-hour certificate. You still apply, show documents, pay the $46 fee, pass the vision and knowledge tests, and get an instruction permit. Practice with a licensed driver 18 or older beside you, then take the same drive test teens take. Your license has no provisional limits.

## 1.10 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

**Moving from another state.** Unlike most states, {{STATE}} tests new residents. Once you become a resident, you have **10 days** to get a {{STATE}} license. Apply online, then visit an office with your documents and pay the fee. You will take the vision test and the **knowledge test**. DMV may waive the drive test once it checks your out-of-state record and finds no suspension or hold.

**Licensed in another country.** {{STATE}} has no license exchange agreement with any country. You take the knowledge test and the **drive test**, follow the same document steps, and show proof of insurance for the drive test. A licensed driver must come with you to and from the test. A visitor 18 or older may drive on a valid license from their home country while still a resident there.

**AB 60 licenses.** {{STATE}} issues driver's licenses to residents who cannot prove lawful presence in the United States, under a law known as AB 60. Applicants prove identity and {{STATE}} residency, pass the same vision, knowledge and drive tests, and follow the same teen rules. The card states that it is not acceptable for official federal purposes, and it cannot be a REAL ID. By law, it may not be used as evidence of citizenship or immigration status, and businesses, employers and government agencies may not discriminate against someone for holding it.

## 1.11 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **{{STATE}} Driver's Handbook**, the source of every test question, is free at dmv.ca.gov in several languages, with sample tests and appointments. Where this book and the current Vehicle Code differ, the law wins. Check the site the week before each test.

## Quick Review

- Instruction permit at 15 and a half, after driver education; 6 hours of training validate it.
- The knowledge test is multiple choice; you need 80 percent, with 3 tries per application.
- Before the drive test: 16 years old, 6 months on the permit, 50 practice hours with 10 at night.
- First 12 months: no driving from 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. and no passengers under 20 without a qualified adult.
- Drivers under 18 may not use a phone at all while driving, except to call for help.
- New residents take the knowledge test; foreign license holders also take the drive test.
