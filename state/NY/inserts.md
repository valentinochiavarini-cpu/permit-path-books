# New York inserts

## school_zone

In {{STATE}}, school speed limits are set locally and posted on the sign. They apply on school days during the hours shown, which fall between 7 a.m. and 6 p.m., or whenever the school zone beacons flash.

A **U-turn is never allowed** in a school zone. Obey every school crossing guard as you would a police officer.

## work_zone

In a {{STATE}} work zone, the minimum speeding fines are **doubled**, even when no workers are there. Speeding in a work zone adds **8 points** to your record.

A work zone speed limit can be posted as low as **25 mph**. Where no limit is posted, you must still slow down. Always obey the flag person.

The Move Over law also covers road crew vehicles with amber lights.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, you may turn **right on red** after a full stop, unless a sign, signal or marking forbids it. You may turn **left on red** only from a one-way street onto another one-way street. Yield to pedestrians and cross traffic first.

**No turn is ever allowed on a red arrow.**

In **New York City**, turning on red is illegal everywhere unless a sign allows it.

## move_over

On every {{STATE}} road, slow down for a stopped police, fire or ambulance vehicle with emergency lights on. The same goes for a tow truck, snowplow or other hazard vehicle with amber lights, and a volunteer's car with a blue or green light.

On a **parkway, interstate or other controlled-access highway** with two or more lanes your way, also **move out of the lane next to it** when you safely can. There the law covers **any stopped vehicle**, with or without lights. A violation is a moving violation.

## school_bus

In {{STATE}}, when a school bus flashes its **red lights**, traffic from **both directions** must stop, on every road, **even on a divided highway**, and in school parking lots. The manual says to stop **at least 20 feet** away. Wait until the bus moves or its driver or a police officer waves you on.

Passing a stopped bus: **8 points** and a **$250 to $400** fine the first time. A third conviction within three years: up to **$1,500** and a license revoked at least six months.

## u_turn

In {{STATE}}, start a U-turn from the **left part of the lane nearest the center line**, never from the right lane. You may not make one:

- where drivers cannot see you from **500 feet** away in either direction;
- in a **business district of New York City**;
- on a **limited access expressway**, even at a median crossover;
- in a **school zone** or where a NO U-TURN sign is posted.

A green left arrow allows a U-turn unless a sign forbids it.

## speed_limits

Unless a sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Anywhere in {{STATE}} with no posted limit | 55 mph |
| New York City | 25 mph |
| Some rural expressways (posted) | Up to 65 mph |

Speeding tickets add points to your record:

| Over the limit by | Points |
|---|---|
| 1 to 10 mph | 3 |
| 11 to 20 mph | 4 |
| 21 to 30 mph | 6 |
| 31 to 40 mph | 8 |
| More than 40 mph | 11 |

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} manual teaches a following distance of at least **two seconds**. In bad weather or behind a large truck, make it **three or four seconds**. In a work zone, keep at least two seconds.

Following too closely adds **4 points** to your record.

## hov

In {{STATE}}, a white **diamond** marks a lane reserved for buses, carpools (HOV), bicycles or other special vehicles. Stay out of it unless your vehicle meets the rule on the signs during the hours shown.

On some city streets, the solid line beside a diamond lane becomes broken near a corner. There, any driver may enter the lane to turn right at the next intersection.

## parking_rules

In {{STATE}}, never park closer than these distances:

| Place | Feet |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant (unless a licensed driver stays in the car) | 15 |
| Crosswalk at an intersection | 20 |
| Fire station driveway (75 across the street) | 20 |
| Traffic light, STOP or YIELD sign | 30 |
| Pedestrian safety area | 30 |
| Railroad crossing | 50 |

Never block a lowered curb. After you parallel park, your wheels must be within **one foot** of the curb.

## bicycle_passing

{{STATE}} law sets no number of feet. Pass a bicycle on its **left at a safe distance**, and stay over until you are well clear. Slow down as you pass, because the rush of air can push a rider off balance.

Never pass a bicycle inside a roundabout. Riders and passengers aged **1 to 13** must wear a helmet.

## crash_reporting

After a {{STATE}} crash, stop. Show your license and insurance card, and give your name, address and insurance details. If someone is hurt and no officer is there, or the owner of a damaged car is not there, report to the nearest police station as soon as you can.

File a **Report of Motor Vehicle Crash (MV-104)** with DMV within **10 days** if anyone was hurt or killed, or if one person's property damage is **$1,000 or more**. Not filing it is a misdemeanor.

## bac_limits

In {{STATE}}, these BAC levels apply:

| Offense | BAC |
|---|---|
| Driving while ability impaired (DWAI) | More than 0.05 |
| Driving while intoxicated (DWI) | 0.08 or more |
| Aggravated DWI | 0.18 or more |
| Zero tolerance, under 21 | 0.02 to 0.07 |

Under 21 at 0.02 to 0.07, your license is suspended for **six months**. To get it back, you pay a **$125** civil penalty and a **$100** fee.

## implied_consent

In {{STATE}}, if you refuse a chemical test after an arrest, your license is **suspended at your court arraignment**. If a DMV hearing confirms the refusal, the license is **revoked**, even if a court finds you not guilty.

- First refusal: at least **one year**, plus a **$500** civil penalty.
- Within five years of an earlier refusal or alcohol or drug offense: at least **18 months**, plus **$750**.
- Under 21, second refusal: one year or until 21, whichever is longer.

## dui_penalties

| First {{STATE}} offense | Fine | Jail | License |
|---|---|---|---|
| DWAI | $300 to $500 | Up to 15 days | Suspended 90 days |
| DWI | $500 to $1,000 | Up to 1 year | Revoked 6+ months |
| Aggravated DWI | $1,000 to $2,500 | Up to 1 year | Revoked 1+ year |

DWI adds an **ignition interlock**, usually 12 months. With a child under 16 aboard, DWI is a felony. Under 21, any alcohol or drug driving conviction revokes your license at least **one year**.

**Open container:** no one in the car may drink alcohol, hold an open container or use cannabis.

## phone_laws

In {{STATE}}, you may not talk on a **hand-held phone** while driving, or use any **portable electronic device** to text, email or browse. Hands-free and emergency calls are allowed.

Each violation adds **5 points**. Fines: up to **$200** for a first offense, **$250** for a second within 18 months and **$450** for a third. A junior or probationary driver's license is suspended **120 days** after a first conviction.

## seat_belts

{{STATE}} is a **primary enforcement** state. The **driver** and every passenger **16 or older**, in any seat, must wear a belt, one person per belt. Each unbelted person can be fined up to **$50**.

The driver must make sure every passenger **under 16** is belted or in a child seat. Each violation costs the driver **$25 to $100** and **3 points**.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} requires:

| Child | Restraint |
|---|---|
| Under 2 | Rear-facing seat, until its limits are outgrown |
| Under 4 | Federally approved child safety seat |
| 4 to 7 | Booster or other child seat, with lap and shoulder belt |
| 8 to 15 | Seat belt |

A child under 8 who is over **4 feet 9 inches** or **100 pounds** may use the seat belt.

## vehicle_rules

In {{STATE}}, a car needs a safety inspection **every year**.

If your insurance will end, turn in your plates to DMV before it does. Driving uninsured for **90 days** suspends your license.

Report an address change to DMV within **10 days**. A new resident must register the car within **30 days**.

## road_test

The {{STATE}} examiner scores leaving the curb, turns and intersections, parallel parking, a **three-point turn**, driving in traffic and vehicle control. You may not use parking assist or cruise control.

Bring your permit and course certificate. Under 18, also bring the signed **MV-262**. Only your accompanying driver may ride along.

Results post online after 6 p.m. After a fail, wait **14 days** to retest. If you fail your first two tests, later tests cost extra.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers under 18 need these hours before the road test:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total | 50 |
| After sunset | At least 15 |
| Moderate to heavy traffic | 10 recommended |

Night hours and driver education hours count toward the 50. A parent or guardian signs the **Certification of Supervised Driving (MV-262)**. Every supervisor must be 21 or older and licensed for the vehicle. Chapter 1 lists the regional limits on who may supervise.

## signal_distance

In {{STATE}}, signal at least **100 feet** before you turn or change lanes, with your turn signal or a hand signal. Signal at least 100 feet before an expressway exit ramp, too.

When you can, signal before you start to brake.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, use low beams within **500 feet** of an oncoming vehicle. Use them within **200 feet** of a vehicle you are following, even if it is in another lane.

## emergency_vehicles

In {{STATE}}, when an emergency vehicle with red lights comes from **any direction**, even in the opposite lane, pull to the **right edge** of the road and stop. On a one-way road with **three or more lanes**, you may use either edge. If you are in an intersection, drive out of it first.

Hear a siren but cannot see it? Pull right and stop until you know where it is going.

Never follow a fire truck with its lights on closer than **200 feet**.

## headlight_use

{{STATE}} requires headlights from **a half hour after sunset to a half hour before sunrise**. You also need them whenever you cannot see **1,000 feet** ahead, and whenever your **wipers** are on for rain, snow, sleet or fog.

**Daytime running lights** and parking lights do not count. Turn your headlights on at dawn, at dusk and in fog.

## police_stop

In {{STATE}}, you must obey a police officer's lawful orders. When the officer asks, show:

- your **driver license** (failing to show one is treated as evidence that you are not licensed);
- the vehicle's **registration certificate** (a photocopy is accepted).

Keep your **insurance ID card** in the car as well. You must show it after any crash.

## underage_alcohol

In {{STATE}}, no one under 21 may have alcohol in order to drink it. The court can order up to a **$50** fine, 30 hours of community service or an alcohol awareness program.

Using a fake ID, or someone else's, to buy alcohol is a violation: up to **$100** and 30 hours of community service the first time. If you used a **driver license**, the court can suspend it: **3 months** the first time, **6 months** the second, then **1 year** or until you turn 21, whichever is longer.

## driver_record

In {{STATE}}, **11 points** within **24 months** can suspend your license. Points count from the violation date for 24 months.

Some point values: any alcohol or drug driving offense, **11**; passing a stopped school bus or speeding in a work zone, **8**; running a light, STOP or YIELD sign, **3**.

A DMV-approved crash prevention course takes up to **4 points** off the suspension count.

**6 points** within 18 months also brings a yearly Driver Responsibility Assessment for three years.

Minimum insurance: **$25,000/$50,000** injury, **$50,000/$100,000** death, **$10,000** property damage.
