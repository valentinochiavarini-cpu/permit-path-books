# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

Getting a {{STATE}} license follows a set order: permit, practice, a short safety course, then a road test. For teens, where you drive also changes when and with whom you may drive. The **{{STATE}} Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV)** runs licensing under the Vehicle and Traffic Law. Rules and fees change, so check dmv.ny.gov before each visit.

> **On the test**
>
> - The 20-question permit test: 14 correct to pass, including 2 of the 4 sign questions.
> - The age and license of the person supervising a learner.
> - The 50 practice hours, 15 of them after sunset.
> - Junior permit and junior license hours: 5 a.m. to 9 p.m.
> - The limit of one passenger under 21 who is not family.

## 1.1 The Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses **graduated licensing**: new drivers earn freedom in steps, because the first months alone behind the wheel carry the highest crash risk.

| Stage | Earliest age | What it allows |
|---|---|---|
| Junior learner permit (DJ) | 16 | Practice with a qualified supervisor |
| Junior license (Class DJ) | 16, after 6 months on the permit | Limited driving alone |
| Senior license (Class D) | 17 with driver education, otherwise 18 | Full privileges |

A **Class D** license covers vehicles rated 26,000 pounds or less; a **junior license**, 10,000 pounds or less. Anyone 18 or older gets a regular learner permit instead of a junior one, with fewer limits (see 1.10).

## 1.2 What to Bring

You apply at a DMV office with the **Application for Driver License (MV-44)**. Bring originals or certified copies, with a certified English translation of anything not in English.

- **Proof of name worth at least six points.** DMV scores each document; a U.S. passport counts 4, a Social Security card 2, a credit card or utility bill 1. At least one must show your signature. The full list is on form **ID-44**.
- **Proof of date of birth**, such as a certified birth certificate or a U.S. passport.
- **Your Social Security number.**
- **Two proofs of {{STATE}} address** if you want a REAL ID or Enhanced card.

**Under 18:** a parent or guardian comes with you to the office and signs in front of DMV staff. A parent can also vouch for your identity on the **Statement of Identity by Parent/Guardian (MV-45)**, worth 4 points. Do not sign it before you arrive.

**Which card to choose.** You pick one of three:

- **Standard:** marked "Not for Federal Purposes"; it will not get you through airport security on its own.
- **REAL ID:** accepted for domestic flights; needs proof of citizenship or lawful status.
- **Enhanced:** for U.S. citizens; also works at land and sea crossings from Canada, Mexico and the Caribbean; costs $30 extra.

**The Green Light law.** Residents 16 and older may apply for a standard permit and license regardless of citizenship or lawful status. Applicants who were never issued a Social Security number sign a sworn statement instead. The same knowledge and road tests apply.

## 1.3 Fees

The total you pay at the permit stage covers the application, your permit and your first license. It depends on your age and on where you live, because residents of New York City and seven nearby counties also pay a small transit fee.

| Your age when you apply | Most counties | NYC area counties |
|---|---|---|
| 16 to 16 and a half | $80.00 | $90.00 |
| 17 to 17 and a half | $92.50 | $102.50 |
| 21 and older | $64.25 to $67.50 | $73.25 to $77.50 |

Other ages fall in between; the DMV fee chart lists each. An Enhanced card adds $30. If you fail your first two road tests, more tests cost extra. Course fees are set by the school.

## 1.4 The Permit Test

The **written test** covers rules of the road, safe driving, road signs and the alcohol and drug laws.

| Feature | The {{STATE}} rule |
|---|---|
| Questions | 20, multiple choice |
| To pass | 14 correct |
| Road signs | At least 2 of the 4 sign questions correct |
| Languages | English and 19 others |

> **Test trap:** Score 16 of 20 and you still fail if 3 of your 4 misses are sign questions. Study signs as their own subject in Chapter 2.

**Where to take it.**

- **At a DMV office**, on a touch screen or on paper.
- **Online from home.** You upload your documents, then take the test on a computer with a working webcam, which photographs you during the test; you must be alone in the frame. A parent or guardian supervises applicants under 18. Online you need **80 percent** of all questions and **half** the sign questions. Fail, and you may try again after **6 hours**. Pass, and you book an office visit to show your original documents, have your photo taken and pay. Give DMV three business days to review your results, or you will be retested at the office.
- **At some high schools.** Students 15 years and 11 months or older may take it through the school's online testing program, then apply for the permit at 16.

## 1.5 The Vision Test

Before the written test you take a **vision test**. You need **20/40 vision in at least one eye**, with or without glasses or contacts. If you need lenses to pass, your license will say so, and you must wear them every time you drive. If you cannot pass it, or you wear special lenses, call a DMV Call Center for next steps.

## 1.6 Driving on a Junior Learner Permit

Every learner, at any age, needs a supervising driver who is **21 or older**, holds a license valid for that vehicle, and sits in the front seat. With a junior permit, nobody else may sit in front.

When and with whom depends on where you are driving:

| Region | 5 a.m. to 9 p.m. | 9 p.m. to 5 a.m. |
|---|---|---|
| Upstate (all other counties) | Any qualified supervisor 21+ | Parent, guardian, driver ed teacher or driving school instructor only |
| Long Island (Nassau, Suffolk) | Parent, guardian, driver ed teacher or driving school instructor | No driving |
| New York City (5 boroughs) | Same people, in a car with dual brakes | No driving |

**Passengers.** No more than **one passenger under 21** who is not immediate family, unless your supervisor is your parent, guardian, driver education teacher or driving school instructor. Everyone wears a seat belt.

**Roads closed to every learner:** DMV road test areas, any street inside a New York City park, bridges and tunnels run by the Triborough Bridge and Tunnel Authority, and the Cross County, Hutchinson River, Saw Mill River and Taconic State parkways in Westchester County.

## 1.7 Before Your Road Test

**The course.** Everyone first finishes an approved course. You have two choices:

- The **5-hour pre-licensing course**, a classroom lesson on risk, habits and alcohol. It includes no driving. Ages 16 and up take it in a classroom or live online class; ages 18 and up may also take it fully online. The classroom certificate (**MV-278**) is good for one year.
- A state-approved **driver education course** at a high school or college, which covers the same requirement and leads to a **Student Certificate of Completion (MV-285)**.

**Under 18, you also need:**

- **Six months** holding the junior permit. Time while it is suspended does not count.
- **50 hours** of supervised practice, at least **15 after sunset**. Your parent or guardian signs the **Certification of Supervised Driving (MV-262)**; driver education hours count toward the 50. DMV also recommends 10 hours in moderate to heavy traffic.

> **Remember it:** 16 for the permit, 6 months of waiting, 50 hours with 15 after dark, and 5 hours in class. Log every drive at the back of this book.

## 1.8 Driving on a Junior License

Pass the road test before 18 and you get a **junior license**. Its limits again depend on the region:

| Region | 5 a.m. to 9 p.m. | 9 p.m. to 5 a.m. |
|---|---|---|
| Upstate | You may drive alone | Alone only straight between home and work or a school course; otherwise with a parent, guardian, driver ed teacher or instructor |
| Long Island | Alone only straight between home and work, a work-study program, college or evening high school classes, farm work or driver education; otherwise supervised | The same list minus regular work |
| New York City | No driving | No driving |

Driving to work alone needs proof: carry a **Certificate of Employment (MV-58A)** filled out by your employer. The one-passenger rule for people under 21 who are not family still applies.

**Penalties.** One violation worth 3 or more points, or two smaller ones, suspends a junior permit or license for **60 days**. A cell phone or texting conviction suspends it for **120 days**.

**Getting out from under the limits.** Finish driver education and you can trade your junior license for a **senior license** at 17, which also lets you drive in New York City. Otherwise the limits last until 18.

> **Test trap:** A junior license holder may never drive in the five boroughs, even with a parent along. If you will drive mostly in the city, DMV suggests waiting to take the road test until you turn 18.

## 1.9 The Road Test

**Booking.** Schedule online or by phone at 1-518-402-2100. You need your permit and your course completion on file. The first open date is often three to five weeks away, and you may test anywhere in the state.

**Bring:**

- Your photo learner permit and any glasses your permit requires.
- Your original MV-278 if you took the 5-hour course in a classroom.
- Under 18: the signed MV-262, every time you test.
- A car that is registered, inspected and insured, with permanent plates, in good working order, and with a clean, working passenger seat belt for the examiner.
- A licensed driver: 21 or older if you drive yourself there. Only that driver may come in the car.

**What is scored.** The examiner records errors in five groups:

- **Leaving the curb:** checking traffic and your blind spot, signaling.
- **Turns and intersections:** judgment, waiting near the center for a left turn.
- **Parking, backing and turnabouts:** parallel parking close to the curb and a **three-point turn** without extra moves.
- **Driving in traffic:** keeping right, following distance, speed, yielding to pedestrians, lane changes.
- **Vehicle control:** steering, braking, smooth speed, reacting to hazards.

DMV does not publish a list of automatic fails. Results are posted online after 6 p.m. that day. If you pass, you print an **interim license** and your photo license arrives by mail in about two weeks. If you fail, your permit stays valid and you must wait at least **14 days** to retest.

## 1.10 Adults Getting a First License

At 18 or older you still need a permit, the written and vision tests, the 5-hour course and a road test. You skip the six-month wait, the supervised hours form and the regional limits. Your supervisor must still be 21 or older. Every new driver who is 18 or older is on **probation for six months**. Speeding, reckless driving, following too closely, a speed contest, driving while impaired by alcohol, or any two violations in that time bring a **60-day suspension**; a cell phone or texting conviction brings **120 days**.

## 1.11 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

After you become a {{STATE}} resident you have **30 days** to get a {{STATE}} license. Living here 90 days is presumed to make you a resident.

| Your current license | What you need |
|---|---|
| U.S. state, territory or Canadian province, issued 6+ months ago | Vision test and documents; turn in your old license |
| Same, but issued less than 6 months ago or expired 2 years or more | Written test, course and road test |
| Any other country | Vision and written tests, 5-hour course and road test |

A foreign license is handed to the examiner when you pass the road test. Visitors may drive on a valid home license; an International Driving Permit is optional but helps police read it.

## 1.12 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **{{STATE}} State Driver's Manual (MV-21)** is free at dmv.ny.gov, with practice quizzes, forms and office locations. Where the printed manual and current rules differ, the law and the DMV website win.

## Quick Review

- Junior permit at 16; the test has 20 questions, 14 correct to pass, and at least 2 of the 4 sign questions.
- You need 20/40 vision in at least one eye.
- Every learner needs a supervisor 21 or older; junior permit holders on Long Island and in NYC may not drive from 9 p.m. to 5 a.m.
- Before the road test under 18: 6 months, 50 hours (15 after sunset) and the 5-hour course or driver education.
- Junior license holders may not drive in the five boroughs; driver education allows a senior license at 17.
- New residents with a U.S. or Canadian license exchange it within 30 days with a vision test.
