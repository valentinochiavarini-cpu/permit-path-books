# Texas inserts

## school_zone

In a {{STATE}} school zone, the lower speed on the School Zone sign applies while its **yellow lights flash**, or during the hours the sign lists. Many zones also post a sign that says **using a wireless device is prohibited**. In a school crossing zone, you may not use a handheld phone while the lower limit is in effect. You may use it only when stopped or in an emergency. Always obey crossing guards and police directing traffic.

## work_zone

In a {{STATE}} construction or maintenance work zone, traffic **fines double** when workers are present. Ignoring a work zone warning sign or driving around a barricade costs up to **$200**, and that fine doubles too when workers are there. When a flagger says stop, stop in your lane. Go only when the flagger waves you on. Driving around a barricade where **water covers the road** is a Class B misdemeanor. The penalty is a fine up to **$2,000**, up to **180 days** in jail, or both.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, you may turn **right** on a **steady red light** after a full stop. You may turn **left** on a steady red light only from a **one-way street onto another one-way street**. Before either turn, yield to people in the crosswalk and to traffic with the green. A posted sign can forbid both turns.

The law allows these turns only on a steady red light. It says nothing about a **red arrow**, so treat a red arrow as no turn. Wait for a green arrow or a green light.

## emergency_vehicles

For an emergency vehicle using its siren or lights, {{STATE}} law says pull to the **right-hand edge or curb**, clear of any intersection, and stop until it passes. One-way streets get no different edge. If you cannot pull right, slow down and leave a clear path. Stay at least **500 feet** behind a fire truck answering an alarm, or an ambulance with red lights flashing. Do not drive or park on the block where a fire truck has stopped for an alarm.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Slow Down or Move Over law covers stopped police, fire and ambulance vehicles, tow trucks, utility vehicles and TxDOT or other road work vehicles with their lights on. On a road with two or more lanes your way, **leave the lane next to them**. If you cannot, **slow to 20 mph below the limit**. Where the limit is under 25 mph, slow to **5 mph**. A first offense costs **$500 to $1,250**. A repeat within five years costs $1,000 to $2,000 and can bring a suspension.

## school_bus

In {{STATE}}, stop **before you reach** a school bus with flashing red lights, from either direction. The law sets no distance in feet. Stay stopped until the bus moves, the driver signals you on, or the red lights stop. You need not stop if the bus is on a **separate roadway** of a divided highway, past a barrier, an unpaved strip or a raised median. A center turn lane does not count. A first offense costs **$500 to $1,250**. A repeat within five years costs $1,000 to $2,000.

## u_turn

{{STATE}} bans a U-turn wherever a NO U-TURN sign is posted. You also may not turn around on a curve or near the top of a hill if drivers coming from either direction cannot see your car from **500 feet** away.

## speed_limits

{{STATE}} speed limits when no sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Alley | 15 mph |
| Public beach (where declared) | 15 mph |
| Urban district | 30 mph |
| Road outside a city without a state or U.S. number | 60 mph |
| Numbered state or U.S. highway outside a city | 70 mph |

Signs on some highways allow **75 to 85 mph**. The same limits apply day and night.

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} Driver Handbook measures following distance in seconds. In good conditions at **30 mph or less**, stay at least **2 seconds** behind the vehicle ahead. **Above 30 mph**, stay **4 seconds** behind. As a new driver, use **4 seconds** at any speed. Leave **4 to 6 seconds** behind a motorcycle. Add more time on wet, crowded or dark roads.

## hov

{{STATE}}'s HOV lanes are mostly in its largest cities. Each one posts its own rules at the entrance: how many riders you need, the hours and the direction. For example, a sign may reserve a lane for **buses and carpools from 6 a.m. to 9 a.m., Monday through Friday**. Some managed lanes also charge a toll. Read the signs before you enter. Enter and leave only at the marked openings.

## parking_rules

In {{STATE}}, do not park within these distances:

| Place | Feet |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant | 15 |
| Crosswalk at an intersection | 20 |
| Fire station driveway, same side | 20 |
| Fire station entrance, across the street | 75 |
| Stop sign, yield sign or traffic signal at the roadside | 30 |
| Nearest rail of a railroad crossing | 50 |

When you parallel park, your wheels must be within **18 inches** of the curb. Parking in a disability space without a plate or placard costs **$500 to $750** the first time.

## bicycle_passing

{{STATE}} law sets **no statewide minimum distance** for passing a bicycle. Leave a safe gap, and change lanes to pass when you can. A cyclist may use the full lane when it is **under 14 feet wide** or too narrow to share. A cyclist may also move left to avoid hazards or to turn left. Some cities set their own passing distance. San Antonio, for example, requires **3 feet** for cars and **6 feet** for large trucks.

## crash_reporting

In {{STATE}}, if no one is hurt and the cars can be driven, move them off the main lanes. A shoulder, frontage road or cross street works. Call police when anyone is hurt or killed, a car cannot be moved, a driver leaves, or a driver seems drunk or on drugs. Show your driver license if another driver asks. {{STATE}} drivers no longer file their own crash report with the state, so keep your own notes and photos.

## bac_limits

In {{STATE}}, the limits are:

| Driver | Illegal to drive with |
|---|---|
| 21 and older | A BAC of **0.08** or more, or any amount that takes away normal use of mind or body |
| Under 21 | **Any detectable amount** of alcohol |

Under 21, any detectable alcohol is the crime of Driving Under the Influence by a Minor, even far below 0.08.

## implied_consent

By driving in {{STATE}}, you agree to give a breath or blood sample if you are arrested for DWI. If you **refuse**, your license is suspended for **180 days**, or up to **2 years** for a repeat. If your test shows **0.08 or more**, the suspension is **90 days**, or up to 1 year for a repeat. Drivers under 21 get the same 180 days for refusing. A first test showing any detectable alcohol suspends a minor's license for **60 days**.

## dui_penalties

| {{STATE}} DWI | Fine | Jail or prison |
|---|---|---|
| First | Up to $2,000 | 72 hours to 180 days |
| Second | Up to $4,000 | 30 days to 1 year |
| Third | Up to $10,000 | 2 to 10 years |

The license suspension is **90 days to 1 year** for a first DWI, and **180 days to 2 years** after that. An **open container** of alcohol in the passenger area on a public road is illegal, with a fine up to **$500**. With a first DWI, an open container raises the minimum jail time to **6 days**.

## underage_alcohol

In {{STATE}}, a person under 21 may not buy or try to buy alcohol, drink it or have it. Lying about your age or showing a **fake ID** to get it is also a crime. Each costs your license, car or not. A first offense brings a fine up to **$500**, **8 to 12 hours** of community service, an alcohol course and a **30-day** suspension. A second brings a **60-day** suspension. A first DUI by a Minor brings up to $500, **20 to 40 hours** of service and the course.

## phone_laws

{{STATE}} bans every driver from **reading, writing or sending text messages** on a handheld device while driving. The fine is up to **$99** the first time and up to **$200** after that. You may text when stopped, use voice (hands-free) features, use navigation and call in an emergency. In a **school crossing zone**, no handheld phone use at all. Drivers **under 18** may not use any wireless device, even hands-free, except in an emergency. School bus drivers carrying children may not use a phone unless stopped.

## seat_belts

In {{STATE}}, the driver and **every passenger in every seat** must wear a seat belt, in pickups and SUVs too. An unbelted driver, or an unbelted passenger 15 or older, is fined **$25 to $50**. For each unbelted passenger under 17, the **driver** is fined **$100 to $200**. No one under 18 may ride in the open bed of a pickup or flatbed truck, except in a few cases such as parades and emergencies.

## child_restraints

In {{STATE}}, a child **under 8** must ride in a child safety seat, used as its maker instructs. The exception is a child **taller than 4 feet 9 inches**, who must wear a seat belt instead. The fine is **$25 to $250**.

## vehicle_rules

{{STATE}} no longer requires a yearly safety inspection for most cars. Instead, you pay a **$7.50** inspection program fee when you register. **Emissions testing** is still required in 18 counties around Houston, Dallas and Fort Worth, Austin, El Paso and San Antonio. Register a newly bought car within **30 days**. Report a new address to DPS within **30 days**. Tires need at least **2/32 inch** of tread.

## road_test

You take the {{STATE}} road test at a DPS office or an approved driving school. Bring a printed ITTD or ITAD certificate dated within the last **90 days**. Only you and the examiner ride along, plus an interpreter if needed. You are graded on control, observation, lane position and signaling. You may parallel park (backup camera allowed, self-parking not), stop quickly from about **20 mph** and back up about **60 feet**. You fail for breaking a law, refusing an instruction, driving dangerously, a crash, or losing **more than 30 points**.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} teen driver education includes this time in the car before a provisional license:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| In-car observation | 7 |
| In-car instruction | 7 |
| Supervised practice, total | 30 |
| Practice at night (part of the 30) | 10 |

Your practice supervisor must be a licensed driver **21 or older** with at least one year of driving experience. A parent or guardian certifies the hours. You must also hold the learner license for at least **six months**.

## signal_distance

{{STATE}} law requires you to signal without a break for at least the **last 100 feet** before you turn. Signal before you slow down or stop, too. Turn the signal off when you finish.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, switch to low beams within **500 feet** of an oncoming vehicle and within **300 feet** of a vehicle you are following. Also use low beams on lighted roads and in fog, heavy rain, sleet, snow or dust.

## headlight_use

In {{STATE}}, headlights must be on from **30 minutes after sunset** to **30 minutes before sunrise**. They must also be on whenever you cannot clearly see people or vehicles **1,000 feet** ahead, as in heavy rain or fog. {{STATE}} law has no separate wipers rule, so the 1,000-foot rule is the one to apply in bad weather. If you must park on an unlit highway at night, leave your parking lights or low beams on. Breaking a lighting rule can cost a fine of up to **$200**.

## police_stop

The {{STATE}} handbook adds a few steps. Stop as far right as you can, or on a nearby well-lit side street. Set the parking brake and turn on your hazard lights. Tell the officer if you carry any weapon. The law requires you to show your driver license when an officer asks. Not sure it is a real police car? Turn on your hazards, drive slowly and call **911** while you head for a well-lit, busy place.

## driver_record

{{STATE}} has **no point system**. Instead, DPS can suspend you after a hearing as a **habitual violator**: **4 or more** moving violation convictions in **12 months**, or **7 or more** in **24 months**. Under 18, the bar is lower (Chapter 1).

Liability insurance minimums are **$30,000** for one person hurt, **$60,000** for two or more, and **$25,000** for property damage. Show proof, on a card or phone, to police and after a crash. Driving without it costs **$175 to $350** the first time, then **$350 to $1,000** plus a suspension.
