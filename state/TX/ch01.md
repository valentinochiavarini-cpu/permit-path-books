# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

In {{STATE}}, the license process starts in a classroom, not at the counter. Anyone under 25 must finish an approved driver education course, and teens move through two restricted stages before full privileges. The {{STATE}} Department of Public Safety (DPS) issues licenses under the {{STATE}} Transportation Code, and the {{STATE}} Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) oversees driver education. Fees and procedures change, so check the DPS website before every visit.

> **On the test**
>
> - Who may supervise a learner, and the 6 month minimum on a learner license.
> - The 30 practice hours, 10 of them at night.
> - Provisional license limits: one young passenger, no driving from midnight to 5 a.m., no phone at all.
> - Zero tolerance for alcohol under 21.

## 1.1 The Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} calls its system the **Graduated Driver License (GDL)** program. Phase One is the learner license; Phase Two is the provisional license. Both end on your 18th birthday.

| Stage | Earliest age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Learner license | 15 | Drive only with a qualified adult beside you |
| Provisional license | 16 | Drive alone, with passenger, night and phone limits |
| Unrestricted license | 18 | Full privileges after you renew |

Cars, pickups and SUVs need a **Class C** license. Motorcycles need Class M, and heavy vehicles need Class A or B.

A teen with a serious family need, such as illness in the family or a job the family depends on, may qualify for a **hardship license** at 15. A parent applies on form DL-77, and the teen must still pass driver education and every exam.

## 1.2 Driver Education: Two Routes

You cannot get a learner license under 18 without starting an approved driver education course. You choose the route:

- **A licensed driver education school or a public school program.** Instructors teach the classroom and in-car lessons.
- **Parent-taught driver education.** A parent or legal guardian (or another person the law allows) teaches using a TDLR-approved course. The parent first requests a Parent Taught Program Guide from TDLR, which costs $20 per student.

Either route covers the same work:

| Part | Hours |
|---|---|
| Classroom | 24 |
| In-car observation | 7 |
| In-car instruction | 7 |
| Supervised practice | 30, at least 10 at night |

You may apply for your learner license after the first classroom block: 6 hours in a concurrent course, or all 24 hours in a block course. Driving practice starts only once you hold the learner license.

Many approved courses also give the **knowledge test** as part of the classroom work, often online. If yours does, the result goes on your driver education certificate and you skip that test at DPS.

> **Remember it:** 24, 7, 7, 30 and 10: classroom hours, observation, instruction, practice and night practice.

## 1.3 Paperwork and What to Bring

Book an appointment online with a DPS driver license office. Applicants under 17 years and 10 months use **form DL-14B**; older applicants use DL-14A.

For a learner license, bring:

- Proof of U.S. citizenship or lawful presence, proof of identity, and proof of {{STATE}} residency.
- Your Social Security number, which DPS checks electronically.
- Your driver education certificate showing the classroom hours done.
- A high school diploma or GED, or a **Verification of Enrollment and Attendance (VOE) form**, DL-104, signed by your school. A VOE is good for 30 days, or 90 days if issued in the summer.
- Proof of insurance for any vehicle you own, or a statement that you own none.
- A parent or guardian, who signs the application with you. If they cannot come, bring the application signed by them and notarized.

At the office you give thumbprints and a signature, have your photo taken, pay the fee and take the vision test. Your parent also confirms receiving information on distracted driving and the zero tolerance law.

A parent who signs may later ask DPS, in a sworn written request, to cancel your license before you turn 18.

**REAL ID.** {{STATE}} issues only REAL ID compliant cards, marked with a gold star cutout in the upper right corner. There is no extra fee and no separate application.

> **Road tip:** Check the VOE date before you leave home. A form older than its 30 day limit is the quickest way to be sent back.

## 1.4 Fees

| Item | DPS fee |
|---|---|
| Learner license, under 18 | $16 |
| Provisional (Class C) license, under 18 | $16 |
| Class C license, 18 and older, eight years | $33 |
| Replacement or change of information | $11 |

Each fee includes a $1 administrative fee, charged once per visit. The fee must be paid before any exam. Driver education courses set their own prices.

## 1.5 The Knowledge Test

The Class C knowledge test checks traffic laws and road signs. You need a score of **70 percent** or better.

| Feature | The {{STATE}} rule |
|---|---|
| Questions | Not published by DPS; course exams usually have 15 on signs and 15 on rules |
| To pass | 70 percent on each exam |
| Through a course | Two exams: Road Signs and Road Rules |
| Earliest age | Your 15th birthday |
| Languages | English and Spanish |
| Where | In an approved course, or at a DPS office |

When an approved course gives the test, it comes as two separate exams, one on road signs and one on road rules, and you must reach 70 percent on each. You may not review the exam before you take it.

If you fail an exam at DPS, your application stays open for **90 days**. After 90 days or three failed exams, you start over with a new application and fee.

> **Test trap:** A strong score on signs cannot cover a weak score on rules. Each exam needs its own 70 percent, so study both halves of this book.

## 1.6 The Vision Test

Your vision is checked at the DPS office when you apply. Students in a school course may be screened at the school; parent-taught students test at DPS. If you need glasses or contacts to pass, your license will require them while you drive. If the result is unclear, DPS may send you to an eye doctor before it decides.

## 1.7 Driving on a Learner License

**Your supervisor** sits in the front passenger seat and must be a licensed driver at least **21** with at least **one year** of driving experience. They must be awake, sober and watching your driving, not busy with anything else.

**No phone.** Under 18, you may not use a cell phone or other wireless device while driving, not even hands-free, except in an emergency.

### The Practice Requirement

During driver education you complete **30 hours** of supervised practice, at least **10 at night**, with a licensed adult 21 or older. A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Course rules credit no more than two hours of behind-the-wheel instruction a day, so spread your lessons out.

You must also hold the learner license (or a hardship license) for at least **six months**. A suspension adds its days to that wait. The six months and the 30 hours are separate conditions: finishing the hours early does not shorten the wait.

> **Remember it:** 21 and 1 for the supervisor, 30 and 10 for the hours, 6 months on the license.

## 1.8 The Provisional License

At 16 or older, you may get a provisional license once you have held the learner license six months, finished all of driver education, completed the **Impact Texas Teen Drivers (ITTD)** program and passed the road test.

**ITTD** is a free DPS video course of about two hours on distracted driving. Take it after your in-car training, as the last step before the road test. The certificate is good for **90 days**; if you do not pass the road test in that window, you repeat ITTD. Bring a printed copy: electronic copies are not accepted.

The provisional license limits you until your 18th birthday:

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Passengers | No more than 1 under age 21 who is not a family member |
| Late night | No driving from midnight to 5 a.m. |
| Phone | No use, even hands-free, except emergencies |

The late night limit has three exceptions: driving needed for work, for a school-related activity, or for a medical emergency.

The provisional license expires on your 18th birthday. Renew it then to receive an unrestricted license.

Two traffic convictions within 12 months, for separate offenses, can lead to suspension for a driver under 18. Any detectable alcohol while you drive under 21 is a crime under the zero tolerance law (see Chapter 11).

> **Test trap:** The curfew starts at midnight, not 11 p.m., and the passenger limit counts people under 21, not under 18.

## 1.9 The Road Test

### Scheduling and What to Bring

You can take the road test at a DPS office, booked online, or at an approved driver education school that runs third party skills testing. Bring:

- Your learner license and driver education certificate.
- The printed ITTD certificate, dated within 90 days.
- Current registration and proof of insurance for the test car.
- A licensed driver to bring you, and to drive you home if you are not licensed that day.

The car must match the license class you want. You will not be asked to do anything illegal.

### What the Examiner Scores

Every applicant is graded on four skills: **control**, **observation**, **positioning** in your lane, and **signaling**. The examiner may also score:

- **Parallel parking.** A backup camera is allowed; an automatic parking feature is not.
- **A quick stop** from about 20 mph without skidding.
- **Backing** straight for about 60 feet, slowly, while you look back or use the camera and mirrors.
- Stop signs, signals, intersections, turns, right-of-way, following distance and lane use, and posture: both hands on the wheel.

Stay quiet and follow instructions. After the drive, the examiner explains every error.

### Automatic Fails

You fail if you break a traffic law, refuse an instruction, drive dangerously or have a crash, or lose **more than 30 points**.

When you pass, you get a temporary license good for 45 days while the card is mailed. If you move later, report your new address to DPS within 30 days.

## 1.10 Adults Getting a First License

Driver education still applies to young adults:

- **Ages 18 to 24:** finish a **six-hour adult driver education course**, then the knowledge test (some courses include it), the vision test, the one-hour **Impact Texas Adult Drivers (ITAD)** video and the road test.
- **25 and older:** no driver education is required. You take the knowledge test, vision test, ITAD and road test.

You skip the six-month wait, the provisional limits and the VOE. If you want to practice first, DPS issues a Class C license with the same supervision rule as a learner license. The ITAD certificate, like ITTD, must be dated within 90 days of your road test.

## 1.11 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

**From another state.** You may drive on your valid license for up to **90 days** after moving. Adults whose license is valid, or expired less than two years, surrender it with no knowledge test, road test, ITAD or driver education. Register every vehicle you own in {{STATE}} first; you must bring proof. Under 18, you skip the knowledge test and receive the matching {{STATE}} license with its limits: a permit becomes a learner license, and a license becomes a provisional license once you pass the road test.

**From another country.** Adult holders of a valid license from **France, Germany, South Korea, Taiwan or the United Arab Emirates** can exchange it without the knowledge or road test. Everyone else follows the first-time steps for their age: knowledge test, vision test, ITAD and road test. A license not in English or Spanish needs a translation.

## 1.12 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **{{STATE}} Driver Handbook (DL-7)** is free from DPS at dps.texas.gov, along with forms, fees and office appointments. TDLR lists approved driver education courses. If this book and the handbook or current law ever disagree, follow the official source.

## Quick Review

- Driver education is required for every applicant under 25; teens start it at 15 to get a learner license.
- The knowledge test needs 70 percent; through a course it is two exams, signs and rules, 70 percent each.
- The supervisor is a licensed driver 21 or older with a year of experience, in the front seat.
- Before a provisional license: six months on the learner license and 30 practice hours, 10 at night.
- ITTD comes last, within 90 days of the road test.
- Provisional limits: one passenger under 21 outside the family, no driving midnight to 5 a.m., no phone.
- Road test fails: a broken law, a crash, dangerous driving, or more than 30 points lost.
