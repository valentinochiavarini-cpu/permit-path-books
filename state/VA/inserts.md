# Virginia inserts

## school_zone

In a {{STATE}} school crossing zone, the limit is **25 mph** or the lower number posted. It applies while the yellow lights flash or during the times shown on the sign.

Speeding there costs a fine of up to **$250**. Some school zones also have speed cameras, marked by a "Speed Photo Enforced" sign. A camera ticket goes to the owner when the car is **10 mph or more** over the limit, with a civil penalty of up to **$100**.

## work_zone

In a {{STATE}} highway work zone, speeding costs a fine of up to **$500** when workers are present and signs post the limit and the penalty.

Holding a phone while driving in a work zone brings a mandatory **$250** fine.

Speed cameras may run in a work zone only while workers are there. They ticket the owner at **10 mph or more** over the limit, with a civil penalty of up to **$100**.

Drive over rumble strips slowly. Do not swerve around them.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, you may turn **right on a steady red light** after a complete stop. Yield to pedestrians and traffic first. A "No Turn on Red" sign forbids it, and so does a bike box painted at the intersection.

**Left on red** is allowed only when both streets are one-way. Stop completely first.

A **red arrow** means no turn in that direction. {{STATE}} bans turns on a red arrow unless a sign says "Right on Red Arrow After Stop" or "Left on Red Arrow After Stop."

## move_over

In {{STATE}}, the Move Over law covers any stopped vehicle showing flashing **blue, red or amber** lights, such as police, fire, rescue, tow and road crews. On a road with at least **four lanes**, two going your way, move to a lane not next to it if you safely can. If you cannot, slow down and pass with care.

Breaking this rule is **reckless driving**. Stopped cars with **hazard lights, flares or caution signs** are covered too; skipping that lane change is a traffic infraction.

## emergency_vehicles

In {{STATE}}, yield to any emergency vehicle using a siren and flashing lights. Clear the intersection, then pull to the right edge and stop. The law says the **nearest edge**, so on a one-way street use whichever side is closer. On an undivided road, do this for one coming toward you as well. Stay stopped until it passes, unless an officer directs you.

Then follow no closer than **500 feet** behind it. Passing a moving emergency vehicle that has its siren and lights on is **reckless driving**.

## school_bus

In {{STATE}}, stop for a school bus stopped to load or unload, from **any direction**, on a highway, private road or school driveway. Stay stopped until everyone is clear and the bus moves again. Stop even if the lights are off while children get on or off.

{{STATE}} law sets no exact stopping distance. Stop far enough back for children to cross safely.

On a **divided highway**, traffic on the far side of a median or barrier need not stop. Passing a stopped bus is **reckless driving**.

## u_turn

In {{STATE}} cities, towns and county business districts, make a U-turn **only at an intersection**. Never make one on a curve or near the top of a hill where drivers coming from any direction cannot see you within **500 feet**.

{{STATE}} treats a U-turn as two left turns, so a **No Left Turn** sign also bans U-turns.

## speed_limits

{{STATE}} limits when no sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| School, business or residential area | 25 mph |
| Unpaved road | 35 mph |
| All other roads | 55 mph |
| Posted interstates and limited access highways | Up to 70 mph |

Driving **20 mph or more over** the limit, or **over 85 mph** anywhere, is **reckless driving**, a criminal offense. Going 81 to 85 mph in a 65 mph zone adds **$100** to the fine. Radar detectors are illegal in {{STATE}}.

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} Driver's Manual ties your gap to your speed on dry roads:

| Speed | Following time |
|---|---|
| Under 35 mph | 2 seconds |
| 35 to 45 mph | 3 seconds |
| 46 to 70 mph | 4 seconds |

Add seconds in bad weather, heavy traffic or behind a truck. Behind a motorcycle, add one more second, and double that gap in bad weather.

## hov

{{STATE}} HOV lanes are marked by a white **diamond**. Signs show how many riders you need and the hours the rule applies. Motorcycles may use them with one rider.

Some lanes behind a barrier are **reversible**: they change direction at set times. Toll express lanes need an **E-ZPass**; if you enter one by mistake, keep going.

An HOV violation costs **$100**. In Northern {{STATE}}, fines rise to **$125, $250, $500 and $1,000**, and repeat offenses add **3 points**.

## parking_rules

In {{STATE}}, do not park:

| Place | Distance |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant | Within 15 feet |
| Fire, ambulance or rescue station entrance | Within 15 feet |
| Intersection | Within 20 feet |
| Railroad crossing | Within 50 feet |
| Fire trucks stopped at an alarm | Within 500 feet |

Park within **1 foot** of the curb. On a two-way street, park on the right; on a one-way street, either side. Never park in a bike lane, on a crosswalk or sidewalk, or in a disability space or its striped aisle.

## bicycle_passing

In {{STATE}}, pass a bicycle, moped or horse-drawn buggy with at least **3 feet** of space. If the lane is too narrow for 3 feet, you must **change lanes** to pass.

Never drive in a marked bike lane to pass, only to turn. Do not use the horn when passing a buggy.

## crash_reporting

In {{STATE}}, report every crash with **injury, death or property damage** to police as soon as you can. If the cars can be moved and no one is hurt, move them out of traffic right away.

Police send a report to DMV when anyone is hurt or killed, or damage is over **$3,000**.

If you hit an unattended car or other property, leave a note with your name, phone number, the date and time, and the damage. Report it to police within **24 hours**.

## bac_limits

{{STATE}} sets these limits:

| Driver | Limit |
|---|---|
| Age 21 or older | 0.08 percent: DUI |
| Under 21 | 0.02 percent: illegal consumption |
| Under 21 at 0.08 or more | DUI, same as an adult |

You can be convicted of DUI below 0.08 if your driving is impaired. Driving under the influence of marijuana or any drug is also DUI, even though adults 21 and older may legally have marijuana.

## implied_consent

By driving in {{STATE}}, you agree to a breath test if police arrest you for DUI.

Refuse it the first time, and the court suspends your license for **one year**, a civil offense. A second refusal within **10 years** is a criminal misdemeanor and costs your license for **three years**.

A refusal, or a BAC of **0.08** or more when charged, also brings an automatic administrative suspension of **7 days** for a first offense.

## dui_penalties

First DUI in {{STATE}}:

| Penalty | Amount |
|---|---|
| Fine | At least $250 |
| Jail, BAC 0.15 to 0.20 | At least 5 days |
| Jail, BAC over 0.20 | At least 10 days |
| License | Lost for 1 year |

With a passenger 17 or younger: **5 more days** in jail and **$500 to $1,000** more.

**Open container:** with an opened container of alcohol in the passenger area, part of it gone, and signs you have been drinking, you can be charged with drinking while driving, a misdemeanor.

## underage_alcohol

{{STATE}} has **zero tolerance**. A driver **under 21** with a BAC of **0.02** to under **0.08** loses the license for **one year**. The court also orders a fine of at least **$500** or at least **50 hours** of community service.

Using a **fake ID** to buy alcohol brings a fine of at least **$500**, at least 50 hours of community service, and a license suspension of **6 months to 1 year**.

Adults who supply alcohol to minors face fines up to **$2,500**.

## phone_laws

In {{STATE}}, no driver may **hold** a phone or other handheld device while the car is moving. You may use a phone hands-free or for GPS, as long as you do not hold it or type on it. You may hold one to report an emergency, or while lawfully parked or stopped.

Fines: **$125** for a first offense, **$250** after that, and a mandatory **$250** in a work zone.

**Live streaming** to social media while driving is also illegal: a fine of up to **$500**, then license suspensions.

## seat_belts

In {{STATE}}, the driver and every passenger 18 or older must wear a seat belt, in **front and back seats**. The fine is **$25**, with no points. Police cannot stop you for an adult belt violation alone.

The driver must make sure every passenger **under 18** is in a seat belt, booster or child seat, in **any** seat.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} rules for children:

| Child | Required |
|---|---|
| Birth to at least age 2 | Rear-facing seat, unless the child reaches the seat maker's forward-facing weight |
| Under 8 | Child seat or booster seat, in the back seat |
| 8 to 17 | Seat belt |

The driver is responsible. A first offense costs **$50**; later ones up to **$500**. No child under **16** may ride in a pickup truck bed, even with a camper shell.

## vehicle_rules

In {{STATE}}, your car must pass a **safety inspection every year** and show the sticker. Some localities also require an emissions inspection.

Minimum liability insurance: **$50,000** per person and **$100,000** per crash for injuries, and **$25,000** for property damage.

Show plates on the **front and rear**, and carry the registration card. New residents must title and register their car within **30 days**. Tell DMV about a new address within **30 days**.

## police_stop

The {{STATE}} manual adds: turn on your flashers. Keep your seat belt on and ask passengers to keep theirs on and their hands in view. The officer may come to either side of the car.

If the officer is in an unmarked car or not in uniform, you may ask for identification.

You will be asked to **sign the ticket**. Signing does not admit guilt, but refusing can lead to arrest.

Eluding police costs your license. If an officer chasing you is killed, it is a felony.

## headlight_use

In {{STATE}}, your headlights must be on:

- from **sunset to sunrise**;
- when rain, fog, smoke, snow or sleet keeps you from seeing people or vehicles **500 feet** ahead;
- whenever your **wipers** are on because of rain, fog, sleet or snow, except brief intermittent use in a mist.

The wiper rule carries no points, and police cannot stop you for it alone.

## driver_record

{{STATE}} gives **3, 4 or 6** demerit points per conviction, for **two years**.

At 18 or older, **12 points in 12 months** or **18 in 24 months** means a driver improvement clinic. **18 in 12 months** or **24 in 24 months** means a **90-day suspension** too.

Each clean calendar year earns **1 safe driving point**, up to **5**. A voluntary clinic earns **5**.

Driving uninsured: your license is suspended until you pay a **$600** fee and your insurer files proof of coverage for **three years**.

## road_test

In {{STATE}}, drivers under 18 take the road skills test at the end of the in-car part of driver education, given by the school.

At DMV, bring your permit and a driver education certificate or the **CSMA 19** checklist. The test is driven on public roads, in English. Failing to wear your belt or to stop fully at a stop sign ends it.

Wait **two days** to retest. After **three failures**, you must pass the in-car part of driver education first.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers under 18 need supervised practice before a license:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total | 45 |
| After sunset | At least 15 |

The 15 night hours count toward the 45. A parent, guardian or foster parent certifies them on the driver education completion certificate. You must also hold the permit at least **nine months**. Your supervisor sits beside you: a licensed driver 21 or older, or a parent, guardian or sibling 18 or older.

## signal_distance

{{STATE}} law sets how far ahead to signal. At **35 mph or less**, signal for at least the last **50 feet**. Above 35 mph, signal for at least the last **100 feet**.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, switch to low beams within **500 feet** of an oncoming vehicle. When you follow another vehicle, switch to low beams within **200 feet** of it.
