# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

In {{STATE}}, licensing is run by the Department of Motor Vehicles, called **DMV**, under Title 46.2 of the Code of {{STATE}}. Its offices are customer service centers. A teen's path also runs through driver education and, often, a short court ceremony. Every step has a document, a fee or a waiting period, and missing one is the usual reason people get sent home. Check dmv.virginia.gov before each visit, because fees and forms change.

> **On the test**
>
> - The two-part knowledge exam: 10 sign questions you must all get right, then 30 general questions.
> - Who may sit beside a permit driver, and how old they must be.
> - The midnight to 4 a.m. rule and the passenger limits for drivers under 18.
> - The 45 supervised hours, 15 of them after sunset, and the nine-month permit hold.

## 1.1 The Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses **graduated licensing**: you earn privileges step by step, because new drivers crash most in their first months.

| Stage | Earliest age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Learner's permit | 15 years and 6 months | Drive only with a qualified licensed driver beside you |
| Driver's license under 18 | 16 years and 3 months | Drive alone, with passenger, night and phone limits |
| Full privileges | 18 | The under-18 limits end on your 18th birthday |

DMV makes no exceptions to these ages. A regular driver's license covers cars, pickups, vans and other vehicles under 26,001 pounds that do not need a commercial license.

## 1.2 Paperwork and What to Bring

Apply in person at any DMV customer service center, with or without an appointment. Your form is the **Application for a {{STATE}} Driver's License (DL 1P)**. If you are under 18, a parent or legal guardian signs it as written consent. A court order showing you are emancipated replaces that signature.

Bring originals, not photocopies:

- **One proof of identity**, such as a birth certificate or passport.
- **One proof of legal presence**, which a U.S. birth certificate or passport also shows.
- **Two proofs of {{STATE}} residency**, such as a utility bill or bank statement.
- **Your Social Security number**, if you have one. DMV can usually check it electronically.
- Glasses or contact lenses, if you wear them.

Use your full legal name on every document. Nicknames and abbreviations are refused. The DMV Document Guide lists every accepted paper.

**REAL ID.** A REAL ID card, marked with a star, works for boarding domestic flights and entering secure federal buildings. In {{STATE}} it is **optional**. A standard license is fine for driving, and a valid passport also gets you on a plane. REAL ID adds a one-time fee.

> **Road tip:** Lay every document out the night before and check that your name is spelled the same on each one. A mismatched name is the fastest way to be turned away.

## 1.3 Fees

| Item | DMV fee |
|---|---|
| Learner's permit | $3, plus the license fee below |
| Driver's license | $4 for each year it is valid ($32 for eight years) |
| REAL ID | $10, one time, added to the license fee |
| Replacement license | $20 |

These figures come from the DMV fee chart (DMV 201). Driver education is paid separately to the school that teaches it.

## 1.4 The Knowledge Exam

You take the knowledge exam on a computer after your vision screening. It has **two parts**, and both come from the {{STATE}} Driver's Manual.

| Part | What it covers | To pass |
|---|---|---|
| Part 1 | 10 traffic sign questions | All **10** correct |
| Part 2 | 30 general knowledge questions | At least **24** correct (80 percent) |

You cannot start Part 2 until you pass Part 1. One missed sign question ends the attempt, so learn every sign in Chapter 2 cold.

**Where.** At any DMV customer service center. Many high schools also give the same exam in their computer labs through DMV's **Remote Testing in Schools** program, with a staff member watching. You get your result at once; if you pass, take the testing receipt to DMV to get your permit. The final exam at the end of an online driver education course is a different test and does not replace this one.

**Languages.** English, American Sign Language and more than 20 other languages, plus an oral version on request. Because so many languages are offered, you may not bring a translator for them.

**Rules.** No help from anyone, no sharing answers and no phone. Breaking these rules fails the exam.

**If you fail.** Under 18, you must wait **15 full days**: fail on the 1st and the 17th is your earliest retest. At 18 or older, you may test once a day. Each retest needs a new application and your documents again. After **three failures**, you must first finish the classroom part of driver education, or DMV's 8-hour course based on the manual if you qualify for it. If you pass but do not get your permit or license within one year, you test again.

> **Test trap:** Part 1 allows no mistakes at all. A perfect score on Part 2 never makes up for one wrong sign answer.

## 1.5 Vision Screening

A DMV staff member has you look into a machine and read letters or numbers. It is a screening, not a medical exam.

- **No restriction:** at least **20/40** in one or both eyes, and at least **110 degrees** of side to side vision.
- **Daylight only:** at least **20/70** and **70 degrees**. You may drive only from half an hour after sunrise to half an hour before sunset.

If you need glasses or contacts to pass, your license shows that restriction and you must wear them every time you drive. If you fail, you may be sent to an eye care professional.

## 1.6 Driving on a Learner's Permit

**Who sits beside you.** A licensed driver at least **21**, or your parent or legal guardian, or a brother, sister, half-sibling or stepsibling at least **18**. That person must hold a valid license, sit in the front passenger seat, and be alert and able to help.

**Passengers.** No more than **one passenger under 21**, unless the others are members of your family or household, or you are driving in an approved driver education course.

**Hours.** No driving between **midnight and 4 a.m.**

**Phones.** A driver under 18 may not use a cell phone at all while driving, even hands-free. The only exception is a driver emergency, and then only with the car lawfully parked or stopped.

### The Practice Requirement

Before you can be licensed under 18, you need at least **45 hours** of supervised driving, with at least **15 hours after sunset**. You must also hold the permit for at least **nine months**. A parent, guardian or foster parent certifies the hours when signing your driver education certificate.

> **Remember it:** 15 and a half for the permit, 45 hours with 15 after dark, nine months, and 16 and 3 months to drive alone.

## 1.7 Driver Education

Every driver under 18 must finish a state-approved driver education program at a public school, private school or driver training school.

- **Classroom:** 36 sessions of 50 minutes in school, or a 30-hour online course through an approved driver training school. You may start at 15 and 6 months, before you have a permit.
- **Parent and teen session:** at least 90 more minutes on teen driving limits and the dangers of drinking and driving. In Northern {{STATE}} (Planning District 8, which includes Arlington, Fairfax, Loudoun and Prince William counties), it must be taken in person with a parent or guardian.
- **In-car:** 7 driving sessions and 7 observation sessions, each 50 minutes, with a permit.

Under 18, the **road skills test** is given at the end of the in-car course by the school, not by DMV.

**Your temporary license.** When you finish, your instructor gives you a **{{STATE}} Driver Training Certificate (DTS B)** and sends a copy to DMV. With your valid permit, it works as your license for **180 days** from its eligibility date, or until you turn 18.

**The licensing ceremony.** If you are under 18 when the license is issued, the Juvenile and Domestic Relations District Court where you live may mail you a notice to attend a short licensing ceremony with a parent or guardian. If the court does not hold one, the license is mailed to you. DMV never hands out licenses at the counter; they all come by mail.

## 1.8 Your First Months Alone: Limits Under 18

**Passengers.** In your first year with a license, you may carry only **one passenger under 21**. After one year, you may carry up to **three** passengers under 21 only when driving to or from a school-sponsored activity, when a licensed driver 21 or older sits in the front seat, or in an emergency. Family and household members do not count toward either limit.

**Night limit.** No driving between **midnight and 4 a.m.**, except:

- to or from work;
- to or from an adult-supervised activity run by a school or a civic, religious or public group;
- with your parent, someone acting as your parent, or your licensed spouse 18 or older in the front passenger seat;
- in an emergency, including answering a call as a volunteer firefighter or rescue squad member.

**Phones.** The ban on all phone use, even hands-free, continues until you turn 18.

**Points and suspensions.** For a traffic conviction that carries demerit points, or a seat belt or child seat conviction, committed while under 18:

| Conviction | What DMV does |
|---|---|
| First | Driver improvement clinic within 90 days, or suspension until you finish it |
| Second | 90-day suspension |
| Third | Revocation for one year or until you turn 18, whichever is longer |

A second passenger or night limit violation can also cost you up to six months of driving. At 18 or 19, one demerit point conviction still sends you to a driver improvement clinic. All the under-18 limits end on your **18th birthday**.

> **Test trap:** The night limit is midnight to 4 a.m., not 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. And the passenger limit counts riders under 21, not under 18.

## 1.9 The Road Skills Test at DMV

Drivers 18 and older who skip driver education take the road skills test at a DMV customer service center, by appointment or as a walk-in.

**Bring:** your learner's permit, plus either a driver education certificate or a completed **In-Car Maneuvers Observation Record (CSMA 19)**, signed by a licensed driver who practiced with you.

**Bring a safe car.** You supply it, and you cannot test in an autocycle. It needs a valid safety inspection sticker, license plates, a registration card, and working brakes, seat belts, horn, lights, turn signals, mirrors and speedometer. A rental car is allowed only if you are listed on the rental agreement.

The test is driven on the road and given in English. You may bring a translator who is at least 18 and holds a U.S. license, but the translator may only repeat instructions.

**Automatic fails** in DMV's testing guide include not wearing your seat belt properly, not stopping fully at a stop sign, breaking a traffic law, avoidable contact with a car, object or person, any unsafe act, and the examiner having to step in to prevent a crash.

**If you fail,** wait **two days** to retest. You may test once per business day. After **three failures** at DMV, you must first pass the in-car part of driver education.

## 1.10 Adults Getting a First License

At 18 or older with no license anywhere, you still need the vision screening, the two-part knowledge exam and a permit. You skip the 45-hour certificate, the nine-month hold and the under-18 limits.

- **Ages 21 and up:** either hold the permit for **60 days** and pass the DMV road skills test, or finish driver education while holding the permit, which includes the road test.
- **Ages 18 to 20:** a recent law change requires both: hold the permit for at least **90 days** and finish driver education. Until DMV starts applying it, the 60-day choice above still applies. Check the start date at dmv.virginia.gov.

## 1.11 New Residents, Visitors and Foreign Licenses

**New residents.** You must get a {{STATE}} license within **60 days** of moving here. With a valid license from another U.S. state, you usually need only the vision screening. A driver under 18 must also show proof of driver education with at least 30 classroom hours and 6 in-car hours, or receive a six-month temporary license while getting it.

**Visitors.** A nonresident may drive on a valid home-state or home-country license for up to six months, until taking a job here.

**Foreign license holders, 18 or older.**

- **Canada, France, Germany, Japan or South Korea:** vision screening only, once DMV verifies your license (form DL 7; Canadians skip this step).
- **Taiwan:** vision screening and the two-part knowledge exam.
- **Every other country:** vision screening, the two-part knowledge exam and the road skills test. The 60-day permit hold may not apply.

Under 18, you cannot exchange a foreign license. An International Driving Permit is only a translation of your home license, not a license.

**Driver Privilege Card.** {{STATE}} offers this card to residents who are not U.S. citizens and cannot or do not wish to show legal presence. You must have reported income from {{STATE}} sources, or been claimed as a dependent on a {{STATE}} tax return, in the past 12 months. The same tests, permit rules and under-18 limits apply. It costs **$50**. It is not a REAL ID and cannot be used to board a flight or enter a secure federal building, and it does not allow a commercial license.

## 1.12 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **{{STATE}} Driver's Manual (DMV 39)** is the source of the exam questions. It is free at dmv.virginia.gov with a sample exam, the fee chart, forms and office locations, or call DMV at (804) 497-7100. If this book and the manual ever disagree, the manual and current law win.

## Quick Review

- Permit at 15 and 6 months with form DL 1P and a parent's signature; license at 16 and 3 months.
- Knowledge exam: Part 1 has 10 sign questions, all 10 correct; Part 2 has 30 questions, 24 correct to pass.
- Under 18, wait 15 full days after a failed exam; after three failures, take driver education classes first.
- Supervisor: licensed and 21 or older, or a parent, guardian or sibling 18 or older, in the front passenger seat.
- Under 18: 45 hours (15 after sunset), nine months on the permit and driver education.
- Under 18: no driving midnight to 4 a.m., one passenger under 21 in the first year, and no phone use at all.
- Points under 18: clinic, then a 90-day suspension, then revocation for a year or until 18.
