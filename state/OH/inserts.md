# Ohio inserts

## school_zone

In a {{STATE}} school zone, the speed limit is **20 mph**. It applies during school recess, while children are arriving or leaving at the start and end of the school day, and wherever a 20 mph school sign is posted. At other times, the normal limit applies.

Driving faster than **35 mph** in a school zone during those times is a more serious offense than ordinary speeding. Obey every crossing guard.

## work_zone

In {{STATE}}, a speeding ticket costs **double** the usual fine in a construction zone where signs warn of increased penalties. Phone fines double there too.

Slow down in every work zone, even when no one is working: lanes are narrower and the pavement may be rough. Obey flaggers, yield to workers and equipment, and keep extra room behind the car ahead.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, you may turn **right on a steady red light** unless a sign forbids it. Stop completely first, then let all crossing traffic and pedestrians clear.

**Left on red** is allowed only from the far left lane of a one-way street onto another one-way street, after a full stop, unless a sign forbids it.

On a **steady red arrow**, do not turn unless a sign allows a turn on the red arrow; then stop first and turn only the way the arrow points.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Move Over law covers stopped vehicles with warning lights flashing: police, fire and other emergency vehicles, road service vehicles such as tow trucks, highway maintenance vehicles and snowplows, waste collection trucks, and public utility commission vehicles.

On a road with two or more lanes your way, move into a lane that is not next to them if it is safe. If you cannot, slow to a safe speed and pass with caution; the law sets no number.

A Move Over ticket costs **double** the usual fine.

## emergency_vehicles

In {{STATE}}, when a police car, fire truck or ambulance comes with flashing lights and a siren, pull as close as possible to the **right** edge or curb, clear of any intersection, and stop until it passes. The law names the right side on every road.

On a **divided highway**, traffic going the opposite way does not have to stop.

Never follow a responding emergency vehicle closer than **500 feet**. Do not drive into or park in the block where fire trucks have stopped at a fire.

## school_bus

When a {{STATE}} school bus stops with **red lights** flashing, stop at least **10 feet** from its front or rear, even if it has no stop arm. Stay stopped until the bus moves or the driver signals you on.

With **fewer than four lanes**, traffic from both directions stops. With **four or more lanes**, only traffic going the bus's direction stops.

Passing a stopped bus: up to **$500**, a court appearance and a possible suspension of up to **one year**.

## u_turn

In {{STATE}}, never make a U-turn on a curve or near the top of a hill, where a driver coming from either direction could not see your car from **500 feet** away. Obey any NO U-TURN sign.

If you miss a freeway exit, take the next one. The openings in the median are for emergency and public safety vehicles only.

## speed_limits

{{STATE}} limits when no sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Alley in a city or village | 15 mph |
| School zone (see Chapter 2) | 20 mph |
| City or village street | 25 mph |
| State route in town, outside a business district | 35 mph |
| Highway outside a city or village | 55 mph |
| Rural freeway | 70 mph |

| Over the limit by | Points |
|---|---|
| More than 5 mph (limit under 55) | 2 |
| More than 10 mph (limit 55 or more) | 2 |
| 30 mph or more | 4 |

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} Driver Manual teaches a minimum gap of **four seconds** behind the vehicle ahead, or at least one car length for every **10 mph**. If you pass the fixed point before you count to four, you are too close.

Leave more room at night, in fog, rain or snow, on slippery roads, behind trucks, buses, RVs and trailers, and behind vehicles that must stop at railroad crossings. Behind a motorcycle, keep at least four seconds, and more when the road is wet.

## hov

The {{STATE}} Driver Manual does not cover HOV lanes. In Columbus, eastbound I-670 has a **SmartLane**: the left shoulder opens as a travel lane at busy times. A **green arrow** above it means open to every vehicle; a **red X** means closed, so stay out. At any special lane, read the overhead signs before you enter.

## parking_rules

Unless police or a sign tell you otherwise, {{STATE}} bans stopping or parking:

| Place | Distance |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant | Within 10 feet |
| Crosswalk at an intersection | Within 20 feet |
| Fire station driveway | Within 20 feet |
| Stop sign, yield sign or signal | Within 30 feet |
| Nearest rail of a railroad crossing | Within 50 feet |
| Another parked vehicle | Within 1 foot |

Park facing the direction of traffic, no more than **12 inches** from the right curb. On a one-way street, you may use either curb.

## bicycle_passing

In {{STATE}}, leave at least **3 feet** between your car and a bicycle when you pass. Slow down, do not honk close to the rider, and never turn sharply in front of one.

You may cross a solid yellow center line to pass a bicycle or other slow vehicle only if it is moving at **less than half** the posted limit, you stay within the speed limit while passing, and the oncoming lane is clear.

## crash_reporting

After a {{STATE}} crash, call 911 if anyone is hurt and notify the Highway Patrol, sheriff or local police. If you hit an unattended vehicle, report it to police or leave your information at the scene.

You may file a **Crash Report, form BMV 3303**, with the BMV within **six months** if the crash caused injury or more than **$400** in damage and the other driver had no insurance. In a crash with more than $400 in damage, every driver involved must show proof of insurance.

## bac_limits

{{STATE}} calls impaired driving **OVI**, operating a vehicle while impaired. It is illegal to drive with a BAC of:

| Driver | Limit |
|---|---|
| Any age | 0.08 percent |
| Commercial driver | 0.04 percent |
| Under 21 | 0.02 percent |

A BAC of **0.17** or more counts as a high test and brings tougher penalties. You can also be convicted below these numbers if alcohol or drugs have impaired your driving.

## implied_consent

If you are arrested for OVI in {{STATE}}, the officer asks for a chemical test within **two hours**. Refuse, and the officer takes your license on the spot. The suspension starts right away and stands even if you are found not guilty.

| Refusal | Suspension |
|---|---|
| First | 1 year |
| With 1 prior refusal or OVI | 2 years |
| With 2 priors | 3 years |
| With 3 or more priors | 5 years |

Testing 0.08 or higher brings a **90-day** suspension the first time. Reinstatement costs **$315**.

## dui_penalties

A first OVI in {{STATE}} brings:

- At least **3 days** in jail or a 72-hour driver intervention program. At a BAC of 0.17 or more: 3 days in jail **plus** the program.
- A fine of **$565 to $1,075**.
- A license suspension of **1 to 3 years**, with limited driving or an ignition interlock possible.

No driver or passenger may have an **opened container** of beer or liquor in a vehicle on a road or in a public parking area. Hired limousine passengers in the back are the exception.

## underage_alcohol

A driver **under 21** with a BAC from **0.02** to under 0.08 commits **OVUAC**, operating after underage alcohol consumption: a suspension of **3 months to 2 years**, a remedial driving course, a new license exam and possibly jail.

Under 21, buying, holding or drinking alcohol is a crime. Under 18 and in a vehicle, you also lose your permit or license for **6 months to 1 year**. A false ID to buy alcohol: **$250 to $1,000**. Furnishing alcohol to anyone under 21: **$500 to $1,000**.

## phone_laws

{{STATE}} drivers 18 and older may not hold, support or type on a phone while driving. Hands-free use, or holding the phone to your ear for a call, is legal if one tap or swipe starts and ends it. You may always call 911.

| Within 2 years | Fine | Points |
|---|---|---|
| First | Up to $150 | 2 |
| Second | Up to $250 | 3 |
| Third or more | Up to $500 | 4 |

A distracted driving course can replace a first fine and points. A third can also bring a 90-day suspension. Fines double in work zones.

## seat_belts

In {{STATE}}, the driver and every front seat passenger must wear a seat belt. Children up to age 15 must be buckled in any seat (see the child seat box).

For adults, belt enforcement is **secondary**: police cannot stop you only for a missing belt. The fine is **$30** for the driver and **$20** for a front seat passenger.

A driver under 18 must make sure everyone in the car is belted, with no more people than belts.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} makes the driver responsible for securing children:

| Child | Required |
|---|---|
| Under 4, or under 40 pounds | Child safety seat |
| 4 to under 8, shorter than 4 feet 9 inches | Booster seat |
| 8 to 15 | Seat belt or child seat |

Every seat must meet federal safety standards. Keep children **12 and under** in the back seat, and never put a rear-facing seat in front of an active airbag.

A first offense costs **$25 to $75**.

## vehicle_rules

{{STATE}} requires tires with at least **1/16 inch** of tread. Studded tires are legal only from **November 1 to April 15**. Window tint must let through at least **70 percent** of light on the windshield and **50 percent** (plus or minus 3) on the front side windows.

Minimum liability insurance: **$25,000** per person and **$50,000** per crash for injury, and **$25,000** for property damage. Show proof with every ticket and after any crash with more than $400 in damage. Driving uninsured can cost your license.

## police_stop

The {{STATE}} manual adds a few steps. Once stopped, shift into park and turn the engine off. Lower your window, keep your seat belt fastened and stay in the car unless the officer says otherwise.

If you have a weapon in the vehicle, tell the officer at first contact. When asked, show your license, registration and insurance information. Do not argue; you can contest the ticket in court.

## headlight_use

In {{STATE}}, your headlights must be on:

- from **sunset to sunrise**;
- in rain, snow, fog or other bad weather, at any time of day;
- whenever you cannot clearly see objects **1,000 feet** ahead;
- whenever your **wipers** are on because of rain or snow.

In fog, use low beams, not high beams.

## driver_record

{{STATE}} counts points within any **two-year** period:

| Points | Result |
|---|---|
| 6 | Warning letter |
| 12 | **6-month** suspension |

To get your license back after a 12-point suspension, finish a remedial driving course, pay fees and file an **SR-22**, a form (not insurance) proving your coverage meets the minimum.

Most moving violations cost **2** points. Reckless driving or 30 mph or more over: **4**. OVI, fleeing police or leaving a crash scene: **6**.

With 2 to 11 points, a remedial driving course earns a **2-point credit**, once every three years.

## road_test

Schedule the test online or at a Driver Exam Station. Both front doors must open from inside and outside.

**Maneuverability:** drive forward through a **9 by 20 foot** box of markers. Stop with your rear bumper even with the center marker, then back up until your front bumper is even with the two rear markers. Knocking down, running over or moving a marker fails you at once.

Then comes the **on-road test**. Lose no more than **25 points** on each part to pass. Chapter 1 gives the retest rules.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers under 21 must log supervised practice before the driving test:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total | 50 |
| At night | At least 10 |

The adult who supervised signs the **Fifty-Hour Affidavit, form BMV 5791**, in front of a notary. Drivers under 21 also need driver education: 24 hours in class or online and 8 hours driving with an instructor. Under 18, you must hold the TIPIC for **six months** too.

## signal_distance

{{STATE}} law requires a turn signal at least **100 feet** before you turn. Use your signal, or a hand and arm signal, to turn, change lanes or merge, and cancel it when you finish.

On a road with four or more lanes, drive in the right lane and use the lane just right of center to pass and to turn left.

## headlight_dimming

{{STATE}} law sets no distance in feet. It requires you to aim your lights so the glare does not shine into an oncoming driver's eyes, and the {{STATE}} manual says to dim your high beams for approaching traffic. Switch to low beams as soon as you see oncoming headlights, and whenever you come up behind another vehicle.
