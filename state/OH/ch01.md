# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

Getting a {{STATE}} license takes steps in a fixed order, and most delays come from a missing paper. The {{STATE}} Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV) runs licensing through two kinds of offices: **Driver Exam Stations** give tests, and **Deputy Registrar License Agencies** sell permits and licenses. Fees and forms change, so check the BMV website before each visit.

> **On the test**
>
> - Who may sit beside a permit driver at each age, and the midnight to 6 a.m. rule.
> - The 50 supervised hours (10 at night) and the six-month permit wait.
> - Probationary license limits: night hours, passengers and phones.
> - Suspensions for young drivers with moving violations.

## 1.1 The Three Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses **graduated licensing**: you earn privileges in steps, because new drivers crash most in their first months alone.

| Stage | Earliest age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary permit (TIPIC) | 15 and a half | Drive only with an eligible adult beside you |
| Probationary license | 16 | Drive alone, with night, passenger and phone limits |
| Full license | 18 | Adult privileges |

Your permit is a **Temporary Instruction Permit Identification Card (TIPIC)**, valid for one year. Cars, pickups and vans need a **Class D** license.

## 1.2 Paperwork and What to Bring

In {{STATE}}, the order is test first, permit second. At 15 years and 6 months you may take the vision screening and the knowledge test. After you pass, buy the TIPIC at a Deputy Registrar within **60 days**. Wait longer and you must take both tests again.

To buy the TIPIC, bring documents from the BMV's **Acceptable Documents List** that prove five things:

- Your full legal name.
- Your date of birth.
- Your Social Security number, if you have one.
- U.S. citizenship or legal presence.
- Your {{STATE}} street address.

Under 18, a parent or guardian must come with you and **co-sign**. The co-signer shares responsibility for damage you cause while driving until you turn 18, and can cancel your permit or license at any time. Any {{STATE}} ID card you hold is canceled when the TIPIC is issued.

**Compliant or Standard card.** The **Compliant** card meets federal ID rules for domestic flights and federal buildings, and needs two proofs of address instead of one. The **Standard** card is just as valid for driving, but to fly you would also need a passport or other ID the TSA accepts. Cards arrive by mail.

> **Road tip:** Lay out every document the night before and check that your name is spelled the same way on each one. A mismatch is the most common reason people are sent home.

## 1.3 Fees

| Item | BMV fee |
|---|---|
| TIPIC (temporary permit) | $26.50 |
| First license at 16 (four years) | $28.75 |
| First license at 17 | $27.50 |
| First license at 21 or older (four years) | $27.50 |

These totals include the Deputy Registrar's service fee. The first license costs a little less each year from 16 to 20, because a license issued under 21 expires on your 21st birthday.

## 1.4 The Knowledge Test

The knowledge test covers traffic laws and road signs, mixed together in one test.

| Feature | The {{STATE}} rule |
|---|---|
| Questions | 40, multiple choice |
| To pass | 75 percent (30 correct, 10 misses allowed) |
| Languages | English, Spanish, Arabic, Chinese, French, Haitian Creole, Japanese, Russian and Somali, plus American Sign Language |
| If you fail | Wait at least one day, then retest |
| Limit | One attempt per day |

**Where to take it.** You have three choices:

- **Online** through BMV Online Services, on a computer with a webcam. You get **two attempts in six months**, and a supervising adult must be with you if you are under 19. Your vision is screened later, at the Deputy Registrar.
- At a **Driver Exam Station** or a Deputy Registrar that offers testing, with no limit on attempts.

For another language, book a Driver Exam Station and bring your own interpreter.

> **Test trap:** A 40-question test feels long, so pace yourself. Ten misses sounds like plenty, but missed questions on permit rules, school buses and alcohol limits add up fast.

## 1.5 The Vision Test

Your vision is screened before you get a permit. If you wear glasses or contacts, bring them. Pass while wearing them and your card shows a restriction: you must wear them every time you drive.

| Vision with both eyes | Result |
|---|---|
| 20/40 or better | No restriction |
| Worse than 20/40, up to 20/70 | Daylight driving only |
| Worse than 20/70 | Not passed |

If you do not meet the standard, you are referred to an eye doctor and have **30 days** to return and screen again.

## 1.6 Driving on a TIPIC

**Who may sit beside you.** The rule depends on your age:

| Your age | Front passenger seat |
|---|---|
| Under 16 | Parent, guardian, custodian, licensed driving instructor, or a person 21 or older acting as your parent |
| 16 and 17 | Any licensed driver 21 or older |
| 18 or older | Any licensed driver 21 or older |

From **midnight to 6 a.m.**, a permit driver under 18 may drive only with a licensed parent, guardian or custodian beside them, or a licensed driver 21 or older named on a notarized **form BMV 2438**.

**Rules at every age:**

- Carry your TIPIC every time you drive.
- The adult beside you must not be intoxicated.
- You and every passenger wear a seat belt, and there may be no more people than belts.
- Do not use a phone or any other electronic device while driving.

### Driver Education and Practice Hours

Every new driver **under 21** must pass driver education at a licensed driver training school: **24 hours** of classroom or online lessons plus **8 hours** of driving with an instructor. You bring the certificate to the driving test.

You also log **50 hours** of supervised driving, with at least **10 hours at night**. The adult who supervised you signs the **Fifty-Hour Affidavit, form BMV 5791**, in front of a notary. Use the log at the back of this book, then copy it onto the form.

If you are under 18, you must also hold the TIPIC for **six months**. All three conditions apply. Finishing 50 hours in two months does not shorten the wait.

> **Remember it:** 24 plus 8 in class and car, 50 and 10 with your adult, then six months on the permit.

## 1.7 The Probationary License

A license issued before you turn 18 is **probationary**. Its limits depend on how long you have held it, not on whether you are 16 or 17.

| Time on the license | No driving alone between | Passengers |
|---|---|---|
| First 12 months | Midnight and 6 a.m. | No more than 1 who is not family, unless a parent or guardian rides along |
| After 12 months, until 18 | 1 a.m. and 5 a.m. | No passenger limit |

**Night exceptions.** Driving with a parent or guardian, to or from work (carry **form BMV 2825** from your employer), or to or from a school or religious event (**form BMV 2826**).

**All the time until 18:**

- Every person in the car wears a belt, and there are no more people than belts.
- **No electronic devices at all**, not even hands-free. Only a navigation device you never touch and an emergency call are allowed. A first offense brings a **60-day** suspension and a **$150** fine. A second brings a **one-year** suspension and a **$300** fine.

**Moving violations before 18.** If you got your license before 17 and are convicted of a moving violation within six months, a court may order a parent or guardian to ride with you for six months or until you turn 17. The BMV also suspends a driver under 18 automatically:

- Two moving violations: **3 months**.
- Three moving violations: **1 year**.
- A conviction for underage drunk driving (OVI): **6 months**.

To drive again, you need a new TIPIC, a juvenile driver improvement program and a new driving test.

> **Test trap:** In the first 12 months, the curfew starts at **midnight**, not 1 a.m. A friend riding along does not lift it.

## 1.8 The Driving Test

### Scheduling and What to Bring

Schedule online or at a Driver Exam Station. Bring your TIPIC, your driver education certificate, the notarized BMV 5791 and a safe car with current registration.

### The Car Check

The examiner checks the turn signals, brake lights, horn, wipers and headlights. Both front doors must open from inside and outside, and any self-parking feature must be off.

### The Maneuverability Test

This is a cone course set up with five markers.

1. **Forward.** Drive through a box **9 feet wide and 20 feet long** formed by four markers. The examiner tells you to pass to the left or right of a fifth, center marker. Stop when your rear bumper is even with the center marker, with the car straight in the course.
2. **Reverse.** Back past the center marker, straighten out, and keep backing through the box. Stop with your front bumper even with the two rear markers.

You lose points for stopping, bumping a marker, misjudging where to stop or ending up crooked. Running over, knocking down or moving a marker is an instant fail. You may lose up to **25 points** and still pass.

### The On-Road Test

The examiner rides beside you. You are scored on starting and stopping, turning around and backing, turns, turn signals, lane position and following distance. Again, you may lose up to **25 points** and still pass.

### If You Fail, and When You Pass

Under 21, you wait **two days** before testing again. After you pass both parts, buy your license at a Deputy Registrar; under 18, a parent or guardian comes along to co-sign.

## 1.9 Adults Getting a First License

**Ages 18 to 20.** You still need the tests, a TIPIC, driver education (24 plus 8) and the 50-hour affidavit, but you skip the six-month wait and the probationary limits.

**Age 21 or older.** You need the vision and knowledge tests, a TIPIC and both driving tests, but no driver education and no 50-hour affidavit. While practicing, a licensed driver 21 or older must sit beside you.

If you are 21 or older and fail either part of the driving test, you must complete an **Abbreviated Adult Driver Training Course** before your second try, unless you finished an approved driver education course within the past year. The course starts with a **4-hour** class, in person or online, followed by either:

- **4 hours** of driving with a licensed driving school, or
- **24 hours** of practice with a licensed driver 21 or older, no more than **4 hours** a day, recorded on the notarized **Twenty-Four Hour Affidavit, form BMV 5789**.

Bring the completion certificate (and the BMV 5789 if you chose 24 hours) to your second test.

## 1.10 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

Once you take a job, sign a lease, buy a home or enroll children in school here, you have **30 days** to get a {{STATE}} license. With a valid out-of-state license, or one expired no more than six months, the BMV may waive the knowledge and driving tests; you still pass the vision screening.

With a license from another country, the rules depend on where it came from:

- **Canada, France, Germany, Japan, South Korea or Taiwan.** {{STATE}} has reciprocal agreements with these countries. With a valid, unexpired license from one of them, you can be licensed without the knowledge and driving tests.
- **Every other country.** You follow the new driver steps: vision and knowledge tests, a TIPIC, then the driving test.

A **temporary resident** (legally present, but not a citizen or permanent resident) who is 21 or older must also complete driver education, log 50 hours with 10 at night, and hold the TIPIC at least **14 days**, unless licensed in one of the six countries above. Every applicant must prove citizenship or legal presence.

## 1.11 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **{{STATE}} Driver Manual** (HSY 7607), the study guide for the knowledge test, is free on the BMV website with a sample test, office locations, fees and scheduling. If this book and the manual ever disagree, the manual and current law win. Check the site the week before each test.

## Quick Review

- Take the vision and knowledge tests at 15 and a half, then buy your TIPIC within 60 days.
- The knowledge test has 40 questions; you need 75 percent (30 correct). Online, you get two tries in six months.
- Under 16, a parent, guardian, custodian or instructor sits beside you; at 16 or older, any licensed driver 21 or older.
- Under 21: driver education (24 hours plus 8 driving) and 50 supervised hours, 10 at night. Under 18: six months on the TIPIC too.
- Probationary license, first 12 months: no driving midnight to 6 a.m. and only 1 non-family passenger. After that: no driving 1 a.m. to 5 a.m.
- No phone use at all under 18, not even hands-free.
- The driving test has a maneuverability part and an on-road part; lose more than 25 points on either and you fail.
