# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

Getting a {{STATE}} license is a process with clear steps, and almost every delay comes from skipping one. People get turned away at the counter every day for a missing signature, a photocopy instead of an original, or a road test car with an expired inspection sticker. None of those has anything to do with how well they drive.

This chapter walks you through the whole route in order: the permit, the knowledge test, the months of practice, the road test and the limits that apply to you as a new driver. If you are 16 or 17, the rules are stricter for you than for an adult, and the test asks about them. If you are 18 or older, read the section for adults, because several steps are shorter for you.

PennDOT, the {{STATE}} Department of Transportation, runs driver licensing. Its rules come from the {{STATE}} Vehicle Code. Fees and forms change from time to time, so check the PennDOT website before you go.

> **On the test**
>
> - The supervising driver rules for a learner's permit: who may sit beside you and where.
> - The passenger limits and the 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. limit on a junior license.
> - The 65 hours of supervised practice, including night and bad weather hours.
> - How long you must hold a permit before the road test.
> - Points and suspensions that hit young drivers harder than adults.

## 1.1 The Three Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses a **graduated licensing** system. You earn driving privileges in steps, and each step removes some of the limits. The idea is simple: new drivers crash most in their first months alone behind the wheel, so the system keeps the riskiest situations off the table until you have more experience.

| Stage | Earliest age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Learner's permit | 16 | Drive only with a qualified adult beside you |
| Junior license | 16 and a half | Drive alone, with a night limit and passenger limits |
| Regular license | 17 and a half (18 automatically) | Full privileges |

**Learner's permit.** You may apply on or after your 16th birthday, not a day before. The permit lets you practice, never alone.

**Junior license.** This is the license you get when you pass the road test before you turn 18. Because you must hold the permit for six months first, 16 and a half is the earliest you can have it.

**Regular license.** Keep the junior license twelve months without a crash you caused or a ticket, complete an approved driver education course, and you can apply to move up before 18. If you do nothing, a junior license becomes a regular license on your 18th birthday.

The license class for cars, pickups and vans is **Class C**. That is the class this book prepares you for. Motorcycles need Class M, and large trucks and big combination vehicles need Class A or B, which start at age 18.

## 1.2 Paperwork and What to Bring

Your first piece of paperwork is the **Non-Commercial Learner's Permit Application, form DL-180**. Download it from the PennDOT website. The back of the form is a medical section that a health care provider fills out after a physical exam. Two timing rules matter here:

- The physical may not be done more than six months before your 16th birthday.
- The completed DL-180 is good for one year from the exam date.

Bring the form in person. Do not mail it.

Applicants under 18 add a second form, the **DL-180TD**, which gives adult consent. It is signed by a parent, a guardian, someone acting as a parent, or a spouse aged 18 or more. If that adult cannot come with you, the signature must be notarized. If your last name is different from theirs, bring proof of how you are related, such as a birth certificate that shows the parent's name.

On test day, bring:

- The completed DL-180 (and DL-180TD if you are under 18).
- Proof of your date of birth and identity. These must be **originals**. Photocopies are refused. The back of the DL-180 lists the documents PennDOT accepts.
- Your Social Security card.
- Payment. Driver License Centers take credit or debit cards, checks and money orders. They do not take cash.
- Glasses or contact lenses if you wear them.

If you are 18 or older, also bring **two proofs of {{STATE}} residency**, such as current utility bills, a lease, mortgage papers, tax records or a W-2.

### REAL ID: Your Choice

A **REAL ID** is a license with a gold star that meets federal rules for boarding a domestic flight and entering some federal buildings. In {{STATE}} it is **optional**. A regular license is still a valid license to drive. If you have a valid U.S. passport or military ID, you can fly without a REAL ID.

To get one, PennDOT must see one document proving identity and lawful status (for example a U.S. birth certificate with a raised seal or a valid passport), one proof of your Social Security number, and two proofs of {{STATE}} residency. If your name has changed, bring the legal record of the change. There is a one-time fee on top of the regular license fee.

> **Road tip:** Put every original document in one folder the night before. Check that the name is spelled the same way on each one. A missing middle name or a married name that does not match is the most common reason people are sent home.

## 1.3 Fees

These are the PennDOT fees for a regular, non-commercial license, as listed on the PennDOT payments and fees page:

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Learner's permit and first four-year license | $45.50 |
| Adding to, extending or replacing a permit | $6.00 |
| REAL ID (one time, added to the license fee) | $30.00 |
| Four-year renewal | $39.50 |

The $45.50 is paid once, when you apply for the permit. It already covers your first license. Some private businesses are certified by PennDOT to give the road test, and they set their own price for it.

## 1.4 The Knowledge Test

You take the knowledge test at a PennDOT Driver License Center, after your vision is checked. It covers traffic signs, {{STATE}} driving laws and safe driving practices. There is no separate sign test: sign questions are mixed in with the rest.

| Feature | The {{STATE}} rule |
|---|---|
| Questions | 18, multiple choice |
| To pass | 15 correct |
| Misses allowed | 3 |
| Languages | English and many others, written or audio, on request |
| If you fail | Retake on the next business day |
| Limit | One attempt per day, at any location |

Eighteen questions is a short test, and that cuts both ways. Each question is worth more than 5 percent of your score. Four wrong answers and you go home, even if you know most of the material.

If you fail, the examiner hands your DL-180 and DL-180TD back to you. Keep them safe. You need them again for the retake, and you will not get a permit without them.

> **Test trap:** Many students study only the road sign pages because signs feel like easy points. On an 18 question test, a handful of law questions (permit rules, school buses, alcohol limits) can sink you just as fast. Study every chapter.

When you pass, you receive your learner's permit on the spot. It is valid for one year from the date it is issued.

## 1.5 The Vision Test

Your vision can be checked in one of two ways. Your health care provider or eye doctor can test it during the physical for the DL-180, or the Driver License Center tests it before your knowledge test.

You can have weak vision in one eye and still get a license. The license may carry a restriction instead, such as:

- You must wear glasses or contact lenses while driving.
- You may drive only in daylight.
- You may drive only vehicles with side mirrors.

If you do not meet the standard, the examiner gives you a **Report of Eye Examination, form DL-102**. An eye doctor or family physician completes it, and then you go back to the Driver License Center to continue.

## 1.6 Driving on a Learner's Permit

The permit is a license to practice under supervision. If you are under 18, these rules apply every time you drive.

**Who may supervise you.** One of these people must sit in the front passenger seat:

- A licensed driver who is at least 21, or
- A licensed parent, guardian, person acting as your parent, or spouse, aged 18 or more.

The supervisor must hold a license for the type of vehicle you are driving and must not be impaired by alcohol or drugs.

**Passengers.** Every passenger needs a seat belt of their own. If the car has five belts, five people is the most it may hold, you included.

**Out of state.** Your permit may not be recognized in another state. Before driving across the state line, check with your insurance company and with that state.

### The Practice Requirement

Before the road test, a driver under 18 must log **65 hours** of supervised driving. At least **10 of those hours must be at night**, and at least **5 must be in bad weather** such as rain or snow. Your parent or guardian signs a certification form, the **DL-180C**, saying you completed the hours.

You must also hold the permit for **six months** from the date it was issued before you can take the road test. Both conditions have to be met. Finishing the 65 hours in two months does not shorten the wait.

> **Remember it:** 65, 10 and 5, then six months. Sixty-five hours in all, ten of them in the dark, five in rough weather, and half a year on the permit.

The log at the back of this book has space to record every session with the date, time, conditions and the supervisor's initials.

## 1.7 The Junior License: Your First Months Alone

Pass the road test before 18 and you hold a **junior license**. You can drive alone, but with limits that target the situations where teens crash most.

**Night limit.** From **11 p.m. to 5 a.m.** you may drive only with a parent, guardian, someone acting as your parent, or your spouse (18 or over) riding along. There are exceptions for a job, public or charitable service, or membership in a volunteer fire company. In those cases you must carry a notarized statement or a certificate from your employer, supervisor or fire chief showing your likely schedule, and keep it with your license.

**Passenger limits.**

| Time on junior license | Passengers under 18 who are not immediate family |
|---|---|
| First 6 months | No more than 1 |
| After 6 months | No more than 3 |

The limits are lifted while a parent, guardian or adult spouse is riding with you. The higher limit after six months is lost for good if you are ever partly or fully at fault in a crash or convicted of any driving violation. The seat belt rule still applies at every stage: one belt per person.

**Points and speed.** A junior driver's license is suspended for **90 days** for collecting **6 or more points** or for a single ticket for going **26 mph or more above the limit**. A later suspension runs 120 days.

**Phones.** {{STATE}} bans holding a phone while driving for every driver, teen or adult. Chapter 11 covers the details.

### Moving Up to a Regular License

You may apply for a regular license after holding the junior license for **one year** if all of these are true:

- You passed a driver training course approved by the {{STATE}} Department of Education.
- You have had no crash in that year in which you were partly or fully at fault.
- You have had no conviction under the Vehicle Code in that year.
- A parent, guardian or adult spouse signs off.

The application is form DL-59. If you skip this step, the junior license turns into a regular license automatically when you turn 18.

> **Test trap:** The night limit runs from 11 p.m. to 5 a.m., not midnight. A 21-year-old friend riding along does not lift the night limit. Only a parent, guardian or adult spouse does.

## 1.8 The Road Test

### Scheduling

You must have an appointment. Book it on the PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services website or by phone at 1-800-423-5542. If you are under 18, you may book up to six months ahead, so book soon after you get your permit for a date on or after the eligibility date printed on it. Some private businesses are certified by PennDOT to give the same test for their own fee.

### What to Bring

All of these must be originals and must be valid:

- Your learner's permit.
- The signed DL-180C for your 65 hours, if you are under 18.
- The registration card for the test car.
- Proof that the test car is insured.
- The driver's license of the adult who drove there with you (someone 21 or over, or a parent, guardian or adult spouse).

Without the originals, you will not be tested.

### The Car Check

Before you drive, the examiner inspects the car. The registration, insurance, inspection sticker and, where required, emissions sticker must be current. The examiner also checks the lights, horn, brakes, wipers, turn signals, mirrors, doors, seats, tires and seat belts. If anything is unsafe or would fail state inspection, the test does not happen that day. You and the examiner both wear seat belts for the whole test.

### What the Examiner Scores

First, the examiner may ask you to work the controls: horn, parking lights, low and high beams, turn signals, wipers, parking brake, hazard flashers and defroster. Failing to work any control is a failed test.

Next comes **parallel parking**. The space is 24 feet long and 8 feet wide, marked by upright poles. You get one attempt and no more than three adjustments. When you finish, the whole car must be inside the space. Touching a pole, crossing the painted line or riding onto the curb is a fail. If your car can park itself, that feature must be off.

Then you drive on the road with the examiner as your only passenger. The examiner watches how you handle signs, stop signs and signals, how you control the car, and how you signal and use the controls.

### Automatic Fails

You fail on the spot for:

- Driving in a dangerous or reckless way.
- Breaking a traffic law, such as rolling through a stop sign.
- Causing a crash.
- Not following the examiner's instructions.
- Not using your turn signals.
- Not being able to work a vehicle control.
- Too many errors in safe driving practice.

### If You Fail

A driver under 18 who fails must let **seven days** pass before the next attempt. You get **three tries** on each permit. After the third failure, or if your permit expires, apply to extend it with form DL-31. If three years pass from your physical exam date without a passed road test, you start over with a new DL-180 and take the knowledge test again.

If you pass at a PennDOT Driver License Center, you get your license that day.

## 1.9 Adults Getting a First License

If you are 18 or older, you still need a learner's permit, a vision test, the knowledge test and the road test. The same DL-180 is used, but no consent form. You do bring two proofs of residency.

Three things are easier for you:

- **No six-month wait.** The six-month holding period applies only to drivers under 18.
- **No 65-hour certificate.** The hour requirement also applies only to drivers under 18. Practice anyway. The road test is the same test a teen takes.
- **No junior license.** You go straight to a regular license with no night or passenger limits.

The supervision rule does not change. On a permit, the person beside you must be licensed and either 21 or over, or your parent, guardian or spouse aged 18 or more. The permit is valid for one year, and the three-try limit on the road test applies to you as well.

## 1.10 Foreign License Holders

If you hold a valid license from another country, you may drive in {{STATE}} for up to **one year** from the date you entered the United States, or until your foreign license expires, whichever comes first. An International Driving Permit is strongly recommended but not required.

To get a {{STATE}} license:

- **France, Germany, South Korea or Taiwan.** {{STATE}} has reciprocity agreements with these four. You can transfer a valid non-commercial license without the knowledge test or road test, but you do take a vision test. A transfer from France or Germany cannot be finished on the same day you apply.
- **Every other country.** You go through the same steps as a new driver: learner's permit, vision test, knowledge test and road test.

If you hold a license from another U.S. state, you do not take a knowledge test as long as your license is valid or has been expired for six months or less. If it has been expired for more than six months, you start with a learner's permit and take both the knowledge test and the road test. Out-of-state learner's permits cannot be transferred.

## 1.11 Where to Find the Official Rules

- **The {{STATE}} Driver's Manual (PUB 95).** Free on the PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services website, in English and many other languages, with an audio version. It is the source of the test questions. This book teaches the same facts in its own words and adds practice. If the two ever disagree, the official manual and the current law win.
- **PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services** online at pa.gov: forms, fees, Driver License Center locations and hours, and road test scheduling.
- **Road test scheduling by phone:** 1-800-423-5542.

Laws and fees change. Check the PennDOT website the week before each test.

## Quick Review

- You can apply for a learner's permit on your 16th birthday with form DL-180, plus DL-180TD if you are under 18.
- Bring original documents and your Social Security card; photocopies are refused.
- The knowledge test has 18 questions; you need 15 correct, and you may retake it the next business day.
- A permit is valid for one year; your supervisor must be 21 or older, or a parent, guardian or spouse who is 18 or older.
- Under 18, log 65 hours (10 at night, 5 in bad weather) and hold the permit six months before the road test.
- Junior license: no driving from 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. without a parent or guardian, and only 1 non-family passenger under 18 for the first six months.
- Six or more points, or 26 mph or more over the limit, means a 90-day suspension for a junior driver.
- The road test includes a vehicle controls check and parallel parking in a 24 by 8 foot space with up to three adjustments.
- Adults skip the six-month wait and the 65-hour certificate but still need a permit and both tests.
