# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

Getting a {{STATE}} license takes clear steps, and almost every delay comes from skipping one. PennDOT, the {{STATE}} Department of Transportation, runs licensing under the {{STATE}} Vehicle Code. Fees and forms change, so check the PennDOT website before you go.

> **On the test**
>
> - Who may supervise a permit driver, and where they sit.
> - Junior license passenger limits and the 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. limit.
> - The 65 supervised hours and the six-month permit wait.
> - Points and suspensions for young drivers.

## 1.1 The Three Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses **graduated licensing**: you earn privileges in steps, because new drivers crash most in their first months alone.

| Stage | Earliest age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Learner's permit | 16 | Drive only with a qualified adult beside you |
| Junior license | 16 and a half | Drive alone, with a night limit and passenger limits |
| Regular license | 17 and a half (18 automatically) | Full privileges |

You may apply for a permit on or after your 16th birthday, not a day before. Pass the road test before 18 and you get a junior license; since you must hold the permit six months first, 16 and a half is the earliest. A junior license becomes a regular license on your 18th birthday, or earlier if you apply (section 1.7).

Cars, pickups and vans need a **Class C** license. Motorcycles need Class M; large trucks and combinations need Class A or B, from age 18.

## 1.2 Paperwork and What to Bring

Your first form is the **Non-Commercial Learner's Permit Application, form DL-180**, from the PennDOT website. A health care provider fills out the medical section on the back after a physical exam. The physical may not be done more than six months before your 16th birthday, and the completed DL-180 is good for one year from the exam date. Bring it in person; never mail it.

Applicants under 18 add the **DL-180TD** consent form, signed by a parent, a guardian, someone acting as a parent, or a spouse aged 18 or more. If that adult cannot come with you, the signature must be notarized. If your last names differ, bring proof of how you are related, such as a birth certificate that shows the parent's name.

On test day, bring:

- The completed DL-180 (and DL-180TD if you are under 18).
- **Original** proof of your date of birth and identity; photocopies are refused. The back of the DL-180 lists accepted documents.
- Your Social Security card.
- Payment by credit or debit card, check or money order. Driver License Centers do not take cash.
- Glasses or contact lenses if you wear them.

If you are 18 or older, also bring **two proofs of {{STATE}} residency**, such as current utility bills, a lease, mortgage papers, tax records or a W-2.

**REAL ID.** A gold star license that meets federal rules for domestic flights and some federal buildings. In {{STATE}} it is **optional**: a regular license is still valid for driving, and a valid U.S. passport or military ID lets you fly without one. To get it, bring proof of identity and lawful status (such as a U.S. birth certificate with a raised seal or a valid passport), proof of your Social Security number, two proofs of residency, and the record of any name change.

> **Road tip:** Put every original in one folder the night before, and check that your name is spelled the same on each. A mismatched name is the most common reason people are sent home.

## 1.3 Fees

| Item | PennDOT fee |
|---|---|
| Learner's permit and first four-year license | $45.50 |
| Adding to, extending or replacing a permit | $6.00 |
| REAL ID (one time, added to the license fee) | $30.00 |
| Four-year renewal | $39.50 |

The $45.50 is paid once, when you apply for the permit, and covers your first license.

## 1.4 The Knowledge Test

You take the knowledge test at a PennDOT Driver License Center, after your vision is checked. It covers traffic signs, {{STATE}} driving laws and safe driving practices, with sign questions mixed in rather than tested separately.

| Feature | The {{STATE}} rule |
|---|---|
| Questions | 18, multiple choice |
| To pass | 15 correct (3 misses allowed) |
| Languages | English and many others, written or audio, on request |
| If you fail | Retake on the next business day |
| Limit | One attempt per day, at any location |

If you fail, keep the DL-180 and DL-180TD the examiner hands back; you need them to retake.

> **Test trap:** Do not study only the sign pages. On an 18 question test, four missed law questions (permit rules, school buses, alcohol limits) mean a fail.

When you pass, you get your learner's permit on the spot. It is valid for one year from the date it is issued.

## 1.5 The Vision Test

Your vision is tested during the DL-180 physical or at the Driver License Center before the knowledge test. Weak vision in one eye does not rule out a license; it may bring a restriction instead, such as glasses or contacts, daylight driving only, or vehicles with side mirrors only.

If you do not meet the standard, the examiner gives you a **Report of Eye Examination, form DL-102**. An eye doctor or family physician completes it, and then you return to the Driver License Center.

## 1.6 Driving on a Learner's Permit

**Who may supervise you.** In the front passenger seat, either a licensed driver at least 21, or a licensed parent, guardian, person acting as your parent, or spouse, aged 18 or more. The supervisor must be licensed for the type of vehicle you drive and must not be impaired by alcohol or drugs.

**Passengers (under 18).** Every passenger needs a seat belt of their own. If the car has five belts, five people is the most it may hold, you included.

**Out of state.** Another state may not recognize your permit; check with your insurer and that state first.

### The Practice Requirement

Before the road test, a driver under 18 must log **65 hours** of supervised driving: at least **10 at night** and at least **5 in bad weather** such as rain or snow. Your parent or guardian certifies the hours on form **DL-180C**.

You must also hold the permit for **six months** from the date it was issued. Both conditions apply: finishing the 65 hours in two months does not shorten the wait.

> **Remember it:** 65, 10 and 5, then six months. Use the log at the back of this book to record every session.

## 1.7 The Junior License: Your First Months Alone

**Night limit.** From **11 p.m. to 5 a.m.** you may drive only with a parent, guardian, someone acting as your parent, or your spouse (18 or over) riding along. Exceptions cover a job, public or charitable service, or a volunteer fire company. Then you must carry a notarized statement or a certificate from your employer, supervisor or fire chief showing your likely schedule, kept with your license.

**Passenger limits.**

| Time on junior license | Passengers under 18 who are not immediate family |
|---|---|
| First 6 months | No more than 1 |
| After 6 months | No more than 3 |

The limits are lifted while a parent, guardian or adult spouse rides with you. You lose the higher limit for good if you are ever partly or fully at fault in a crash or convicted of any driving violation. One belt per person still applies.

**Points and speed.** A junior driver's license is suspended for **90 days** for **6 or more points** or for a single ticket for **26 mph or more above the limit**. A later suspension runs 120 days.

**Phones.** {{STATE}} bans holding a phone while driving for every driver (see Chapter 11).

### Moving Up to a Regular License

After **one year** on a junior license, you may apply for a regular license on form DL-59 if you passed a driver training course approved by the {{STATE}} Department of Education, had no crash that year in which you were partly or fully at fault, had no Vehicle Code conviction that year, and a parent, guardian or adult spouse signs off. Otherwise the junior license becomes a regular license automatically at 18.

> **Test trap:** The night limit runs from 11 p.m. to 5 a.m., not midnight. A 21-year-old friend riding along does not lift it. Only a parent, guardian or adult spouse does.

## 1.8 The Road Test

### Scheduling and What to Bring

Book an appointment on the PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services website or by phone at 1-800-423-5542. Under 18, you may book up to six months ahead, for a date on or after the eligibility date on your permit. Some private businesses are certified by PennDOT to give the same test for their own fee.

Bring these, all original and valid, or you will not be tested:

- Your learner's permit.
- The signed DL-180C, if you are under 18.
- The test car's registration card and proof of insurance.
- The driver's license of the adult who drove you there (21 or over, or a parent, guardian or adult spouse).

### The Car Check

The examiner first inspects the car. The registration, insurance, inspection sticker and, where required, emissions sticker must be current, and the lights, horn, brakes, wipers, turn signals, mirrors, doors, seats, tires and seat belts must work. If anything is unsafe or would fail state inspection, there is no test that day. You both wear seat belts throughout.

### What the Examiner Scores

The examiner may ask you to work the controls: horn, parking lights, low and high beams, turn signals, wipers, parking brake, hazard flashers and defroster. Failing to work any control is a failed test.

Next comes **parallel parking** in a space 24 feet long and 8 feet wide, marked by upright poles. You get one attempt and no more than three adjustments, and the whole car must end up inside. Touching a pole, crossing the painted line or riding onto the curb is a fail. Any self-parking feature must be off.

Then you drive on the road with the examiner as your only passenger. You fail on the spot for dangerous or reckless driving, breaking a traffic law (such as rolling through a stop sign), causing a crash, not following instructions, not using your turn signals, not being able to work a control, or too many errors in safe driving practice.

### If You Fail

A driver under 18 must wait **seven days** before retesting. You get **three tries** on each permit. After the third failure, or if the permit expires, apply to extend it on form DL-31. If three years pass from your physical exam date without a passed road test, you start over with a new DL-180 and retake the knowledge test.

Pass at a PennDOT Driver License Center and you get your license that day.

## 1.9 Adults Getting a First License

At 18 or older, you still need a permit, a vision test, the knowledge test and the road test, using the same DL-180 with no consent form but with two proofs of residency. You skip the six-month wait, the 65-hour certificate and the junior license; those apply only under 18. Practice anyway, since the road test is the same. The supervision rule, the one-year permit and the three-try road test limit all still apply.

## 1.10 Foreign and Out-of-State License Holders

With a valid license from another country, you may drive in {{STATE}} for up to **one year** from entering the United States, or until that license expires, whichever comes first. An International Driving Permit is strongly recommended but not required.

- **France, Germany, South Korea or Taiwan.** Under reciprocity agreements, you can transfer a valid non-commercial license with a vision test but no knowledge or road test. A transfer from France or Germany cannot be finished on the day you apply.
- **Every other country.** You follow the new driver steps: permit, vision test, knowledge test and road test.

With a license from another U.S. state, you skip the knowledge test if your license is valid or expired six months or less. Expired more than six months, you start with a permit and take both tests. Out-of-state learner's permits cannot be transferred.

## 1.11 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **{{STATE}} Driver's Manual (PUB 95)**, source of the test questions, is free at pa.gov in many languages and in audio, with forms, fees, center locations and test scheduling. If this book and the manual ever disagree, the manual and current law win. Check the site the week before each test.

## Quick Review

- Apply on or after your 16th birthday with form DL-180 (plus DL-180TD under 18), bringing originals only.
- The knowledge test has 18 questions; you need 15 correct and may retake it the next business day.
- The supervisor rides in the front passenger seat: a licensed driver at least 21, or a parent, guardian or spouse at least 18.
- Under 18: 65 hours (10 at night, 5 in bad weather) and six months on the permit before the road test.
- Junior license: no driving 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. without a parent or guardian, and 1 non-family passenger under 18 for six months.
