# Pennsylvania inserts

## school_zone

In a {{STATE}} school zone, the speed limit is **15 mph**. It applies while the yellow lights on the School Speed Limit sign flash, or during the times printed on the sign. At other times, the normal posted limit applies. Speeding in a school zone adds **3 points** to your driving record, on top of the fine.

## work_zone

In {{STATE}}, your **headlights** must be on in every posted work zone, even when no one is working. If police stop you there without them, you pay an extra **$25**.

An **active work zone** has workers present and a flashing "Active Work Zone When Flashing" sign. There, speeding fines **double**. Going **11 mph or more over** the limit costs your license for **15 days**.

Speed cameras there ticket the owner at 11 mph or more over: a warning letter, then **$75**, then **$150**. Camera tickets add no points.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, you may turn **right on a steady red light** unless a sign forbids it. Stop completely first, then yield to pedestrians and cross traffic.

**Left on red** is allowed only between two one-way streets, from the left lane, after a full stop.

A **steady red arrow** allows the same two turns, with the same full stop first, unless a sign says no.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Move Over law covers an **emergency response area**: police, fire, ambulance, tow trucks or road crews with lights on. It also covers a **disabled vehicle** marked with at least two warnings, such as hazard lights, flares or signs.

Move into a lane that is not next to them. If you cannot, slow to **20 mph or more under the limit**.

Fines: up to **$500**, then $1,000, then $2,000, plus **2 points**. Seriously injuring or killing someone brings a suspension of 90 days to one year.

## emergency_vehicles

In {{STATE}}, this applies to any vehicle using a siren or flashing red, or red and blue, lights, from any direction. On a **one-way street**, pull toward the **nearest roadside**, left or right, and stop. Obey any uniformed police officer or fire police directing traffic.

After it passes, check for a second one. Then follow no closer than **500 feet** behind it.


## school_bus

When a {{STATE}} school bus flashes its **red lights** with the **stop arm out**, stop at least **10 feet** away. This applies from behind, from the front and at an intersection.

Wait until the children are safe too. A wave from the bus driver is not enough.

The one exception: on a **divided highway** with a median, such as a barrier, guide rail or strip of grass, traffic on the other side may pass with care.

Breaking this law: **5 points**, a **60-day suspension** and a **$250** fine.

## u_turn

In {{STATE}}, make a U-turn only where it will not put anyone in danger. Drivers coming from every direction must be at least **500 feet** away and able to see you clearly. Never make one on a curve, near the top of a hill, or where a sign forbids it. If you miss an exit on an Interstate, go on to the next exit. Never turn through the median.

## speed_limits

{{STATE}} limits when no sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| School zone, lights flashing | 15 mph |
| Local road in a residence district | 25 mph |
| Urban district | 35 mph |
| Other roads | 55 mph |
| Posted freeways | 65 or 70 mph |

The state maximum is **70 mph**.

| Over the limit by | Points |
|---|---|
| 6 to 10 mph | 2 |
| 11 to 15 mph | 3 |
| 16 to 25 mph | 4 |
| 26 mph or more | 5 |

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} Driver's Manual teaches a **4-second** following distance on dry pavement. If you reach the fixed point before you count to four, you are too close.

On wet roads, slow down by 5 to 10 mph and stretch the gap to **5 or 6 seconds**. In work zones, double your usual gap. Behind a truck or a motorcycle, keep at least four seconds.

## hov

{{STATE}} has very few HOV lanes. The best known is a reversible lane on I-279 near Pittsburgh: it runs inbound in the morning and outbound in the evening. At any HOV or reversible lane, read the entrance signs for the direction, hours and number of riders. Never enter against a red X or a closed gate.

## parking_rules

Unless police tell you otherwise, {{STATE}} bans stopping or parking within:

| Place | Feet |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant | 15 |
| Crosswalk at an intersection | 20 |
| Fire station driveway | 20 |
| Stop sign, yield sign or signal | 30 |
| Nearest rail of a railroad crossing | 50 |

Park **within 12 inches** of a curb, facing the way traffic moves. If you must park on the roadway, turn on your flashers and make sure drivers can see your car from **500 feet** both ways.

A disability space without a plate or placard: **$50 to $200** and a possible tow.

## bicycle_passing

In {{STATE}}, leave at least **4 feet** between your car and a bicycle when you pass it. You may cross a double yellow center line to do this, but only when the other lane is clear and it is safe. If it is not safe yet, stay behind the cyclist and wait.

## crash_reporting

In {{STATE}}, police must be called when a crash injures or kills anyone, or leaves a car that has to be towed.

If police do not come to investigate a crash like that, you must report it yourself. Send PennDOT **form AA-600**, the Driver's Accident Report, within **5 days**.

## bac_limits

{{STATE}} sets three limits:

| Driver | DUI at a BAC of |
|---|---|
| Age 21 or older | 0.08 percent |
| Commercial driver | 0.04 percent |
| Under 21 | 0.02 percent |
| School bus driver | 0.02 percent |

Even below these numbers, you can be convicted if alcohol has made you unsafe to drive.

## implied_consent

By driving in {{STATE}}, you agree to a breath or blood test when police suspect DUI.

If you refuse, you lose your license for **one year**. The suspension stands even if a court later finds you not guilty of DUI. With a past DUI or refusal, it is **18 months**.

A refusal also puts you in the highest DUI penalty tier if you are convicted. To get your license back, you need an ignition interlock and must pay a **$653** restoration fee the first time.

## dui_penalties

First DUI in {{STATE}}:

| BAC | Sentence | Fine | License |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.08 to 0.099 | 6 months probation | $300 | No suspension |
| 0.10 to 0.159 | 2 days to 6 months in jail | $500 to $5,000 | 12 months |
| 0.16 or more, or refusal | 3 days to 6 months in jail | $1,000 to $5,000 | 12 months |

Every tier also means Alcohol Highway Safety School.

No driver **or passenger** may drink alcohol or hold an **open container** of it on the road. Passengers in a bus, taxi or limousine are the main exception.

## underage_alcohol

In {{STATE}}, a driver **under 21** with a BAC of **0.02** or more is guilty of DUI. A first offense brings the middle tier penalties in the table above, including a **12-month** suspension.

Off the road, buying, trying to buy, drinking, holding or carrying alcohol under 21 brings a fine of up to **$500**, then up to $1,000. Using a **false ID** is a separate offense. None of these brings an automatic license suspension.

Adults who give alcohol to minors pay at least **$1,000**.

## phone_laws

{{STATE}} bans **texting** while the car is moving. That means sending, reading or writing a text, email or other message.

**Paul Miller's Law** also bans **holding** a phone or other device while you drive, even when you are stopped in traffic or at a red light. Hands-free calls, GPS and music are allowed, and you may always call 911 in an emergency.

Each law carries a **$50** fine plus court costs. To use a phone in your hand, pull off the road and stop somewhere safe first.

## seat_belts

In {{STATE}}, the driver and every front seat passenger must wear a seat belt. Passengers aged **8 to 17** must be belted in **any** seat, and the driver is responsible for them.

A driver **under 18** must be belted and may carry only as many people as there are seat belts.

A belt ticket for anyone under 18 can be written on its own. For adults in the front seat, the **$10** belt fine applies only along with another traffic ticket.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} makes the driver responsible for securing every child, in any seat:

| Age | Required |
|---|---|
| Under 2 | Rear-facing seat, until the child outgrows the seat's limits |
| 2 to under 4 | Child safety seat |
| 4 to under 8 | Booster seat with lap and shoulder belt |
| 8 to under 18 | Seat belt |

Breaking this law costs a **$75** fine.

## vehicle_rules

In {{STATE}}, your car needs a **safety inspection every 12 months**. Many counties also require an **emissions inspection**. Either may be done up to 90 days early.

Minimum insurance: **$15,000** per person and **$30,000** per crash for injuries, **$5,000** for property damage and $5,000 in medical benefits. Keep proof in the car. Uninsured **31 days** or more: the registration is suspended for 3 months, and your license too if you drove it or let someone else drive it.

Report a name or address change to PennDOT within **15 days**.

## police_stop

The {{STATE}} manual adds a few steps. Lower your window before the officer reaches you, and keep your passengers' hands where the officer can see them. If you carry a firearm, tell the officer. Hand over your license, registration and insurance card, not your wallet.

Flashing blue lights alone do not mean a police car. If you are unsure, drive slowly to a well lit, busy place and ask to see identification.

**Fleeing** from police costs your license for **one year**, plus a **$500** fine.

## headlight_use

In {{STATE}}, your headlights must also be on:

- on gray days or in heavy traffic, when your car can blend in;
- in rain, snow, sleet, hail, fog or smoke;
- whenever you cannot see people or vehicles **1,000 feet** ahead;
- in every posted work zone;
- whenever your **wipers** are on because of the weather, even on a slow setting.

Daytime running lights alone do not count, because your **taillights** must be on too. Breaking the wiper rule carries a **$25** fine, close to $100 with costs.


## driver_record

{{STATE}} counts points. The **first time** you reach **6 points**, you choose a written special point exam (passing removes 2 points) or a driver improvement school (removes 4). The **second time**, you must attend a hearing and the school, and you may be suspended up to **15 days**.

At **11 points**, your license is suspended: **5 days per point** the first time.

Each **12 months** in a row with no violation or suspension removes **3 points**.

## road_test

The {{STATE}} road test starts with car and controls checks, then **parallel parking** between upright poles in a space **24 by 8 feet**. You get one try with up to **three adjustments**. Touching a pole, crossing a line or driving onto the curb fails you.

The examiner is your only passenger, and self-parking features must be off. Pass at a PennDOT Driver License Center and you leave with your license that day. Under 18, you wait **7 days** to retest, and you get **three tries** on each permit.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers under 18 must log supervised practice before the road test:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total | 65 |
| At night | At least 10 |
| In bad weather | At least 5 |

Night and bad weather hours count toward the 65. A parent or guardian signs them off on form **DL-180C**. Your supervisor sits in the front passenger seat and must be a licensed driver 21 or older, or your licensed parent, guardian or spouse 18 or older. You must also hold the permit for **six months**.

## signal_distance

{{STATE}} law sets how early to signal. Below **35 mph**, signal for at least the last **100 feet** before you turn. At 35 mph or more, signal for at least the last **300 feet**.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, switch to low beams when an oncoming vehicle is within **500 feet**. When you follow another vehicle, switch to low beams within **300 feet** of it.
