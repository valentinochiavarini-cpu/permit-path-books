# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

A {{STATE}} license comes in steps, and each step has its own paperwork, test or waiting period. The {{STATE}} Department of Motor Vehicles (**SCDMV**) runs every step through its branch offices. Fees and forms change from time to time, so check the SCDMV website before each visit.

> **On the test**
>
> - When a permit driver may drive, and who must sit beside them.
> - The 180-day permit wait and the 40 practice hours, 10 of them at night.
> - Conditional and special restricted license limits: hours and passengers.
> - What happens when a young driver collects points.

## 1.1 The Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses **graduated licensing**. You earn freedom in stages because new drivers crash most often in their first months alone.

| Stage | Earliest age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner's permit | 15 | Drive only with a qualified licensed adult |
| Conditional license | 15 and a half | Drive alone in daytime, with limits |
| Special restricted license | 16 | Same limits as the conditional license |
| Full privileges | 17, or after one clean year | No teen limits |

Cars, pickups and vans need a **Class D** license. It also covers a moped.

## 1.2 Paperwork and What to Bring

For a first permit, bring original documents that prove:

- U.S. citizenship and your identity, date and place of birth (a government birth certificate or a valid U.S. passport works).
- Your Social Security number.
- Your current {{STATE}} physical address.

The full list is on the **US Citizens' Checklist, form MV-93**. If you are a lawfully present non-U.S. citizen, use the **form MV-94** checklist instead and go to a branch that handles international customers.

**Under 18.** A qualified adult must sign your consent at the branch and show a photo ID. That adult takes on responsibility for damage you cause while driving. A driver training school can also submit the consent for you.

**REAL ID or not.** A REAL ID card needs **two** proofs of address. You need it for domestic flights unless you carry a passport. A non-REAL ID license is just as valid for driving.

> **Road tip:** Check that your name is spelled the same way on every document before you leave home. A mismatch is the fastest way to be sent back.

## 1.3 Fees

| Item | SCDMV fee |
|---|---|
| Knowledge test | $2.00 |
| Beginner's permit (each one, or a renewal) | $2.50 |
| Driver's license (eight years) | $25 |
| New license with a special restricted waiver | $25 |

You pay for the permit only after you pass the knowledge test.

## 1.4 The Knowledge Test

The knowledge test checks traffic laws, road signs and safe driving rules. Take it at **any SCDMV branch**, arriving before **4 p.m.**, or with an SCDMV-approved driving school that tests through the **One Stop** program.

The SCDMV does not publish the number of questions or the passing score. Study the whole manual and every chapter of this book, and take the free practice test on the SCDMV website.

**Test room rules.** You fail at once if you:

- leave the test area before you finish;
- talk to anyone;
- bring in notes, books or any electronic device, such as a phone, laptop or tablet.

> **Test trap:** Do not count on a phone in your pocket being harmless. Leave it with your parent or in the car.

## 1.5 The Vision Test

You pass a vision screening before your first permit. You may take it at the branch, or have a licensed eye care professional examine you and send the SCDMV a **Certificate of Vision Examination** (an eye doctor licensed in {{STATE}} submits it online). A certificate counts for **36 months**.

The minimum standard is **20/40 in at least one eye**, with glasses or contacts if you need them. If you pass only with lenses, your license shows restriction **code A**, and driving without them is against the law. If you fail at the branch, an eye care professional must test you.

## 1.6 Driving on a Beginner's Permit

A permit lasts **12 months**. You can renew it, or get a new one, for $2.50.

| Time of day | Who must sit beside you |
|---|---|
| 6 a.m. to midnight | A licensed driver **21 or older** with at least **one year** of driving experience, in the front seat |
| Midnight to 6 a.m. | Only a **qualified supervisor** (list below) |

**Qualified supervisors** are your father, mother or legal guardian; a person with custody of you; a person approved in writing by the Department of Social Services; a person who has stood in place of a parent for at least **60 days**; or a responsible adult with notarized written permission from one of those people. For the Social Services approval and the notarized permission, the SCDMV needs a copy.

### How Long You Hold the Permit

| Your age | Minimum time on the permit |
|---|---|
| 17 and under | **180 days** (apply on day 181) |
| 18 and older | **30 days** (apply on day 31) |

### Driver Education and Practice

If you are **15 or 16** and want your first license, all of these must be true:

- You held the permit **more than 180 days**.
- You finished driver education: **8 hours** in a classroom and **6 hours** of driving.
- You are enrolled in school, not suspended or expelled, with satisfactory attendance. Home school counts.
- You practiced at least **40 hours**, with **10 at night**, with a qualified supervisor.

Your driver education program gives you the **Certification of School Attendance, Driver's Education and Driving Practice, form PDLA**. Bring it to the road test. Use the log at the back of this book to track your hours.

> **Remember it:** 15 for the permit, 180 days on it, 8 plus 6 in driver ed, then 40 hours with 10 after dark.

## 1.7 Conditional and Special Restricted Licenses

These two licenses carry the same limits. Which one you get depends on your age:

- **Conditional license:** age 15 and a half to under 16.
- **Special restricted license:** age 16 to under 17, or at 16 when you already hold a conditional license.

For either one, meet the requirements above and pass the vision and road tests.

| Time | How you may drive |
|---|---|
| 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. (8 p.m. in daylight saving time) | Alone |
| 6 p.m. (or 8 p.m.) to midnight | Only with a licensed driver 21 or older |
| Midnight to 6 a.m. | Only with a qualified supervisor |

**Passengers.** No more than **two passengers under 21**, unless a licensed adult 21 or older rides along. Family members, and students you drive to or from school, do not count.

### The Waiver at 16

At 16, a **special restricted license with a waiver** lets you drive alone until **midnight** for work or certain school, church or activity events. It never covers driving after midnight. At a branch you need:

- the **Application, form 447-NC**;
- the **Consent for Minor, form 447-CM**, completed by a qualified adult in front of SCDMV staff;
- a letter from the school, church, employer or activity explaining the need;
- a letter from your parent or guardian giving permission;
- a passed vision test and the **$25** fee.

### Points Before 17

The SCDMV sends a 15 or 16-year-old a warning letter at **2 points** and again at **4 points**. A permit, conditional or special restricted holder who reaches **6 points** is suspended. If you are under 17 and reach 6 points within your first year on the license, the suspension lasts **six months**. A defensive driving course will not lift this suspension.

### Full Privileges

You earn full privileges at **17**, or after holding the conditional or special restricted license for **one year** with no traffic offenses and no at-fault crashes. You do not visit a branch. The SCDMV mails you a **sleeve** for your license, and you must keep the license in it to use full privileges.

> **Test trap:** In daylight saving time, the "alone" window ends at **8 p.m.**, not 6 p.m. Driving alone after midnight is never allowed, even with a waiver.

## 1.8 The Road Test

### Scheduling and What to Bring

Branches take walk-ins for road tests from **8:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m.** (from 9:30 a.m. on Wednesdays). From **2 to 4 p.m.** you need an appointment, which you can book on the SCDMV website. An approved third-party tester can also give the test.

Bring your permit, a licensed driver, and liability insurance information from a company licensed in {{STATE}}. Teens also bring the PDLA.

### The Car

You supply a safe vehicle with a valid plate, registration card and insurance card. Headlights, brakes, mirrors, wipers, seat belts, turn signals, speedometer, defroster and horn must all work. In a rental car, your name must be on the contract as an insured driver. An unsafe car means the test is postponed.

### What Is Scored

Only you and the examiner may be in or around the car. You may be asked to:

- show the headlights, wipers, turn signals, four-way flashers and brake lights;
- change lanes and make right and left turns;
- identify road signs;
- parallel park and park on a hill;
- back up in a straight line for **100 feet**;
- make a reverse two-point turnabout, a forward two-point turnabout and a three-point turnabout;
- drive on an interstate.

Not wearing your **seat belt** is an automatic fail. You may not use features such as parking assist or cruise control.

### If You Fail

| Failed attempt | Wait before the next try |
|---|---|
| First | 2 business days |
| Second | 7 calendar days |
| Third or later | 30 calendar days |

These waits apply at any age. Even after you pass, the SCDMV may pick you at random to take the road test again before your license is issued.

## 1.9 Adults Getting a First License

**At 17.** You hold the permit **180 days**, then pass the road test. The teen license limits end at 17, so you receive a regular license.

**At 18 or older.** You still start with a beginner's permit, the vision test and the knowledge test, and practice only with a licensed driver 21 or older with one year of experience. You hold the permit only **30 days**. Driver education, the PDLA and the 40-hour rule apply only to 15 and 16-year-olds, but practice anyway: the road test is the same for everyone.

## 1.10 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

**New residents.** Once you move to {{STATE}} for good, you have **45 days** to get a {{STATE}} license, and you surrender your old one. You pass a vision test. If your out-of-state license has been expired more than **nine months**, you also take the knowledge and road tests. Active-duty military and federal employees are exempt from the 45-day rule. Temporary residents, such as students and military members, may keep driving on a valid license from home.

**License from another country.** Apply at a branch that serves international customers, with the **form MV-94** documents. {{STATE}} waives the knowledge and road tests only for a license from a country that has a formal **reciprocity agreement** with the state, and only if it has not been expired more than nine months. Ask the SCDMV whether your country qualifies. Everyone else takes the vision, knowledge and road tests.

**Visitors.** A valid license from home lets you drive here. From a non-English-speaking country, carry an **International Driving Permit** with it. It translates your license; it is not a license itself.

## 1.11 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **{{STATE}} Driver's Manual** is free on the SCDMV website (dmv.sc.gov), along with the practice test, the teen guide **Ready for the Road? (DMVB-35)**, forms and branch locations. If this book and the manual ever disagree, the manual and current law win. Check the site the week before each test.

## Quick Review

- Beginner's permit at 15: pass the vision and knowledge tests, then pay $2.50.
- Permit driving: 6 a.m. to midnight with a licensed driver 21 or older (one year of experience); midnight to 6 a.m. only with a qualified supervisor.
- Hold the permit 180 days if 17 or under, 30 days if 18 or older.
- Ages 15 and 16: driver education (8 hours class, 6 driving), 40 practice hours with 10 at night, school attendance and the PDLA form.
- Conditional (15 and a half) and special restricted (16) licenses: alone only 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. (8 p.m. in daylight saving time); no more than two passengers under 21.
- Full privileges at 17, or after one year with no offenses and no at-fault crashes.
- Road test retest waits: 2 business days, then 7 days, then 30 days. No seat belt means an automatic fail.
- New residents: 45 days, vision test, no other tests unless the old license expired more than nine months ago.
