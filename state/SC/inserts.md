# South Carolina inserts

## school_zone

{{STATE}} law sets no single school zone speed for the whole state. Each school zone has its own limit, posted on a sign that often adds flashing yellow lights or school hours. When the lights flash or the posted hours apply, that lower limit is the law.

School zone and school crossing signs are often **fluorescent yellow-green**. Obey every crossing guard.

## work_zone

In {{STATE}}, **endangerment of a highway worker** means driving through a work zone in a lane you are not allowed to use, or ignoring the work zone's signs, cones or flaggers.

With no one hurt, the fine is **$500 to $1,000** and **2 points**. If a worker is injured, the points rise to **4** and the fines climb higher. Judges may not lower these fines.

Slow down, do not tailgate, and be ready to stop suddenly.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, you may turn **right on a steady red light** after a full stop, unless a sign forbids it. Stop before the crosswalk, then yield to pedestrians and to all traffic already using the intersection.

**Left on red** is allowed only from a one-way street onto another one-way street, after the same full stop.

A **steady red arrow** means you may not make that turn. Stay stopped until a green signal allows it.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Move Over law covers any stopped emergency vehicle with flashing red, blue, red and white, or amber lights, and any stopped vehicle with its hazard lights on.

Slow down **a lot** and pass with care. On a road with at least **two lanes going your way**, move to a lane not next to the stopped vehicle if you can do it safely. If you cannot, keep a safe, slower speed.

A violation costs **$300 to $500**.

## emergency_vehicles

In {{STATE}}, the rule depends on the road:

- **Two-lane road:** pull as close as you can to the **right** edge or curb, stop, and stay there until the emergency vehicle passes, unless an officer tells you otherwise.
- **Multilane road:** stay in your lane or move to a spot that lets the vehicle pass safely.

The same rule applies to a police car using its siren or lights. Keep a safe distance behind any emergency vehicle, and never follow one to its call.

## school_bus

In {{STATE}}, when a school bus stops with **red lights flashing**, drivers coming from **both directions** must stop before reaching it. Stay stopped until the bus moves or the red lights go off. The law sets no distance in feet.

On a highway with **four or more lanes**, at least two each way, traffic coming **toward** the bus does not stop. Never pass a bus showing **amber** lights.

Passing a stopped bus: at least **$500** or up to **30 days** in jail, and **6 points**.

## u_turn

In {{STATE}}, a U-turn is legal only when you can make it safely without getting in anyone's way.

Never make one on a curve, or near the top of a hill, where a driver coming from either direction could not see your car from **500 feet** away. Obey every NO U-TURN sign.

## speed_limits

{{STATE}} limits when no sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Urban district | 30 mph |
| Unpaved road | 40 mph |
| Other roads | 55 mph |
| Multilane divided primary highway, where posted | 60 mph |
| Interstate or other freeway, where posted | 70 mph |

| Over the limit by | Points |
|---|---|
| 10 mph or less | 2 |
| More than 10, under 25 mph | 4 |
| 25 mph or more | 6 |

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} Driver's Manual teaches a minimum following distance of **four seconds** behind the vehicle ahead.

Add more time on slippery roads, at night, in fog, and behind large vehicles such as trucks, buses and vehicles pulling trailers. Following too closely costs **4 points**.

## parking_rules

{{STATE}} bans standing or parking:

| Place | Distance |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant | Within 15 feet |
| Crosswalk at an intersection | Within 20 feet |
| Fire station driveway | Within 20 feet (75 feet across the street, where posted) |
| Stop sign, yield sign, signal or flashing beacon | Within 30 feet |
| Nearest rail of a railroad crossing | Within 50 feet |

Never stop on a sidewalk, crosswalk, bridge, railroad track or controlled-access highway, or in an intersection. At a curb, park no more than **18 inches** away.

## bicycle_passing

{{STATE}} law requires you to keep a **safe operating distance** between your car and a bicycle at all times. The law sets no number in feet, so give as much room as you can.

Before you pass a cyclist in a narrow lane, wait until the opposite lane is clear. Slow down when you pass. Cyclists must ride with traffic, in the same direction you are going.

## crash_reporting

After a {{STATE}} crash with **injury or death**, notify police at once: city police inside a city, or the sheriff or Highway Patrol outside one.

If a crash causes injury, death or at least **$1,000** in property damage and no officer investigates it, the driver or owner must send a written report to the SCDMV within **15 days**.

When an officer does investigate, the officer files the report.

## bac_limits

In {{STATE}}, driving with a BAC of **0.08** or more is **DUAC**, driving with an unlawful alcohol concentration. You can also be charged with **DUI** at a lower BAC if alcohol or drugs impair your driving.

| Driver | Limit |
|---|---|
| 21 or older | 0.08 percent |
| Under 21 | 0.02 percent |

A test result of **0.15** or more brings its own license suspension on top of any DUI penalty.

## implied_consent

By driving in {{STATE}}, you agree to a breath, blood or urine test if police suspect impaired driving. Refuse, and your license is suspended right away.

| Refusal | Suspension |
|---|---|
| First | 6 months |
| Second | 9 months |
| Third | 12 months |
| Fourth or more | 15 months |

A test of **0.15** or more brings a **1-month** suspension the first time. You have **30 days** to request a hearing, and may get a $100 temporary alcohol license while you wait.

## dui_penalties

A first DUI in {{STATE}}, by BAC:

| BAC | Fine | Or jail |
|---|---|---|
| Under 0.10 | $400 | 48 hours to 30 days |
| 0.10 to under 0.16 | $500 | 72 hours to 30 days |
| 0.16 or more | $1,000 | 30 to 90 days |

Your license is suspended. To drive again, you need an **ignition interlock** for **six months**.

**Open containers:** an opened beer, wine or liquor container may ride only in the trunk, luggage compartment or a cargo area apart from the passengers. Fine: up to **$100**, or up to 30 days in jail.

## underage_alcohol

A {{STATE}} driver **under 21** with a BAC of **0.02** or more loses the license **three months** (six with a recent prior). Refusing the test: **six months** (one year with a recent prior). To get it back, you must attend an alcohol and drug safety program (ADSAP).

Under 21, buying, drinking or having alcohol, or lying about your age for it, costs **$100 to $200** or up to 30 days in jail, plus an 8-hour alcohol course. Your license is also suspended **120 days** (one year for a repeat).

## phone_laws

{{STATE}}'s Hands-Free and Distracted Driving Act applies to every driver. While driving, you may not:

- hold or support a phone or other device with any part of your body;
- read, write or send texts, email or app messages;
- watch videos, games or video calls.

Earpieces, wrist devices, and voice or navigation use without holding or typing are allowed. You may call to report a crash or emergency.

First offense: **$100**. Second within three years: **$200** and **2 points**.

## seat_belts

{{STATE}} has a **primary** seat belt law. An officer who clearly sees a driver or passenger without a belt, or a child not in the right seat, may stop the car for that reason alone.

The fine is up to **$25**, with no points on your record. Wear the shoulder strap across your chest, never under your arm or behind your back, and keep the lap belt low on your hips.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} rules for children under 8, all in a **rear seat**:

| Child | Required |
|---|---|
| Under 2 | Rear-facing car seat, until it is outgrown |
| 2 and older, or outgrew rear-facing | Forward-facing seat with a harness |
| 4 and older, outgrew forward-facing | Booster seat with lap and shoulder belt |
| 8 and older, or 57 inches tall | Adult belt, if it fits properly |

Never use a booster with a lap belt alone, and never put a rear-facing seat in front of an airbag.

## vehicle_rules

{{STATE}} minimum liability insurance: **$25,000** per person and **$50,000** per crash for injuries, and **$25,000** for property damage. Carry your license, registration and insurance card every time you drive.

Driving your own car uninsured keeps your license and registration suspended until you pay a **$700** fee, and you must file an **SR-22** for three years.

Secure every load so nothing can slide, fall or blow off. An unsecured load is a crime. Replace a tire if a penny test shows all of Lincoln's head.

## police_stop

In {{STATE}}, police cars use **blue** lights. When one signals you with lights or a siren, pull to the right side of the road and stop as far from traffic as you can.

Have your license, registration and insurance card ready to show. Every driver must carry all three.

Failing to stop for a police signal is a crime: at least a **$500** fine and a license suspension of at least **30 days** for a first offense, and a felony after that.

## headlight_use

In {{STATE}}, your headlights must be on:

- from **a half hour after sunset** to **a half hour before sunrise**;
- whenever bad weather or other conditions keep you from clearly seeing people and vehicles **500 feet** ahead;
- whenever your **wipers** are on because of rain, sleet or snow (not when you use them only now and then in a light mist).

The fine is up to **$25**.

## driver_record

{{STATE}} points drop **by half** one year after the violation date. Most moving violations cost **2** to **4** points; reckless driving, hit and run with property damage, and passing a stopped school bus cost **6**.

| Points (age 17 or older) | Result |
|---|---|
| 6 | Warning letter |
| 12 to 15 | 3-month suspension |
| 16 to 19 | 4 or 5-month suspension |
| 20 or more | 6-month suspension |

An 8-hour classroom **defensive driving course** taken in {{STATE}} removes up to **4 points**, once every **three years**. It does not cancel a suspension that has already started.

## road_test

The {{STATE}} road test may include parallel parking, parking on a hill, backing **100 feet** in a straight line, two-point and three-point turnabouts, lane changes, sign identification and interstate driving.

Not wearing your seat belt fails you at once. Parking assist and cruise control are not allowed.

After a failure, wait **2 business days**, then **7** days after a second failure, and **30** days after a third. Chapter 1 has the full rules.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers who are 15 or 16 must log supervised practice before the road test:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total | 40 |
| At night | At least 10 |

The supervisor must be a qualified licensed adult (see Chapter 1). Teens also finish driver education (**8 hours** of class and **6 hours** of driving) and hold the permit **more than 180 days**. The driver education program provides the **PDLA** certification form.

## signal_distance

{{STATE}} law requires you to signal continuously for at least the last **100 feet** before you turn. Signal at least 100 feet before you change lanes or pass, too, and cancel the signal when you finish.

You may use a hand and arm signal instead of the signal lights.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, switch to low beams within **500 feet** of an oncoming vehicle, so the glare does not shine into the other driver's eyes.

When you follow another vehicle, use low beams within **200 feet** of it, unless you are passing. Failing to dim costs **2 points**.
