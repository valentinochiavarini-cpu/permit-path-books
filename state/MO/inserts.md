# Missouri inserts

## school_zone

{{STATE}} has no single statewide school zone speed. Cities and the state post the limit on signs. Obey the lower limit while the lights flash or children are present.

At a school crossing, stop and yield when a **school patrol member** signals you to.

Using a phone in your hand in a school zone can cost up to **$500**, more than the usual fine.

## work_zone

In a {{STATE}} work zone, obey the orange signs and every **flagger**. Expect lower limits, narrow lanes and workers nearby.

**Speeding or passing** in a work zone on a state road costs at least **$250** the first time and at least **$300** after that. These fines come on top of any other fine.

Using a phone in your hand in a work zone can cost up to **$500**.

In a moving work zone, a truck's flashing arrow panel shows which lane to use.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, you may turn **right on a steady red light** after a complete stop. Yield to pedestrians and to traffic with the green. A NO TURN ON RED sign forbids it.

{{STATE}} law allows only that right turn. There is **no left turn on red**, not even from a one-way street onto a one-way street.

At a **steady red arrow**, stop and wait. You may go only when a green arrow or a flashing yellow arrow appears.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Move Over law covers any **stopped** vehicle showing flashing **red**, **red and blue**, **amber**, or **amber and white** lights. That includes police, ambulances, fire trucks, tow trucks and highway department trucks.

With two or more lanes in your direction, move to a lane **not next to** it, if safe.

If you cannot move over, or you are on a two-lane road, **slow down** to a safe speed.

Breaking this law is a **class A misdemeanor**.

## emergency_vehicles

For a police car, fire truck or ambulance using a siren or flashing red or blue lights, pull as far to the **right** as you can and stop. The law gives no left-side option, even on a one-way street. If you are in an intersection, drive through it first.

Stay stopped until it passes, and obey orders from its **loudspeaker**. More may follow, so pull out only when the way is clear.

In a roundabout, never stop: exit, then pull right.

## school_bus

Amber lights flash about **500 feet** before a {{STATE}} school bus stops. When it shows flashing **red lights** and its **stop arm**, stop **before you reach the bus**; the law sets no exact distance.

Going the **opposite way**, you need not stop on a **divided highway** or a road with **four or more lanes**.

Wait until the bus moves or its driver signals.

Passing illegally: **5 points**, a fine up to **$1,000** and a **90-day** suspension.

## u_turn

In {{STATE}}, make a U-turn only where it is safe. Never make one on a **curve** or near the **top of a hill**, where other drivers cannot see you.

U-turns are also banned where a **No U-Turn** sign is posted, and at any intersection controlled by a **traffic signal** or a **police officer**. Some cities ban them entirely.

## speed_limits

{{STATE}} limits when no sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| City, town or village | 25 mph |
| Lettered roads | 55 mph |
| Urban freeways; other rural roads | 60 mph |
| Rural expressways | 65 mph |
| Rural interstates and freeways | 70 mph, or 75 mph where posted |

On an interstate, you may not drive slower than **40 mph** in normal conditions. A speeding ticket adds **3 points** under state law, or **2** under a city or county ordinance.

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} Driver Guide teaches a **3-second** following distance. Count "one thousand one" to "one thousand three." If you reach the fixed point before you finish, you are too close.

On **snow or ice**, leave about **8 to 10 seconds**.

## parking_rules

{{STATE}} bans parking:

- in an intersection, on a crosswalk or sidewalk, across a driveway or on a bridge;
- blocking a fire hydrant;
- beside a curb painted **yellow**;
- on the **left side** of a two-way street, or facing against traffic.

A disability space needs a placard or plate, and the disabled person must be riding, or being dropped off or picked up. Its striped access aisle is off limits too. Fine: **$50 to $300**.

## bicycle_passing

{{STATE}} law requires you to leave a **safe distance** when you pass a bicycle, and to stay clear until you are well past it. The Driver Guide says to give bicycles and mopeds a **full lane width**.

If the lane is too narrow to share, stay behind and wait for a clear stretch of road.

Passing too closely is an infraction. If it causes a crash, it becomes a misdemeanor.

## crash_reporting

In {{STATE}}, stop at every crash you are in. Leaving the scene adds **12 points** to your record.

Report the crash in writing to the Department of Revenue within **30 days** if an uninsured driver was involved and anyone was hurt or killed, or property damage was over **$500**. If you were uninsured yourself, you must report too.

Not filing a required report can cost you your license.

## bac_limits

{{STATE}} sets three limits:

| Driver | Illegal BAC |
|---|---|
| Age 21 or older | 0.08 percent |
| Commercial driver | 0.04 percent |
| Under 21 | 0.02 percent |

You can also be charged with driving while intoxicated (DWI) below these numbers. That applies to alcohol, illegal drugs, prescription medicine, over-the-counter medicine, or any mix.

## implied_consent

By driving in {{STATE}}, you agree to a chemical test of your breath, blood or urine if police suspect DWI.

If you **refuse**, your license is revoked for **one year**.

To get it back, you pay a **$45** fee and finish a Substance Awareness Traffic Offender Program (**SATOP**). You also keep proof of insurance (an **SR-22**) on file for **two years**. Some drivers must also use an **ignition interlock** for at least six months.

## dui_penalties

{{STATE}} DWI or BAC convictions:

| Offense | Jail | Fine | License |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Up to 6 months | Up to $500 | 90-day suspension |
| Second | Up to 1 year | Up to $1,000 | 1-year revocation; 5-year denial if within 5 years |
| Third | Up to 4 years | Up to $5,000 | 10-year denial |

A first conviction adds **8 points**.

{{STATE}} has **no statewide open container ban** for passenger cars, but many cities, such as St. Louis, ban open containers. Obey local rules.

## underage_alcohol

In {{STATE}}, a driver **under 21** with a BAC of **0.02** or more loses the license under the same rules as an adult at 0.08.

**Abuse and Lose:** under 21, you can lose your license for **90 days** for an alcohol-related driving offense, using or holding alcohol or drugs while driving, or an altered or misused license.

**Minor in Possession** (buying, trying to buy or holding alcohol under 21): **30 days** the first time, **90 days** the second, **one year** the third.

## phone_laws

{{STATE}}'s hands-free law covers **every driver**. While driving, you may not hold or support a phone with your hand or body, or type, text, scroll, video call, watch video or take photos.

Allowed: hands-free calls, a **single touch or swipe**, mounted GPS and music. You may hold the phone while **lawfully parked or stopped** or to report an emergency.

Fines: up to **$150**, then **$250**, then **$500** within two years. Police cannot stop you for this alone.

## seat_belts

In {{STATE}}, the driver and every **front seat** passenger must wear a seat belt.

With an **intermediate license** or an instruction permit, **every passenger in every seat** must be belted.

Children **under 16** must be secured in any seat; the driver is responsible.

Anyone under 18 riding in a pickup truck must be belted.

For adults, the fine is up to **$10**, with no points, and police cannot stop you for the belt alone.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} rules for children:

| Child | Required |
|---|---|
| Under 4, or under 40 pounds | Child safety seat |
| Age 4 to 7, 40 to 79 pounds, under 4 feet 9 inches | Child seat or booster seat |
| Age 8 or older, 80 pounds or more, or over 4 feet 9 inches | Seat belt or booster seat |

Children **12 and under** are safest in the back seat. Never put a rear-facing seat in front of an airbag.

Breaking the car seat or booster rule: up to **$50** plus court costs.

## vehicle_rules

{{STATE}} requires liability insurance of at least:

- **$25,000** for injury or death of one person;
- **$50,000** for two or more people in one crash;
- **$25,000** for property damage.

Driving uninsured adds **4 points**, and you show proof of insurance to register a vehicle.

New residents must register their vehicle within **30 days**. When you buy a vehicle, apply for the title within **30 days** or pay a penalty. When you sell one, file a Notice of Sale within 30 days.

## police_stop

The {{STATE}} Driver Guide adds a few steps. Ask passengers to stay calm, stay inside and keep their hands in view. No one in the car should use a phone. If you have a **weapon** in the car, tell the officer at first contact.

An officer not in uniform will show credentials, or you may ask to see them.

Signing a ticket does **not** admit guilt, but refusing to sign can get you arrested.

## headlight_use

In {{STATE}}, your headlights must be on:

- from **half an hour after sunset** to **half an hour before sunrise**;
- whenever you cannot clearly see people and vehicles **500 feet** ahead;
- whenever the weather calls for your **windshield wipers**;
- in **fog**, even if your wipers are off.

Breaking the weather or fog rule costs **$10**, with no court costs.

## driver_record

{{STATE}} counts points:

| Points | Result |
|---|---|
| 4 in 12 months | Warning letter |
| 8 in 18 months | Suspension: 30 days, then 60, then 90 |
| 12 in 12 months, 18 in 24, or 24 in 36 | One-year revocation |

After reinstatement, your total drops to **4 points**. Each clean year cuts it more: by one-third after one year, by half after two, to **zero** after three.

To reinstate, file proof of insurance (an SR-22) and pay a **$20** fee.

## road_test

On the {{STATE}} driving test, losing **more than 30 points** fails you. So does a crash that is your fault, hitting a pedestrian, dangerous driving or any traffic violation.

You **parallel park** in a space **25 by 7 feet**, within **18 inches** of the curb, in **two minutes**. You also park on a hill, back up and turn.

Only the examiner rides with you. You get **one test a day**; after **three** failures, you need more training and DOR approval.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers under 18 must finish supervised practice before the intermediate license:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total | 40 |
| At night | At least 10 |

Night hours count toward the 40. A parent, stepparent, legal guardian or certified trainer gives the instruction and confirms the hours at the license office. You must also hold the instruction permit for at least **six months (182 days)**.

## signal_distance

In {{STATE}}, signal at least **100 feet** before you turn or change lanes.

If a street or driveway lies between you and your turn, wait until you pass it before you signal. Otherwise, a driver there may think you are turning into it.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, switch to low beams when an oncoming vehicle comes within **500 feet**, about one block. When you follow another vehicle, switch to low beams within **300 feet** of it.
