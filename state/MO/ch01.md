# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

In {{STATE}}, two agencies share the job of licensing you. The Missouri State Highway Patrol gives the tests at its driver examination stations. The Department of Revenue (DOR) issues the permit or license itself at a license office. Passing a test is only half the trip; you may drive only after the license office issues your document.

> **On the test**
>
> - Who may ride with you on an instruction permit, and where they sit.
> - The 40 hours (10 at night) and the six months on the permit.
> - Intermediate license passenger limits and the 1 a.m. to 5 a.m. limit.
> - Points, alcohol limits for drivers under 21, and how a license is lost.

## 1.1 The Three Steps for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}}'s **Graduated Driver License (GDL)** law makes every first-time driver under 18 earn privileges in steps.

| Step | Earliest age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Instruction permit | 15 | Drive only with a qualified adult in the front seat |
| Intermediate license | 16 | Drive alone, with passenger and late night limits |
| Full license | 18 | Full privileges (marked as under 21 until you turn 21) |

Cars, pickups and vans need a **Class F** license, also called an operator license. Motorcycles need Class M or an M endorsement. Driving people or goods for pay needs a Class E license, from age 18.

Every permit and license issued before age 21 is marked to show you are a minor.

## 1.2 What to Bring

**At the examination station (tests).** Bring proof of who you are, such as a certified birth certificate, a valid passport or a Missouri ID that has not been expired more than six months. School IDs, Social Security cards and laminated birth certificates are refused for teens taking the written test.

**At the license office (your permit or license).** Bring:

- **Lawful status and identity** with your full legal name and date of birth, such as a certified U.S. birth certificate from a vital records office (not a hospital certificate) or a valid U.S. passport.
- **Social Security number**, shown by your original signed card (not laminated), a W-2, a 1099 or a pay stub with your name and number.
- **{{STATE}} residency**, such as two current utility bills or a property tax receipt. A post office box is not a home address. Under 21, a parent or guardian may supply a residency document for you.
- **Proof of any name change**, such as a certified marriage license or court order.
- **Your test results.** The examiner's record of your passed tests is not a permit; take it to the license office.
- **A parent, stepparent, legal guardian or certified trainer**, who comes with you to sign permission for a permit.

**REAL ID.** A REAL ID license has a star and works at airport security. In {{STATE}} it is a choice. For REAL ID you need **two** residency documents; a standard license needs one.

> **Road tip:** Check that your name is spelled the same way on every document before you leave home. One mismatch can send you back.

## 1.3 Fees

The tests themselves are **free**. You pay at the license office when you buy the document.

| Item | DOR fee |
|---|---|
| Instruction permit | $10.00 |
| Intermediate license | $14.00 |
| Full license, up to 3 years | $16.50 |
| Full license, up to 6 years | $33.00 |

License offices add a processing fee to each document, and card payments carry a small convenience fee. Offices take cash, checks, money orders and major cards. Under 21, a full license runs no more than three years.

## 1.4 The Four-Part Exam

A new {{STATE}} driver passes four tests: **written**, **vision**, **road sign** and **driving**. You take the first three to get a permit, and the driving test to move up to a license.

| Part | The {{STATE}} rule |
|---|---|
| Written test | 25 multiple choice questions |
| To pass | 20 correct (80 percent, so 5 misses allowed) |
| Road sign test | 6 signs; identify at least 4 |
| Retakes | Up to two written tests a day until you pass |
| Languages | The Class F written test comes in 20 languages, plus a sign language video version |
| Where | Any Highway Patrol driver examination station; no appointment needed |

The written questions come only from the official Driver Guide, not from the commercial vehicle chapter. The test is closed book. You may take it on a touch screen, on paper or orally.

The road sign test is a separate part. You are shown six signs and must correctly identify at least four of them. Chapter 2 teaches every sign by shape and color.

Using a phone or any recording device during a test is **fraud** and can cost you your driving privilege for up to one year.

> **Test trap:** Passing the written test is not enough. You also need 4 of the 6 signs, so study Chapter 2 as its own subject.

## 1.5 The Vision Test

Every new or renewal applicant takes a vision test for sharpness and for side (peripheral) vision.

- **Sharpness:** at least **20/40** with either or both eyes. Wear your glasses or contacts if you need them.
- **Side vision:** at least **55 degrees** in each eye, or **85 degrees** in one eye.

Fall short and the examiner gives you a form for an eye doctor or physician. If you are prescribed lenses, you may have to wait for them before testing. Vision worse than **20/160** both with and without lenses, or combined side vision under **70 degrees**, rules out a license. A weaker result may bring a restriction, such as corrective lenses or daylight driving only.

## 1.6 Step One: The Instruction Permit

You may apply at **15**. Pass the vision, road sign and written tests, then buy the permit at a license office with your parent, stepparent, legal guardian or certified trainer there to sign. The permit is good for up to **12 months**, and you can renew it without retesting.

**Who may ride with you.** The person sits in the **front seat**.

| Your age | Who may supervise |
|---|---|
| Under 16 | A parent, stepparent, grandparent, legal guardian, qualified driving instructor, or a person at least 25, licensed at least three years, with written permission from your parent or guardian |
| 16 or older | Any licensed driver at least 21 |

If your parent, stepparent, grandparent or guardian is physically disabled, they may name up to two people to give you behind the wheel lessons.

**Seat belts.** You and every passenger must be buckled.

Carry the permit itself. Your test record is not legal for driving. License offices give out free PERMIT DRIVER window signs.

### The Practice Requirement

Before you can get an intermediate license, you must:

- Hold the permit for at least **six months (182 days)**, counted from the day after it was issued. The clock starts with the permit, not with your test date.
- Complete **40 hours** of driving instruction with a parent, stepparent, legal guardian or certified trainer, including at least **10 hours at night**.
- Have **no alcohol-related conviction** in the last 12 months and **no traffic conviction** in the last six months.

> **Remember it:** 40 and 10, then six months. Use the log at the back of this book for every drive.

## 1.7 Step Two: The Intermediate License

At **16** or older, you can move up. A parent, grandparent, stepparent, legal guardian or certified trainer comes with you to the license office to confirm your 40 hours. You pass the driving test, and you retake the vision, road sign and written tests only if your earlier results are more than one year old. The intermediate license costs $14 and lasts up to two years.

**Passenger limits.** These count passengers **under 19** who are **not immediate family**.

| Time on the intermediate license | Limit |
|---|---|
| First 6 months | No more than 1 |
| After 6 months | No more than 3 |

Immediate family means your parents, grandparents, brothers, sisters, stepbrothers, stepsisters, and adopted or foster children who live with you. The passenger limits may not apply while you do farm work.

**Late night limit.** You may not drive alone between **1 a.m. and 5 a.m.** The exceptions are driving to or from a school activity, a job or an emergency, or having a licensed driver **21 or older** with you.

**Seat belts.** You and every passenger, in every seat, must be buckled.

**Phones.** {{STATE}}'s hands-free law covers every driver, not only teens (see Chapter 11).

> **Test trap:** The late night limit starts at 1 a.m., not midnight or 11 p.m. A friend who is 19 or 20 does not lift it; the companion must be a licensed driver 21 or older.

### Step Three: The Full License

At **18**, you may trade up to a full license if you hold a valid intermediate license, your privilege is not suspended, revoked or denied, and you have **no alcohol-related offense or traffic conviction in the last 12 months**. You pass the vision and road sign tests again; the written and driving tests are not repeated.

## 1.8 The Driving Test

No appointment is needed, though some stations let you book the driving test online, and the Patrol recommends it where offered.

### The Car

You supply the car. It does not have to be in your name, but it must be properly registered and insured. The examiner checks it first and will not test you if it is unsafe. It needs current plates, a valid inspection sticker, proof of insurance, and working seat belts, doors, horn, foot brake and parking brake, plus a clean seat for the examiner.

Only you and the examiner ride in the car.

### What the Examiner Scores

First you find and use controls such as the turn signals and wipers without searching. Then the examiner scores:

- Smooth starts and stops.
- **Parallel parking** in a space **25 feet long and 7 feet wide**, no more than **18 inches** from the curb, near the center, within **two minutes**.
- Backing in a straight line while looking over your right shoulder.
- At least **two right and two left turns**.
- **Parking on a hill**: wheels turned the right way, parking brake set, no more than 18 inches from the curb.
- Intersections, signs, signals, following distance, speed and lane position.

### Your Grade

The examiner subtracts points for each error. Lose **more than 30 points** and you fail. Parallel parking is worth only a few points, so a poor attempt alone does not fail you. You fail at once for a crash that is your fault, hitting a pedestrian, driving dangerously, breaking a traffic law, or refusing to drive as asked.

Pass and you get a result form. It is **not** a license. Take it to a license office first.

### If You Fail

You may take **one driving test a day**. After **three failures**, you need written approval from the DOR to test again. The DOR sends a letter requiring more behind the wheel training, and you must send proof of that training before retesting.

## 1.9 Adults Getting a First License

The GDL steps apply only to first-time drivers under 18. At 18 or older, you pass the same four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving), and there is no six-month wait and no 40-hour rule. You may also buy an instruction permit first to practice; any licensed driver 21 or older may supervise you. A full license for a driver aged 18 to 20 still requires no alcohol-related offense or traffic conviction in the last 12 months.

## 1.10 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

**New residents from another state.** You must get a {{STATE}} license once you live here, even if your old one is still valid. If it is valid or expired no more than **184 days**, you take only the **vision and road sign tests**, show the usual documents, and surrender the old license. Expired longer than 184 days, you take all four parts. Out-of-state permits and IDs are invalidated when you transfer.

**Licenses from another country.** The transfer rule covers licenses from other states only. With a foreign license, expect the full four-part exam: written, vision, road sign and driving tests. Bring your passport with a valid U.S. visa and I-94, or another accepted immigration document. A license for a noncitizen is tied to the length of lawful status shown on those papers.

## 1.11 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **{{STATE}} Driver Guide**, published by the Department of Revenue, is the source of every written test question. It is free at dor.mo.gov, with fees, document lists and license offices; the Highway Patrol site lists examination stations. If this book and the Guide ever disagree, the Guide and current law win.

## Quick Review

- Permit at 15: pass the written, vision and road sign tests, then buy it at a license office.
- Written test: 25 questions, 20 to pass. Road sign test: 6 signs, 4 to pass.
- Before the intermediate license: six months on the permit and 40 hours, 10 at night.
- Intermediate license: no driving alone 1 a.m. to 5 a.m.; one non-family passenger under 19 for six months, then three.
- Full license at 18 with no alcohol offense or traffic conviction in 12 months.
- New residents with a valid license from another state take only the vision and road sign tests.
