# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

In {{STATE}}, three different groups help you get a license. The **Secretary of State (SOS)** issues every permit and license at its branch offices. A certified **driver education provider** teaches teens and gives the written state exams. An independent **driver testing business** approved by the SOS gives the driving skills test.

> **On the test**
>
> - The ages for Segment 1, the Level 1 license and the Level 2 license.
> - The 50 supervised hours, with 10 at night.
> - Level 2 limits: no driving from 10 p.m. to 5 a.m. and no more than one passenger under 21.
> - Kelsey's Law: no phone use for Level 1 and Level 2 drivers.

## 1.1 The Teen Path: Graduated Driver Licensing

{{STATE}} uses **Graduated Driver Licensing (GDL)** for everyone under 18. You earn privileges in steps, and each step has a minimum age and a minimum wait.

| Step | Earliest age | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|
| Driver education, Segment 1 | 14 years 8 months | Classroom and first lessons |
| Level 1 Learner's License | 14 years 9 months | Driving with a supervising adult |
| Level 2 Intermediate License | 16 | Driving alone, with limits |
| Level 3 Full License | 17 | No GDL limits |

GDL ends on your 18th birthday. A Level 2 or Level 3 driver then receives a regular operator's license marked "Under 21," which expires at 21. A Level 1 license simply expires at 18, and you start over as an adult.

## 1.2 Driver Education: Segment 1 and Segment 2

Home-taught and online-only courses do not count. You must attend a program certified by the state. Providers set their own prices.

**Segment 1** needs a parent or guardian's permission and includes:

- At least **24 hours** of classroom lessons, no more than 2 hours a day.
- At least **6 hours** behind the wheel with an instructor.
- At least **4 hours** of observation, riding in the training car while other students drive.
- A written state exam. You need **70 percent** to pass.

**Segment 2** comes later, once you have held your Level 1 license for at least **3 months** and logged at least **30 hours** of supervised driving, including **2 at night**. It is at least **6 hours** of classroom lessons, again with a state exam you pass at **70 percent**.

## 1.3 What to Bring for Your Level 1 License

With your Segment 1 certificate in hand, visit an SOS office with a parent or legal guardian. Book an appointment and pre-apply online first. Bring originals or certified copies, never photocopies:

- Your **Segment 1 Certificate of Completion**.
- Proof of your **Social Security number**, or a letter from the Social Security Administration saying you are not eligible for one, issued within the last 60 days.
- Proof of **U.S. citizenship or legal presence**, such as a certified birth certificate or a passport.
- Proof of **identity**.
- **Two** proofs of {{STATE}} residency, such as a utility bill or bank statement from the last 90 days. A parent's documents can work if you can show the family link.

The SOS form **SOS-428** lists every accepted document. Names and birth dates must match across your papers; bring a court order or marriage record for any name change.

**REAL ID.** To board a domestic flight with your license alone, it must be **REAL ID compliant**, shown by a star in the upper right corner. An **enhanced** license is REAL ID compliant and also lets you return by land or sea from Canada, Mexico, Bermuda and the Caribbean. A standard card without the star is fully valid for driving.

Driver education providers and driver testing businesses set their own fees. Ask for prices and refund rules before you sign up.

## 1.5 The Knowledge Test

How you take the written test depends on your age.

**Under 18.** You do not take a separate test at the SOS. Your knowledge is tested by the **Segment 1** and **Segment 2** state exams at your driver education program, each passed at **70 percent**.

**18 and older.** You take the SOS knowledge test before you can get a permit. It has two parts, and you must pass **both**:

| Part | Covers | Time allowed |
|---|---|---|
| Operator | Traffic laws and safe driving | About 45 minutes |
| Signs | Road signs | About 15 minutes |

The SOS does not publish the number of questions or the passing score for this test.

**Where to take it:**

- **At an SOS office**, free, by appointment. Arrive at least **one hour** before closing so you have time to finish. If you fail, you come back on another day.
- **Online at home**, for **$6.50** per attempt, on a computer or laptop with a keyboard, mouse and webcam (no phones or tablets). The webcam takes photos during the test, and cheating disqualifies you. You may try up to **twice a day**. If you pass one part online but not the other, you can take the full test at an SOS office for free.

A passed test stays valid for **one year**. The test comes in English and several other languages, in written and audio form, and you may use an approved interpreter.

> **Test trap:** The Signs part is scored on its own. A strong score on the Operator part cannot make up for a failed Signs part, so study Chapter 2 until every sign is automatic.

## 1.6 The Vision Test

Your vision is checked at the SOS office before you get a Level 1 license or a permit. If you need glasses or contacts to pass, your license will say you must wear them to drive.

If you do not pass, an eye care specialist must fill out a **vision statement**. Based on it, the SOS may limit your license, for example to **daylight driving only**. The office also asks a few general health questions.

## 1.7 Driving With a Level 1 License

**Who sits beside you.** A licensed parent or guardian, or a licensed adult **21 or older** whom your parent or guardian has designated in a signed letter. That adult must sit in the front passenger seat every time you drive.

**Your phone.** Under **Kelsey's Law**, a Level 1 or Level 2 driver may not use a cell phone while driving at all, not even hands-free. The only exception is calling for help in an emergency, such as reporting a crash, a crime or a medical emergency.

**Your driving log.** Record every practice drive. Before the driving skills test you need at least **50 hours**, including at least **10 at night**. The log at the back of this book works well for this.

> **Remember it:** 30 and 2 to start Segment 2. 50 and 10 to take the road test.

## 1.8 The Level 2 Intermediate License

You qualify for Level 2 when you:

- are at least **16**;
- have held Level 1 for at least **6 months**;
- have finished Segment 2 and passed the driving skills test;
- have had **no** convictions, civil infractions, suspensions or at-fault crashes in the **90 days** before you apply.

If your Level 1 license is a plastic photo card, Level 2 is granted automatically once you meet these rules.

**Level 2 limits:**

| Limit | Rule |
|---|---|
| Night driving | Not between **10 p.m. and 5 a.m.** |
| Passengers | No more than **one** passenger under 21 |
| Phone | No use at all (Kelsey's Law) |

Both the night limit and the passenger limit lift when a parent, guardian or designated licensed adult 21 or older rides with you, when you drive to, from or for work, or when you drive to or from an authorized activity such as a school event, official sports practice or a religious event. Family members do not count toward the passenger limit.

**Breaking a Level 2 limit** is a civil infraction that adds **2 points** to your record. Your parent or guardian is notified, you must stay on Level 2 for 12 more months, and you must attend a driver reexamination, which can end in a suspension.

**Level 3** comes automatically at **17** if you have held Level 2 for at least **6 months** and have gone **12 months in a row** with no moving violation, at-fault crash, suspension or GDL violation. Your parent or guardian may ask the SOS to hold you back if they feel you are not ready.

### Probation for Every New Driver

Separate from GDL, every new driver of any age is on **probation for at least three years**. The last **10 months** must be free of traffic convictions, suspensions and at-fault crashes, or probation is extended. A new driver may be called to a review after only one or two violations.

## 1.9 The Driving Skills Test

### Before You Book

Choose a driver testing business from the SOS website. Under 18, you must be at least **15**, have held Level 1 for **6 months**, finished Segment 2, logged **50 hours** (10 at night) and have a clean record for the past **90 days**. Bring your Level 1 license, Segment 2 certificate, signed log, and the car's registration and proof of {{STATE}} no-fault insurance. A parent or guardian comes along.

You may not use notes, a phone or self-parking features. You may take only one test in 24 hours.

### Three Parts

1. **Vehicle inspection.** The examiner checks lights, signals, brakes, horn, mirrors, belts, tires, wipers and doors.
2. **Basic control skills.** Three exercises on a closed course: stopping your front bumper within **2 feet** of a stop line without crossing it, backing on a curved path into a driveway-style space on the driver's side (a turnabout), and backing into a parallel parking space on the passenger side. You pass with **six errors or fewer**.
3. **On-road test.** About residential, urban, rural and expressway driving. You pass with **25 points or fewer** taken off.

Failing any part ends the test. These end it at once: refusing to wear your belt, running a red light, failing to yield, passing a stopped school bus, speeding **10 mph** or more over the limit and driving the wrong way. A crash can too.

## 1.10 Adults Getting a First License

At 18 or older, you skip driver education and GDL. The steps:

1. Pass the vision test and the SOS knowledge test (both parts).
2. Get a **temporary instruction permit (TIP)** at an SOS office for **$25**. It is valid for **180 days**.
3. Practice with a licensed adult for at least **30 days**. Driving alone on a TIP is illegal. An adult driver education course is optional but recommended.
4. Pass the driving skills test before the TIP expires.

After you pass, you can print a **temporary operator's permit** from your online SOS account, and the plastic license arrives by mail in about three weeks.

If you have never been licensed and have **two or more** moving violation convictions in the past three years, the SOS will not issue you a license.

## 1.11 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

**From another state.** Apply as soon as you live here. With a valid out-of-state license, you pass the **vision test** and present your documents; there is no written test, TIP or skills test. A license expired for **four years or more** means a TIP and a skills test. Under 18, GDL is waived if you have held a valid license from another state for more than a year.

**From another country.** With a license from **Canada, Germany or South Korea**, you may switch to a {{STATE}} license without the written test, TIP or skills test. With a license from any other country, you follow the adult path: knowledge test, TIP, practice and the driving skills test. Every applicant must prove legal presence.

## 1.12 Where to Find the Official Rules

The free SOS manual, **What Every Driver Must Know**, is the study guide for the written tests, and the **Driving Skills Test Study Guide** explains the road test. Both are on Michigan.gov/SOS with office appointments and forms. If this book and the official rules ever disagree, the official rules win.

## Quick Review

- Segment 1 at 14 years 8 months; Level 1 at 14 years 9 months, with a parent, the Segment 1 certificate and your documents.
- Segment 1 and Segment 2 state exams: 70 percent to pass.
- Adults take the SOS knowledge test with an Operator part and a Signs part, and must pass both.
- Level 1: drive only with a licensed parent, guardian or designated adult 21 or older; log 50 hours, 10 at night.
- Level 2 at 16 after 6 months on Level 1: no driving 10 p.m. to 5 a.m., no more than one passenger under 21.
- Kelsey's Law: no phone use at Level 1 or Level 2, except emergencies.
- Level 3 at 17 after 6 months on Level 2 and 12 clean months.
