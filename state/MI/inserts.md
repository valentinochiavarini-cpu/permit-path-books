# Michigan inserts

## school_zone

A {{STATE}} school zone limit is the number on the posted School Speed Limit sign. It may be up to **20 mph** below the normal limit, but never lower than **25 mph**.

The lower limit applies during the times shown on the sign, usually starting up to **30 minutes** before school begins and ending up to 30 minutes after it lets out. Outside those times, the normal limit returns. Obey every school crossing guard.

## work_zone

In a {{STATE}} work zone, the limit is **45 mph** where signs post it. Some zones use 45 mph only while workers are present.

**Fines double** for every moving violation in a work zone, and speeding adds extra points:

| Over the limit | Points |
|---|---|
| 10 mph or less | 3 |
| 11 to 15 mph | 4 |
| More than 15 mph | 5 |

Hurting or killing someone in a work zone can mean up to **15 years** in prison. Obey construction workers who direct traffic, even over a signal.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, after a full stop you may turn **right on a steady red light** unless a sign forbids it. Yield to pedestrians, bicycles and cross traffic first.

You may also turn **left on red** onto a **one-way street** after a full stop, both from another one-way street and from a two-way street. Turning from a two-way street, also yield to oncoming drivers turning right.

A **steady red arrow** means stop, and you may not turn until the signal changes.

## move_over

When you approach a stopped emergency vehicle with red, blue, white or amber lights flashing, {{STATE}} law requires two things:

- Slow to at least **10 mph below** the posted limit.
- If there are two or more lanes your way, move over a lane when it is safe.

If you cannot move over, slow down and pass with care. Tow trucks and road service vehicles count. Also pass stopped garbage, utility and road maintenance trucks with lights flashing with caution, ready to yield. Injuring an emergency worker is a felony.

## emergency_vehicles

In {{STATE}}, when an emergency vehicle comes with flashing lights and a siren, pull as close as you can to the **right** edge or curb, clear of any intersection, and stop. Keep your foot on the brake so your brake lights show you have stopped.

Stay stopped until it passes, and watch for a second one. Afterward, stay at least **500 feet** behind any moving emergency vehicle using its lights and siren, and never pass it unless its driver or an officer waves you on.

## school_bus

In {{STATE}}, when a school bus has its **red** lights flashing, stop at least **20 feet** away and stay stopped until the red lights go off or the bus moves on.

Traffic in **both directions** stops, unless the road is divided by a barrier such as a concrete or grass median or an island. Then only traffic on the bus's side stops.

**Yellow** lights flashing on top mean prepare to stop. Passing a stopped bus costs **double** the usual fine and **3 points**.

## u_turn

{{STATE}} drivers meet a special turn called the **Michigan Left**. At these divided roads, a direct left turn is banned. Drive straight through the intersection (or turn right), then make a U-turn at the marked median crossover and come back.

Treat an unmarked crossover as one lane. Never use a freeway median crossover; signs say it is for authorized vehicles only. If you miss an exit, go on to the next one.

## speed_limits

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} manual calls for at least **3 to 4 seconds** behind the vehicle ahead. If you reach the fixed point before you count three, you are too close.

At higher speeds or in poor conditions, stretch it to **6 seconds** or more. When rain first falls, leave at least twice your normal gap. Keep 3 to 4 seconds behind motorcycles, mopeds and bicycles too. Look **20 to 30 seconds** down the road to spot problems early.

## hov

In {{STATE}}, an HOV lane open at peak hours requires **two or more** people in the vehicle. Outside the posted hours, anyone may use it. Motorcycles may use it at any time.

On a freeway with two lanes your way, stay in the right lane except to pass. With three or more lanes, you may use any lane, but stay right if you drive near the minimum speed.

## parking_rules

{{STATE}} bans parking:

| Place | Distance |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant | Within 15 feet |
| Intersection with no crosswalk | Within 15 feet |
| Marked crosswalk | Within 20 feet |
| Fire station driveway, same side | Within 20 feet |
| Stop sign, traffic light or flashing beacon | Within 30 feet |
| Nearest rail of a railroad crossing | Within 50 feet |
| A fire or a crash | Within 500 feet |

Park with the flow of traffic, no more than **12 inches** from the curb. Never park on a bridge or in a bike lane.

## bicycle_passing

In {{STATE}}, leave at least **3 feet** when you pass a bicycle. If that is not possible, wait or pass at a safe distance and a safe speed.

You may pass a bicycle in a **no-passing zone** when it is safe. Do not use a bike lane as a right turn lane, and check for riders before you turn right. Some cities require **5 feet**.

## crash_reporting

After a {{STATE}} crash, stop; leaving the scene is a crime. Get help for anyone hurt.

If no one is seriously hurt and your car can be driven, move it off the main road to the shoulder or a nearby lot. Call the police if anyone is injured or damage is more than **$1,000**.

Hit a deer? Report it to local police, the sheriff, the State Police or the Department of Natural Resources. Do not swerve for a deer: brake firmly and stay in your lane.

## bac_limits

In {{STATE}}, driving with these BAC levels is a crime:

| Driver | Limit |
|---|---|
| 21 or older | 0.08 |
| Under 21 | 0.02 |

A BAC of **0.17** or more is a **high BAC**, with tougher penalties. You can be arrested at any BAC if you show signs of impairment. Driving high on marijuana carries the same penalties as alcohol, and no one in the car may smoke it.

## implied_consent

In {{STATE}}, refusing a chemical test after an arrest brings a license suspension even if you are never convicted:

| Refusal | Suspension |
|---|---|
| First | 1 year |
| Second within 7 years | 2 years |

Refusal also adds **6 points**. Under 21, refusing a roadside preliminary breath test adds **2 points**.

## dui_penalties

A first {{STATE}} drunk driving conviction (**OWI**) brings:

| Penalty | BAC 0.08 to 0.16 | BAC 0.17 or more |
|---|---|---|
| Jail | Up to 93 days | Up to 180 days |
| Fine | $100 to $500 | $200 to $700 |
| License | 180-day suspension (none of it drivable for 30 days) | 1-year suspension |

Up to **360 hours** of community service and **6 points** may also apply. A high BAC driver may drive after 45 days only with an **ignition interlock**. Open alcohol is banned in the passenger area; carry it in the trunk.

## underage_alcohol

A {{STATE}} driver under 21 with a BAC of **0.02** up to 0.08 faces a fine up to **$250**, up to **360 hours** of community service, a **30-day** license suspension or restriction and **4 points**.

Buying, drinking or having alcohol under 21: up to **$100** the first time, more for repeat offenses. A fake ID to buy alcohol: up to **93 days** in jail. Any drug conviction, even off the road, suspends your license for **6 months**.

## phone_laws

In {{STATE}}, no driver may hold or use a phone or other mobile device while driving: no calls, texts, videos or social media in your hand.

Drivers with a Level 3 or regular license may use one tap or swipe, voice control, a car's built-in screen or a mounted device. Level 1 and Level 2 drivers may not use a phone at all (Kelsey's Law).

| Offense | Fine | Points |
|---|---|---|
| First | $100 | 0 |
| Second or later | $250 | 1, then 2 |

Fines double if you cause a crash.

## seat_belts

In {{STATE}}, the driver and every front seat passenger must wear a seat belt. Passengers under **16** must be buckled in every seat.

Enforcement is **primary**: an officer may stop you only for a missing belt. A belt ticket adds no points. If a medical condition keeps you from wearing a belt, carry a doctor's statement.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} car seat rules:

| Child | Required |
|---|---|
| Under 2 | Rear-facing seat, unless outgrown |
| Under 5 | Car seat with harness, unless outgrown |
| Under 8 and under 4 feet 9 inches | Booster seat |
| 8 to under 16 | Seat belt |

A child in a car seat rides in the back if the car has one. A rear-facing seat goes in front only with the airbag off. Never take a child out of a car seat in a moving car.

## vehicle_rules

{{STATE}} car tires need at least **2/32 inch** of tread. Tint or film may cover only the top **4 inches** of the windshield and none of the front side windows.

Every car must carry no-fault insurance. Liability limits start at **$250,000** per person and **$500,000** per crash for injury, unless you choose lower limits of at least $50,000 and $100,000, plus **$10,000** for property damage. Under 18, no one may ride in an open pickup bed above **15 mph**.

## police_stop

The {{STATE}} State Police advise: pull to the **right** side of the road as soon as it is safe. Stay in the car, lower your window and keep both hands on the steering wheel.

When the officer asks, show your **license**, **registration** and **proof of insurance**. You may ask why you were stopped. When you are told you may go, buckle up, signal and merge back into traffic safely.

## headlight_use

In {{STATE}}, your headlights must be on:

- from **one half hour after sunset** to **one half hour before sunrise**;
- whenever you cannot clearly see people and vehicles **500 feet** ahead;
- in fog, rain or snow during the day (use low beams).

Driving with only parking lights on when headlights are required is illegal.

## driver_record

{{STATE}} points stay on your record for **two years** from conviction. You are called in for a driver reexamination if you reach **12 points** in two years, have **three crashes** in two years, or break a license restriction. That review can bring restrictions or a suspension.

| Violation | Points |
|---|---|
| OWI, reckless driving, leaving a crash | 6 |
| Under 21 with alcohol, more than 15 mph over | 4 |
| Careless driving, running a stop sign or red light | 3 |
| Most other moving violations | 2 |

## road_test

{{STATE}} driving skills tests are given by approved driver testing businesses. The test has three parts, and failing any one ends it:

1. **Vehicle inspection.**
2. **Basic control skills:** a forward stop within **2 feet** of a line, a backing turnabout and parallel parking. Pass with **six errors or fewer**.
3. **On-road test:** pass with **25 points or fewer**.

A crash, a missing belt, running a red light, passing a stopped school bus or speeding **10 mph** or more over fails you at once. One test per 24 hours.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers under 18 must log supervised practice with a licensed parent, guardian or designated adult 21 or older:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Before Segment 2 | 30, at least 2 at night |
| Before the driving skills test | 50, at least 10 at night |

You must also hold your Level 1 license for **6 months** before the skills test. Adults 18 and older practice at least **30 days** on a TIP.

## signal_distance

{{STATE}} law requires a signal before you turn or stop but sets no distance in feet. Signal well before you turn or change lanes so drivers behind you have time to react.

When multiple turn lanes are marked, finish your turn in the lane that matches the one you turned from. Hand signals are used mostly by bicyclists, moped riders and motorcyclists, so learn to read them.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, it is illegal to use or even flash high beams within **500 feet** of an oncoming vehicle. Also dim them for pedestrians and bicyclists.

The {{STATE}} manual sets no distance for following, but says never use high beams behind another vehicle. If an oncoming driver keeps high beams on, look toward the right edge of the road.
