# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

In {{STATE}}, licensing belongs to the Department of Driver Services, called **DDS**, which works under Title 40 of the Official Code of {{STATE}}. Its offices are Customer Service Centers. Every step below has a document, a fee or a waiting period attached, and missing one is the usual reason people get sent home. Fees and forms change, so check dds.georgia.gov before each visit.

> **On the test**
>
> - The two parts of the knowledge exam: road signs and road rules.
> - Who may sit beside a permit driver, and how old they must be.
> - Class D limits: the midnight to 5 a.m. ban and the passenger rules.
> - Joshua's Law, ADAP and the 40 hours of practice.
> - Points and suspensions for drivers under 18 and under 21.

## 1.1 The Three Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses **graduated licensing**. You earn privileges in steps, because new drivers crash most often in their first months alone.

| Stage | Earliest age | What it allows |
|---|---|---|
| Class CP instructional permit | 15 | Driving with a licensed adult 21 or older beside you |
| Class D provisional license | 16 | Driving alone, with a night ban and passenger limits |
| Class C license | 18 | Full privileges |

You may apply for the permit on your 15th birthday or any day after. You may apply for a Class D once you are 16 **and** have held the permit for **one year and one day**. At 18, a clean Class D becomes a full Class C.

Cars, pickups and vans fall under Class C (Class D is its provisional form). Motorcycles need Class M; larger vehicles need a commercial license.

## 1.2 Paperwork and What to Bring

You can fill out your application online before you go, but the tests happen in person at a Customer Service Center. Bring originals; photocopies are refused.

- **Proof of identity and lawful presence**, such as a certified birth certificate or a valid U.S. passport.
- **Your full Social Security number.** You need to know it; DDS checks it electronically.
- **Two proofs of {{STATE}} residence**, such as a utility or phone bill, dated within the last six months.
- **Proof of school status** if you are under 18: usually a Certificate of School Enrollment (**form DS-1**) from your school, dated within 30 days. A diploma, GED or home school declaration also works.
- **A responsible adult** if you are under 18: a parent, legal guardian or other qualifying adult aged 18 or older signs your application. That adult can ask DDS to cancel your permit or license at any time before you turn 18.
- **Glasses or contacts**, if you wear them.

**REAL ID.** A first {{STATE}} permit or license is issued to the federal REAL ID standard and shows a **star** in the upper right corner. You now need a REAL ID card (or another accepted ID, such as a passport) to board a domestic flight or enter many federal buildings. The documents above are the REAL ID documents. If your name differs from your birth certificate, also bring the certified marriage certificate, divorce decree or court order that links the names.

> **Road tip:** Check that your name is spelled the same way on every document. A mismatch is the most common reason a teen is turned away at the counter.

## 1.3 Fees

| Item | DDS fee |
|---|---|
| Class CP permit (valid 2 years) | $10 |
| Class D provisional license (5 years) | $10 |
| Class C license (8 years) | $32 |
| Replacement permit or license | $10 |

The $10 permit fee is paid **before each attempt** at the knowledge exam and is not refunded if you fail. Customer Service Centers take cash, credit and debit cards, and mobile pay, but not checks or money orders. Some online transactions earn a $5 discount.

## 1.4 The Knowledge Exam

You take the knowledge exam on a walk-in basis at any Customer Service Center; no appointment is needed. Start at least **30 minutes** before closing. DDS offers a free practice test online, but the real exam is given only in person.

The exam has **two separate parts**, and you must pass both.

| Part | Questions | To pass |
|---|---|---|
| Road Signs | 20 | 15 correct |
| Road Rules | 20 | 15 correct |

The **road signs** part asks you to identify signs, signals and markers, and it is given in **English only**. The **road rules** part covers your duties as a driver, traffic laws and safe driving; it is offered in some other languages. Every driver must still read the simple English used on road signs.

Leave pens, paper and your phone outside the testing area, and speak only to the examiner. Breaking these rules counts as a failed exam.

**If you fail a part,** you retake only that part. After a **first** failure, you wait until the **next day**. After a **second or later** failure, you wait **7 days**. Remember to pay the $10 again.

> **Test trap:** Each part is graded on its own. A perfect 20 on road signs cannot make up for 14 on road rules. Study both.

## 1.5 The Vision Test

Your eyes are checked with a machine at the Customer Service Center, before the knowledge exam. The minimum for a car license is **20/60** in at least one eye, with or without glasses or contacts, and a side vision field of at least **140 degrees**.

If you need lenses to pass, your license will carry a corrective lenses restriction. If you cannot pass, DDS gives you a **Vision Report, form DS-274**, for an eye doctor to complete and send back.

## 1.6 Driving on a Class CP Permit

**Who rides with you.** The person in the front passenger seat beside you must be at least **21**, licensed to drive a Class C vehicle, and fit and able to take control of the car.

**How long it lasts.** The permit is valid for up to **two years**. Keep it with you every time you drive.

### Joshua's Law: Driver Education

To get a Class D at **16 or 17**, you must finish a DDS-approved driver education course. This rule is known as **Joshua's Law**. A course has two pieces:

1. **Classroom:** **30 hours** of instruction at a certified driver training school, or the same hours in a certified online course.
2. **Behind the wheel:** either **6 hours** with a certified instructor, or the **parent-taught** route using the DDS **Parent/Teen Driving Guide**.

### The Practice Requirement

You also need at least **40 hours** of supervised driving, with at least **6 hours at night**. You do not hand in a log, but a parent or guardian signs an affidavit at the road test saying you completed the hours. If you took the 6 hours with an instructor, the school's completion document is needed as well.

### ADAP

Every applicant under 18 must also complete the **Alcohol and Drug Awareness Program (ADAP)**. Most {{STATE}} high schools teach it, and it is also available online.

> **Remember it:** 15 for the permit. One year and one day on the permit. 30 plus 6 for Joshua's Law. 40 hours, 6 at night. ADAP done.

## 1.7 The Class D Provisional License

At the road test appointment you bring your permit, school enrollment proof, ADAP certificate, driver education proof and your responsible adult. Pass, and you leave with a temporary license; the card arrives by mail.

**Night ban.** You may not drive between **midnight and 5 a.m.**, with **no exceptions**: not for work, not for school, not with a parent riding along.

**Passenger limits.** The limits apply to passengers who are not your **immediate family** (parents, stepparents, grandparents, brothers, sisters, stepsiblings, your children and anyone who lives in your home).

| Time since your Class D was issued | Non-family passengers allowed |
|---|---|
| First 6 months | None |
| Second 6 months | 1 under age 21 |
| After 12 months | Up to 3 under age 21 |

**Phones.** {{STATE}}'s hands-free law covers every driver, teens included (see Chapter 11).

### Losing Your License as a Young Driver

{{STATE}} is strict with young drivers. A driver **under 21** loses the license for **6 months** for a first conviction (12 months for a second) of reckless or aggressive driving, racing, fleeing police, hit and run, or any **4-point** offense, such as passing a stopped school bus or going **24 mph or more** over the limit. A driver **under 18** is also suspended for collecting **4 points** in any 12 months. Chapter 12 explains the point system.

### Moving Up to a Class C

At **18**, you may trade your Class D for a Class C, in person or online, if you have had **no major traffic conviction** in the past 12 months. U.S. citizens need no further tests. The Class C costs $32 and lasts eight years.

> **Test trap:** The Class D night ban starts at **midnight**, not 11 p.m., and nothing lifts it. In the first six months, a friend riding with you is a violation even if your parent is also in the car.

## 1.8 The Road Test

### Scheduling and the Car

Road tests are **by appointment only**; book through DDS Online Services. You supply the car. It must have four wheels, a valid registration and plate (or a temporary dealer tag with a recent bill of sale), and **liability insurance**, shown with your insurance card. The examiner checks that it is safe. A rental car is allowed only if the rental contract permits testing and lists you as a driver.

DDS gives the test in one of two ways. In a **traditional** test, the examiner rides in the passenger seat. In a **virtual** test, the examiner watches remotely through a camera and a hands-free phone, and a licensed adult **21 or older** rides with you.

### What the Examiner Scores

- **Parallel parking** between two upright standards, with your car no more than **18 inches** from the curb.
- **Straight line backing** for about **50 feet**, slowly and smoothly, turning your head to look behind you.
- A **three-point turn** in a narrow space.
- Stopping correctly for signs and signals, yielding to pedestrians, signaling before turns, lane position, safe following and passing, and keeping both hands on the wheel.

Driver assistance features may be on, but you must show you can drive without them. You need a score of at least **75 percent**.

### If You Fail

The same waits apply as for the knowledge exam: try again the **next day** after a first failure, and wait **7 days** after any later one.

## 1.9 Adults Getting a First License

At **18 or older**, you skip Joshua's Law, ADAP, the school form and the one year and one day wait. You have two choices:

- Apply straight for a **Class C**: pass the vision test, the knowledge exam and the road test, and sign an affidavit that you have **40 hours** of supervised driving, **6** of them at night.
- Get a **Class CP** permit first and practice with a licensed driver 21 or older. When you are ready, pass the road test and sign the same affidavit.

## 1.10 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

You must apply for a {{STATE}} license within **30 days** of becoming a resident. A valid license from another U.S. state or the District of Columbia (or one expired less than two years) transfers with a **vision test** only; you surrender the old license and bring the usual documents. Under 18, you must also meet {{STATE}}'s school, driver education and ADAP requirements.

Visitors 16 or older may drive on a valid license from their home state or country. Once you live here, a license from another country does not transfer: you take the vision test, the knowledge exam and the road test.

- **Reciprocity.** {{STATE}} has agreements with some countries, including **South Korea** and **Taiwan**. Holders of an equivalent car license from those countries trade it in with a **vision test** only. South Korean drivers also bring a certification letter from their consulate. DDS keeps the current list.
- Most non-citizens may keep their foreign license after they receive a {{STATE}} one.

## 1.11 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **{{STATE}} Drivers' Manual** is free at dds.georgia.gov and in the DDS 2 GO app, along with forms, fees, office locations and road test booking. The knowledge exam covers its topics. If this book and the manual or current law ever disagree, the manual and the law win. Beware of private websites that look official; the real one ends in **.gov**.

## Quick Review

- Permit at 15 with a responsible adult's signature, school proof and a $10 fee per attempt.
- The knowledge exam has two parts, 20 road sign and 20 road rule questions; you need 15 right in each.
- Your supervisor must be 21 or older, licensed and in the seat beside you.
- Class D at 16 after one year and one day on the permit, Joshua's Law, ADAP and 40 hours (6 at night).
- Class D: no driving from midnight to 5 a.m., and no non-family passengers for the first six months.
- Under 21, a single 4-point offense suspends your license.
