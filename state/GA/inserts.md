# Georgia inserts

## school_zone

In {{STATE}}, a five-sided school sign stands **150 to 700 feet** before the school grounds or crossing. The school speed limit is the number posted on the School Speed Limit sign. It applies while the sign's yellow lights flash, or during the hours shown.

Many {{STATE}} school zones also have **speed cameras**. They mail a civil ticket to the car's owner.

## work_zone

In {{STATE}}, speeding in a marked work zone is a serious misdemeanor: a fine of **$100 to $2,000**, up to **12 months** in jail, or both. It applies when workers are present, or when barriers, work vehicles or pavement drop-offs create a hazard.

If the work zone limit is at least **10 mph** lower, a Reduced Speed Ahead sign comes at least **600 feet** before it. Yield to work vehicles flashing **amber** lights.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, you may turn **right on a steady red light** after a complete stop, unless a sign forbids it. Yield to pedestrians and cross traffic first.

**Left on red** is allowed only from the **left lane of a one-way street onto another one-way street**, after a full stop, unless a sign says no.

A **steady red arrow** means no turn in that direction, even after a stop. Wait until the turn is allowed.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Move Over law covers stopped **emergency vehicles** with flashing yellow, amber, white, red or blue lights. It also covers stopped **tow trucks, road maintenance and utility vehicles** using cones or flashing yellow, amber or white lights.

Move into a lane **not next to** them. If you cannot do that safely, slow to **below the speed limit** and be ready to stop.

Fines: up to **$500** for emergency vehicles, up to **$250** for the others.

## emergency_vehicles

In {{STATE}}, this applies to emergency vehicles and police cars using a siren and flashing lights. Yield, pull as close as you can to the **right-hand edge** or curb, clear of any intersection, and stop until they pass. The law gives no separate rule for one-way streets.

After they pass, stay at least **200 feet** behind a fire truck or other emergency vehicle answering a call. Never park within **500 feet** of a fire truck stopped at a fire.

## school_bus

In {{STATE}}, when a school bus shows **flashing red lights** and its stop arm, traffic from **both directions** must stop before reaching it. This includes multi-lane roads and roads with a center turn lane.

The main exception: on a highway split by a **grass median, unpaved area or barrier**, traffic on the other side need not stop. The law sets no exact distance; stop well back.

Stay stopped until the bus moves or turns off its lights. Penalty: a fine of at least **$1,000** and **6 points**.

## u_turn

In {{STATE}}, never make a U-turn on a curve or near the top of a hill, where drivers coming from either direction cannot see you. Never make one where a sign forbids it. On a divided highway, cross the median only at an authorized opening or crossover.

## speed_limits

{{STATE}} limits when no sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Urban or residential district | 30 mph |
| Unpaved county road | 35 mph |
| Most other roads | 55 mph |
| Divided state highways, where posted | 65 mph |
| Interstates, where posted | 70 mph |

**Super Speeder:** at **85 mph or more** anywhere, or **75 mph or more** on a two-lane road, you owe a **$200** state fee on top of the fine. Fail to pay within 90 days and your license is suspended.

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} Drivers' Manual teaches a **2-second count**: "one thousand one, one thousand two." If you reach the fixed point before you finish, you are too close.

Leave more space at night, in bad weather, in construction zones and in heavy traffic. Behind a motorcycle, keep **3 to 4 seconds**. Before you pull in front of a truck, leave about **one car length for every 10 mph** of speed.

## hov

{{STATE}} HOV lanes are marked with a diamond and posted hours. Buses, motorcycles and cars with alternative fuel plates may also use them. Fines run up to **$75**, then $100, then $150; a fourth offense adds **1 point**.

**Express Lanes** charge a toll and need a registered **Peach Pass**. On the I-85 Express Lanes, carpools of **three or more** ride free. Never enter the reversible I-75 Express Lanes when the gates are closed or closing.

## parking_rules

{{STATE}} bans stopping, standing or parking on a sidewalk, crosswalk, bridge or railroad track, within an intersection, beside a parked car, or on a controlled-access highway.

You may stop only to pick up or drop off a passenger:

| Within | Feet |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant | 15 |
| Crosswalk at an intersection | 20 |
| Fire station driveway, same side | 20 |
| Across from a fire station driveway | 75 |
| Stop sign, yield sign or signal | 30 |
| Railroad crossing | 50 |

If your car breaks down, leave it visible from **200 feet** each way.

## bicycle_passing

In {{STATE}}, when you pass a bicycle, first try to move into a lane **not next to** the rider. If you cannot do that safely, slow to **10 mph below the limit or 25 mph, whichever is higher**, and keep at least **3 feet** between your car and the bicycle the whole time.

Breaking this law costs up to **$250**. Never drive or park in a bike lane except to cross it for a turn, and yield to riders in it.

## crash_reporting

In {{STATE}}, you must notify police **immediately**, by the fastest means, if a crash injures or kills anyone or causes damage of **$500 or more**. Inside a city, call the city police; outside one, call the sheriff or State Patrol.

On a multi-lane road, if no one seems seriously hurt and the cars can be driven, move them out of traffic. Doing so does not make you at fault.

## bac_limits

{{STATE}} sets three limits:

| Driver | Under the influence at a BAC of |
|---|---|
| Age 21 or older | 0.08 |
| Under 21 | 0.02 |
| Commercial vehicle driver | 0.04 |

You can also be convicted below these numbers if alcohol or drugs make you unsafe. A prescription is no defense if the medicine impairs your driving.

## implied_consent

By driving in {{STATE}}, you agree to state tests of your blood, breath, urine or other bodily substances when police suspect DUI.

Refuse, and your license is suspended for at least **one year**. Refusing a blood or urine test can be used as evidence at trial.

After you take the state's test, you may get your **own test** from qualified people you choose, at your own cost.

## dui_penalties

A first DUI conviction in {{STATE}}, age 21 or older:

| Penalty | Amount |
|---|---|
| Fine | $300 to $1,000 |
| Jail | 10 days to 12 months (at 0.08 or more, at least 24 hours served) |
| Community service | At least 40 hours |
| License | 12 months suspended; early reinstatement possible after 120 days |

You must also finish a DUI risk reduction program.

**Open container:** no one may drink or hold an open alcoholic drink in the passenger area. Fine: up to **$200**, plus **2 points** for a driver. Passengers in a hired vehicle are exempt.

## underage_alcohol

Under 21, a {{STATE}} driver with a BAC of **0.02** or more is guilty of DUI. A first conviction suspends the license for **6 months**, or **12 months** if the BAC was 0.08 or more. No limited permit is allowed.

Off the road, buying, trying to buy or knowingly holding alcohol under 21 can bring up to **$300** and **6 months** in jail. Lying about your age or using a **false ID** to get alcohol is also a crime, and so is giving alcohol to anyone under 21.

## phone_laws

{{STATE}}'s **hands-free law** covers every driver. You may not hold or support a phone with any part of your body. You may not write, send or read texts, email or web content, or watch or record video.

Allowed: calls through an earpiece, a watch or a mounted phone; voice-to-text; GPS navigation; and reporting an emergency. The rules do not apply while lawfully parked. A red light is not parked.

| Conviction | Fine | Points |
|---|---|---|
| First | Up to $50 | 1 |
| Second | Up to $100 | 2 |
| Third or more | Up to $150 | 3 |

## seat_belts

In {{STATE}}, everyone in the **front seat** must wear a seat belt. Passengers aged **8 to 17** must be belted in **every** seat, and the driver gets the ticket if they are not.

The fine is up to **$15** for an unbelted adult and up to **$25** for the driver of an unbelted minor. Belt tickets add no points. Police may stop you for a belt violation alone.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} requires every child **under 8** to ride in a car seat or booster that fits the child's height and weight, in a **rear seat**. The front seat is allowed only when no rear seat exists or every rear spot is taken by other children.

A child under 8 who is **taller than 4 feet 9 inches** may use a seat belt instead.

Fines: up to **$50** and **1 point** for a first offense; up to **$100** and **2 points** after that.

## vehicle_rules

{{STATE}} requires liability insurance of at least **$25,000** per person and **$50,000** per crash for injuries, plus **$25,000** for property damage. Driving uninsured can cost you your license.

Gasoline cars and light trucks registered in **13 metro Atlanta counties** need an **emissions test every year**; the three newest model years are exempt.

Report a new address to DDS within **60 days**. Never coast downhill in neutral, and never let anyone ride in a trailer being towed.

## police_stop

In {{STATE}}, when a marked police car signals you to stop, pull to the **right-hand edge** without blocking traffic. If that spot is unsafe, turn on your flashers or signal and keep to the speed limit until you reach a safe place.

Turn off the engine and the music, and turn on the inside light at night. Tell the officer at first contact if a **weapon** is in the car.

Signing a ticket is **not** an admission of guilt, but refusing to sign can lead to arrest.

## headlight_use

In {{STATE}}, your headlights must be on:

- from a **half hour after sunset** to a **half hour before sunrise**;
- any time it is **raining**;
- whenever you cannot clearly see people and vehicles **500 feet** ahead.

In fog, the {{STATE}} manual says to use low beams, not parking lights or hazard flashers.

## driver_record

{{STATE}} suspends your license at **15 points in 24 months**. Young drivers are suspended sooner (Chapter 1).

| Offense | Points |
|---|---|
| Speeding 15 to 18 mph over | 2 |
| Speeding 19 to 23 mph over | 3 |
| Speeding 24 to 33 mph over, reckless driving | 4 |
| Speeding 34 mph or more over, passing a stopped school bus, aggressive driving | 6 |

A certified **6-hour** defensive driving course can remove up to **7 points**, once every **5 years**.

## road_test

The {{STATE}} road test includes **parallel parking** between two standards, ending no more than **18 inches** from the curb. You also back in a straight line for about **50 feet** at no more than **10 mph**, and make a **three-point turn**. You need **75 percent** to pass.

In a **virtual** test, the examiner watches by camera and a licensed adult 21 or older rides with you. After a first failure, retest the next day; after later failures, wait **7 days**.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} drivers under 18 must complete supervised practice before the Class D road test:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total | At least 40 |
| At night | At least 6 |

Night hours count toward the 40. A parent or guardian signs an affidavit at the road test. Your supervisor sits beside you and must be a licensed Class C driver at least **21**. You must also hold the permit for **one year and one day**.

## signal_distance

{{STATE}} law sets no fixed distance. Signal **continuously**, long enough to warn drivers behind you, before every turn and lane change, and signal before you stop or slow suddenly.

Never flash your turn signal to tell a driver behind you it is safe to pass; that is illegal. Enter a shared center turn lane no more than **300 feet** before your left turn.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, switch to low beams when an oncoming vehicle is within **500 feet**. When you follow another vehicle, switch to low beams within **200 feet** of it.
