## Core text vs Georgia law (not edited: outside scope)

1. **Ch 11.3, Implied Consent:** "The refusal can also be used against you in court." In Georgia only a blood or urine refusal can; a breath test refusal cannot (Elliott v. State). The GA box now says this correctly, but the core sentence stated as universal is not true in Georgia. Suggest "In many states, the refusal can also be used against you in court."
2. **Ch 10.1 and its Quick Review:** "Turn on your headlights from sunset to sunrise." Georgia's rule is a half hour after sunset to a half hour before sunrise (40-8-20); the box gives it, but GA-128 keys "a half hour after sunset" and lists "From sunset exactly" as a wrong answer, so the core line works against the keyed answer. Suggest "from about sunset to sunrise (the box gives your state's exact times)".
3. **Ch 6.5, step 2:** the core count runs to "one thousand three" while the Georgia box teaches a 2-second count (GA-102 keys 2 seconds). Not a legal conflict, but a reader may take 3 seconds from the steps. Suggest ending the sample count at the number the box gives, or saying "count the seconds".
4. **Ch 4.7:** "Stay stopped until the red lights stop flashing and the stop arm is folded." Georgia also lets you go when the bus resumes motion (40-6-163(a)); not a conflict in practice.
