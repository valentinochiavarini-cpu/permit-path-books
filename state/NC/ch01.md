# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

In {{STATE}}, a teen earns a license in three levels, each with its own clock, limits and paperwork. The Division of Motor Vehicles (NCDMV), part of the {{STATE}} Department of Transportation, runs licensing under Chapter 20 of the {{STATE}} General Statutes. Fees and forms change, so check the NCDMV website before every visit.

> **On the test**
>
> - The two written parts: rules of the road and a separate road sign test.
> - Who may supervise a Level 1 driver, and where they sit.
> - The 9 p.m. to 5 a.m. limit and the passenger limit at Level 2.
> - The 60 hour driving log and the nine month permit wait.
> - The phone ban for every driver under 18.

## 1.1 Three Levels for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses **graduated licensing**: you gain privileges in steps, because new drivers crash most in their first months behind the wheel.

| Level | Earliest age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| 1: Limited Learner Permit | 15 | Drive only with a supervising driver |
| 2: Limited Provisional License | 16 | Drive alone in the daytime, with passenger limits |
| 3: Full Provisional License | 16 and a half | Drive alone at any hour; phone ban until 18 |

You may apply for Level 1 at **15**, after driver education. You must hold it **nine months** and be at least 16 to move up to Level 2. After **six months** at Level 2 you may move up to Level 3, so 16 and a half is the earliest. At 18 you may trade a Level 2 or Level 3 license for a regular license.

## 1.2 Driver Education Comes First

Before you can even test for a Level 1 permit, you must pass an approved **driver education course**: at least **30 hours** in the classroom and **6 hours** behind the wheel. You may enroll at 14 and a half, through your high school or a licensed commercial driving school. The course gives you a **Driver Education Certificate**, which you bring to the NCDMV office.

{{STATE}} used to require a Driving Eligibility Certificate from your school as well. State law dropped that requirement, so you no longer need it. Older guides may still list it.

## 1.3 Paperwork and What to Bring

A **parent or guardian must come with you** when you test for a Level 1 permit or a Level 2 license. If someone else brings you, they need written consent from your parent or guardian.

For Level 1, bring:

- **One document proving your identity and date of birth**, such as a certified birth certificate or a valid U.S. passport. Photocopies are refused unless certified by the issuing agency.
- **Your Social Security card** or one document showing your full Social Security number, such as a W-2.
- **One document proving your {{STATE}} address.**
- **Your Driver Education Certificate.**

If you were not born in the United States, bring one document proving legal presence. Every name must match; if it changed, bring the marriage certificate or court order.

For Level 2 you add **printed proof of liability insurance** (a picture on a phone is not accepted), your driving log and the test car's registration card.

**REAL ID.** A REAL ID card carries a **star**. You need one, or another federal ID such as a passport, to board a domestic flight or enter some federal buildings. In {{STATE}} it is **optional** and costs nothing extra, but you must apply in person and bring **two** proofs of address instead of one. A card without the star says "Not for Federal Identification" and is still valid for driving.

> **Road tip:** Put every original in one folder the night before, and check that your name is spelled the same on each. A mismatched name is a common reason people are sent home.

## 1.4 Fees

| Item | NCDMV fee |
|---|---|
| Level 1 Limited Learner Permit | $25.50 |
| Level 2 Limited Provisional License | $25.50 |
| Level 3 or regular Class C license | $6.50 per year of the license |
| Duplicate license | $16.75 |
| REAL ID | No extra charge |

A regular license issued between ages 18 and 65 lasts **eight years**, so it costs $52.00.

## 1.5 The Knowledge Test and the Sign Test

Your written exam has **two separate parts**, and you must pass each one. You take them at a driver license office, along with the vision test; the knowledge test is on a computer. Book an appointment at skiptheline.ncdot.gov, or walk in, check in with your phone at the office and wait for a text that it is your turn.

| Feature | The {{STATE}} rule |
|---|---|
| Knowledge test | Traffic laws and safe driving; **80 percent** correct to pass |
| Sign test | **12** signs; identify at least **9** |
| How signs are tested | By shape and color, and you explain each one |
| Languages | Several languages; oral tests on request |
| If you fail | Retake the knowledge test after **seven calendar days** |

The NCDMV does not publish a fixed number of knowledge questions, so aim to know every chapter, not a passing count.

> **Test trap:** On the sign test you may see a sign with no words on it. Learn every shape and color in Chapter 2 so you can name a sign from its outline alone.

## 1.6 The Vision Test

Your vision is checked at the driver license office for a permit, a first license and an in-person renewal. For a license with no vision restriction you need **20/40** or better. If you need glasses or contact lenses to meet the standard, your license will say so, and you must wear them every time you drive. Driving without them counts the same as driving with no license.

## 1.7 Level 1: Driving on a Limited Learner Permit

**Who may supervise you.** Your parent, grandparent or guardian, or a responsible adult your parent or guardian approves. The supervisor must hold a valid license and must have been licensed for at least **five years**.

**Where everyone sits.** Your supervisor sits in the **front seat beside you**. No one else may ride in the front seat. Every passenger wears a seat belt or rides in a child safety seat.

**When you may drive.** For the first **six months**, only from **5 a.m. to 9 p.m.**, always with your supervisor. After six months, you may drive at any hour, still always supervised.

### The Driving Log

Before you can apply for Level 2, you must log at least **60 hours** of driving with your supervisor, and at least **10** of them must be at night. Night means after sunset, once daylight is gone. No more than **10 hours in one week** count toward the total. Your supervisor signs the log, and you turn it in, printed or digital, when you apply. A falsified log means you start a new log and wait six more months.

You must also hold the permit for **nine months**, and you must have **no conviction for a moving violation, seat belt infraction or phone violation** in the six months before you apply. Finishing the 60 hours early does not shorten the wait.

> **Remember it:** 60 hours, 10 at night, no more than 10 a week, and nine months. Use the log at the back of this book.

## 1.8 Level 2: Your First Months Alone

**Night limit.** You may drive alone only from **5 a.m. to 9 p.m.** From **9 p.m. to 5 a.m.** you need a supervising driver beside you. Two exceptions: driving straight to or from work, and straight to or from a volunteer fire, rescue or emergency medical service you belong to.

**Passenger limit.** When you drive without a supervisor, you may carry only **one passenger under 21** who is not a member of your household. One more under 21 non-household passenger may ride if that passenger is a student you are driving straight to or from school. If every passenger lives in your household, there is no limit beyond the seat belts. Household passengers under 21 may not ride along while an under 21 non-household passenger is in the car.

> **Test trap:** The Level 2 limit counts passengers **under 21**, not under 18. A 19-year-old friend counts toward it.

## 1.9 Level 3 and Rules for Every Driver Under 18

After **six months** at Level 2 with no moving violation, seat belt infraction or phone violation in the last six months, you may move up to Level 3. No new road test or driving log is needed, and the NCDMV may issue it without an office visit. Level 3 drops the hour, supervisor and passenger limits.

Some rules stay until your 18th birthday at every level:

- **No phone at all.** While the car is moving, you may not use a mobile phone or anything linked to it, hands-free or not. The only exceptions are calls to your parent, guardian or spouse, or about an emergency to 911, police, fire, an ambulance service, a hospital or a doctor. The fine is **$25**.
- **No alcohol or drugs.** Driving after drinking any amount of alcohol, or after using drugs, costs a provisional licensee the license for **one year**.
- **Moving violations count faster.** A second moving violation within 12 months can bring a suspension of up to **30 days**, a third up to **90 days**, and a fourth up to **six months**.

Your permit or Level 2 license expires on your **18th birthday**. At 18 you may upgrade straight to a regular license, online.

## 1.10 The Road Test

### Scheduling and What to Bring

You take the road test when you apply for Level 2. You may test at an NCDMV driver license office or at a commercial driving school certified by the NCDMV. Bring:

- Your Level 1 permit.
- Your driving log, printed or digital.
- Printed proof of liability insurance in your name, or a policy listing you as a driver.
- The test car's registration card.
- A parent or guardian, who must be present.
- If you tested at a driving school, the certificate showing you passed.

Bring your own car. Before you drive, the examiner checks that it is safe; a car that fails this pre-trip inspection cannot be used.

### What the Examiner Scores

The examiner watches how you approach intersections, stop signs and signals, start, stop, use your lanes, follow other cars, pass and are passed, yield to pedestrians and other vehicles, signal and use the horn, back up, and sit at the wheel. You will be asked for a **quick stop**, stopping as fast as you safely can, and a **three-point turnabout**: pull to the right, steer left across the road, back to the other side, then pull forward. Chapter 5 walks through it.

### If You Fail

You may retake the road test after **seven calendar days**.

## 1.11 Adults Getting a First License

At 18 or older, you skip the levels, the driver education course and the 60 hour log. You still pass the vision test, the sign test, the knowledge test and the road test, bring the same identity, Social Security and address documents, and show **printed proof of liability insurance** for the license.

You may first get a **learner permit**. While you drive on it, a person licensed to drive that car must sit beside you.

## 1.12 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

**From another state.** You have **60 days** after moving here to get a {{STATE}} license. With a valid license from another state, you take the **road sign test** and the **vision test**; the knowledge and road tests are usually waived, though an examiner may still require them. A license expired more than two years means the full exam. Teens under 18 who move here are placed in a level based on their earlier license and training; ask a driver license office.

**From another country.** A visitor 16 or older may drive here on a valid license from their home country. An International Driving Permit alone is not a license. Once you live here, a foreign license counts as no {{STATE}} license at all: you take the **full exam** like a first-time driver, with the knowledge, sign, vision and road tests. Bring proof of legal presence, such as your passport with visa and I-94 record.

## 1.13 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **{{STATE}} Driver Handbook** is free at MyNCDMV.gov, along with fees, office locations and appointments. Laws change faster than printed handbooks: the current handbook still lists rules that have since changed, such as the Driving Eligibility Certificate. If this book and current law ever disagree, the law wins. Check the NCDMV website the week before each test.

## Quick Review

- Level 1 at 15 needs driver education: 30 hours in class and 6 hours behind the wheel.
- The written exam has two parts: knowledge (80 percent to pass) and 12 signs (9 to pass).
- Your supervisor is a parent, grandparent, guardian or approved adult, licensed at least five years, beside you in front.
- Level 1 limits you to 5 a.m. to 9 p.m. for the first six months.
- Before Level 2: 60 logged hours (10 at night), nine months on the permit, and a road test.
- Level 2 alone: 5 a.m. to 9 p.m., and one non-household passenger under 21.
- Under 18, no phone use while the car is moving.
