# North Carolina inserts

## school_zone

A {{STATE}} school zone limit applies only on school days, during the hours posted on the sign or while its lights flash. It can be set no lower than **20 mph**.

Speeding in a school zone costs a **$250** fine and **3 points** on your license.

Some towns use **speed cameras** in school zones, with a warning sign posted ahead. A camera ticket is a **$250** civil penalty with no points.

## work_zone

A {{STATE}} highway work zone runs from the first work zone sign to the sign that marks its end.

Speeding in a work zone is always illegal. Where signs at the start of the zone post the penalty, a speeding ticket there adds an extra **$250** on top of the usual fine.

If failing to yield in a work zone seriously injures someone, the penalty is a **$500** fine and a **90-day** license revocation.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, you may turn **right on a steady red light** after a full stop, unless a sign says NO TURN ON RED. Yield to traffic and to pedestrians in or about to enter the crosswalk. Failing to yield to a pedestrian there costs **$100 to $500**.

**Left on red is never allowed**, not even from one one-way street onto another.

A **steady red arrow** means stop, with **no turn** in that direction while it shows.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Move Over law covers a vehicle stopped within **12 feet** of the road with warning lights on: emergency vehicles, tow trucks, utility trucks, garbage trucks and highway maintenance vehicles.

On a road with two or more lanes your way, move into a lane **not next to** it. If you cannot, or the road has one lane your way, slow down and be ready to stop.

The fine is **$250**. Seriously injuring a responder can also cost your license for up to **six months**.

## emergency_vehicles

In {{STATE}}, pull to the **right-hand edge** or curb and stop when an emergency vehicle with siren and lights approaches from either direction. Exception: oncoming traffic on a divided, limited-access highway with four lanes.

Never follow a fire truck going to a fire closer than **one block** in town or **400 feet** outside town. Never drive over a fire hose. Do not park within **100 feet** of emergency vehicles working a crash.

Failing to yield is a misdemeanor and **3 points**.

## school_bus

When a {{STATE}} school bus shows **flashing red lights** or its **stop arm**:

| Road | Who stops |
|---|---|
| Two lanes, even with a center turn lane | Both directions |
| Four lanes, no median | Both directions |
| Divided highway with a median | Only traffic behind the bus |
| Four or more lanes with a center turn lane | Only traffic behind the bus |

The law sets no distance. Stay stopped until the arm folds, the lights stop and the bus moves.

Passing a stopped bus: **5 points**, at least **$500**. Twice within three years: **one-year** revocation.

## u_turn

In {{STATE}}, a U-turn on an Interstate or other controlled-access highway is illegal except through an opening built for it. Never cross the median. If you miss an exit, go on to the next one.

When you make a U-turn on a green arrow, yield to drivers on the opposite side turning right on their own green arrow.

In a single-lane roundabout, a U-turn means going all the way around the circle.

## speed_limits

{{STATE}} limits when no sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Unpaved road or no center line | 25 mph |
| Inside cities and towns | 35 mph |
| Outside cities and towns | 55 mph |
| Posted Interstates | up to 70 mph |

Speeding **over 55 mph** adds **3 points**; most other speeding adds **2**.

Going **more than 15 mph over** the limit, or **over 80 mph**, is a crime. Either one, at a speed above 55, also costs your license for at least **30 days**.

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} Driver Handbook teaches the **two-second rule** in good conditions. If the front of your car reaches the fixed point before you finish counting two seconds, you are too close.

On wet pavement, drive **5 to 10 mph** slower and leave at least **twice** your normal gap. On snow or ice, allow at least **three times** your normal stopping distance.

Following too closely adds **4 points** to your license.

## hov

{{STATE}} has few carpool lanes. Near Charlotte, the old HOV lanes on **I-77** became **express toll lanes**. Vehicles with **three or more people** ride free if the driver declares it through NC Quick Pass before entering. Other drivers pay a toll that changes with traffic.

At any HOV or express lane, read the entrance signs for the hours, the number of riders and the toll. Enter and leave only where the lines are broken.

## parking_rules

{{STATE}} bans parking:

| Place | Distance |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant or fire station entrance | 15 feet |
| Intersection curb line (no curb: 15 feet) | 25 feet |
| Fire or fire truck, outside city limits | 400 feet |
| Fire or fire truck, inside city limits | 1 block |

Never park in front of a driveway, on a crosswalk, sidewalk or bridge, or on the traveled part of a highway. You may park on a shoulder only if drivers can see your car from **200 feet** both ways.

Never park on an Interstate except in an emergency.

## bicycle_passing

In {{STATE}}, pass any vehicle, a bicycle included, at least **2 feet** to its left. The safest way to pass a cyclist is to change lanes.

You may pass a bicycle or moped in a **no-passing zone** only if all of this is true: it is going your way, it is not turning left or signaling a left turn, and you leave at least **4 feet** or move completely into the left lane.

Forcing a cyclist out of the lane by not signaling costs at least **$200**.

## crash_reporting

In {{STATE}}, a crash is **reportable** if anyone is killed or injured, or if total property damage looks like **$1,000** or more. You must report it **at once** to the nearest police officer or agency.

If no one is hurt and your car can be driven, move it out of the travel lanes onto the shoulder.

Failing to report a reportable crash adds **3 points** and can cost your license. Tell your insurance company too.

## bac_limits

{{STATE}} sets these limits:

| Driver | Illegal at a BAC of |
|---|---|
| Age 21 or older | 0.08 percent |
| Commercial vehicle driver | 0.04 percent |
| Under 21 | Any alcohol at all |

A driver under 18 who drives after drinking any alcohol, or using drugs, loses the license for **one year**.

Even under these numbers, you are guilty of impaired driving if alcohol or drugs have noticeably harmed your driving.

## implied_consent

In {{STATE}}, refusing a breath or blood test when charged with impaired driving brings:

- an **immediate** revocation of at least **30 days**;
- a further **12-month** revocation by the NCDMV.

Your refusal can also be used as evidence in court. A judge may grant limited driving after **six months** in some cases.

Before the test you may call a lawyer and pick a witness, but the test will not wait more than **30 minutes**.

## dui_penalties

{{STATE}} sentences impaired driving in levels set by the judge:

| Level | Jail | Fine up to |
|---|---|---|
| Five (lowest) | 24 hours to 60 days | $200 |
| Three | 72 hours to 6 months | $1,000 |
| One | 30 days to 24 months | $4,000 |

A first conviction also revokes your license for **one year**. A BAC of **0.15** or more means an ignition interlock.

**Open containers:** no one may keep or drink from an open container of alcohol in the passenger area, glove box included, on the road. Paid transport passengers are exempt.

## underage_alcohol

In {{STATE}}, a driver **under 21** with any alcohol or drug in the body commits a misdemeanor and loses the license for **one year**.

Off the road, a **one-year** revocation also follows if you are under 21 and:

- buy or try to buy alcohol;
- help someone else under 21 buy it;
- use a fake ID or someone else's license to get it.

Lending your license to someone under 21 for alcohol, or giving alcohol to anyone under 21, brings the same one-year revocation.

## phone_laws

In {{STATE}}, **no driver** may type a text or email, or read one, while driving. The fine is **$100** plus court costs, with no points. Hands-free voice use and GPS are allowed, and so is texting while legally parked.

Drivers **under 18** may not use a phone at all while the car is moving, even hands-free. They may call only a parent, guardian or spouse, or emergency help. That fine is **$25**.

School bus drivers may not use a phone while driving.

## seat_belts

{{STATE}} requires **every person** in the car to be belted, in every seat, whenever it moves.

| Who is unbelted | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Driver or front passenger | $25.50 plus court costs |
| Rear seat passenger | $10, no court costs |

Seat belt tickets add no points. Police may stop you for an unbelted driver or front passenger, but not for a rear passenger alone.

Children **under 16** may not ride in the open bed of a pickup, with a few exceptions.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} makes the driver responsible for every passenger under 16:

| Child | Required |
|---|---|
| Under 8 and under 80 pounds | Child restraint fit for the child's weight |
| Under 5 and under 40 pounds | Back seat, if there is a front airbag and a back seat |
| Under 16 | Restraint or seat belt |

If no seat has a lap and shoulder belt, a child under 8 weighing 40 to 80 pounds may use a lap belt.

The penalty: up to **$25** and **2 points**.

## vehicle_rules

{{STATE}} requires a yearly **safety inspection**, plus an **emissions** test in some counties, within **90 days** before your registration renews.

Minimum liability insurance: **$50,000** per person and **$100,000** per crash for injuries, and **$50,000** for property damage. Coverage must never lapse; there is no grace period. A lapse can cost the plate for **30 days** plus a fine.

Window tint may not block more than **65 percent** of light. Report an address or name change within **60 days**. Plastic plate covers are illegal.

## police_stop

No safe place to stop? Turn on your **flashers** and slow by about **10 mph** until you reach one. Unsure about an unmarked car? You may call **911** first.

Once stopped, turn off the engine and lower your window, including a tinted one on the officer's side. At night, turn on the interior light. Rear passengers rest their hands on the front seat backs.

State law requires you to show your license and registration. If you carry a concealed handgun under a permit, tell the officer.

## headlight_use

In {{STATE}}, your headlights must be on:

- from **sunset to sunrise**;
- whenever you cannot clearly see a person **400 feet** ahead;
- whenever weather, smoke or fog keeps you from seeing **500 feet** ahead;
- whenever your **wipers** are on because of rain, sleet, snow, smoke or fog.

Wipers used now and then in a light mist do not count. Breaking the wiper rule brings a small fine and no points.

Use **low beams** in fog and when driving in a city at night.

## driver_record

{{STATE}} counts license points:

- At **7 points**, you may be sent to a driver improvement clinic (**$83.50**). Finishing it removes **3 points**, once every five years.
- At **12 points** within three years, your license can be suspended: up to **60 days** the first time, **six months** the second, **one year** after that.
- Back from a suspension, **8 points** within three years can mean another.

Insurance companies keep a separate point system, which can raise your rates.

## road_test

The {{STATE}} road test includes a **quick stop** (stopping as fast as you safely can when told), **backing** and a **three-point turnabout**, plus turns, lanes, signals and yielding.

Bring your own car, its registration and printed proof of insurance. The car must pass a pre-trip safety check first. Under 18, a parent or guardian must be present, and you may test at an NCDMV office or a certified driving school.

If you do not pass, you may retest after **seven calendar days**.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} teens on a Level 1 permit must log supervised driving before applying for Level 2:

| Category | Hours |
|---|---|
| Total | 60 |
| At night | At least 10 |
| Most that count in one week | 10 |

Night hours count toward the 60. Your supervisor signs the log; NCDMV takes a printed or digital copy. The supervisor must be your parent, grandparent, guardian or an approved adult, licensed at least **five years**, seated beside you. You must also hold the permit **nine months**.

## signal_distance

{{STATE}} law sets how early to signal. Signal for at least the last **100 feet** before you stop or turn.

Where the speed limit is **45 mph** or more, signal for at least the last **200 feet** before you turn.

## headlight_dimming

In {{STATE}}, switch to low beams when an oncoming vehicle is within **500 feet**. When you follow another vehicle, switch to low beams within **200 feet** of it, unless you are passing it.
