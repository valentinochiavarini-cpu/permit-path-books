# Alabama inserts

## school_zone

{{STATE}} posts each school zone limit on a sign that shows the hours it applies. Some zones use a lighted sign with **flashing yellow lights** instead: the lower limit applies while the lights flash.

You must stop when a **school safety patrol** member holds an official flag in the STOP position, and stay stopped until the flag is lowered.

## work_zone

In a {{STATE}} construction zone **where workers are present**, any moving violation, such as speeding or following too closely, costs **$250 or double the usual fine**, whichever is more.

Ignoring construction or maintenance zone markers adds **3 points** to your record. Large flashing arrow panels tell you which lanes are closed ahead; merge early.

## turn_on_red

In {{STATE}}, after a **complete stop** you may turn **right on a steady red** unless a sign forbids it. From a one-way street, a **left on red** is legal only into a one-way street whose traffic flows to your left.

The law allows these turns facing **any steady red signal** but has no separate rule for a **red arrow**, so it does not clearly allow a turn on one. When a red arrow is lit, wait for the green.

Yield to pedestrians and to traffic in the intersection. Treat a dark signal as a four-way stop.

## move_over

{{STATE}}'s Move Over law covers stopped emergency vehicles with lights flashing, tow trucks with amber lights, utility and road crews with flashing lights, and working garbage trucks.

| Road | What you do |
|---|---|
| Two or more lanes your way | Leave the next lane; if you cannot, slow to **15 mph below** the limit |
| Two-lane road | Move away within your lane and slow to **15 mph below** the limit (limit 20 mph or less: drive **10 mph**) |

Fines: **$200**, **$250**, then **$300** plus at least a **90-day** suspension.

## emergency_vehicles

When an emergency vehicle with **flashing red or blue lights** and a siren comes from either direction, pull over to the **right-hand edge or curb** and stop. Stay stopped until it has passed. If you are in an intersection, clear it first.

Unless you are on official business, never follow a responding emergency vehicle within **500 feet**. Do not drive over a fire hose unless a police officer or fire official tells you to.

In a roundabout, finish to your exit first, then pull over.

## school_bus

In {{STATE}}, stop at least **20 feet** from a school bus or church bus that has stopped with its stop arm out and **red lights** flashing. Wait until the arm folds and the lights stop.

Traffic from **both directions** stops on two-lane roads and on undivided highways of four to six lanes. On a **divided highway of four or more lanes**, oncoming traffic need not stop.

Passing a stopped bus: **5 points** and a fine of **$150 to $300** the first time. A second offense adds a **30-day** suspension.

## u_turn

{{STATE}} bans turning around on a curve or near the top of a hill where drivers coming from either direction cannot see you within **500 feet**, and on interstate highways. Cities may ban more, sometimes without a sign.

If you miss a freeway exit, do not stop, back up or turn around. Go to the next exit and come back.

## speed_limits

{{STATE}} limits when no sign says otherwise:

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Urban district | 30 mph |
| Unpaved road | 35 mph |
| Paved county road | 45 mph |
| Other roads | 55 mph |
| Highway with four or more lanes | 65 mph |
| Interstate | 70 mph |

| Over the limit by | Points |
|---|---|
| 1 to 25 mph | 2 |
| 26 mph or more | 5 |

A posted minimum speed is the law too, in normal conditions.

## following_distance

The {{STATE}} manual teaches the **two-second rule**: count "one thousand one, one thousand two." If you reach the fixed point before you finish, you are too close.

In bad weather or on poor roads, stretch the gap to **four or five seconds**, and do the same when you drive something longer than a car. Behind a motorcycle, keep at least two seconds, and more when it is dark or wet. Following too closely costs **3 points**.

## hov

{{STATE}} has no carpool lane rules in its manual. Two lane rules matter more here:

- On an interstate, do not stay in the **left lane** for more than **1.5 miles** in a row unless you pass at least one vehicle, or traffic or weather make it necessary.
- Over a lane, a **red X** means do not drive in it, a **yellow X** means you may use it only with caution, ready to leave it, and a **green arrow** means it is open.

## parking_rules

{{STATE}} bans parking:

| Place | Distance |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant | Within 15 feet |
| Crosswalk at an uncontrolled intersection | Within 20 feet |
| Flashing beacon, stop sign or signal | Within 30 feet |
| Nearest rail of a railroad crossing | Within 50 feet |

Never park in an intersection, on a crosswalk or sidewalk, blocking a driveway, on any bridge, inside a tunnel, double-parked, or at a **yellow curb**. On a rural highway, move a stopped car off the pavement.

## bicycle_passing

In {{STATE}}, leave at least **3 feet** between your car and a person on a bicycle when you pass. If the road is too narrow, stay back and do not honk.

Cyclists may ride **two abreast**, must use a bike lane where one exists, and may not ride on the sidewalk. Riders under **16** must wear a helmet. You may enter a bike lane only to park, to enter or leave the road, or to get ready for a turn.

## crash_reporting

In {{STATE}}, report any crash with an injury or death at once: to local police in a city, or to State Troopers on a state highway. From a cell phone, dial **\*HP (\*47)** to reach the Troopers.

If anyone was hurt or killed, or damage reached **$500**, and the at-fault driver had no insurance, send **form SR-31** to ALEA within **30 days**. If both drivers were insured, no SR-31 is needed. You can file it online.

## bac_limits

{{STATE}} makes it illegal to drive at or above these limits:

| Driver | Limit |
|---|---|
| 21 or older | 0.08 percent |
| Commercial driver | 0.04 percent |
| Under 21 | 0.02 percent |
| School bus or day care driver | 0.02 percent |

You can also be convicted below these numbers if alcohol or drugs make you unable to drive safely. At **0.15** or more, the minimum punishment **doubles**.

## implied_consent

By driving in {{STATE}}, you agree to a breath, blood or urine test when an officer arrests you for DUI. Refuse, and ALEA suspends your license for at least **90 days**, apart from any DUI case.

A test result of **0.08** or more also brings an ALEA suspension: **90 days** the first time, longer with a prior offense in the past **10 years**. A first DUI with a refusal also requires an ignition interlock for **one year**.

## dui_penalties

| DUI conviction | Fine | Jail | License |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | $600 to $2,100 | Up to 1 year | 90-day suspension |
| Second in 10 years | $1,100 to $5,100 | At least 5 days, or 30 days community service; up to 1 year | 1-year revocation |
| Third | $2,100 to $10,100 | 60 days to 1 year | 3-year revocation |

A fourth is a felony. A child under 14 in the car doubles the minimum punishment.

**Open containers:** no open alcohol in the passenger area on a public road. Fine: up to **$25**.

## underage_alcohol

Under 21 in {{STATE}}, a BAC of **0.02** up to 0.08 is DUI. A first offense brings a **30-day** suspension and a required DUI or substance abuse program. At 0.08 or more, adult penalties apply.

Buying or having alcohol under 21, or using a **fake ID** to get it, is a crime: up to **3 months** in jail, a **$50 to $500** fine and a **3 to 6 month** license suspension, even with no car involved.

## phone_laws

Under {{STATE}}'s **hands-free law**, holding or supporting a phone, or watching or recording video, is a violation when an officer also sees you cross lanes without a signal, swerve or otherwise drive improperly. Voice, Bluetooth, one tap or swipe to start or end a call, and emergency calls are allowed. Never text while you drive.

| Within 24 months | Fine | Points |
|---|---|---|
| First | $50 | 1 |
| Second | $100 | 2 |
| Third | $150 | 3 |

Stage II drivers may not use any handheld device (Chapter 1).

## seat_belts

{{STATE}} requires **every person** in the car to be buckled, front and back. Children through age 15 follow the child restraint law in the box below.

Enforcement is **primary** in the front seat: an officer can stop you only for a missing belt. In the back seat it is **secondary**: you can be cited only after a stop for something else.

## child_restraints

{{STATE}} makes the driver responsible for buckling children:

| Child | Required |
|---|---|
| Until at least age 1 or 20 pounds | Rear-facing seat |
| Until at least age 5 or 40 pounds | Forward-facing seat |
| Until age 6 | Booster seat |
| Until age 15 | Seat belt |

The law covers front and back seats of cars, pickups, SUVs and vans. Fine: **$25**, plus **1 point** the first time and **2 points** after that.

## vehicle_rules

{{STATE}} requires at least **1/16 inch** of tire tread, mirrors that show **200 feet** behind, and a muffler with no leaks. Only the top **6 inches** of the windshield may be tinted, and side and rear windows on cars must let through at least **32 percent** of light.

Minimum liability insurance: **$25,000** per person and **$50,000** per crash for injury, and **$25,000** for property. Driving uninsured: up to **$500** the first time, **$1,000** after, and a possible **six-month** suspension.

## police_stop

The {{STATE}} manual adds these steps. Signal and pull over as soon as it is safe. Turn off the engine and the radio, and at night turn on the inside light. Put your phone down. Keep both hands on the wheel, and ask passengers to keep their hands in view.

Tell the officer at first contact if there are **weapons** in the vehicle. Show your license, proof of insurance and registration when asked. Signing a ticket is not an admission of guilt; refusing to sign can lead to arrest.

## headlight_use

In {{STATE}}, your headlights must be on:

- from **half an hour after sunset** to **half an hour before sunrise**;
- whenever you cannot see clearly for at least **500 feet**;
- whenever your **wipers** are on because of rain, sleet or snow. Wipers on an occasional intermittent setting do not count.

Parking lights alone are never enough. When in doubt, turn on your low beams.

## driver_record

{{STATE}} suspends a license for points within **two years**:

| Points | Suspension |
|---|---|
| 12 to 14 | 60 days |
| 15 to 17 | 90 days |
| 18 to 20 | 120 days |
| 21 to 23 | 180 days |
| 24 or more | 365 days |

Points count for two years from the conviction but stay on your record. Examples: reckless driving or alcohol offenses **6**, failure to yield **5**, illegal passing **4**, improper turn **2**. Getting a license back after any suspension costs at least **$100**.

## road_test

The {{STATE}} examiner checks your brake lights, turn signals and horn, plus your proof of insurance and registration. A 16-year-old comes with a parent or guardian.

You may be asked to back up about **50 feet**, make a **three-point turn**, park on a hill within **18 inches** of the curb, and signal **100 feet** before turns. A crash, a broken traffic law, reckless driving or refusing to cooperate fails you at once.

## supervised_hours

{{STATE}} teens need **one** of these before a Stage II license:

| Option | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Practice | At least **50 hours** behind the wheel, certified on ALEA form DL-31 by a parent, guardian, grandparent or certified driving instructor |
| Driver education | A course approved by the State Department of Education |

{{STATE}} sets no separate night-hour count. You must also hold your learner license at least **six months** if you are under 18.

## signal_distance

{{STATE}} requires your turn signal at least **100 feet** before you turn. Keep it on until you are ready to turn, and cancel it afterward.

In daylight you may add hand signals: arm **straight out** for left, arm **bent up** for right, arm **bent down** to slow or stop.

## headlight_dimming

{{STATE}} law requires low beams within **500 feet** of an oncoming vehicle and within **200 feet** of a vehicle you are following. If the other driver keeps the brights on, keep yours dimmed anyway.
