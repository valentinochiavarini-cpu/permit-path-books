# Chapter 1: Your Path to a {{STATE}} License

In {{STATE}}, licenses come from the **Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA)**, Driver License Division. You take your tests at an ALEA driver license examining office. Renewals and duplicates can also be handled at a county Probate Judge or License Commissioner office, or online. Fees and forms change, so check the ALEA website before every visit.

> **On the test**
>
> - Who may sit beside a 15-year-old learner.
> - The six months you must hold a learner license before a restricted license.
> - Stage II limits: midnight to 6 a.m., one passenger outside the family, no handheld devices.
> - What happens when a teen breaks a restriction or gets a second ticket.

## 1.1 The Three Stages for a Teen Driver

{{STATE}} uses a **graduated driver license (GDL)**. You earn privileges in three steps, because new drivers crash most during their first months alone.

| Stage | Earliest age | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Stage I: learner license | 15 | Drive only with a qualified adult beside you |
| Stage II: restricted license | 16 | Drive alone, with night, passenger and device limits |
| Stage III: unrestricted license | 17 | Full privileges |

Your learner license is a **Class D** license with a **"Y" restriction**, which means you must have a supervisor in the seat next to you. Class D covers ordinary cars, pickups and vans. A license runs for **four years**. A learner license issued at 15 is also good for four years and can be renewed once, so you never have to rush the road test.

## 1.2 What to Bring

Book a visit at **AlabamaDL.alea.gov**, where you can also pre-apply. At 15, bring:

- A **certified U.S. birth certificate** from a vital records office. Photocopies are refused.
- Your **Social Security card**.
- A **school enrollment form (DL-1/93)** signed at your school, or proof of graduation. Every first-time applicant under 19 needs one.

At 16 or older, you may instead prove identity with two documents from ALEA's list, at least one with your photo and one from its "primary" list, plus your Social Security card. Every document must be an original or a copy certified by the office that issued it. A paper with white-out, a missing signature or a tear across key details is refused.

**Stay in school.** {{STATE}} will not license an applicant under 19 who has not finished secondary school and is not enrolled, with a few exceptions. A teen from 15 through 18 who drops out can also lose a license already issued.

Every applicant must prove that federal law allows them to be in the United States. Non-citizens need valid immigration papers with enough time left on them; check ALEA's current list before you go.

**STAR ID.** {{STATE}}'s REAL ID card carries a star and is called the **STAR ID**. Choose it if you want to use your license to board domestic flights; it needs a few extra documents, listed on the ALEA website.

> **Road tip:** Check that your name is spelled the same way on every document the night before. A mismatch is the most common reason people are sent home.

## 1.3 Fees

| Item | ALEA fee |
|---|---|
| Knowledge test (each attempt) | $5 |
| Learner license | $36.25 |
| Driver license | $36.25 |
| Stage III license with the restriction removed | $31.25 |

ALEA takes no checks. Cash, money orders and cards are fine, and a card adds a **4 percent** fee. Some counties charge slightly more where local law allows it.

## 1.4 The Knowledge Test

The knowledge test checks {{STATE}} traffic laws, road signs and safe driving rules, all drawn from the **Alabama Driver Manual**.

| Feature | The {{STATE}} rule |
|---|---|
| Format | Multiple choice on an automated testing station |
| Cost | $5 for each attempt |
| Languages | English, plus Arabic, Chinese, Farsi, French, German, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Spanish, Thai and Vietnamese |
| Help | Oral testing for applicants who cannot read the questions |
| Where | An ALEA driver license examining office |

ALEA does not publish the number of questions or the passing score, and it gives no online test. Study the whole manual, signs included, because sign questions are mixed in with the law questions. If you fail, you pay the $5 again to retest.

> **Test trap:** Many questions turn on a number: 100 feet, 500 feet, six months, 0.02. Learn the numbers in this chapter and in the state boxes of every chapter.

## 1.5 The Vision Screening

Your eyes are screened at the office. Bring your glasses or contacts. If you need lenses to pass, your license will say so, and you must wear them every time you drive, including on the road test.

If you do not pass, see a licensed eye specialist and bring back the report. If it shows you see well enough to drive, you may finish your tests.

## 1.6 Driving on a Learner License

**Who sits beside you.** The supervisor must ride in the front passenger seat:

| Your age | Who may supervise |
|---|---|
| 15 | A parent or legal guardian, a licensed driver 21 or older, or a licensed or certified driving instructor |
| 16 or older | Any licensed driver, according to the ALEA manual |

**Carry your license.** Every {{STATE}} driver, learners included, must carry the license and hand it to an officer who asks for it.

### Getting Ready for Stage II

Before you may apply for a restricted license under 18, all of these must be true:

- You are at least **16**.
- You have held your learner license for at least **six months**.
- A parent, legal guardian or consenting grandparent signs ALEA's **Graduated Driver License form (DL-31)**, giving permission.
- You show **one** of the following: the same form certifying at least **50 hours** of behind-the-wheel practice, signed by a parent, guardian, grandparent or licensed driving instructor, **or** a certificate from a driver education course approved by the State Department of Education.

{{STATE}} sets no separate night-hour count. Practice at night and in rain anyway, and use the log at the back of this book.

> **Remember it:** Fifteen to learn, six months on the permit, 50 hours or driver ed, then Stage II at 16.

## 1.7 Stage II: The Restricted License

You hold a Stage II license if you are 16, or 17 with a license held less than six months. You pass the road test, surrender your learner license, and receive a license with the "Y" restriction removed. The manual says this costs nothing extra, while ALEA's GDL page lists $36.25, so ask your office. The limits below then apply.

**No driving from midnight to 6 a.m.**, unless:

- a parent or legal guardian rides with you;
- a licensed driver 21 or older rides with you, with your parent's consent;
- you are going to or from work;
- you are going to or from an event sponsored by your school or a religious group;
- you are going to or from hunting or fishing, with a valid hunting or fishing license on you;
- you are driving because of a medical, fire or police emergency.

**Passengers.** No more than **one** passenger who is not family. A parent, guardian or supervising licensed driver 21 or older does not count.

**Devices.** No handheld phone or other handheld communication device while driving. Adults face a hands-free law too; Chapter 11 explains it.

### Breaking a Restriction

Breaking a restriction is a traffic violation. Every time, your restricted period is **extended by six months**, or until you turn 18. Under current law, each conviction also brings a fine of $150 to $350 plus court costs and two points. A first offense sends you to **defensive driving school**. A second offense suspends your Stage II privileges and puts you back on a **learner license for six months**. A parent who knowingly allows repeat violations can be fined too. (The ALEA manual still prints the older rule of no points.)

A second **moving violation**, or a conviction for reckless driving, racing, fleeing police, illegal passing, driving on the wrong side of the road, leaving a crash scene without giving aid or information, or any offense worth **4 or more points**, brings an automatic **60-day suspension**, and your restricted period is extended as well.

Parents can go further than the law. The ALEA manual includes a **Parent/Teen Driving Agreement** that families sign together, with house rules on passengers, phones and night driving.

> **Test trap:** The curfew starts at **midnight**, not 11 p.m., and a friend your age in the car never lifts it.

## 1.8 Stage III: The Unrestricted License

The limits end when you reach **17** and have held your Stage II license for at least **six months** with no moving violations in the past six months, or when you turn **18**. Swap your card for one with the restriction removed ($31.25).

The GDL rules also do not apply to a driver who is married and head of a household, or one legally relieved of minority.

## 1.9 The Road Test

### Scheduling and What to Bring

Some offices let you book the road skills test online up to two weeks ahead; walk-ins are welcome at all testing sites. A 16-year-old must come with a parent or guardian. Bring:

- your learner license, which you surrender;
- your DL-31 form or driver education certificate;
- a car with working brake lights, left and right turn signals and horn;
- the car's **proof of insurance** and **registration**.

A rental car is allowed only if your name is on the rental agreement as a driver.

### What Is Scored

The examiner may test any of these: using the controls, posture, a smooth quick stop, backing about **50 feet** straight, stop signs and signals, a **three-point turn** on a narrow street, turns signaled at least **100 feet** ahead, parking on a hill within **18 inches** of the curb, starting on a hill, lane use, passing, horn use and a **two-second** following distance.

### Automatic Fails

You fail at once for a crash, breaking a traffic law, reckless or dangerous driving, or refusing to cooperate. Several errors from habit or too little practice also fail you.

## 1.10 Adults Getting a First License

The GDL stages do not apply at **18 or older**. There is no six-month hold, no 50-hour form and no curfew. You still pass the vision screening, the knowledge test and the road test. Bring a licensed driver, an insured car that passes the examiner's check, and your identity documents. Many adults finish all testing in one visit.

If you need practice first, a learner license is open to anyone 16 or older. Applicants under **19** still need the school enrollment form or proof of graduation.

## 1.11 New Residents and Foreign License Holders

After you move to {{STATE}}, you may drive on your valid license from your old state for **30 days**. Transfer it at an ALEA office. If your out-of-state license is valid or expired no more than **one year**, you take no knowledge or road test. You surrender your old license and pay a $5 transfer fee plus the license fee.

{{STATE}} has license agreements with a few countries, including Germany, South Korea and Taiwan, whose licensed drivers skip the road test. With a license from any other country, you follow the new driver steps: the vision screening, the knowledge test and the road test. You also prove legal presence with your passport and immigration papers. {{STATE}} does not license applicants who cannot prove legal presence.

Some people may drive here without an {{STATE}} license:

- Visitors 16 or older with a valid license from their home state or country.
- Full-time students from out of state, with a valid home license.
- Military members from other states stationed here, and their families.

## 1.12 Where to Find the Official Rules

The **Alabama Driver Manual** is free on **alea.gov**, the study source for the knowledge test. ALEA's online services, appointments and office list are at **AlabamaDL.alea.gov**. If this book and the manual ever disagree, the manual and current law win.

## Quick Review

- Stage I learner license at 15: a parent, guardian, licensed driver 21 or older, or instructor sits beside you.
- The knowledge test costs $5 an attempt; ALEA publishes no question count or passing score.
- Before Stage II under 18: six months on the learner license, parent consent, and 50 practice hours or approved driver ed.
- Stage II: no driving midnight to 6 a.m., one non-family passenger at most, no handheld devices.
- Breaking a restriction adds six months to Stage II; a second moving violation brings a 60-day suspension.
- Stage III at 17 after six clean months on Stage II, or at 18.
- New residents have 30 days and skip the tests if the old license is valid or expired no more than one year.
