# Chapter 13: Test Day: Knowledge Test and Road Test

Test day problems are rarely gaps in knowledge. They are rushing, misreading, forgetting a document or freezing. Read this chapter a week before your knowledge test and again before your road test.

> **On the test**
>
> - The road test starts with a check of your car and its controls before you drive.
> - Examiners score stops, turns, lane changes, backing, parking and how you watch traffic.
> - Some errors end the road test at once, such as a rolling stop or the examiner having to step in.

## 13.1 The Last Week Before the Knowledge Test

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 7 to 5 days out | Take Mock Exam 1 cold. Mark every miss. |
| 4 to 3 days out | Reread the sections behind your misses. Drill signs and numbers. |
| 2 days out | Take Mock Exam 2. Review only what you missed. |
| Day before | Light review of signs and state numbers. Pack your documents. Sleep. |

**Study your misses, not your strengths.** For each miss, note whether you did not know the rule (study it) or misread the question (read slower).

**Make a numbers sheet.** Speed limits, BAC limits and similar numbers differ by state, so take them from Chapter 1 and the state boxes, not from memory. Write them on one page and read it daily.

**Drill signs** from Chapter 2 by shape and color; some states give a separate sign test.

## 13.2 Tactics in the Testing Room

The test is usually on a computer at a licensing office, though some states use paper or allow some applicants to test online. In most states, using a phone or notes during the test can end it.

- **Read the whole question** before the answers. Watch for words that flip the meaning: **not**, **except**, **always**, **never**, **only**, **first**. Then read all four answers.
- **Rule out what you know is wrong,** such as answers that speed past a hazard or assume another driver will do the right thing.
- **Change an answer only for a real reason,** such as a word you misread.
- **Skip and return.** If your test allows it, skip a hard question, finish the ones you know, then come back.

> **Test trap:** Each question has one answer based on the official manual. When two sound reasonable, pick the one that matches the manual's rule, not what you see other drivers do.

## 13.3 What to Bring

Chapter 1 gives your state's list. In general, bring:

- **Proof of identity** and other documents, usually originals.
- **Your learner permit** for the road test.
- **Glasses or contact lenses** if you need them. If you pass the vision test wearing them, your license will likely say you must drive with them.
- **The fee**, and **a parent or guardian** if you are under 18 and your state requires one.
- **Forms** such as a driver education certificate or a supervised driving log.

For the road test, you need a car and, in most states, a licensed adult to bring you. Bring the car's **current registration** and **proof of insurance**. Without them, most offices will not start it.

## 13.4 The Road Test: How It Works

On the **road test**, an examiner in the passenger seat gives you directions and scores how you drive. If you did not hear an instruction, ask; that is never marked against you.

### The vehicle check

Before you leave, the examiner checks that the car is safe and legal. If it is not, most states cancel the test and you must reschedule.

| Item | What the examiner checks |
|---|---|
| Papers | Current registration and insurance |
| Lights | Headlights, brake lights, turn signals |
| Horn and brakes | Horn works; firm pedal; parking brake holds |
| Mirrors and windshield | Present, clear, not cracked |
| Tires | Good tread, no bald spots |
| Seat belts | Every belt working |

### The controls check

The examiner may ask you to find the headlights and high beams, turn signals, wipers, defroster, emergency flashers, horn and parking brake. This checks that you can find and use the car's controls. Some states require you to turn off self-parking or other driver assistance features.

## 13.5 The Maneuvers Examiners Score

The examiner watches **control** and **observation** (looking before you move).

**Leaving the curb.** Signal, check mirrors, turn your head to check your blind spot, then pull out when safe.

**Stops.** Come to a **complete stop**, wheels no longer rolling, behind the stop line, or before the crosswalk, or where you can see cross traffic. Behind another car, leave enough room to see its rear tires touching the road.

**Intersections.** Look left, right and ahead, turning your head. Never enter if you cannot get all the way through. At a green with nothing blocking you, check that the intersection is clear and go.

**Turns.** Signal well ahead, slow down before the turn (not during it) and turn into the correct lane (Chapter 5). The most common errors are swinging wide across part of another lane and not checking for bikes and pedestrians before a right turn.

**Lane changes.** Use the same order. A missing head check is an error even if the lane was clear.

**Backing.** Check all around the car, then turn your body and look over your right shoulder through the rear window. A backup camera does not show everything. Back slowly.

**Parking and turning around.** Your state may test parallel parking, hill parking, angle or perpendicular parking, a three-point turn or pulling over to the curb (Chapter 8). Many states allow a few corrections in parking, but hitting the curb hard, climbing it or hitting a cone counts against you.

**Speed.** Drive at or near the posted limit when conditions allow.

> **Remember it:** Signal, mirror, shoulder, then move. Use this order for every lane change, pull out and turn.

## 13.6 Automatic Fails

Some errors fail you on their own, and some states end the test on the spot. Common ones:

| Error | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Examiner must step in | You were not in control |
| Hitting a car, object or person | A real crash risk |
| Driving onto the curb or sidewalk | A loss of control |
| Rolling through a stop sign or red light | Breaks the law |
| Not yielding to a pedestrian | Puts someone in danger |
| Passing a stopped school bus | Children may be crossing |
| Not pulling over for an emergency vehicle | Breaks the law |
| Speeding, or driving far too slow | Unsafe for traffic |
| No head check before a lane change or backing | You could hit what you did not see |

**Examiner intervention** means the examiner had to grab the wheel, use a dual brake or tell you to stop. The test is then usually over. A **rolling stop**, slowing to a crawl without stopping, is the classic fail.

> **Test trap:** An examiner's direction never makes an illegal move legal. Told to turn right at a light with a No Turn on Red sign, wait for the green.

## 13.7 Handling Nerves

Practice in the car you will test in, eat, arrive early and breathe slowly. If you make a small mistake, let it go and focus on the next block.

> **Road tip:** If you miss a turn, do not swerve or brake hard. Keep driving safely and let the examiner give you a new route.

## 13.8 After the Test

**If you pass**, in most states you leave with a temporary paper license and the card comes later. Teens usually move to the next stage of the **graduated driver license**, with limits on night driving and passengers (Chapter 1).

**If you do not pass**, read the score sheet, ask what went wrong and practice those exact errors. Most states set a wait before you retest, may limit attempts and may charge a fee; Chapter 1 and the box in Section 13.4 give yours. After a failed knowledge test, most states let you retake it after a short wait.

## Quick Review

- In the last week, study the questions you missed and read your numbers sheet daily.
- Read every question to the end, watch for NOT and EXCEPT, and rule out unsafe answers.
- Bring your permit, the car's registration and insurance, and a car whose lights, horn and brakes work.
- Signal, mirror, shoulder, then move; stop completely behind the line.
- If the examiner has to step in, the test is usually over; if you fail, use the score sheet to study.
