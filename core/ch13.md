# Chapter 13: Test Day: Knowledge Test and Road Test

Test day problems are rarely gaps in knowledge. They are rushing, misreading, a missing document or a car that fails the safety check. Use this chapter as your checklist the day before each test.

> **On the test**
>
> - The road test starts with a check of your papers, your car and its controls.
> - Examiners score stops, turns, lane changes, backing, parking and how you watch traffic.
> - Some errors end the road test at once, such as running a stop sign or the examiner having to step in.

## 13.1 Test-Day Checklist

Chapter 1 lists the exact documents and fees. Pack them the night before, sleep, eat and arrive early.

| Bring or check | Why |
|---|---|
| Identity documents, usually originals | No documents, no test |
| Your learner permit | Required for the road test |
| Glasses or contacts, if you need them | Your license may then require them |
| The fee, forms, a parent if required | Missing ones stop the visit |
| Car registration and proof of insurance | No papers, no road test |
| Working lights, horn, brakes, belts | The examiner checks them first |

If you pass the vision test wearing glasses or contact lenses, your license will likely say you must drive with them.

## 13.2 The Knowledge Test

Using a phone or notes during the test can end it on the spot.

- **Read the whole question** before the answers. Watch for words that flip the meaning: **not**, **except**, **always**, **never**, **only**, **first**. Then read all four answers.
- **Rule out what you know is wrong,** such as answers that speed past a hazard or assume another driver will do the right thing.
- **Change an answer only for a real reason,** such as a word you misread.
- **Skip and return.** If your test allows it, skip a hard question, finish the ones you know, then come back.

> **Test trap:** Each question has one answer based on the official manual. When two sound reasonable, pick the one that matches the manual's rule, not what you see other drivers do.

## 13.3 How the Road Test Works

An examiner in the passenger seat gives you directions and scores how you drive. If you did not hear an instruction, ask; that is never marked against you. Friends and family usually cannot ride along.

**The vehicle check.** Before you leave, the examiner checks the papers, lights, brake lights and turn signals, horn, brakes and parking brake, mirrors and windshield (no cracks that block your view), tires and seat belts. If the car is not safe and legal, the test is not given and you must reschedule.

**The controls check.** The examiner may ask you to find the headlights and high beams, turn signals, wipers, defroster, emergency flashers, horn and parking brake. This shows you can find and use the car's controls without looking for them.

## 13.4 Road Test Skills

The examiner watches **control** and **observation** (looking before you move).

**Leaving the curb.** Signal, check mirrors, turn your head to check your blind spot, then pull out when safe.

**Stops.** Come to a **complete stop**, wheels no longer rolling, behind the stop line, or before the crosswalk, or where you can see cross traffic. Behind another car, leave enough room to see its rear tires touching the road.

**Intersections.** Look left, right and ahead, turning your head. Never enter if you cannot get all the way through. At a green with nothing blocking you, check that the intersection is clear and go: an unneeded stop is an error.

**Turns.** Signal well ahead, slow down before the turn (not during it) and turn into the correct lane (Chapter 5). Check for bikes and pedestrians before you turn.

**Lane changes.** Signal, mirror, then a head check over your shoulder. A missing head check is an error even if the lane was clear.

**Backing.** Check all around the car, then turn your body and look over your right shoulder through the rear window. A backup camera does not show everything. Back slowly.

**Parking and turning around.** The test may include maneuvers such as parallel parking, parking on a hill, a three-point turn or pulling over to the curb (Chapters 5 and 8). A small correction is usually allowed, but hitting the curb hard, climbing it or hitting the cones or poles counts against you.

**Speed.** Drive at or near the posted limit when conditions allow. Driving far too slowly is an error too.

> **Remember it:** Signal, mirror, shoulder, then move. Use this order for every lane change, pull out and turn.

## 13.5 Common Mistakes and Automatic Fails

The errors examiners mark most often are rolling stops, a missing head check, swinging wide on turns, poor speed control and not yielding to pedestrians. Some errors fail you on their own, and the examiner may end the test on the spot:

| Error | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Examiner must step in | You were not in control |
| Hitting a car, object or person | A real crash risk |
| Driving onto the curb or sidewalk | A loss of control |
| Rolling through a stop sign or red light | Breaks the law |
| Not yielding to a pedestrian | Puts someone in danger |
| Passing a stopped school bus | Children may be crossing |
| Not pulling over for an emergency vehicle | Breaks the law |
| Speeding, or driving far too slow | Unsafe for traffic |

**Examiner intervention** means the examiner had to grab the wheel, use a dual brake or tell you to stop. The test is then usually over. A **rolling stop**, slowing to a crawl without stopping, is one of the most common reasons to lose points or fail.

> **Test trap:** An examiner's direction never makes an illegal move legal. Told to turn right at a light with a No Turn on Red sign, wait for the green.

> **Road tip:** If you miss a turn, do not swerve or brake hard. Keep driving safely and let the examiner give you a new route. If you make a small mistake, let it go and focus on the next block.

## 13.6 After the Test

**If you pass**, you have earned your license; Chapter 1 explains how you receive it. Teens move on to the next stage of the **graduated driver license**, with its limits on night driving and passengers (Chapter 1). [[generic]]In many states you leave with a temporary paper license, and the card comes later.[[/generic]]

**If you do not pass**, read the score sheet, ask what went wrong and practice those exact errors before you retest. Chapter 1 and the box in 13.3 give any waiting period your state sets.

## Quick Review

- Pack documents, permit, glasses, fee, registration and insurance the night before.
- Read every question to the end, watch for NOT and EXCEPT, and rule out unsafe answers.
- Signal, mirror, shoulder, then move; stop completely behind the line.
- Look over your right shoulder to back up; a camera is not enough.
- If the examiner has to step in, the test is usually over; if you fail, practice what the score sheet shows.
