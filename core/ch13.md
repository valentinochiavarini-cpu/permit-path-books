# Chapter 13: Test Day: Knowledge Test and Road Test

You already know more than you think. If you have worked through the chapters and quizzes in this book, the knowledge test will feel familiar. What trips people up on test day is rarely a gap in knowledge. It is rushing, misreading a question, forgetting a document, or freezing when an examiner sits down beside them.

This chapter is about the day itself. It covers how to use the last week, how to work through the questions on screen, what to have in your bag, and what happens on the road test from the moment the examiner walks up to your car. Each state runs its tests a little differently, but the core is the same everywhere: the examiner wants to see a driver who is safe, legal and in control.

Read this chapter twice: once a week before your knowledge test, and again a few days before your road test.

> **On the test**
>
> - The knowledge test checks signs, right-of-way, speed, space, alcohol and phone laws, and your state's own rules.
> - The road test starts with a check of your car and its controls before you drive.
> - Examiners score stops, turns, lane changes, backing, parking and how you watch traffic.
> - Some errors end the road test at once, such as a rolling stop or the examiner having to step in.
> - Both tests have state rules for retakes; Chapter 1 and the box in this chapter give yours.

## 13.1 The Last Week Before the Knowledge Test

Cramming the night before does not work well for this test. The questions are short, but many of them turn on one word or one number. Spread your review over several days so the details stick.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 7 to 5 days out | Take Mock Exam 1 cold. Mark every miss. |
| 4 to 3 days out | Reread the sections behind your misses. Drill signs and numbers. |
| 2 days out | Take Mock Exam 2. Review only what you missed. |
| Day before | Light review of signs and state numbers. Pack your documents. Sleep. |

### Study your misses, not your strengths

It feels good to reread the chapters you already know. It does not raise your score. After each mock exam, write down every question you missed and the reason: did you not know the rule, or did you misread the question? Those are two different problems. The first needs study. The second needs slower reading.

### Make a numbers sheet

Many wrong answers on the knowledge test come from numbers: speed limits, following distance, how far before a turn to signal, BAC limits, parking distances, passenger limits for new drivers. Many of these differ by state, so take them from Chapter 1 and the state boxes in each chapter, not from memory or the internet. Write them on one page. Read it each day this week.

### Drill signs until they are automatic

Sign questions are the easiest points on the test, and some states give a separate sign test you must pass on its own. Go back to Chapter 2 and quiz yourself on shape and color first. The online flashcards show the signs in full color, which helps because the test screen will too.

> **Road tip:** Sleep matters more than one last hour of study. A rested brain reads questions more carefully, and careful reading is where most points are won or lost.

## 13.2 Tactics in the Testing Room

The knowledge test is usually taken on a computer screen at a licensing office, though some states still use paper or allow some applicants to test online. You will often take a short vision test first. Phones, notes and talking are not allowed once the test starts, and using them can end your test.

### Read the whole question

Read the question to the end before you look at the answers. Watch for small words that flip the meaning: **not**, **except**, **always**, **never**, **only**, **first**. "Which of these is NOT a reason to slow down?" asks the opposite of what a quick reader expects.

Then read all four answers, even if the first one looks right. Test writers often put a close but wrong answer early.

### Rule out what you know is wrong

If you are unsure, cross off the answers you know are wrong. Most questions have one or two choices that break a basic safety rule. Removing them turns a guess between four into a choice between two.

Answers that ask you to speed up to get past a hazard, to go before you can see, or to assume another driver will do the right thing are almost never correct. The test rewards the safe, patient choice.

### Do not overthink it

Each question is written to have one clear answer based on the official manual. If you catch yourself building a story ("but what if the other car is speeding?"), stop. Answer the question as written, using the rule as the manual states it.

Change an answer only when you find a real reason, such as a word you misread. Second guesses made out of nerves are more often wrong than right.

### Manage your pace

Most knowledge tests give you more time than you need. If your test lets you skip a question and come back, use that for the hard ones. Some states end the test as soon as you reach the pass mark, or as soon as you have missed too many to pass. Do not panic if the screen stops early; read the result.

> **Test trap:** A question may describe a situation that seems to need a judgment call, but the answer is still a rule from the manual. When two answers both sound reasonable, pick the one that matches the rule word for word, not the one you have seen other drivers do.

## 13.3 What to Bring

Every state has its own list, and Chapter 1 gives yours. In general, plan to bring:

- **Proof of identity** and other documents your state asks for, usually originals, not copies.
- **Your learner permit** for the road test, as a physical card if your state requires it.
- **Glasses or contact lenses** if you need them to see. If you pass the vision test wearing them, your license will likely say you must drive with them.
- **The fee**, in a form the office accepts.
- **A parent or guardian** if you are under 18 and your state requires their signature or presence.
- **Forms and certificates** your state asks for, such as a driver education certificate or a signed log of supervised driving hours.

For the road test, you also need a car and, in most states, a licensed adult to bring you there. Bring the car's **current registration** and **proof of insurance**. If either is missing or expired, most testing offices will not start the test.

Check the night before whether you need an appointment, what time you must arrive, and whether the office has posted any closings. Many offices ask you to arrive early. Arriving late can cost you your slot.

## 13.4 The Road Test: How It Works

The **road test** (also called the driving test, skills test or behind the wheel test) is a drive on real roads, or on a closed course and then real roads, with a state examiner in the passenger seat. The examiner gives you directions, such as "At the next light, turn right." You follow them, and the examiner scores how you drive.

The examiner is not trying to trick you. Each instruction is given clearly and in plenty of time. If you did not hear or understand one, ask them to repeat it. That is never marked against you.

Friends and family usually cannot ride along; many states allow only you and the examiner in the car, and the box below says who may ride in yours. Both of you must wear seat belts for the whole drive.

### The vehicle check

Before you leave the lot, the examiner checks that the car is safe and legal. If it is not, most states cancel the test and you must reschedule. A failed vehicle check is the most avoidable way to lose a test day.

| Item | What the examiner checks |
|---|---|
| Papers | Current registration and insurance |
| Plates | Plates on the car as your state requires |
| Lights | Headlights, brake lights, turn signals front and back |
| Horn | It works |
| Brakes | Firm pedal; parking brake holds |
| Mirrors | Present and not cracked |
| Windshield | Clear view, no cracks in the way |
| Tires | Good tread, no bald spots |
| Doors and seats | Doors open from inside and out; seats secure |
| Seat belts | Every belt clean and working |

Do your own check a day or two before the test with a helper. Stand behind the car while your helper presses the brake and works each signal. A burned out brake light bulb is cheap to replace on Tuesday and very costly on Thursday.

### The controls check

The examiner may ask you to find and use the car's controls before you move. Expect to be asked about the headlights and high beams, turn signals, windshield wipers, defroster, emergency flashers (hazard lights), horn and parking brake. Practice this in the car you will test in until you can reach each one without looking for it.

If your car has self-parking or other driver assistance features, some states require you to turn them off for the test. Plan to do every maneuver yourself, and know how to switch those features off.

## 13.5 The Maneuvers Examiners Score

Each state uses its own score sheet, but the same skills show up on nearly all of them. The examiner watches two things in every maneuver: **control** (smooth steering, braking and speed) and **observation** (looking where you need to look before you move).

### Getting ready and leaving the curb

Adjust your seat and mirrors, fasten your belt, and check that your passenger has done the same before you start the engine. Hold the wheel with both hands, at about the 9 and 3 o'clock positions. Before you pull out, signal, check your mirrors, then turn your head to check your blind spot. Pull out only when there is a safe gap.

### Stops

Come to a **complete stop**, with the wheels no longer rolling, at every stop sign and red light. Stop behind the stop line. If there is no line, stop before the crosswalk. If there is no crosswalk, stop where you can see cross traffic, before you enter the intersection.

When you stop behind another car, leave enough room to see its rear tires touching the road. That gap lets you steer around it if it stalls.

Brake smoothly and early. A hard, jerky stop tells the examiner you were not watching far enough ahead.

### Intersections

As you approach any intersection, look left, right and ahead, and check again before you start moving. Turn your head; do not only move your eyes. Watch for pedestrians in the crosswalk. Never enter an intersection if you cannot get all the way through it before the light changes or traffic stops; blocking it is an error on most score sheets.

At a green light, keep going unless something blocks you. Stopping at a green light for no reason confuses other drivers and can cost you points.

### Turns

For each turn: check traffic, signal well before the turn, slow down before you turn (not during it), and turn into the correct lane. A right turn goes into the right lane. A left turn goes into the lane closest to the center that matches your direction. Keep both hands on the wheel through the turn and let it straighten smoothly. Chapter 5 covers each turn step by step.

The most common turn errors are a turn that swings too wide, cutting across the corner of the other lane, and forgetting to look for bikes and pedestrians on your right before a right turn.

### Lane changes

Signal, check your mirrors, turn your head to check your blind spot, then move over smoothly when there is a safe gap. Keep your speed steady as you move. Turn off the signal when you are done. Many score sheets mark an error when the head check is missing, even if the lane was clear. The examiner cannot see your eyes in your mirrors, so make the head turn clear.

### Backing

Most road tests include some backing, such as backing in a straight line for a short distance or backing into or out of a parking space. Before you back, check all around the car. Then turn your body and look over your right shoulder through the rear window. A backup camera can help, but it does not show everything, and many examiners want to see you looking back. Back slowly, at a walking pace or less.

### Parking and turning around

Depending on your state, the examiner may ask you to do some of these:

- **Parallel parking** between cones, poles or other cars.
- **Parking on a hill**, with your wheels turned the right way and the parking brake set.
- **Angle or perpendicular parking** in a lot.
- **A three-point turn** (turning the car around on a narrow street using forward and reverse).
- **Pulling over** to the curb and stopping, then pulling back into traffic.

Chapter 8 covers each one. Practice the ones your state tests until you can do them without hurry. Many states allow a small number of corrections in parking. Touching the curb hard, climbing it, or hitting a cone or pole counts against you.

### Speed and space

Drive at or near the posted limit when conditions allow. Driving too fast is an error. Driving far below the limit when the road is clear is often an error too, because it can block traffic. Keep a safe following distance (Chapter 6) and adjust it for weather and traffic.

> **Remember it:** Signal, mirror, shoulder, then move. Use this order for every lane change, every pull out from the curb and every turn. Examiners notice the shoulder check more than anything else.

## 13.6 Automatic Fails

Most road tests let you make a few small mistakes and still pass. Some mistakes are serious enough that one is enough to fail. Each state has its own list, and some states end the test on the spot. These are found on nearly every list:

| Error | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Examiner must step in | Shows you were not in control |
| Hitting a car, object or person | A real crash risk |
| Driving onto the curb or sidewalk | A loss of control |
| Rolling through a stop sign or red light | Breaks the law |
| Not yielding to a pedestrian | Puts someone in danger |
| Passing a stopped school bus with red lights flashing | Breaks the law; children may be crossing |
| Not pulling over for an emergency vehicle | Breaks the law |
| Speeding, or driving far too slow | Unsafe for traffic |
| Another driver must swerve or brake for you | You caused a hazard |
| No head check before a lane change or backing | You could hit what you did not see |
| Turning from the wrong lane | Unsafe and illegal |
| Refusing to try a maneuver | No chance to score you |

**Examiner intervention** means the examiner had to grab the wheel, use a dual brake, or tell you to stop to prevent a problem. If that happens, the drive is usually over.

A **rolling stop** is the classic road test fail. Many new drivers slow to a crawl at a stop sign and think it counts. It does not. Feel the car settle back after the wheels stop, count "one, two" in your head, then look and go.

> **Test trap:** Following the examiner's direction never makes an illegal move legal. If you are told to turn right at a light that has a No Turn on Red sign, wait for the green. The examiner is testing whether you see the sign.

## 13.7 Handling Nerves

Almost everyone is nervous on road test day. A little nerves keeps you alert. Too much makes your hands tight and your eyes narrow. These steps help:

- **Practice the test area.** If your state allows it, drive the streets near the testing office with your supervising driver in the days before. Learn the speed limits, signals and tricky corners. Do not practice on the test course itself if your state forbids it.
- **Practice in the test car.** Know where every control is and how the brakes feel.
- **Eat, and arrive early.** Hunger and rushing both make nerves worse.
- **Breathe slowly** while you wait. Breathe in for a count of four and out for a count of six. Slow breathing calms your body.
- **Talk if it helps.** Some drivers say what they see under their breath: "Stop sign ahead, checking left." That keeps your mind on the road. Ask your examiner first if you are unsure whether it is fine.
- **Let a mistake go.** If you make a small error, do not replay it. Put your attention on the next block. A small error is usually only a few points. Dwelling on it causes the next one.

> **Road tip:** If you are unsure about a direction, ask. If you miss a turn, do not swerve or brake hard to make it. Keep going safely and let the examiner give you a new route. A missed turn is a small thing. A sudden, unsafe move is not.

## 13.8 After the Test

### If you pass

The examiner tells you right away, or when you park. In most states you leave with a temporary paper license, and the card arrives by mail later. Keep the paper one with you whenever you drive until the card comes.

For a teen, passing usually means moving to the next stage of the **graduated driver license**: you can drive alone, but with limits, such as on night driving and on how many young passengers you can carry. Chapter 1 lists your state's limits. Breaking them can cost you your license, and they exist because crash risk is highest in the first months of driving alone.

### If you do not pass

Plenty of drivers fail a first road test and pass on a later one. The examiner will tell you, or show you on the score sheet, what went wrong. Read it carefully and ask questions. That sheet is a free lesson from an expert.

Most states make you wait a set number of days before you retest, may limit how many times you can test on one permit, and may charge a fee to retest. Your permit usually stays valid until it expires, so you can keep practicing with a supervising driver. Use the time to work on the exact errors on your sheet. Chapter 1 and the box in Section 13.4 give your state's wait and limits.

If you fail the knowledge test, the same idea applies. Most states let you retake it after a short wait. Go back to your list of misses, study those topics, and take the mock exams again before you return.

## Quick Review

- Spread your review over the last week; study the questions you missed, not the ones you know.
- Write your state's numbers on one page and read it daily.
- Read every question to the end, watch for NOT and EXCEPT, and rule out unsafe answers.
- Bring original documents, your permit, glasses if you need them, and the car's registration and insurance.
- Check your car's lights, signals, horn, brakes, tires and belts before test day; an unsafe car means no test.
- Signal, mirror, shoulder, then move, every time.
- Stop completely behind the line; a rolling stop is a common automatic fail.
- When backing, turn and look through the rear window; a camera does not show everything.
- If the examiner has to step in, the test is usually over.
- If you fail, use the score sheet as your study guide and try again after your state's wait.
