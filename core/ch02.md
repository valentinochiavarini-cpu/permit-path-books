# Chapter 2: Road Signs: Read Them at a Glance

At 40 mph your car covers about 60 feet every second. You do not have time to read a sign word by word. Good drivers know what a sign means from its shape and color before they can make out a single letter, and that is the skill this chapter builds.

Signs are also the easiest points on the knowledge test. Almost every state puts a block of sign questions on the exam, and some states run a separate sign test that you must pass on its own. The questions are short and the answers are fixed. Once you learn the system, you will not lose a point here.

The system is national. Every state follows the federal manual for signs, signals and road markings, or its own version that must closely match it, so a stop sign in one state looks like a stop sign in every other state. Learn it once and it works wherever you drive.

> **On the test**
>
> - Name a sign from its shape alone (octagon, triangle, circle, pennant, pentagon, diamond).
> - Say what a sign color means: red, yellow, orange, fluorescent yellow-green, green, blue, brown.
> - Tell a rule you must obey from a warning about what is ahead.
> - Know what to do at school zones, in work zones and when a flagger signals you.
> - Sort out look-alike signs: Do Not Enter and Wrong Way, Merge and Lane Ends, Divided Highway begins and ends.

## 2.1 Three Jobs a Sign Can Do

Every road sign does one of three jobs. Sort a sign into the right job and you already know how to respond.

- **Regulatory signs** state a law. Stop, Yield, Speed Limit and One Way are regulatory. Breaking one can get you a ticket.
- **Warning signs** tell you about a hazard or a change in the road ahead. They give you time to slow down and get ready.
- **Guide signs** tell you where you are and how to get where you are going: route numbers, exits, distances, services and points of interest.

| Job | What it tells you | Common look |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory | A rule you must obey | White rectangle, or red and white |
| Warning | A hazard ahead | Yellow diamond |
| Guide | Where to go | Green, blue or brown |

Two special groups borrow from these three jobs. **Work zone signs** warn about road work and are orange. **School signs** warn about children and school crossings and are fluorescent yellow-green. You will meet both later in the chapter.

## 2.2 Shape Comes First

You can see a sign's outline from far away, long before the words are clear, and at night your headlights pick up the reflective edge first. That is why the most important signs have a shape that no other sign is allowed to use.

| Shape | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Octagon (8 sides) | Stop | Stop sign |
| Triangle, point down | Yield | Yield sign |
| Circle | Railroad crossing ahead | Railroad advance warning |
| Pennant (sideways triangle) | No passing zone | No Passing Zone sign |
| Pentagon (5 sides, house shape) | School | School zone and school crossing |
| Crossbuck (white X) | Railroad crossing here | Railroad crossbuck |
| Diamond | Warning | Curve, merge, deer crossing |
| Vertical rectangle | Regulatory | Speed Limit, Keep Right |
| Horizontal rectangle | Mostly guide | Exit and distance signs |

The first six shapes in that table are reserved. If you see an octagon, it is a stop sign, even when snow covers the word STOP or the back of the sign faces you. If you see an upside down triangle, you must yield.

> **Remember it:** The octagon is the only eight-sided sign, and it always demands a full stop. A circle has no corners and gives you the earliest warning, because a train cannot stop for you.

## 2.3 Color Is the Second Clue

The print in this book is black and white, so each color is named in words. On the road, the color tells you the sign's job before you can read it.

| Color | General meaning |
|---|---|
| Red | Stop, yield or a prohibition |
| White | A rule (regulatory) |
| Yellow | General warning |
| Orange | Road work (temporary traffic control) |
| Fluorescent yellow-green | School, pedestrian, bicycle or playground warning |
| Fluorescent pink | Crash or incident scene ahead |
| Green | Direction, distance, movement allowed |
| Blue | Services for drivers |
| Brown | Parks, recreation and historic sites |
| Black | Letters and symbols; One Way signs |

Two colors are reserved and you are not likely to see them yet: coral and light blue. Purple shows up only in toll areas, on signs for lanes that only drivers with an electronic toll account may use.

> **Test trap:** A test question may describe a sign by color only, such as "What does an orange sign mean?" The answer is road work, not a general warning. Yellow is the general warning color. Orange is the work zone color.

## 2.4 Regulatory Signs: The Rules

A **regulatory sign** carries the force of law. Most are white rectangles with black letters. Red is used on regulatory signs that tell you to stop, to yield, or that something is not allowed.

### Stop and Yield

The **stop sign** has eight sides, white letters and a red background. Come to a complete stop with your wheels not rolling. Stop at the stop line if there is one. If there is no stop line, stop before the crosswalk. If there is no crosswalk either, stop at the point where you can see cross traffic, before you enter the intersection. Then go only when the way is clear.

A small red plaque under the stop sign that reads ALL WAY means every direction at this intersection has a stop sign. Chapter 4 covers who goes first at an all way stop.

A **yield sign** is a downward pointing triangle with a red border and a white center. Slow down and be ready to stop. Let traffic and pedestrians that are already in or close to the intersection go first. If the way is clear, you may keep moving without a full stop.

### Do Not Enter and Wrong Way

A **Do Not Enter** sign is a red circle with a white bar across it, on a white square. It means you may not go in from this direction. You will see it at the exit end of a freeway ramp and at the far end of a one way street.

A **Wrong Way** sign is a red rectangle with white letters. It often stands just past a Do Not Enter sign. If you see it facing you, you are already driving against traffic.

> **Road tip:** If you pass a Do Not Enter or Wrong Way sign by mistake, do not try to fix it at speed. Slow down, pull as far to the side as you safely can, and turn around only when no traffic is coming. At night, if headlights are coming at you in your lane, slow down and move toward the edge of the road.

### Speed Limit Signs

A **Speed Limit sign** is a white vertical rectangle with black letters. The number is the highest legal speed in good conditions. It is not a target. In rain, fog or heavy traffic, the safe speed is lower, and you can get a ticket for driving too fast for conditions even when you are under the posted number. Some highways also post a minimum speed on a white sign. Chapter 6 covers speed in detail.

### Turn, Lane and Movement Signs

A **red circle with a slash** through a black symbol means the action shown is not allowed. A black arrow curving left inside a red circle with a slash means no left turn. A U shaped arrow under the slash means no U-turn. A truck symbol means trucks may not use the road.

**Lane use signs** hang over the lanes or stand beside them, showing black arrows on white. Each arrow tells you which moves are allowed from that lane. If the sign says ONLY under an arrow, that lane must go that way. If you are in a left turn only lane, you must turn left even if you meant to go straight.

A **One Way** sign is a black rectangle with a white arrow. Traffic on that street moves only in the direction of the arrow.

A **Keep Right** sign is a white vertical rectangle showing an arrow passing to the right of a raised island. Stay to the right of the island or divider ahead.

### Passing Signs

A **Do Not Pass** sign is a white rectangle that marks the start of a stretch where you may not pass. A **Pass With Care** sign marks where that stretch ends. The **No Passing Zone** sign is the yellow pennant, and it stands on the left side of the road, where you will look when you think about pulling out to pass.

## 2.5 Warning Signs: What Is Coming Up

A **warning sign** is usually a yellow diamond with a black symbol or black words. It does not tell you what you must do. It tells you what is ahead so you can adjust your speed and position in time.

### Curves and Turns

A black arrow bent at a sharp right angle means a sharp turn. A gently bending arrow means a curve. A wavy arrow means a winding road with several curves. A small square yellow plaque with a speed, such as 25 MPH, may sit under the sign. This is an **advisory speed**: the top safe speed for the curve in good conditions. It is advice, not the legal limit, but taking the curve faster is a common way to lose control.

On the outside edge of a sharp curve you may see a row of **chevron** signs: yellow rectangles with a black arrowhead shape pointing in the direction the road bends. Follow them through the curve.

### Lanes and Merging

- **Merge:** a black line joins your road from the side. Traffic from another road will enter your lane. Be ready to adjust speed and make room.
- **Lane Ends:** your road narrows, often shown as one line bending in toward the other. One lane is about to end and its drivers must merge.
- **Added Lane:** a lane joins from the side but keeps going. No one has to merge.
- **Divided Highway:** the road splits around a median ahead. Traffic in the other direction will be on the far side of the divider.
- **Divided Highway Ends:** the median ends and the road becomes two way. Oncoming traffic will be beside you with no divider.
- **Two Way Traffic:** two arrows, one up and one down. You are leaving a one way road or a divided road, and traffic is coming the other way.

### Intersections and Controls Ahead

A black plus sign means a crossroad ahead. A T shape means the road you are on ends and you must turn left or right. A Y shape means the road splits. A side road symbol shows a road joining from one side only.

Some warning signs show the control that is coming: a small red octagon (stop ahead), a small red and white triangle (yield ahead), or a traffic light (signal ahead). They stand where a hill or curve hides the real control. Start slowing when you see one.

### Road Surface and Clearance

A car with wavy skid marks behind it means **slippery when wet**. Slow down in rain and avoid hard braking or sharp steering. A truck on a slope means a steep hill ahead: check your speed going down and use a lower gear if needed. A sign showing a height, such as 12 FT 6 IN, means **low clearance**: a bridge or overpass ahead is lower than normal. It matters when you drive a tall rental truck or carry a load on the roof.

### People, Bikes and Animals

A walking person means a **pedestrian crossing**. A bicycle means cyclists often cross or share the road. A running deer, a cow or a horse means animals may be on the road. Pedestrian, bicycle and playground signs may be yellow or fluorescent yellow-green, a bright lime color that is easier to see at dusk.

### Railroad Crossings

A round yellow sign with a black X and the letters RR is the **railroad advance warning** sign. It stands before the tracks and tells you to slow down, look and listen. At the tracks you will see the **crossbuck**: a white X-shaped sign with RAILROAD CROSSING in black letters. Treat the crossbuck like a yield sign. Be ready to stop, and never start across unless you can clear the tracks completely. A plaque under the crossbuck may show the number of tracks. Chapter 5 covers railroad crossings step by step.

> **Test trap:** Advisory speed plaques are yellow and sit under a warning sign. Speed Limit signs are white. If a test question asks which sign shows the maximum legal speed, choose the white one.

## 2.6 School Zones

School signs use the **pentagon**, a five-sided sign shaped like a house with a pointed roof. It is fluorescent yellow-green with a black symbol of two children walking. On older roads you may still see a plain yellow version.

- The pentagon alone, at the start of a stretch, marks the beginning of a **school zone**.
- The pentagon with a small plaque below showing a **downward pointing arrow** marks the school crossing itself. The crosswalk is right there.
- A **School Speed Limit** sign is a white Speed Limit sign with a SCHOOL plaque above it. A plaque below tells you when the lower limit applies, such as WHEN CHILDREN ARE PRESENT, WHEN FLASHING (with yellow lights that flash during school hours), or a list of times.
- An **End School Zone** sign, where one is posted, marks where the zone ends. Return to the normal limit only after you pass it.

A **crossing guard** is an adult in a bright safety vest who stands in the road with a handheld STOP paddle to stop traffic so children can cross. Obey a crossing guard the same way you obey a stop sign. Stay stopped until the guard and every child are out of the road and the guard signals you on.

## 2.7 Work Zones

A **work zone** is any stretch where crews are building, repairing or inspecting the road. Work zone signs are orange. Most are orange diamonds with black letters or symbols, and the first one you see is usually ROAD WORK AHEAD. Others warn of a lane closed ahead, a detour, a flagger ahead, or tell you to be prepared to stop. An END ROAD WORK sign marks where the zone finishes.

Slow down when you see the first orange sign, not when you see the workers. Lanes shift, the pavement can be rough, and traffic may stop with little notice. Rear end crashes are a large share of work zone crashes, so leave extra room in front of you. Put your phone away and keep your eyes up.

### Flaggers

A **flagger** is a worker who directs traffic through the zone, usually with an eight-sided paddle on a pole. One face reads STOP in white letters on red. The other reads SLOW in black letters on orange.

- STOP toward you: stop and stay stopped until the flagger turns the paddle or waves you on.
- SLOW toward you: move ahead slowly and watch for workers and equipment.
- Inside the work zone, the flagger's directions come first. Follow them even if a sign or signal nearby seems to say something else.

### Channeling Devices

**Channeling devices** guide you through the zone and keep you out of the work area. They are orange, often with white reflective stripes.

- **Cones** and **tubular markers** (tall orange posts) line the edge of a lane.
- **Drums** are large orange barrels with white stripes.
- **Vertical panels** are narrow upright boards with orange and white diagonal stripes.
- **Barricades** are frames with one, two or three horizontal rails striped orange and white. A barricade across the road with a ROAD CLOSED sign means you may not drive past it.

The direction of the diagonal stripes points the way traffic should pass: the stripes slope down toward the side you should drive on.

### Arrow Boards

An **arrow board** is a large lighted panel on a trailer or truck. A flashing arrow pointing left or right means the lane ahead is closed: merge in the direction of the arrow early and smoothly. A double arrow pointing both ways means you may pass on either side. Lights flashing in the four corners, or a flashing diamond pattern, with no arrow, is the **caution mode**: work is on or near the shoulder or road ahead, and no lane merge is shown. Slow down, stay alert and follow any other signs or flaggers.

### Incident Signs

A fluorescent pink sign, such as one that reads INCIDENT AHEAD, means a crash or other emergency is blocking the road. Treat it like a work zone: slow down, watch for responders and be ready to stop.

> **Remember it:** Orange means people are working. Pink means people are helping. Both mean slow down now.

## 2.8 Guide Signs: Finding Your Way

**Guide signs** tell you where you are, where the road goes and how far it is. Most are green rectangles with white letters.

### Route Markers

Highways are marked by number on route markers. An **Interstate** marker is a shield, colored red across the top and blue below, with the number in white. A **U.S. route** marker is a white shield with black numbers. **State route** markers use a shape each state picks, and **county routes** may use a blue pentagon.

Interstate numbers follow a pattern you can use to find your direction:

- Odd numbers (5, 35, 95) run mostly north and south.
- Even numbers (10, 40, 80) run mostly east and west.
- A three-digit number is a branch of the main route named by its last two digits. If the first digit is even, it usually loops around a city and rejoins the main route. If the first digit is odd, it usually branches into a city and stops.

### Exits and Mile Markers

Freeway guide signs give the exit number, the road you can reach and the direction. In most states, exit numbers match the mile markers: Exit 47 is near mile 47. Small green **mile markers** along the shoulder count the miles from the state line or the start of the route. They help you tell a 911 dispatcher exactly where you are.

### Services and Recreation

**Blue signs** point to services: gas, food, lodging, rest areas, phone, and hospitals (a white H on blue). **Brown signs** point to parks, campgrounds, historic sites and recreation areas.

## 2.9 Signs That Trip Up Test Takers

These pairs look alike or sound alike. Test writers like them for that reason.

| Signs | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|
| Do Not Enter vs Wrong Way | Do Not Enter: do not go in; Wrong Way: you are already going against traffic |
| Merge vs Lane Ends | Merge: traffic joins your road; Lane Ends: your road loses a lane |
| Divided Highway vs Divided Highway Ends | Begins: a median splits the road; Ends: oncoming traffic, no divider |
| Keep Right vs Divided Highway | Keep Right is a white rule; Divided Highway is a yellow warning |
| Advisory speed vs Speed Limit | Yellow advice under a warning sign; white legal limit |
| Railroad advance warning vs crossbuck | Round yellow sign before the tracks; white X at the tracks |
| Do Not Pass vs No Passing Zone | White rectangle on the right; yellow pennant on the left |
| Orange vs yellow diamond | Orange is road work; yellow is a general warning |

> **Test trap:** A test picture of the school sign may be plain yellow instead of fluorescent yellow-green, because older signs are still standing. Let the shape decide. Five sides like a house means school, whatever the shade.

## Quick Review

- Regulatory signs state a rule, warning signs flag a hazard, and guide signs show the way.
- An octagon is always stop; a downward pointing triangle is always yield.
- A circle means a railroad crossing is ahead; the white X crossbuck is at the tracks.
- The yellow pennant on the left side of the road marks a no passing zone.
- The pentagon marks school zones and school crossings; obey crossing guards like a stop sign.
- Orange means road work; fluorescent pink means an incident scene; slow down for both.
- Obey a flagger's STOP and SLOW paddle over other signs and signals.
- Green guides you, blue points to services, brown points to recreation.
- Odd Interstate numbers run north and south; even numbers run east and west.
