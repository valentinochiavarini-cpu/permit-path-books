# Chapter 2: Road Signs: Read Them at a Glance

At 40 mph your car covers about 60 feet every second, so learn to read a sign from its shape and color before you can make out a letter. Sign questions are part of every knowledge test, often with a picture of the sign. Signs follow one national standard, so what you learn here works in any state.

> **On the test**
>
> - Name a sign from its shape alone (octagon, triangle, circle, pennant, pentagon, diamond).
> - Say what a sign color means: red, yellow, orange, fluorescent yellow-green, green, blue, brown.
> - Tell a rule you must obey from a warning about what is ahead.
> - Know what to do at school zones, in work zones and when a flagger signals you.
> - Sort out look-alike signs: Do Not Enter and Wrong Way, Merge and Lane Ends, Divided Highway begins and ends.
> - Name a sign from a picture: large arrows, side roads, bridges, shoulders, flagger and worker symbols.

## 2.1 Three Jobs a Sign Can Do

Every road sign does one of three jobs, and the job tells you how to respond.

- **Regulatory signs** state a law you must obey, such as Stop, Yield, Speed Limit and One Way.
- **Warning signs** tell you about a hazard or a change in the road ahead.
- **Guide signs** show where you are and how to get where you are going: route numbers, exits, distances, services and points of interest.

Two special groups have their own colors: orange work zone signs and fluorescent yellow-green school signs.

## 2.2 Shape Comes First

You can see a sign's outline long before the words are clear, even at night, so the most important signs have a shape no other sign may use. Six shapes are reserved: the octagon for stop, the downward pointing triangle for yield, the circle for a railroad crossing ahead, the pennant for a no passing zone, the pentagon for school, and the white X crossbuck for the railroad crossing itself. The diamond means warning. Rectangles are used for rules and guide signs. The figure shows each shape with its usual color.

![Each reserved shape has one meaning: the red octagon is stop, the red and white triangle is yield, the yellow circle is a railroad ahead, the yellow pennant is a no passing zone and the yellow-green pentagon is school.](fig/signs_shapes.svg)

If you see an octagon, it is a stop sign, even when snow covers the word STOP or the back of the sign faces you.

## 2.3 Color Is the Second Clue

The print in this book is black and white, so colors are named in words.

| Color | General meaning |
|---|---|
| Red | Stop, yield or a prohibition |
| White | A rule (regulatory) |
| Yellow | General warning |
| Orange | Road work (temporary traffic control) |
| Fluorescent yellow-green | School, pedestrian, bicycle or playground warning |
| Fluorescent pink | Crash or incident scene ahead |
| Green | Direction, distance, movement allowed |
| Blue | Services for drivers |
| Brown | Parks, recreation and historic sites |
| Black | Letters and symbols; One Way signs |

> **Test trap:** A question may describe a sign by color only, such as "What does an orange sign mean?" The answer is road work. Yellow is the general warning color; orange is the work zone color.

## 2.4 Regulatory Signs: The Rules

Most **regulatory signs** are white rectangles with black letters. Red marks stop, yield and things that are not allowed. Know these six.

![The six regulatory signs you must know on sight: the red octagon Stop, the red and white Yield triangle, the white Speed Limit sign, the red Do Not Enter circle, the red Wrong Way rectangle and the black One Way sign.](fig/signs_regulatory_core.svg)

**Stop (red octagon, white letters).** Come to a complete stop with your wheels not rolling. Stop at the stop line. If there is none, stop before the crosswalk. If there is no crosswalk either, stop where you can see cross traffic, before you enter the intersection. If your view is still blocked after the stop, creep forward slowly until you can see. Go only when the way is clear. A red ALL WAY plaque under the sign means every direction stops (Chapter 4).

**Yield (downward triangle, red border, white center).** Slow down and be ready to stop. Let traffic and pedestrians already in or close to the intersection go first. If the way is clear, you may keep moving without a full stop.

**Do Not Enter (red circle, white bar) and Wrong Way (red rectangle, white letters).** Do Not Enter means you may not go in from this direction. You will see it at the exit end of a freeway ramp and at the far end of a one way street. A Wrong Way sign often stands just past it. If one faces you, you are already driving against traffic: slow down, pull as far to the side as you safely can, and turn around only when no traffic is coming.

**Speed Limit (white rectangle, black letters).** The number is the highest legal speed in good conditions, not a target. In rain, fog or heavy traffic you can be ticketed for driving too fast for conditions even under it (Chapter 6).

**One Way (black rectangle, white arrow).** Traffic on that street moves only in the direction of the arrow.

### Turn, Lane and Passing Signs

These signs set which moves are allowed and where you may pass.

![A red circle and slash forbids the move shown; white signs set lane and passing rules; the yellow No Passing Zone pennant stands on the left side of the road.](fig/signs_regulatory_more.svg)

A **red circle with a slash** means the action shown inside is not allowed: a left turn, a U-turn, or trucks on the road. **Lane use signs** show black arrows on white for the moves allowed from each lane. If ONLY is under an arrow, that lane must go that way, even if you meant to go straight. **Keep Right**, a white sign, means stay to the right of the island or divider ahead.

A white **Do Not Pass** sign on the right side marks the start of a stretch where you may not pass, and a **Pass With Care** sign marks where it ends. The yellow **No Passing Zone** pennant stands on the left side of the road, where you look before pulling out to pass.

A few more rule signs come up often. No Parking, No Right Turn and No Turn on Red forbid the move shown, and Slower Traffic Keep Right means leave the left lane for passing.

![A red circle and slash bans parking or a right turn, a white sign can ban turning on red, and the red ALL WAY plaque under a stop sign means every direction stops; the white wheelchair symbol on blue reserves a space for a disability placard or plate (Chapter 8).](fig/signs_regulatory_extra.svg)

### Crossings, Plaques and Exit Lanes

A small white **plaque** under a stop or yield sign changes who must stop or yield. Read it before you act.

![A white Divided Highway Crossing sign, a yellow EXIT ONLY panel on a green sign, a white LEFT TURN YIELD ON sign with a green ball, and white plaques under the red stop and yield signs.](fig/signs_regulatory_lanes.svg)

| Sign | Shape and color | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Divided Highway Crossing | White rectangle, two arrows crossed by a line | You will cross two separate roadways |
| EXIT ONLY | Yellow panel on a green sign | This lane leaves the freeway; stay in it and you must exit |
| Left Turn Yield on Green | White rectangle, green circle | On a green ball, turn left only after yielding to oncoming traffic |
| EXCEPT RIGHT TURN | White plaque under a stop sign | Drivers turning right need not stop; all others stop |
| TO ONCOMING TRAFFIC | White plaque under a yield sign | Yield to vehicles coming toward you, often at a one lane bridge |
| Center Lane Left Turn Only | White rectangle, two opposite turn arrows | The center lane is only for left turns, from both directions |

At a **divided highway crossing**, treat each roadway as its own street. On the first roadway, traffic comes only from your left, so you may turn right onto it. To go left, cross the first roadway and the median, then turn left onto the second roadway, where traffic comes from your right.

## 2.5 Warning Signs: What Is Coming Up

A **warning sign** is usually a yellow diamond with a black symbol or black words. It tells you what is ahead so you can adjust your speed and position in time.

![Warning signs are yellow with black symbols: read the symbol as a picture of the road ahead, and treat the square yellow speed plaque as advice, not the legal limit.](fig/signs_warning.svg)

**Curves.** A square yellow plaque with a speed, such as 25 MPH, is an **advisory speed**: the top safe speed for the curve in good conditions. It is advice; the legal limit is on the white Speed Limit sign. Going faster is still a common way to lose control. **Chevrons** on the outside of a sharp curve point the way the road bends.

**Lanes.** **Merge** means traffic from another road will enter your lane, so make room. **Lane Ends** means one lane is about to end and its drivers must merge. **Divided Highway** means a median will split the road ahead. **Divided Highway Ends** means the median ends and oncoming traffic will be beside you with no divider. **Two Way Traffic** (one arrow up, one down) means you are leaving a one way or divided road and traffic is coming the other way.

**Intersections.** A plus sign is a crossroad, and a T means your road ends. A small red octagon, red and white triangle or traffic light warns of a stop, yield or signal hidden by a hill or curve: start slowing.

**Road surface.** A car with wavy skid marks means **slippery when wet**: slow down in rain and avoid hard braking or sharp steering. A height such as 12'-6" (12 feet 6 inches) means a **low clearance** bridge or overpass ahead.

**People and animals.** A walking person, bicycle or animal means they may be on the road. Pedestrian, bicycle and playground signs may be yellow or fluorescent yellow-green.

The figure adds more symbols you may meet, from a winding road to the yellow EXIT speed sign, which is advice for the ramp like the curve plaque.

![More yellow warning signs: read each symbol as a picture of the road ahead, and treat the yellow EXIT 25 MPH sign as advice, not the legal limit.](fig/signs_warning_more.svg)

**Railroads.** The round yellow **railroad advance warning** sign, with a black X and the letters RR, stands before the tracks: slow down, look and listen. At the tracks, the **crossbuck** is a white X-shaped sign with RAILROAD CROSSING in black letters. Treat it like a yield sign, and never start across unless you can clear the tracks completely (Chapter 5).

### Road Shape and Intersections

A **turn** sign (an arrow with a right angle) warns of a sharper, slower bend than a **curve** sign (a smoothly bent arrow).

![Yellow warning signs for sharp turns and intersections: the large arrow and double arrow are yellow rectangles; the others are yellow diamonds.](fig/signs_warning_extra.svg)

| Sign | Shape and color | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Large Arrow | Yellow rectangle, one big arrow | The road turns sharply the way the arrow points |
| Double Arrow | Yellow rectangle, arrow both ways | Your road ends; turn left or right |
| Reverse Curve | Yellow diamond, S-shaped arrow | The road bends one way, then the other |
| Reverse Turn | Yellow diamond, arrow with two right angles | Two sharp turns close together |
| Side Road | Yellow diamond, a line joins from one side | A road enters from that side; watch for cars |
| Y Intersection | Yellow diamond, Y shape | The road splits into two |
| Circular Intersection | Yellow diamond, three arrows in a circle | A roundabout is ahead (Chapter 5) |
| Truck Rollover | Yellow diamond, tipping truck | A curve where trucks have tipped; slow down |

### Narrow Roads, Shoulders and Dead Ends

These yellow diamonds warn about the road's width and edges.

![Yellow warning diamonds for narrow roads and bridges, a low shoulder, a shoulder drop-off with its yellow plaque, horse-drawn vehicles and roads with no way through.](fig/signs_warning_road.svg)

| Sign | Shape and color | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Road Narrows | Yellow diamond, words | The road gets narrower; watch the traffic beside you |
| Narrow Bridge | Yellow diamond, words | The bridge is narrower than the road; slow down |
| One Lane Bridge | Yellow diamond, words | Room for one vehicle at a time; yield to a car already on it |
| Low Shoulder | Yellow diamond, words | The shoulder sits a little lower than the road |
| Shoulder Drop-Off | Yellow diamond, tilted car, yellow plaque | The shoulder drops more than 3 inches |
| Horse-Drawn Vehicle | Yellow diamond, horse and buggy | Slow buggies use the road (Chapter 9) |
| Dead End | Yellow diamond, words | The road ends with no way through |
| No Outlet | Yellow diamond, words | The streets ahead have only one way out |

A drop at the edge can pull a wheel off the road. Chapter 10 explains how to steer back on safely.

## 2.6 School Zones

School signs use the **pentagon**, a five-sided sign shaped like a house with a pointed roof, in fluorescent yellow-green with a black symbol of two children walking. Older signs may be plain yellow; five sides still means school. Each sign in the figure marks a different part of the zone.

![The fluorescent yellow-green pentagon starts a school zone; with a downward arrow plaque it marks the crossing itself, and the white School Speed Limit sign carries a plaque that says when the limit applies (your state sets the number).](fig/signs_school.svg)

- The pentagon alone marks the beginning of a **school zone**.
- With a small plaque below showing a **downward pointing arrow**, it marks the school crossing itself.
- A **School Speed Limit** sign has a SCHOOL plaque above and a plaque below saying when the lower limit applies: WHEN CHILDREN ARE PRESENT, WHEN FLASHING (while its yellow lights flash during school hours), or a list of times.
- An **End School Zone** sign, where one is posted, marks where the zone ends. Return to the normal limit only after you pass it.

A **crossing guard** stands in the road with a handheld STOP paddle so children can cross. Obey a crossing guard like a stop sign: stay stopped until the guard and every child are out of the road and the guard signals you on. Never pass a vehicle stopped at a crosswalk.

## 2.7 Work Zones

Work zone signs are orange, mostly diamonds with black letters or symbols. The first is usually ROAD WORK AHEAD; END ROAD WORK marks the end. Slow down at the first orange sign, not when you see the workers. Rear end crashes are a large share of work zone crashes, so leave extra room in front of you.

Inside a work zone:

- Yield to workers, flaggers and work vehicles, and obey every flagger's signal.
- Where a solid white line separates the lanes, stay in your lane.
- Do not pass a slow moving work vehicle, such as a line painting truck, unless a flagger or sign tells you to.
- Expect higher penalties: work zone fines are often doubled.

The figure shows the work zone devices you must know.

![Orange means road work: obey the flagger's red STOP or orange SLOW paddle, pass a striped panel on the side the stripes slope down to, merge the way a flashing arrow points, and slow down for a pink incident sign.](fig/signs_work_zone.svg)

**Flaggers.** A **flagger** directs traffic with a paddle: STOP in white letters on red, SLOW in black letters on orange. STOP toward you: stop and stay stopped until the flagger turns the paddle or waves you on. SLOW toward you: move ahead slowly and watch for workers. Inside the work zone, the flagger's directions come first, even if a sign or signal nearby seems to say something else.

**Channeling devices** guide you through the zone and keep you out of the work area. They are orange, often with white stripes: cones, tubular markers, drums, vertical panels and barricades. A barricade across the road with a ROAD CLOSED sign means you may not drive past it. Diagonal stripes slope down toward the side you should drive on.

**Arrow boards.** A flashing arrow on this lighted panel means the lane ahead is closed: merge in the direction of the arrow early and smoothly. A double arrow means pass on either side. Flashing corner lights with no arrow mean **caution mode**: work is near the road ahead and no merge is shown, so slow down.

**Warning signs ahead of the work.** Orange signs tell you what the work zone holds before you reach it.

![Orange work zone signs: the flagger and worker symbols, a lane closed ahead, the DETOUR signs and the END ROAD WORK sign.](fig/signs_work_zone_more.svg)

| Sign | Shape and color | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Flagger Ahead | Orange diamond, person holding a paddle | Slow down and be ready to stop |
| Workers | Orange diamond, person with a shovel | People are working in or near the road |
| Lane Closed Ahead | Orange diamond, words | Merge into the open lane early |
| Detour | Orange diamond or rectangle | The road is closed; follow the DETOUR arrows |
| End Road Work | Orange rectangle | The work zone ends |

**Incident signs.** A fluorescent pink sign, such as INCIDENT AHEAD, means a crash or other emergency is blocking the road.

> **Remember it:** Orange means people are working. Pink means people are helping. Both mean slow down now.

## 2.8 Guide Signs: Finding Your Way

Most **guide signs** are green rectangles with white letters.

An **Interstate** marker is a red and blue shield. Odd Interstate numbers (5, 35, 95) run mostly north and south; even numbers (10, 40, 80) run mostly east and west.

On most freeways, exit numbers match the mile markers: Exit 47 is near mile 47. Small green **mile markers** on the shoulder help you tell a 911 dispatcher exactly where you are.

**Blue signs** point to services: gas, food, lodging, rest areas and hospitals. **Brown signs** point to parks, historic sites and recreation areas.

A U.S. route marker is a white shield with a black number, and each state designs its own route marker.

![Route markers, the green exit sign with its exit number tab and the green mile marker show where you are; blue signs show services and brown signs show recreation.](fig/signs_guide.svg)

## Quick Review

- Regulatory signs state a rule, warning signs flag a hazard, guide signs show the way.
- Octagon is stop, downward triangle is yield, circle is railroad ahead, pennant is no passing.
- The pentagon means school; obey crossing guards like a stop sign.
- Orange means road work, pink an incident; obey the flagger, yield to workers, never pass a moving work vehicle.
- Blue points to services, brown to recreation; odd Interstates run north and south.
