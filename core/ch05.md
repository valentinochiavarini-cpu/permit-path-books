# Chapter 5: Turns, Roundabouts and Railroad Crossings

Turning is a series of small jobs done in order: check, signal, slow, look, steer, and pick the correct lane. Most intersection crashes happen when one of those jobs gets skipped.

> **On the test**
>

> - Where U-turns are not allowed, and the three-point turn.
> - How to use signals, brake lights, flashers and the horn to talk to other drivers.
> - Roundabouts, arm signals and railroad crossings.

## 5.1 Every Turn, Step by Step

Use the same routine for every turn.

1. **Plan early.** Check mirrors and your blind spot, then move into the correct lane well ahead of the intersection.
2. **Signal** before you start slowing down, so drivers behind you know why you are braking. The box below gives the minimum distance. Do not signal so early that drivers think you are turning into an earlier driveway.
3. **Slow down** while your wheels are still straight. Braking in the middle of a turn can upset the car.
4. **Look** left, right, ahead and left again, including both crosswalks.
5. **Turn** smoothly at a low speed, looking where you want the car to go, not at the curb.
6. **Finish in the correct lane** and make sure your signal turns off.

## 5.2 Right Turns

For a **right turn**, start from the rightmost lane, or from the right turn lane if there is one. Stay close to the curb as you turn, without riding over it, and finish in the right lane of the new road.

Avoid **swinging wide**, drifting left before the turn or across lanes after it. A car or a bicycle may slip into the gap on your right. Before you start, look over your right shoulder for bicycles, scooters and motorcycles beside you.

On every turn, your rear wheels follow a shorter path than your front wheels and cut inside them. This is **off-tracking**. Do not start a right turn so tight that your back tire rides over the curb. Long trucks and buses off-track far more, which is why they swing wide.

### Turning Across a Bike Lane

A **bike lane** is marked by a solid white line and a bicycle symbol. When you turn right across one, cyclists riding straight in it have the right-of-way. Signal early, look over your shoulder, and let any cyclist pass before you turn.

[[generic]]Where the bike lane line changes from solid to broken near the corner, some states let you, or even require you to, move into the bike lane just before your turn, after yielding to cyclists.[[/generic]] Where the bike lane line stays solid up to the corner, turn from your own lane. Never use the bike lane to pass.

## 5.3 Left Turns

For a **left turn**, start from the lane next to the center line, or from the left turn lane if there is one. Turn into the lane just right of the center line on the new road. Do not cut the corner, or you will enter the lane of cars waiting to turn left from the other road.

### Waiting to Turn Left

On a green light without a green arrow, oncoming cars may keep coming. Pull forward to wait only if no one ahead of you is turning left. Keep your wheels pointed straight while you wait. If a car hits you from behind while your wheels are turned, you will be pushed into oncoming traffic.

Do not turn just because an oncoming driver has a right turn signal on. Wait until you can see that the car is really turning.

> **Test trap:** Asked how your wheels should point while you wait to turn left, the answer is straight ahead, not turned to the left.

## 5.4 Turns on One Way Streets

The rule stays the same on a **one way street**: a left turn starts from the left side of the road you are on, which is the far left lane, and a right turn from the right.

| Turn | Start in | End in |
|---|---|---|
| Two way to one way, left | Lane nearest the center | Left lane of the one way street |
| One way to two way, left | Far left lane | Lane just right of the center line |
| One way to one way, left | Far left lane | Left lane |
| One way to one way, right | Far right lane | Right lane |

The figure shows the main paths.

## 5.5 Multiple Turn Lanes

When two or three lanes turn the same way, stay in your lane from start to finish: the left lane turns into the left lane, the next lane into the next lane. Pavement arrows and lane signs show which lanes may turn, and dotted lines often trace the path. Never change lanes in the middle of the turn; change only after it is done, with a signal.

## 5.6 U-Turns and Three-Point Turns

### U-Turns

Make a **U-turn** only where it is legal and safe, from the far left lane or the left turn lane, yielding to oncoming traffic and pedestrians.

U-turns are almost never allowed:

- where a NO U-TURN sign is posted
- on or near a curve or the top of a hill, where drivers cannot see you in time
- on a freeway or other limited access highway
- at or on a railroad crossing
- anywhere it would block or endanger traffic

### Three-Point Turns

A **three-point turn** (or **K turn**) turns your car around on a narrow street with no driveway or side road to use. Choose a spot with good sight lines, away from hills and curves.

1. Signal right and pull over to the right edge. Stop. Then signal left and check all directions, including your blind spot.
2. Turn the wheel sharply left and move forward slowly across the road. Stop before your front wheels touch the far curb or edge.
3. Check traffic again. Shift to reverse, turn the wheel sharply right, look over your shoulder and back up slowly. Stop before your rear wheels reach the curb or edge you started from.
4. Shift to drive, check traffic, turn the wheel left and pull forward into the lane going the other way.

## 5.7 Roundabouts

A **roundabout** is an intersection built as a ring around a raised island.

### How to Drive One

1. **Slow down** and read the signs for your exit and lane.
2. **Yield** to pedestrians in the crosswalk before the roundabout, then stop at the yield line if needed.
3. **Look left.** Traffic already in the roundabout has the right-of-way. Wait for a safe gap.
4. **Enter to the right** and travel **counterclockwise** around the island.
5. **Keep moving.** Do not stop inside the roundabout unless you must to avoid a crash. Do not pass anyone, and do not change lanes inside it.
6. **Signal right** just before your exit, and yield to pedestrians in the crosswalk as you leave.

If you miss your exit, go around again. Never back up or stop in the circle.

### Multi-Lane Roundabouts

In a roundabout with two lanes or more, choose your lane before you enter.

| Where you are going | Lane to enter |
|---|---|
| Right (first exit) | Right lane |
| Straight ahead | Either lane, as signs show |
| Left or back the way you came | Left lane |

Stay in your lane all the way around, and leave from the same lane you entered unless signs tell you otherwise. The figure shows both lanes at work.

![In a two lane roundabout, yield to people in the crosswalk, then to circling traffic coming from your left, and go counterclockwise: the right lane takes the first exit to the right, the left lane goes around to a left turn.](fig/roundabout_two_lane.svg)

### Large Vehicles and Emergency Vehicles

Large vehicles may roll their back wheels onto the **truck apron**, a raised strip around the island. Do not drive beside a large truck in a roundabout. It may need both lanes.

If an emergency vehicle comes while you are inside, do not stop. Leave at your exit, then pull over to the right. If you have not entered yet, pull to the right and let it through first.

> **Test trap:** In a roundabout you turn right to enter and keep the island on your left. Any answer that has you entering to the left, or yielding to cars still waiting to enter, is wrong.

## 5.8 Talking to Other Drivers

Other drivers cannot read your mind. Tell them what you plan to do, early and clearly.

- **Turn signals:** signal well before every turn, lane change or pull away from the curb, even when no one seems to be around. A signal left blinking after you finish tells others you are about to turn.
- **Brake lights:** before you slow down or stop where others may not expect it, such as for a driveway or a hazard only you can see, tap the brake pedal lightly a few times. The flashing brake lights warn the driver behind you.
- **Hazard flashers:** turn them on when your car is stopped on or beside the road, or is disabled and creeping to a safe place.
- **Horn:** a quick, light tap warns a child, a person walking or a driver who is about to move into your path without seeing you. Save a long, firm blast for real danger, such as a car about to hit you. Never honk out of anger, and do not honk near cyclists, horses or a blind pedestrian, where the noise can startle them.
- **Eye contact:** before you pull out in front of a driver, cyclist or person walking, try to catch their eye. If they have not looked at you, assume they have not seen you.

### Hand Signals

Drivers everywhere use the same three arm signals, given with your left arm out the driver's window, as the figure shows. Use them when your turn signals fail, and know them to read cyclists.

![Seen from behind, the driver's left arm straight out means a left turn, bent up means a right turn, and bent down means slowing or stopping.](fig/hand_signals.svg)

Many cyclists signal a right turn by holding the right arm straight out to the right. Treat that as a right turn signal too.

## 5.9 Railroad Crossings

A train cannot swerve and can need a mile or more to stop. At a crossing, the train always goes first.

### What You Will See

- **Advance warning:** a round yellow sign with a black X and RR, often with RR painted on your lane. Get ready to stop.
- **Crossbuck:** a white X-shaped sign at the tracks that works like a yield sign.
- **Flashing red lights:** a train is coming. Stop.
- **Gates:** red and white striped arms. Stay stopped until the gates are fully up and the lights have stopped flashing.

### Where to Stop

When you must stop for a train, the painted line is your stopping point. Without one, stop no closer than 15 feet from the nearest rail and no more than 50 feet away. The train hangs out past the rails by about three feet on each side.

![Approaching a crossing you pass the round yellow sign, then the white RXR marking on your lane, then the white stop line at least 15 feet from the nearest rail, before the crossbuck with its flashing red lights and red and white gate.](fig/railroad_crossing.svg)

### Crossing Safely

- Slow down, look both ways and listen.
- Never drive around lowered gates or race a train to the crossing.
- After one train passes on a crossing with more than one track, wait: a second train may be hidden behind the first.
- Do not stop on the tracks. Start across only when there is room on the far side for your whole car.
- Do not shift gears while on the tracks.

> **Remember it:** The train is always closer and faster than it looks. If lights flash or gates move, stop and stay stopped.

### If Your Car Stalls on the Tracks

1. **Get everyone out right away**, even if no train is in sight.
2. **Move away from the tracks.** If a train is coming, walk or run toward the train but away from the tracks at an angle, about 45 degrees. When a train hits a car, wreckage is thrown ahead, the same way the train is heading.
3. **Call the number on the blue sign.** The blue **Emergency Notification System** sign at public crossings shows a phone number and a crossing ID number. Call it so the railroad can warn trains. If there is no sign, call 911.

School buses and trucks carrying hazardous materials must stop at almost every crossing. If you are behind one, be ready to stop too.

## Quick Review

- Signal before you slow down, and slow down before you steer.
- Turn right into the right lane; turn left into the lane just right of the center line.
- Keep your wheels straight while you wait to turn left.
- Tap your brakes before an unexpected stop; tap the horn to warn, never in anger.
- In a roundabout, yield to circling traffic and travel counterclockwise.
- With no stop line, stop 15 to 50 feet from the nearest rail; if stalled, get out and move toward the train at an angle.
