## Unresolved

1. **Headlight dimming distances (ch10, C10-001 explanation).** "Most states set 500 feet for oncoming" and "commonly 200 to 300 feet" for following still rest on a secondary summary (YourMechanic); only Missouri was confirmed on a primary page (500 and 300). The wording is likely right, but a state-by-state primary check, or moving the figures to an insert, would remove the risk. CORE_SPEC has no insert key for this.
2. **Knowledge test ending early (ch13 13.2, C13-008).** No official state page was found that says the computer test stops once the result is certain (searched PA, CA, VA, GA, MD, CT, OR, NY). It is widely reported for several states but not verified. Chapter and question already say "some states" and "may". Owner may prefer to cut the sentence and C13-008 or confirm it during state fact checks.
3. **Head restraint height (ch12, C12-023).** Guidance differs by source: Mazda manuals say center even with the top of the ears (matches the text); Consumer Reports says the top should reach at least the top of the ears, preferably the top of the head; AARP says middle of restraint level with the middle of the head. The keyed answer is defensible against the distractors, so the text was left as written. IIHS page could not be loaded.
4. **Three types of distraction (ch11, C11-020) and teen passenger risk (ch11).** CDC page was blocked. Both are standard and widely published, but not confirmed on a primary page this session.
5. **"Opening a window or turning up the radio does not work" for drowsiness (ch11, C11-025 distractors).** Not confirmed on a primary page; standard manual advice.
6. **Bike lane entry before a right turn (ch09, "many states").** California's 200 feet rule could not be loaded (handbook page failed). Wording left as "many states"; low risk since it is permissive.
7. **Transit bus yield laws (ch09, "some states").** Not checked on statutes; wording is already hedged.
