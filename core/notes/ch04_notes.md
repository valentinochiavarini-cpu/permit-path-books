# Chapter 4 notes: Right-of-Way

Written 2026-10-27.

## Facts verified
- Uncontrolled intersections: first to arrive goes first; at the same time, yield to the vehicle on the right; all way stop same rule; left turners yield: California Driver Handbook, Laws and Rules of the Road (https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/california-driver-handbook/laws-and-rules-of-the-road/) and New York State Driver's Manual, Chapter 5 (https://dmv.ny.gov/new-york-state-drivers-manual-practice-tests/chapter-5-intersections-and-turns).
- T intersection without signs: through road has right-of-way: California handbook, Navigating the Roads.
- Driveways: stop and yield to pedestrians and road traffic; left turn yields to both directions; left turn into a driveway yields to oncoming traffic: New York manual Chapter 5.
- Do not enter an intersection unless you can clear it: New York manual Chapter 5.
- Blind pedestrians (white cane, guide dog) always have the right-of-way; do not honk; cane pulled back means go; quiet hybrid and electric cars: California handbook.
- Never pass a vehicle stopped at a crosswalk: New York manual Chapter 6.
- Emergency vehicles: pull to right edge and stop, clear intersection first, stop even if it is in the opposite lane of a two way road, pull over if you hear a siren and cannot locate it: New York manual Chapter 5.
- Move Over: all 50 states; move out of the adjacent lane or slow down; 19 states and DC extend it to any vehicle with flashing or hazard lights: NHTSA, https://www.nhtsa.gov/move-over-its-law
- School bus: yellow flashing = preparing to stop; red flashing and stop arm = stop until lights off, arm in and bus moving; passing illegal in every state: NHTSA, https://www.nhtsa.gov/road-safety/school-bus-safety. New York requires stopping even on a divided highway (Chapter 6), which confirms that the divided highway rule varies and belongs in the insert.

## From general knowledge, not fetched
- Facing vehicles at an all way stop, straight goes before left turn (follows from the left turn yield rule in both manuals).
- One way street: pull to nearest edge for emergency vehicles (several state manuals).
- Funeral procession right-of-way through intersections once the lead vehicle enters lawfully: common in state statutes; written as "many states". Florida and Pennsylvania manuals not fetched (site errors).
- Transit bus yield on re-entry: some states (for example Florida, Oregon, Washington by statute); written as "some states require".
- Stop arm is on the left (driver) side and is a red octagon: standard school bus equipment (FMVSS 131).
