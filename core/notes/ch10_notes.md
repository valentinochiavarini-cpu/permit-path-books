# Chapter 10 notes: Night, Weather and Emergencies

Written 2026-10-27.

## Facts verified
- Headlights from 30 minutes after sunset to 30 minutes before sunrise and when wipers are needed (MO); dim within 500 feet of oncoming and 300 feet when following (MO); headlights reveal about 350 feet; fog: low beams, pull off and use flashers if you cannot see; rain mixes with oil, slow down, no cruise control; hydroplaning can start above 35 mph, ease off the gas, do not brake hard; skids: off the gas, steer the way the rear is sliding; snow and ice: clear all windows and lights, 8 to 10 seconds following distance, bridges freeze first, 4WD does not help on ice; brake failure: pump, parking brake gradually, low gear, steer off; blowout: hold the wheel, ease off, brake when nearly stopped; stuck accelerator: neutral, brake, steer off, then turn off ignition; off road recovery. Missouri Driver Guide Chapter 9, https://dor.mo.gov/pdf/Chapter9.pdf
- Dimming distances by state: 500 feet oncoming in about 37 states plus DC, 1,000 feet in IA, MI, MN, MT, OK, none stated in several; following distance 150 to 600 feet, most commonly 200 or 300. Headlights with wipers in about 18 states; in rain or similar weather in about 10 more. YourMechanic state summary (secondary, used only to see the spread), https://yourmechanic.com/article/headlight-use-laws-for-all-50-states . The chapter states "most states set 500 feet for oncoming" and "commonly 200 to 300 feet" for following, with no single figure as universal.
- Flood water: 6 inches of fast water knocks over an adult, 12 inches carries away most cars, 2 feet carries away SUVs and trucks. NWS, https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood-turn-around-dont-drown
- Driver removal ("move it") laws require moving vehicles out of travel lanes in minor crashes with no injuries; some states have them. FHWA Quick Clearance Laws, https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop09005/exec_summary.htm . Written as "many states require this".

## Facts from general knowledge, primary page not fetched (confirm in the fact-check pass)
- About half of traffic deaths occur in the dark (NHTSA FARS; often cited as about 50 percent). Written as "about half".
- ABS: press hard, hold, do not pump, pulsing is normal (NHTSA ABS guidance).
- Turning off the engine while moving can lose power steering and brakes and may lock the steering: common manual wording.
- Engine fire: move about 100 feet away, do not open the hood: common manual and fire service advice.
- Wet brakes: dry by light braking at low speed.
- Carbon monoxide when stuck in snow: keep the exhaust clear.
- Duty to stop at every crash, exchange of information, note for an unattended vehicle: universal in state vehicle codes (Uniform Vehicle Code 10-102 to 10-105 pattern).
