# Chapter 11 notes: Alcohol, Drugs, Distraction and Fatigue

Written 2026-10-27.

## Facts verified
- 0.08 illegal in all 50 states, DC and Puerto Rico; Utah lower at 0.05. NHTSA: https://www.nhtsa.gov/risky-driving/drunk-driving ; Utah: https://highwaysafety.utah.gov/05-bac-law/
- No second state at 0.05 as of October 2026: Washington SB 5067 (0.05) failed in House committee, February 2026. https://www.kxly.com/news/wa-bill-to-lower-legal-bac-limit-to-0-05-fails-in-house-committee/article_93e4ca80-9dc1-45ed-8133-efcf284f7235.html
- BAC effects table (0.02 to 0.15) and crash risk (about 4x at 0.08, at least 12x at 0.15): NHTSA drunk driving page above. Deaths: about 32 per day, one every 44 minutes (chapter says "roughly one every 45 minutes").
- Minimum legal drinking age 21 in all states: NHTSA page above.
- Standard drink (12 oz beer 5 percent, 5 oz wine 12 percent, 1.5 oz 80 proof spirits; about 14 g alcohol): https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/alcohols-effects-health/what-standard-drink
- Texting at 55 mph for 5 seconds = a football field: https://www.nhtsa.gov/risky-driving/distracted-driving
- GHSA (https://www.ghsa.org/state-laws/issues/distracted-driving): 49 states ban texting for all drivers; 33 states ban handheld use for all drivers; 36 states and DC ban all phone use by novice drivers. Chapter says "nearly every", "many", "most".
- Drowsy driving: peaks midnight to 6 a.m. and late afternoon; micro-sleeps of 4 or 5 seconds cover more than 100 yards at 55 mph; caffeine alone not enough; 20 minute nap at a safe place; rumble strips as a sign: https://www.nhtsa.gov/risky-driving/drowsy-driving

## From general knowledge, not fetched this session (confirm in fact check)
- Zero tolerance in all states (federal requirement 23 U.S.C. 161 from the 1995 NHS Designation Act; all states complied by 1998). Usual limit 0.02 or lower; chapter gives no number, insert does.
- Commercial driver BAC 0.04 (49 CFR 382.201 / 383.51).
- Implied consent law in every state; refusal triggers administrative suspension in most states.
- Open container: chapter says "most states". Federal Section 154 (23 U.S.C. 154) pushes states to ban open containers; a few states do not fully comply (Mississippi is often cited as allowing drivers below the limit to drink). Keep "most".
- Liver clears roughly one standard drink per hour (written as "roughly ... for many people").
- Three types of distraction (visual, manual, cognitive): CDC distracted driving page; NHTSA page fetched did not list them.
- Teen crash risk rising with each teen passenger: IIHS/CDC teen driver research.
- "Opening a window or turning up the radio does not work": common in state manuals and NSF/NHTSA guidance; NHTSA page fetched does not state it.

## Rules that vary by state (insert keys)
- bac_limits: under 21 limit (0.00, 0.01 or 0.02), commercial limit wording, Utah 0.05.
- implied_consent: refusal penalties and suspension lengths.
- dui_penalties: offense name (DUI, DWI, OUI, OVI), fines, jail, suspension, interlock rules, open container rule.
- phone_laws: texting ban, handheld ban, novice driver ban, school zone and work zone rules, primary vs secondary enforcement.
