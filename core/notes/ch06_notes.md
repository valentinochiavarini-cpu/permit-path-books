# Chapter 6 notes: Speed and Space

Written 2026-10-27.

## Facts verified
- Following distance counted in seconds from a fixed object; when to add space (wet or unpaved roads, being passed or tailgated, trucks and buses blocking view, heavy loads and trailers, slow vehicles and bicycles, school buses and hazmat trucks, downhill); six-area space cushion; side space and escape paths; steer a middle course between equal hazards; avoid riding alongside other vehicles; space from parked cars; dealing with tailgaters (move right, or slow gently when the road ahead is clear); blind spots behind each shoulder, head check required, do not rely on mirrors or sensors; scan 12 to 15 seconds ahead (about a block in town, a quarter mile on the highway). PennDOT Driver's Manual, Managing Space: https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dmv/driver-services/pennsylvania-drivers-manual/online-drivers-manual/everyday-driving-skills/managing-space
- Signal and lane basics, turnouts for slow drivers with five or more vehicles behind (CA rule, used only as "pull into a turnout or safe wide spot"): California Driver Handbook, https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/california-driver-handbook/navigating-the-roads/
- Speed Limit sign black on white: MUTCD 11th ed., Section 2B.21 (see ch02 notes). Advisory speed plaque: MUTCD 2C.59.

## Facts from general knowledge, primary page not fetched (confirm in fact check)
- 60 mph = 88 feet per second (arithmetic).
- Perception plus reaction about 1 to 2 seconds for an alert driver; braking distance grows with the square of speed ("double the speed, four times the braking distance"). Standard physics and in many state manuals; no single state manual page fetched this session.
- "Roughly the length of a football field" to stop from highway speed on dry pavement: commonly quoted for about 55 to 60 mph (about 270 to 300 ft total). Soften if the fact check prefers.
- Scan range written as "10 to 15 seconds" (PA says 12 to 15; many manuals say 10 to 15 or 20 to 30 seconds on highways). Kept as a range.
- Left, right, left at intersections: standard in state manuals.
- Mirror setting (barely see own car in inner edge): common manual guidance.

## Rules that vary by state (covered by inserts)

- Required following distance in seconds (PA teaches 4 seconds; CA 3 seconds; NY 2 seconds; others 3 to 4): `following_distance`. The chapter gives no single number on purpose.

## Uncertain
- Driving below a posted minimum in bad weather: legal everywhere under the basic speed law as far as known, but phrasing of minimum speed statutes differs. Hazard lights while moving are banned in some states, so the chapter does not mention them in 6.3.
