# Chapter 8 notes: Parking, Backing and Leaving the Curb

Written 2026-10-27.

## Facts verified
- Hill parking: downhill wheels toward the curb; uphill wheels away from the curb and roll back until the tire touches; no curb wheels toward the side (right) so the car rolls away from the center; set parking brake and park. California Driver Handbook, https://dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/california-driver-handbook/navigating-the-roads-cont1/
- Curb colors red, yellow, white, green, blue and their meanings (same CA page). MUTCD 11th ed., Section 3B.18 lets local agencies set curb colors and says curb markings without signs should carry a word message; so colors are presented as "common meaning" with the state insert. https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/pdfs/11th_Edition/part3.pdf
- Parallel parking steps: space at least three feet longer, pull alongside about two feet out, align rear bumpers, back to about 45 degrees, straighten, within 18 inches of the curb (CA). Chapter says "two to three feet" out and gives the curb distance only as "often 12 or 18 inches" with the exact figure in the insert.
- Never park: crosswalk, sidewalk, in front of a driveway, near hydrant, double parked, wrong side, on a freeway except in emergency, tunnel or bridge unless permitted, crosshatched area beside disability space (CA). Distances (CA 15 ft hydrant, 20 ft crosswalk) not printed: they vary by state.
- Blue disability parking spaces, white parking space markings: MUTCD 3B.27.

## Facts from general knowledge, primary page not fetched (confirm in fact check)
- No Parking / No Standing / No Stopping definitions follow the Uniform Vehicle Code and New York usage. Some states use only No Parking. Kept general.
- Opening the door with the far hand ("Dutch reach"): safety guidance in several state manuals (for example Massachusetts, Illinois).
- Backing: turn the wheel the way you want the rear to go; look back over the right shoulder; front swings out while backing and turning. Standard manual content.
- Stop before crossing the sidewalk when leaving an alley or driveway: Uniform Vehicle Code 11-704 and most states; written as "most states".
- Angle and perpendicular parking setup distances (five to six feet from parked cars): common teaching guidance, not law.
