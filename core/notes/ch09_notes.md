# Chapter 9 notes: Sharing the Road

Written 2026-10-27.

## Facts verified
- Trucks and buses: no-zones, "if you cannot see the driver in the mirror, the driver cannot see you", wide right turns (may move left first), up to two football fields to stop, pass on the left, do not pass downhill, roll back on hills, wind gusts. FMCSA, https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/ourroads/tips-driving-safely-around-large-trucks-or-buses
- Do not squeeze between a turning truck and the curb; see the whole front of the truck in the rearview mirror before moving in; right side blind area is the largest. NY GTSC, https://trafficsafety.ny.gov/tips-commercial-motor-vehicle-cmv-drivers-and-passenger-cars
- Truck stopping distance vs car, truck wide turns, motorcycle full lane, never pass a motorcycle in your lane, check for motorcycles before opening a door, road maintenance vehicles with an orange and red triangle travel 25 mph or less, slow down near livestock and follow handler directions. California Driver's Handbook, https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/california-driver-handbook/laws-and-rules-of-the-road-cont1/
- Most multi-vehicle motorcycle crashes: other driver did not see the rider. NHTSA, https://www.nhtsa.gov/road-safety/motorcycle-safety
- Pedestrians: stop well back from the crosswalk, never pass a vehicle stopped at a crosswalk, extra care at night and when backing. NHTSA, https://www.nhtsa.gov/road-safety/pedestrian-safety
- Bicycles: yield as to motorists, do not underestimate their speed, pass with room and change lanes when safe, look for cyclists approaching from the right rear before a right turn. NHTSA, https://www.nhtsa.gov/road-safety/bicycle-safety
- Bicycle passing distance: 34 states plus DC have a 3 foot or greater minimum; PA and NJ 4 feet; NH and SD scale with speed; NC general rule is 2 feet; several states require a lane change when possible. NCSL, https://www.ncsl.org/transportation/safely-passing-bicyclists-chart . So the chapter says "most states set a minimum of at least 3 feet, and some require more or a lane change" and the figure goes in the insert.
- SMV emblem: fluorescent orange triangle, red retroreflective border, point up, for vehicles designed for 25 mph or less (ASABE S276). Ohio State Ag Safety, https://agsafety.osu.edu/smv-emblem
- Deer: groups, high risk at dusk and dawn, do not swerve, use high beams when no oncoming traffic. Missouri Driver Guide Chapter 8, https://dor.mo.gov/pdf/Chapter8.pdf
- 44 feet per second gain = 30 mph speed difference (55 minus 25); arithmetic.

## Facts from general knowledge, primary page not fetched (confirm in the fact-check pass)
- White cane laws exist in all 50 states (American Council of the Blind, https://acb.org/whitecane, not fetched). Cane pulled back means driver may go: common manual wording, confirm.
- Passing a vehicle stopped at a crosswalk is illegal in most states (Uniform Vehicle Code 11-502(d) pattern). Written as "most states".
- Sharrow definition (MUTCD 11th ed., Part 9; section number not confirmed; src written as Chapter 9E).
- Bike lane: entering before a right turn is allowed or required in many states (CA: within 200 feet). Written as "many states let or require you".
- Tractor trailer 20 to 30 times a car's weight (IIHS large truck page, not fetched).
- Some states require yielding to a transit bus re-entering traffic (for example FL, OR, WA). Written as "some states".
- Dutch reach (open the door with the far hand): safety advice, not law.
