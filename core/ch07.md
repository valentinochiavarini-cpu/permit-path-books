# Chapter 7: Lanes, Passing and Highway Driving

Lane changes, passing and freeways follow a few simple ideas: see the whole picture, signal early, move one lane at a time and never force your way in.

> **On the test**
>
> - Which lane to use, and the steps for a safe lane change.
> - Where passing is not allowed, when you may pass on the right, and what to do when you are passed.
> - Entering and leaving a freeway, weave lanes, and what to do if you break down.

## 7.1 Choosing a Lane

| Lane | Best use |
|---|---|
| Right lane | Slower traffic, entering and leaving, getting ready to turn right |
| Middle lanes | Steady through traffic |
| Left lane | Passing and getting ready to turn left |

The basic rule is **keep right except to pass**. Drive in the right lane, or the lanes to the right, unless you are passing, turning left or the right lane is ending or blocked. [[generic]]Some states make it a ticketable offense to stay in the left lane when you are not passing.[[/generic]] Do not weave from lane to lane to gain a few car lengths.

## 7.2 Changing Lanes Safely

1. **Check your mirrors**, inside and on the side you want to move to, and find a gap.
2. **Signal** early enough to warn the drivers around you: at least as far ahead as for a turn (Chapter 5), and several seconds ahead on a freeway.
3. **Head check.** Glance over your shoulder toward the lane you are entering to clear your blind spot.
4. **Move smoothly.** Keep your speed steady, or speed up a little, and steer gently into the center of the new lane. Do not slow down as you change lanes on a highway.
5. **Cancel your signal.**

If a car speeds up in the next lane during the move, stay where you are or ease back into your own lane.

To cross several lanes, move one lane at a time, checking and signaling each time. On a road with three or more lanes, a car two lanes away may move into the same open lane you want at the same moment, so look at the lane beyond your target lane as well.

A broken white line between lanes means you may change lanes when it is safe. A solid white line means changing lanes is discouraged. A **double solid white line** means you may not cross it at all. Chapter 3 covers these markings.

## 7.3 Passing on a Two-Lane Road

On a road with one lane each way, passing puts you in the lane of oncoming traffic.

### When You May Pass

Pass on the left only when all of these are true:

- The center line on your side is broken yellow, and no sign says passing is not allowed.
- You can see far enough ahead to finish the pass with plenty of room before any oncoming car reaches you.
- The left lane is clear well ahead and no one behind you has started to pass you.
- You can pass without going over the speed limit.

A pass takes longer than it looks. Even going about 10 mph faster than the other car, it can take 10 to 20 seconds, and the oncoming lane must stay clear the whole time. At 45 mph you need about a quarter mile of open road ahead. Pass one vehicle at a time and finish quickly.

### How to Pass

The figure shows the pass from start to finish.

![Pass only where the center line on your side is broken yellow, move fully into the left lane, and return when both headlights of the car you passed show in your inside mirror.](fig/passing_two_lane.svg)

1. Stay well back from the vehicle ahead so you can see down the road, and check for oncoming traffic, side roads and driveways.
2. Check your mirrors and blind spot, then signal left.
3. Move fully into the left lane and speed up smoothly.
4. When you can see both headlights of the car you passed in your inside mirror, you have room to return.
5. Signal right and move back into your lane. Do not slow down until you are back in your own lane. Cancel your signal.

If an oncoming car appears and you cannot finish in time, brake, drop back behind the car you were passing and return to your lane. Giving up on a pass is always better than forcing it.

### Where Passing Is Not Allowed

Do not pass in these places, even if no sign warns you:

- Where a solid yellow line is on your side of the center line.
- On a curve or near the top of a hill, where you cannot see far enough ahead.
- Within **100 feet** of an intersection or a railroad crossing.
- Within **100 feet** of a bridge, a viaduct or a tunnel.
- In a work zone where passing is not permitted.
- When a vehicle ahead has stopped at a crosswalk to let someone cross.
- When a school bus has stopped with its red lights flashing.

> **Test trap:** A broken yellow line does not override what you can see. If a curve or hill blocks your view of oncoming traffic, you may not pass.

## 7.4 Passing on the Right

Passing on the right is allowed only when you can do it safely and:

- The driver ahead is turning left and you can go around on the right without leaving the paved road, or
- You are on a road with two or more lanes going your way, such as a freeway or a one way street.

You may never drive onto the shoulder, a bike lane or the edge of the road to pass, unless a sign or an officer tells you to.

## 7.5 Being Passed

When another driver passes you, help them get by safely:

- Stay in your lane, keep a steady speed or slow down slightly. Never speed up while someone is passing you; it is against the law and it traps the passing driver in the oncoming lane.
- At night, switch your headlights to low beam as the passing car moves alongside, so you do not blind the driver.

If many cars are passing you on the right on a multi-lane road, you are in the wrong lane. Move right when it is safe.

## 7.6 Entering a Freeway

A **freeway** is a divided highway with no cross traffic. You enter and leave only at ramps called **interchanges**.

The **acceleration lane** at the end of the on-ramp gives you room to reach the speed of freeway traffic. The figure shows how to merge.

![Use the on-ramp to find a gap, reach the speed of traffic in the acceleration lane, then check your blind spot and move into the gap in the right lane.](fig/freeway_merge.svg)

1. On the ramp, look for gaps in freeway traffic as early as you can.
2. Signal, and in the acceleration lane speed up to match the traffic you are joining.
3. Pick your gap, check your blind spot and move smoothly into the right lane.
4. Cancel your signal and build a safe following distance.

Traffic already on the freeway usually has the right of way. Never use the acceleration lane to pass cars ahead of you on the ramp. Avoid stopping at the end of the acceleration lane. Joining traffic moving at 65 mph from a stop is very hard and dangerous. Stop only if there is truly no gap, then wait for a large one.

> **Test trap:** Merging slowly feels careful, but it is not. Reach the speed of traffic in the acceleration lane, then merge.

## 7.7 Driving on the Freeway

Keep up with the flow of traffic within the speed limit and leave a larger following gap than on city streets.

As you near an on-ramp, watch for cars entering. If it is safe, move one lane to the left to give them room. If you cannot, adjust your speed so they can fit in ahead of or behind you.

Long, straight drives can lull you into a dull, half-awake state called **highway hypnosis**: your eyes are open, but you notice less. Keep your eyes moving, check your mirrors often, take a break at least every two hours, and stop driving if your eyes start closing. After a long stretch at high speed, 45 mph can feel like a crawl, so check your speedometer on the exit ramp.

### Weave Lanes

At some interchanges, one lane serves both as an on-ramp and an off-ramp: drivers enter the freeway from it while others leave the freeway through it. In this **weave lane**, both must take turns. Entering drivers yield to traffic already on the freeway, including drivers moving over to exit. Exiting drivers signal early and move over smoothly. Never race another driver for the same gap.

### Truck Escape Ramps

On long, steep downgrades you may pass a lane of deep gravel or sand that runs off the side of the road, marked by a yellow RUNAWAY TRUCK RAMP sign. It stops trucks whose brakes have failed. **Never stop or park in it or at its entrance**: a runaway truck may need it at any moment.

## 7.8 Leaving a Freeway

Know your exit before you get on the freeway, and move into its lane early, one lane at a time.

Many exits have a **deceleration lane**, an extra lane on the right that leads to the off-ramp. Signal several seconds before the exit, move into the deceleration lane, and do your slowing down there, not in the freeway lane.

If you miss your exit, go on to the next one. Never stop, back up or turn around on a freeway, and never cut across the painted area between the ramp and the freeway.

> **Remember it:** Missed your exit? Take the next one. Never back up on a freeway.

## 7.9 HOV, Express and Toll Lanes

A **high occupancy vehicle (HOV) lane**, or carpool lane, is reserved for vehicles carrying a minimum number of people. It is marked with a white **diamond** painted on the pavement and with signs. HOV 2+ means at least two people must be in the vehicle, counting the driver. The signs also show the hours the rule applies; outside those hours, the lane may be open to everyone.

Some HOV lanes are separated by a barrier, a painted buffer or a double solid white line. Enter and leave only where the lines are broken or where signs show an opening. Buses may use HOV lanes, and so may motorcycles unless signs say otherwise.

An **express lane** runs next to the regular lanes and charges a toll, which may change with traffic; signs show the current price. In some, carpools ride free or pay less.

## 7.10 Breakdowns and Emergencies on the Highway

1. Turn on your **hazard lights** (the flashers).
2. Check your mirrors, signal and steer onto the right shoulder, as far from traffic as you can, or to an exit or a wide spot if you can reach it safely.
3. Stop with your wheels turned slightly away from traffic, set the parking brake and turn off the engine if the problem allows it.
4. Call for roadside assistance or, if anyone is hurt or the car is in a dangerous spot, call 911. Use mile markers and the last exit to say where you are.

In most breakdowns on a busy highway, staying in the car with your seat belt on is the safer choice. Get out only if the car is on fire, is smoking, is in a lane of traffic, or you can move to a safe place well away from the road, such as behind a guardrail. Leave through the door on the side away from traffic.

If it is safe, place warning triangles or flares behind your car.

If a tire blows out, hold the wheel firmly, keep the car straight, ease off the gas and do not brake hard. Steer to the shoulder once you have control. Chapter 10 covers other emergencies, and Chapter 4 covers the Move Over law.

## Quick Review

- Keep right except to pass; for a lane change, use mirrors, signal, head check, then move.
- Pass on the left only with a broken line on your side and a long, clear stretch ahead, never within 100 feet of an intersection, railroad crossing, bridge or tunnel.
- When you are being passed, hold your lane and do not speed up.
- Reach freeway speed in the acceleration lane; slow in the deceleration lane; if you miss your exit, take the next one.
- A white diamond marks an HOV lane; if you break down, get onto the shoulder and usually stay buckled in.
