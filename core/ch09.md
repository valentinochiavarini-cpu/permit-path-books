# Chapter 9: Sharing the Road

Pedestrians, cyclists, motorcycles, trucks, tractors and deer each move, see and stop differently. The law puts most of the duty of care on the driver of the larger, faster vehicle, and that is usually you.

> **On the test**
>
> - When you must yield to pedestrians, including at unmarked crosswalks and to blind pedestrians.
> - How to pass a cyclist, why a motorcycle gets a full lane, and where a truck's no-zones are.
> - The slow moving vehicle emblem, horses, and what to do when an animal is in the road.

## 9.1 Pedestrians

A **pedestrian** is anyone on foot, including a person in a wheelchair or on a mobility scooter. Pedestrians are hard to see at dusk, at night and in rain. Near schools, parks and homes, expect a child to run out after a ball.

### Crosswalks You Can See and Ones You Cannot

A **marked crosswalk** has white painted lines across the road. An **unmarked crosswalk** has no paint: it is the place where the sidewalk would continue across the street at an intersection. In every state, you must yield to a pedestrian crossing in a crosswalk, marked or unmarked.

- Stop before the crosswalk, not on it, and far enough back that drivers in the next lane can see the person too.
- Wait until the pedestrian has crossed your half of the road, or is safely past your path, before you move.

> **Test trap:** A question may describe an intersection with no painted lines and a person waiting to cross. Do not pick "the pedestrian must wait because there is no crosswalk." The corner is an unmarked crosswalk, and you must yield.

### Never Pass a Car Stopped at a Crosswalk

If the car in the next lane stops at a crosswalk for no clear reason, assume a hidden person is crossing in front of it, and stop too. Never pass a vehicle stopped for a pedestrian.

### Turning and Backing

When you turn on a green light, people crossing the street you are turning into often have a green or a WALK signal too. Look into that crosswalk before you start the turn, and yield.

When you back out, children can be below the line of your rear window. If children are nearby, walk around the car before you get in, then back slowly.

### Blind and Low Vision Pedestrians

A person carrying a **white cane** (often with a red tip) or walking with a **guide dog** may be blind or have low vision. White cane laws require you to yield to them.

- Stop and stay stopped until the person has crossed, even if your light is green.
- Do not honk. The person listens to traffic sounds to decide when to cross, and a horn can confuse or startle them.
- Do not stop where your car blocks their path.
- If the person pulls the cane back and steps away from the curb, that usually means they want you to go on.

## 9.2 Bicycles

A person riding a bicycle on the road has the same rights and duties as the driver of a vehicle. Treat a cyclist as a slow vehicle.

### Where Cyclists Ride

Cyclists usually keep to the right side of the lane, but they may move left to avoid parked cars, debris or potholes, to set up for a left turn, or when a lane is too narrow to share. A cyclist near the center of a narrow lane is doing that on purpose, to stop drivers from squeezing past.

A **bike lane** is usually marked with a solid white line and a bicycle symbol. Do not drive or park in it. You may cross it to reach a driveway or parking space, or to turn, after you yield to any cyclist in it. [[generic]]Many states let or require you to enter it just before a right turn.[[/generic]] A **sharrow**, a bicycle symbol with two arrowhead shapes above it, marks a lane that cars and bikes share: expect cyclists in it.

### Passing a Cyclist

- Slow down as you approach, and pass only when you can see far enough ahead.
- Leave at least the gap the law requires (see the box below), and more at higher speeds. [[generic]]Most states set a minimum of at least 3 feet, and some require more or require you to change lanes when you can.[[/generic]]
- If oncoming traffic or a curve leaves no room, wait behind the cyclist.
- After you pass, do not cut back in sharply or slow down in front of the rider.

### Turning and Opening Doors

The **right hook** is a crash in which a driver passes a cyclist and then turns right across the cyclist's path. Before a right turn, check your right mirror and look over your right shoulder; if a cyclist is close, let them go first. Turning left, wait if you are not sure of the gap to an oncoming cyclist.

A car door swung open on the street side can throw a passing cyclist into traffic. Before you open it, check your side mirror and look back over your shoulder.

## 9.3 Motorcycles

A **motorcycle** has the same right to a full lane as a car. Most car and motorcycle crashes happen because the driver did not see the rider.

A motorcycle can hide behind a windshield pillar or another car, and its small size makes it look farther away and slower than it is. When you turn left in front of one, assume it is closer and faster than it looks.

- Never share a lane with a motorcycle, even if the rider stays near one edge.
- Pass it as you would a car, and return only when you can see the whole motorcycle in your rearview mirror.
- Follow at a greater distance than you would a car. A motorcycle can stop quickly, and a rider may slow by easing off the throttle or downshifting, which does not turn on the brake light.
- Many motorcycle turn signals do not shut off by themselves. Before you pull out in front of a signaling motorcycle, wait until you are sure it is really turning.
- Give riders extra room in rain and wind.

## 9.4 Large Trucks

A loaded tractor trailer can weigh 20 to 30 times as much as a car. Know where the driver cannot see, how far the truck needs to stop and how it turns.

### The No-Zones

A **no-zone** is any area around a truck or bus where the driver cannot see you:

- **In front:** just ahead of the cab.
- **Behind:** a long area straight behind the trailer.
- **Left side:** alongside the cab, under the driver's side mirror.
- **Right side:** the largest, along the passenger side. It can cover two or more lanes and most of the trailer's length.

Look for the driver's face in the truck's big side mirror: **no face in the mirror means you are invisible to the driver.** Do not linger beside a truck: pass it steadily or drop back.

![The red striped areas are a truck's no-zones; the one on the right side is the largest and can cover two lanes.](fig/truck_no_zones.svg)

### Following and Passing

Stay well back: too close, you are in the rear no-zone and the trailer hides the road ahead. Stopped behind a truck on a hill, leave extra room, because it may roll back slightly when it starts.

- Pass on the left, where the driver can see you better, and pass steadily.
- Move back in front only when you can see the whole front of the truck in your rearview mirror. Then signal and move over without slowing down.
- Never cut in close and brake. A truck at highway speed can need close to the length of two football fields to stop.
- Do not pass a truck on a long downhill grade. Trucks pick up speed going down, and the gap can close fast.

### Wide Turns

Trucks swing wide to turn. To turn right, a truck often moves left first or starts from the second lane. If a truck is signaling a right turn, stay back and never squeeze into the space between it and the curb. That gap closes as the trailer swings around.

> **Remember it:** Trucks need more of everything: more space to stop, more room to turn, more time to speed up. Give it to them.

## 9.5 Buses

When a **transit bus** signals that it is pulling back into traffic, slow down and let it in. [[generic]]Some states require you to yield to it.[[/generic]] Buses have no-zones and make wide turns like trucks, so the same rules apply.

**School buses** have their own stopping law, with flashing red lights and a stop arm. Chapter 4 covers it.

## 9.6 Slow Vehicles, Horses and Buggies

A yellow **SHARE THE ROAD** plaque under a warning sign with a bicycle, tractor or buggy symbol means that kind of vehicle uses the lane with you. Slow down and give it room.

The **slow moving vehicle emblem** is a fluorescent orange triangle with a red reflective border, pointing up, on the back of a vehicle. It means the vehicle is built to go no faster than about 25 mph. Slow down at once: a car at 55 mph gains about 44 feet every second on a tractor going 25.

- Farm equipment may move onto the shoulder to let you by, but the shoulder may not hold its weight, so do not count on it.
- Do not pass a tractor that is slowing near a gate, field or farm entrance. It may be about to turn left, and farm vehicles may have no turn signals, or ones you cannot see.

![The fluorescent orange triangle with a red border marks a slow vehicle; on a school bus, flashing yellow lights mean it is about to stop, and flashing red lights with the red stop arm mean you must stop.](fig/smv_emblem.svg)

A yellow diamond with a horse and buggy symbol warns that **horse-drawn vehicles** use the road. A buggy often carries the orange triangle and has only dim lights at night. A horse can spook at a sudden noise and jump into the road.

- Slow down well before you reach a horse and rider or a buggy. Do not honk, rev your engine or flash your lights.
- Pass slowly with plenty of room, and move back only when you can see the horse in your rearview mirror.
- Stopped behind a buggy on a hill, leave extra space: it can roll back as it starts.

## 9.7 Animals in the Road

Animal crashes happen most often at dawn, at dusk, at night and in fall. Yellow diamond signs with an animal symbol mark places where animals often cross.

- Where animals cross, slow down and scan both shoulders. At night, use high beams when no one is coming toward you; an animal's eyes often shine in your headlights first.
- Deer usually move in groups. If one crosses, expect more to follow.
- If an animal is in your path, brake firmly and stay in your lane. Swerving can send you into oncoming traffic, a tree or a ditch, often a worse crash than hitting the animal.
- If you hit an animal, pull off the road if you can, turn on your hazard flashers and do not walk up to it. Chapter 10 covers what to do after a crash.

## 9.8 People Working on the Road

- Slow down at the first orange sign and keep your speed down until the END ROAD WORK sign.
- Obey a flagger's STOP and SLOW paddle and hand signals over other signs and signals in the zone.
- Leave extra space, and change lanes only where signs or channeling devices tell you to.
- For a stopped tow truck, utility truck or crew vehicle with flashing lights on the shoulder, move over a lane if you safely can or slow down (the Move Over law, Chapter 4).

## Quick Review

- Yield in every crosswalk, marked or unmarked, and never pass a car stopped at one.
- A white cane or guide dog means stop and wait; do not honk.
- Pass cyclists slowly with a wide gap; a motorcycle gets the full lane.
- No driver's face in a truck's mirror means no view of you; never squeeze between a turning truck and the curb.
- Orange triangle or buggy: slow down at once and pass quietly with room. Deer in your lane: brake firmly and stay in your lane.
