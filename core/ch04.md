# Chapter 4: Right-of-Way: Who Goes First

When two road users meet, the **right-of-way** rules decide who goes first. The test asks about them often, and the rules fit on a short list. Hold on to one idea: the law never gives anyone the right-of-way. It only says who must **yield**.

> **On the test**
>
> - Know who goes first at a four way stop, including when two cars arrive together.
> - Know who yields where nothing controls the intersection, and where one road ends at another.
> - Know what to do when you enter a road from a driveway, an alley or a parking lot.
> - Know when to yield to pedestrians, including a person with a white cane or a guide dog, and how big a gap you need to cross or turn.
> - Know what to do for an emergency vehicle, a stopped school bus and a funeral procession.

## 4.1 Yielding Is the Rule, Not Winning

To **yield** means to let another road user go first. You slow down, or stop if you need to, and wait until your path is clear. At a yield sign you may keep rolling if no one is coming.

- If the other driver should yield but does not, let them go. Being right is no help in a crash.
- If someone waves you on when you have to yield, check for yourself before you move.
- Do not give up your turn in a way that confuses others. Hesitation causes crashes.

> **Remember it:** The law tells you when to yield. It never tells you when to insist.

## 4.2 Intersections with Signs or Signals

- **Traffic signal:** follow the light (Chapter 3). On green, still yield to pedestrians in the crosswalk and to vehicles already in the intersection.
- **Stop sign on your road only:** stop, then yield to all cross traffic and pedestrians.
- **Yield sign:** slow down and let cross traffic and pedestrians go first. Stop if you must.

### Turning Left

A left turn crosses the path of oncoming traffic, so the left turner almost always yields. On a green light without a green arrow, wait for oncoming cars going straight or turning right to pass, and for pedestrians to clear the crosswalk you are turning into. The same applies when you turn left into a driveway or parking lot in the middle of a block: oncoming traffic goes first.

### Turning Right

When you turn right, check the crosswalk you are crossing and look over your right shoulder for bicycles or motorcycles coming up beside you. They have the right-of-way when they are going straight.

### Judging a Gap

To cross or join traffic, you need a gap long enough to clear the intersection and reach the speed of traffic without making anyone brake. Where cars move at about 30 mph, plan on roughly 6 seconds of open space to drive straight across, about 8 seconds to turn right and about 9 seconds to turn left. Faster traffic, a wet road or a car that is slow to pick up speed needs a bigger gap. Count how many seconds an approaching car needs to reach you; if the count is shorter than the gap you need, wait.

## 4.3 Four Way and All Way Stops

At an **all way stop**, every direction has a stop sign, usually with an ALL WAY plaque (older signs may say 4-WAY).

1. **First to stop, first to go.**
2. **A tie between cars at right angles:** the car on the **right** goes first.
3. **A tie between cars facing each other:** both may go if both are driving straight or turning right. If one is turning left, the driver going straight or turning right goes first.

Every driver must come to a full stop, even when no one else is around.

![At an all way stop the first car to stop goes first; in a tie at right angles the car on the right goes; facing each other, the car going straight goes before the left turner.](fig/ch04_four_way_stop.svg)

> **Test trap:** At an all way stop, the larger, faster or main road vehicle does not get to go first. Only the order of arrival and the yield to the right rule count.

## 4.4 Intersections with No Signs or Signals

An **uncontrolled intersection** has no signal, stop sign or yield sign. Slow down as you approach, look both ways and be ready to stop.

- The vehicle that reaches the intersection first goes first.
- If two vehicles arrive together, yield to the one on your right.
- A driver turning left yields to oncoming traffic going straight or turning right.

### T Intersections

At a **T intersection** with no sign or signal, traffic on the **through road** (the road that continues) has the right-of-way. If your street is the one that ends, yield to traffic from both directions before you turn.

### Entering a Road from a Driveway, Alley or Parking Lot

When you pull out of a driveway, alley, parking lot or private road, you yield to everyone. Stop before the sidewalk, because people walking have the right-of-way across the driveway. Then stop again if you need to where you can see the road, and wait for a gap in traffic from both directions.

## 4.5 Pedestrians

A **pedestrian** is anyone on foot, including a person using a wheelchair. The rules put them first in most situations.

- **Crosswalks:** yield to pedestrians in a marked crosswalk and in the unmarked crosswalk that exists at most intersections. If someone has started across, wait until they are out of your path.
- **Turning:** before any turn, check the crosswalk you are turning into.
- **Stopped cars at a crosswalk:** if a car in the next lane has stopped at a crosswalk, assume someone is crossing in front of it. Stop too, and never pass it.

A pedestrian who crosses in the middle of a block is supposed to yield to cars, but you still must do everything you can to avoid hitting them. Slow down and stop if you have to.

### Blind Pedestrians

A person carrying a **white cane** or walking with a **guide dog** is usually blind or has low vision. The law requires you to yield to them. Stop and wait until they are well clear of your path. Do not honk, rev your engine or call out instructions. If the person pulls the cane back and steps away from the curb, they are usually telling you to go ahead.

## 4.6 Emergency Vehicles

An **emergency vehicle** is a police car, fire truck, ambulance or other authorized vehicle using a siren, flashing lights or both. When one approaches:

1. Pull over to the **right edge** of the road, as close as you safely can.
2. **Stop** and stay stopped until it has passed.
3. Watch for a second emergency vehicle before you pull back out.

**Never stop in an intersection.** If you are in one, drive through it first, then pull over. If you hear a siren but cannot tell where it is, slow down and be ready to pull over.

Once it has passed, **do not follow it** to get through traffic. Stay well back: it may stop suddenly, and other emergency vehicles may be coming behind it. The box below gives the rule for one way streets and how far back to stay.

### Move Over

A **Move Over law** protects people working beside the road. When you approach an emergency vehicle stopped on or next to the road with its lights flashing, you must:

- **Move over** out of the lane next to it, if you can do so safely, or
- **Slow down** well below the speed limit if you cannot move over.

## 4.7 School Buses

A school bus flashes **yellow lights** as it gets ready to stop, then **red lights** once it has stopped.

- **Flashing yellow lights** mean the bus is about to stop to load or unload children. Slow down and get ready to stop.
- **Flashing red lights** and a **stop arm** (a red octagon that swings out from the left side of the bus) mean the bus has stopped. You must stop.

Passing a school bus that is stopped with red lights flashing and the stop arm out is against the law. Behind the bus, stop a safe distance back. Coming toward it on a two lane road, stop before you reach it. Stay stopped until the red lights go off and the stop arm folds in, unless a police officer or, where the box below allows it, the bus driver signals you to go.

Children are hardest to see near the bus and may run across in front of it. When you start again, go slowly and watch the roadside.

## 4.8 Transit Buses, Funeral Processions and Others

**Transit buses:** when a city bus signals to pull back into traffic from a stop, let it in when you safely can. [[generic]]Some states require you to yield to it.[[/generic]] Watch for passengers stepping out from in front of a stopped bus.

**Funeral processions:** cars traveling together behind a hearse, usually with headlights on and often with flags or hazard lights. [[generic]]Many states let a properly led procession continue through an intersection together once the lead vehicle has entered legally, even if the light changes.[[/generic]] Do not cut into the line, drive through it or join it. Wait for it to pass, then go.

**Others:**

- **Flaggers and crossing guards:** obey them over signs and signals (Chapter 2).
- **Trains:** a train always has the right-of-way at a crossing (Chapter 5).
- **Vehicles already in a roundabout:** yield to them when you enter (Chapter 5).

## Quick Review

- At an all way stop, the first to stop goes first; in a tie, yield to the car on the right.
- A driver turning left yields to oncoming traffic going straight or turning right.
- Leaving a driveway or alley, yield to pedestrians on the sidewalk and to all road traffic.
- For an emergency vehicle, clear any intersection, pull to the right edge and stop; never follow it closely.
- To cross or turn, wait for a gap long enough to clear the road and reach the speed of traffic.
- For a school bus with red lights and stop arm out, stop until the lights go off, the arm folds in and the bus moves.
