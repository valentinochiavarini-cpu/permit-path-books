# Chapter 3: Signals, Lights and Pavement Markings

Signals and markings tell you what to do right now. Test questions often describe them in words, so learn colors and patterns by name.

> **On the test**
>
> - Steady, flashing and arrow lights, including the flashing yellow arrow.
> - Dark or broken signals, and pedestrian signals.
> - Yellow versus white lines, solid versus broken.
> - When you may cross a line or use a special lane.

## 3.1 How a Traffic Signal Works

Most signals stack red on top, yellow in the middle and green on the bottom. Side to side signals put red on the left. If you are color blind or the sun is behind the signal, the lamp's position still tells you its color.

A light can be **steady**, **flashing** or an **arrow**, which controls only the movement it points to. A round lamp applies to every movement unless an arrow beside it says otherwise.

## 3.2 Steady Lights

### Steady Green

A **steady green light** means you may go straight or turn, but not that the way is clear. Yield to pedestrians in the crosswalk and vehicles already in the intersection. Turning left, also yield to oncoming traffic close enough to be a danger (an **unprotected left turn**, Chapter 5).

Do not enter on green if traffic ahead is backed up and you would be stuck in the intersection when the light changes. Wait until there is room to get all the way through.

### Steady Yellow

A **steady yellow light** means a red light is coming next. Do not speed up. Stop if you can do it safely before the stop line. If a hard stop would be dangerous, or you are already in the intersection, continue through with care.

### Steady Red

A **steady red light** means stop. Your stopping point is the white stop line when one is painted, the near edge of the crosswalk when there is no line, and the edge of the intersection when there is neither.

### Turning on Red

Every state lets you turn right on a steady red light after a complete stop, unless a sign says NO TURN ON RED. Before you turn, yield to pedestrians and to drivers who have the green. Most states also permit a left turn on red from a one way street onto a one way street. Some cities and states add limits.

## 3.3 Flashing Lights

- **Flashing red**: treat it exactly like a stop sign: stop completely, then go when safe.
- **Flashing yellow**: slow down and go ahead with caution. You do not have to stop.

A flashing green light is not allowed under the national standard, so it is never a correct answer.

## 3.4 Arrow Lights

| Arrow | What it means for that turn |
|---|---|
| Steady green arrow | Go. Oncoming traffic is stopped |
| Steady yellow arrow | The protected turn is ending |
| Flashing yellow arrow | Turn allowed, but yield first |
| Steady red arrow | Do not make that turn |
| Flashing red arrow | Stop, then turn when safe |

A **green arrow** gives you a **protected turn**: traffic that would cross your path is stopped. Still yield to anyone already in the intersection.

States differ on whether you may ever turn on a red arrow after a full stop, so treat a red arrow as a firm no unless your state box above says otherwise.

The figure shows a left turn signal.

![A left turn signal: a green arrow means go, a steady yellow arrow means the protected turn is ending, a flashing yellow arrow means turn only after you yield, and a red arrow means do not turn.](fig/ch03_arrow_signals.svg)

> **Test trap:** The flashing yellow arrow does not give you the right of way. You may turn only after you yield to oncoming traffic and pedestrians.

## 3.5 When the Signal Is Dark or Broken

Treat a completely dark signal as an **all way stop** (Chapter 4). If it is stuck on one color for a very long time or flickering oddly, slow down and be ready to stop.

A police officer or other person legally directing traffic overrides the signal: if an officer waves you through a red, go; if an officer signals stop on your green, stop.

## 3.6 Lane Control Signals and Ramp Meters

### Lane Control Signals

**Lane control signals** are rectangular signals over each lane, used where a lane's direction or use changes and at toll plazas, tunnels and bridges.

| Light over the lane | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Green arrow pointing down | You may drive in this lane |
| Steady yellow X | Leave this lane safely; it is about to close |
| White two way left turn arrow | Left turns only, for both directions |
| Flashing yellow X (some states only) | Use this lane only to turn left |
| Steady red X | Do not drive in this lane |

### Ramp Meters

A **ramp meter** on a freeway on ramp lets cars on one at a time so merging stays smooth. Stop at the line on red. Usually one car goes per green unless a sign says otherwise. If the meter is dark, use the ramp normally.

## 3.7 Pedestrian Signals

**Pedestrian signals** tell you what people on foot will do next.

- **Walking person** (or WALK), shown in white: people may start to cross.
- **Flashing raised hand** (or flashing DONT WALK), shown in orange: do not start to cross; anyone already crossing keeps going.
- **Steady raised hand**, shown in orange: people should not enter the street.

Many add a **countdown**: the seconds left for people to finish crossing, shown while the hand flashes.

A pedestrian already in the crosswalk keeps the right of way under any hand signal. If someone is still crossing when you get the green, wait until the person is out of your path.

![Pedestrian signals show a white walking person, a flashing orange hand with the seconds left, or a steady orange hand; over the lanes, a green down arrow opens a lane, a steady yellow X tells you to leave it, and a red X closes it.](fig/signals_ped_lane.svg)

> **Road tip:** If the walk countdown shows 3 as you approach a green, expect yellow soon. Ease off.

## 3.8 Pavement Lines: Color First

- **Yellow lines** separate traffic moving in **opposite** directions.
- **White lines** separate lanes moving in the **same** direction. They also mark the right edge of the road.

On a one way road or divided highway, yellow belongs on your left. If the yellow line is on your right, you are going the wrong way.

> **Remember it:** Yellow means traffic coming the other way. White means traffic going your way. White never separates opposing traffic.

## 3.9 Line Patterns: Solid, Broken and Double

A **broken line** may be crossed when safe, to pass or change lanes. A **solid line** means crossing is discouraged or not allowed, and a **double line** means stronger limits. A **dotted line** extends a lane line through an intersection or warns that a lane ends or turns off ahead.

### Center Lines on Two Way Roads

| Center line | What you may do |
|---|---|
| Single broken yellow | Pass when safe, from either side |
| Solid yellow beside broken yellow | Pass only from the broken side |
| Double solid yellow | No passing from either side |

With a solid and a broken line side by side, the line closest to you decides, as the figure shows.

![Three yellow center lines on a two lane road: a single broken line lets either side pass, a solid line beside a broken line lets only the broken side pass, and a double solid line means no passing.](fig/ch03_center_lines.svg)

You may cross a **double solid yellow line** to turn left into or out of a driveway, an alley or a side road. Some states also allow a left turn across it for a U-turn (Chapter 5), and some let you cross it to pass a bicycle when the way is clear.

Two double yellow lines with a wide space between form a painted **median**: do not drive on or cross it.

### Lane Lines Between Lanes Going Your Way

A **broken white line** means you may change lanes when it is safe. A **solid white line** means stay in your lane unless you must change, such as to avoid a hazard. A **double solid white line**, often beside a carpool lane, may not be crossed at all.

### Edge Lines

A **solid white edge line** marks the right edge of the travel lane; the **shoulder** beyond it is for emergency stops, not driving or passing. A **solid yellow edge line** marks the left edge on a divided highway, a one way road or a ramp. At night or in fog or heavy rain, steer by the right edge line, not oncoming headlights. An edge line angling inward means the road narrows.

## 3.10 Special Lanes

### Shared Center Turn Lanes

A **shared center turn lane** (or **two way left turn lane**) runs down the middle of some busy streets. Each side has a solid yellow line outside and a broken yellow line inside.

Drivers from both directions use it only to turn left, including out of a driveway. Signal, check your mirror and blind spot, and move in shortly before your turn. Watch for a driver from the other direction in the same space. Never use it to pass or as a travel lane.

![A shared center turn lane has a solid yellow line outside and a broken yellow line inside on each side, with white arrows for both directions; you wait in it for a gap, then turn left.](fig/ch03_center_turn_lane.svg)

### Reversible Lanes

A **reversible lane** changes direction at set times of day. Each side is marked with a **double broken yellow line**. Check the overhead signal before you enter.

### Diamond Lanes

A white **diamond symbol** in a lane marks a reserved lane, often for carpools, buses or other vehicles named on the signs. Chapter 7 explains these lanes.

## 3.11 Stop Lines, Crosswalks and Words on the Pavement

A **stop line** is a wide solid white line across your lane at a stop sign or signal. Stop with the front of your car behind it. With no line, stop before the crosswalk, or else before you enter the intersection where you can see cross traffic.

A **yield line** (**shark teeth**) is a row of white triangles across the lane, pointing toward you. If you must yield, stop behind the triangles.

A marked **crosswalk** has two parallel white lines or a ladder of wide bars. Every intersection also has unpainted crosswalks: the legal crosswalk usually continues across the street from the sidewalk. Yield to pedestrians in marked and unmarked crosswalks, and do not stop on a crosswalk.

White **arrows** painted in a lane show which way you may go from it. The word **ONLY** with an arrow means traffic in that lane must go that way. Choose your lane before the solid white line near the intersection.

Painted words such as STOP AHEAD reinforce a sign. A large **X with the letters RR** painted across your lane means a railroad crossing is ahead (Chapter 5).

If signals, signs and markings ever seem to disagree, follow a police officer or flagger first, then the signal, then the sign.

## Quick Review

- Steady yellow means red is coming: stop if you safely can; a right turn on red needs a full stop first.
- Flashing red works like a stop sign; a dark signal is an all way stop.
- A flashing yellow arrow allows a left turn only after you yield to oncoming traffic and pedestrians.
- Yellow lines separate opposite directions; white lines separate lanes going your way.
- Pass only from the broken line side; a double solid yellow line means no passing for anyone.
