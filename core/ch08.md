# Chapter 8: Parking, Backing and Leaving the Curb

Parking looks like the easy part of driving because the car moves slowly. Yet many road tests end in a parking space. A tapped curb, a bumped cone, a car left rolling on a hill or a pull out without a head check can cost you points or the whole test.

Slow speed also hides real danger. When you back up, you see less than at any other time, and the people most likely to be behind a car are small children. When you pull away from the curb, you move into traffic that is already rolling. Both moments need full attention.

The knowledge test adds rules you must simply know: which way to turn your wheels on a hill, what a painted curb means and where you may never park. This chapter covers the skills and the rules together.

> **On the test**
>
> - Know which way to turn your front wheels when you park uphill or downhill, with or without a curb.
> - Know what the common curb colors mean.
> - Know the places where parking is never allowed.
> - Know how to back up safely and how to check behind the car.
> - Know the steps for pulling away from the curb into traffic.
> - Know the steps for parallel parking.

## 8.1 Before You Park: The Basics

Whatever kind of space you use, finish every stop the same way:

1. Stop fully in the space, inside any lines.
2. Shift to park (or, with a manual transmission, into first gear or reverse).
3. Set the **parking brake**.
4. Turn off the engine and take the key or fob with you.
5. Check your mirrors and look over your shoulder for cars, bikes and people before you open your door.

That last step matters. A door opened into traffic can hit a passing cyclist or be torn off by a car. A good habit is to open the door with the hand farther from it. Reaching across turns your body and makes you look back.

Never leave a child or a pet alone in a parked car, even for a minute. Inside a closed car the temperature rises fast, even on a mild day.

## 8.2 Angle and Perpendicular Parking

### Angle Parking

**Angle parking** spaces sit at a slant to the curb or aisle, often along store fronts and in some lots. They are easy to enter because you turn less.

1. Watch for traffic behind you and signal toward the space.
2. Keep about five to six feet from the parked cars as you approach, so you have room to turn.
3. When you can see straight down the center of the space, steer sharply into it.
4. Straighten the wheel as the car enters, and stop when the front is just inside the end of the space.

### Perpendicular Parking

**Perpendicular parking** spaces are at a right angle to the aisle, as in most parking lots.

1. Signal and move as far as you safely can to the side away from the space, to give yourself room to turn.
2. Creep forward until your front bumper passes the first line of the space, or until your side mirror lines up with it.
3. Turn the wheel sharply toward the space and move in slowly.
4. Straighten the wheels and stop with the car centered, without crossing into the space in front.

### Leaving an Angle or Perpendicular Space

Backing out is the risky part. Cars beside you block your view of the aisle. Back out very slowly, check both ways and over both shoulders, and be ready to stop for people walking behind you. When you can, choose a space you can pull through, so you can leave by driving forward.

> **Road tip:** In a parking lot, drive slowly down the middle of the aisle. People, carts and children come out from between cars, and drivers back out without seeing you.

## 8.3 Parallel Parking

**Parallel parking** means parking in line with the curb, between two cars. Many road tests include it, and it is the skill most new drivers fear. It is a pattern, and patterns can be learned.

Choose a space at least several feet longer than your car. Then follow these steps:

1. **Signal and stop beside the car ahead of the space.** Stop about two to three feet out from it, with your rear bumper even with its rear bumper.
2. **Check traffic.** Look in your mirrors and over your shoulder. Keep your signal on so drivers behind know what you are doing.
3. **Back and turn toward the curb.** Shift to reverse, look back, and back up slowly while turning the wheel sharply toward the curb. Back until your car sits at about a 45 degree angle to the curb.
4. **Straighten the wheel.** Keep backing slowly in a straight line until your front bumper clears the back of the car ahead.
5. **Turn away from the curb.** Turn the wheel sharply the other way and keep backing slowly. Your car swings in line with the curb.
6. **Center the car.** Shift to drive and pull forward to leave room in front and behind.

When you are done, your car should be parallel to the curb and close to it. Most states set a maximum distance from the curb, often 12 or 18 inches. Do not let your tires climb or hit the curb.

If the first try goes badly, pull out and try again. A second smooth try is better than many small corrections and a tire on the curb.

> **Remember it:** Even, angle, straight, swing, center. Line up bumpers, back to a 45 degree angle, straighten, swing in, then center.

## 8.4 Parking on Hills

When you park on a slope, the brakes and the transmission are holding the car. If they fail, the car rolls. Turning your front wheels the right way makes the car roll into the curb or off the road, not into traffic.

Always set the parking brake and shift to park on any hill. Then turn the wheels as follows.

| Situation | Turn the front wheels | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Downhill, with curb | Toward the curb (right) | The car rolls forward into the curb |
| Uphill, with curb | Away from the curb (left) | The car rolls back into the curb |
| Uphill or downhill, no curb | Toward the edge of the road (right) | The car rolls off the road, not into traffic |

### How to Set the Wheels

- **Downhill with a curb:** turn the wheels toward the curb and let the car roll forward slowly until the front tire rests against it.
- **Uphill with a curb:** turn the wheels away from the curb, then let the car roll back slowly until the back of the front tire rests against the curb.
- **No curb:** turn the wheels toward the shoulder, so the car rolls away from the center of the road.

These directions assume you are parked on the right side of the road. On a one way street parked on the left side, the curb is on your left, so the same logic flips: toward the curb downhill, away from it uphill.

When you leave a hill space, check traffic, then turn the wheels straight as you pull out. On an uphill start, hold the brake until the engine is pulling, so the car does not roll back.

> **Remember it:** Uphill with a curb is the only time you turn away from the curb. Every other case points the wheels to the side of the road.

## 8.5 Curb Colors

Many cities paint curbs to show parking rules. The colors and their exact meanings are set by state law and local rules, and some states do not use them at all. Where they are used, this is the common pattern:

| Curb color | Common meaning |
|---|---|
| Red | No stopping, standing or parking |
| Yellow | Loading zone; stop only to load or unload |
| White | Short stop to pick up or drop off people or mail |
| Green | Parking for a limited time |
| Blue | Parking only for people with a disability placard or plate |

Signs posted next to the curb tell you the hours and time limits. When a sign and a painted curb are both present, read the sign. When you are unsure, find another spot.

### Disability Parking

Spaces for people with disabilities are marked with the white wheelchair symbol, often on a blue background, on a sign and on the pavement. Only a vehicle with a valid disability placard or plate, carrying the person it was issued to, may park there. Do not park on the striped area next to the space either. That area is where a person loads a wheelchair or uses a ramp. Fines are high in every state.

## 8.6 Where You May Not Park

Some places are off limits for parking in every state, with or without a sign, because a parked car there blocks a lifesaving path or hides people from view.

Never park:

- On a sidewalk or in a crosswalk.
- In an intersection.
- In front of a driveway, blocking anyone's way in or out.
- Next to a fire hydrant, within the distance your state sets.
- Near a crosswalk, a stop sign or a traffic signal, within the distance your state sets, where you would hide people from drivers.
- On or near railroad tracks.
- On a bridge, in a tunnel or on an overpass, unless signs allow it.
- **Double parked**: on the street side of a car already parked at the curb.
- In a bike lane or a bus stop.
- On the left side of a two way road, facing traffic.
- In a space or zone marked for fire lanes, emergency vehicles or loading.
- On a freeway, except in an emergency.
- Anywhere a sign, a painted curb or pavement marking says No Parking.

The distances (for example from a fire hydrant, crosswalk, stop sign or railroad crossing) differ by state. Your state's figures are in the box in Section 8.5.

### Stopping, Standing and Parking

Signs use three words that mean different things:

- **No Parking:** you may stop briefly to load or unload people or things, but you may not leave the car.
- **No Standing:** you may stop only long enough to pick up or drop off people. No waiting, no loading things.
- **No Stopping:** you may not stop at all, except to obey a signal, an officer or to avoid a crash.

> **Test trap:** No Stopping is the strictest of the three. If a question asks which sign forbids even a quick drop off, choose No Stopping.

## 8.7 Backing Up Safely

When you back up, you cannot see the area right behind your car well, and a small child can be completely hidden there. Treat every backing move as slow and careful.

### Before You Back

- Walk around the car first if you have parked where children play or where you could not see well. Look for toys, bikes and pets as well as people.
- Check your mirrors and look over both shoulders.
- Use your backup camera and sensors as helpers, not as your only check.

### While You Back

1. Put your foot on the brake and shift to reverse.
2. Turn your body to the right and look back through the rear window. Put your right hand on the top of the passenger seat back if it helps you turn.
3. Steer with your left hand at the top of the wheel. To make the back of the car go right, turn the wheel to the right. To make it go left, turn it to the left.
4. Back slowly, at walking speed or less. Control the speed with the brake.
5. Glance to the front and sides now and then, because the front of the car swings out to the side when you turn while backing.

### Backing Into the Street

Backing out of a driveway into a street is a common cause of crashes. Back slowly, stop at the sidewalk to look for people walking, then check both directions for traffic before you go into the street. When you can, back into your driveway and drive out forward. You see the street much better that way.

On a freeway or highway, never back up. If you go past a turn or an exit, go on to the next safe place to turn around.

> **Road tip:** Do not back farther than you need to. The longer you back up, the more chances someone has to step into a space you cannot see.

## 8.8 Pulling Away from the Curb

Leaving a parking space at the curb means joining traffic that is already moving. Drivers on the road have the right of way, so you must wait for a safe gap.

1. **Start the car** and keep your foot on the brake. Release the parking brake.
2. **Signal** toward the lane you are entering, so drivers know you plan to move.
3. **Check mirrors and look over your shoulder** toward the traffic lane. Your blind spot on that side is where a car or bike can hide.
4. **Wait for a gap** large enough to reach the speed of traffic without making anyone brake.
5. **Move out smoothly** and speed up to match the traffic. Cancel your signal.

If you are parked close behind another car, back up a little first, so you can pull out without hitting it.

When pulling out from a parallel space, also look for people getting out of parked cars ahead and for cyclists coming up from behind. A cyclist in the lane next to you can be moving faster than you think.

### Leaving a Driveway or Alley

When you drive out of a driveway, alley or parking lot onto a street, stop before you cross the sidewalk. Yield to people walking, then to traffic on the street. Most states require a full stop before the sidewalk when you leave an alley or private drive in a business or residential area.

## Quick Review

- After parking: park position, parking brake, engine off, look before you open the door.
- Parallel park by lining up bumpers, backing to a 45 degree angle, straightening, then swinging in.
- Downhill with a curb: wheels toward the curb. Uphill with a curb: wheels away from the curb.
- With no curb, turn the wheels toward the edge of the road either way.
- Common curb colors: red no stopping, yellow loading, white short pick up, green time limit, blue disability parking.
- Never park on a sidewalk, in a crosswalk or intersection, in front of a driveway, beside a hydrant, on tracks or double parked.
- No Stopping is the strictest sign; No Parking still allows a brief stop to load.
- Back up slowly while looking over your shoulder; cameras only help.
- Pull away from the curb only after a signal, a head check and a safe gap.
