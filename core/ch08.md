# Chapter 8: Parking, Backing and Leaving the Curb

Many road tests end in a parking space, and a tapped curb or a pull out without a head check can cost you points. Slow speed also hides danger: when you back up, you see less than at any other time.

> **On the test**
>
> - Which way to turn your front wheels when you park uphill or downhill, with or without a curb.
> - Where parking is never allowed, and what No Parking, No Standing and No Stopping mean.
> - How to back up safely, pull away from the curb and parallel park.

## 8.1 Before You Park: The Basics

Whatever kind of space you use, finish every stop the same way:

1. Stop fully inside any lines.
2. Shift to park (or, with a manual transmission, into first gear or reverse).
3. Set the **parking brake**.
4. Turn off the engine and take the key or fob with you.
5. Check your mirrors and look over your shoulder for cars, bikes and people before you open your door.

A door opened into traffic can hit a cyclist or be struck by a car. Open it with the hand farther from it: reaching across turns your body and makes you look back.

Never leave a child or a pet alone in a parked car, even for a minute: the temperature inside rises fast, even on a mild day.

## 8.2 Angle and Perpendicular Parking

**Angle parking** spaces sit at a slant to the curb or aisle. Signal, keep about five to six feet from the parked cars as you approach, and steer sharply in when you can see straight down the center of the space. Straighten the wheel as the car enters and stop when the front is just inside the end of the space.

**Perpendicular parking** spaces are at a right angle to the aisle, as in most parking lots. Signal and move as far as you safely can to the side away from the space. Creep forward until your front bumper passes the first line of the space, or until your side mirror lines up with it, then turn sharply and move in slowly. Straighten the wheels and stop centered, without crossing into the space in front.

### Leaving an Angle or Perpendicular Space

Backing out is the risky part. Back out very slowly, check both ways and over both shoulders, and be ready to stop for people walking behind you. When you can, choose a space you can pull through, so you can leave by driving forward. In the lot itself, drive slowly down the middle of the aisle: people, carts and children come out from between cars.

## 8.3 Parallel Parking

**Parallel parking** means parking in line with the curb, between two cars. Many road tests include it.

![Line up your rear bumper with the car ahead, back to about 45 degrees, straighten, swing in, then center the car.](fig/parallel_parking.svg)

Choose a space at least several feet longer than your car.

1. **Signal and stop beside the car ahead of the space.** Stop about two to three feet out from it, with your rear bumper even with its rear bumper.
2. **Check traffic** in your mirrors and over your shoulder. Keep your signal on.
3. **Back and turn toward the curb.** Shift to reverse, look back, and back up slowly while turning the wheel sharply toward the curb, until your car sits at about a 45 degree angle to the curb.
4. **Straighten the wheel.** Keep backing slowly in a straight line until your front bumper clears the back of the car ahead.
5. **Turn away from the curb.** Turn the wheel sharply the other way and keep backing slowly. Your car swings in line with the curb.
6. **Center the car.** Shift to drive and pull forward to leave room in front and behind.

End parallel to the curb and close to it. [[generic]]Most states set a maximum distance from the curb, often 12 or 18 inches.[[/generic]] Do not let your tires climb or hit the curb.

If the first try goes badly, pull out and try again: a second smooth try beats many small corrections and a tire on the curb.

> **Remember it:** Even, angle, straight, swing, center. Line up bumpers, back to a 45 degree angle, straighten, swing in, then center.

## 8.4 Parking on Hills

If the brakes or transmission fail on a slope, the car rolls. Turned the right way, your front wheels send it into the curb or off the road, not into traffic. Always set the parking brake and shift to park on any hill, then turn the wheels as follows.

| Situation | Turn the front wheels | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Downhill, with curb | Toward the curb (right) | The car rolls forward into the curb |
| Uphill, with curb | Away from the curb (left) | The car rolls back into the curb |
| Uphill or downhill, no curb | Toward the edge of the road (right) | The car rolls off the road, not into traffic |

![The front wheels point toward the curb downhill, away from the curb uphill, and toward the road edge when there is no curb.](fig/hill_parking.svg)

- **Downhill with a curb:** turn the wheels toward the curb and let the car roll forward slowly until the front tire rests against it.
- **Uphill with a curb:** turn the wheels away from the curb, then let the car roll back slowly until the back of the front tire rests against the curb.

These directions assume you park on the right side of the road. On the left side of a one way street, the same logic flips: toward the curb downhill, away from it uphill.

On an uphill start, hold the brake until the engine is pulling, so the car does not roll back.

> **Remember it:** Uphill with a curb is the only time you turn away from the curb. Every other case points the wheels to the side of the road.

## 8.5 Where You May Not Park

Never park in these places, with or without a sign:

- On a sidewalk, in a crosswalk or in an intersection.
- In front of a driveway, blocking anyone's way in or out.
- Close to a fire hydrant, a crosswalk, a stop sign or a traffic signal.
- On or near railroad tracks.
- On a bridge, in a tunnel or on an overpass, unless signs allow it.
- **Double parked**: on the street side of a car already parked at the curb.
- In a bike lane or a bus stop, or in a fire lane, emergency or loading zone.
- On the left side of a two way road, facing traffic.
- On a freeway, except in an emergency.
- Anywhere a sign or pavement marking says No Parking.

### Disability Parking

Disability spaces show a white wheelchair symbol, often on a blue background, on a sign and on the pavement. Only a vehicle with a valid disability placard or plate, carrying the person it was issued to, may park there. Do not park on the striped area next to the space either: a person needs it to load a wheelchair or use a ramp.

### Stopping, Standing and Parking

Three sign words mean different things:

- **No Parking:** you may stop only while you load or unload people or things.
- **No Standing:** you may stop only long enough to pick up or drop off people. No waiting, no loading things.
- **No Stopping:** you may not stop at all, except to obey a signal, an officer or to avoid a crash.

> **Test trap:** No Stopping is the strictest of the three. If a question asks which sign forbids even a quick drop off, choose No Stopping.

## 8.6 Backing Up Safely

A small child can be completely hidden right behind your car.

### Before You Back

- Walk around the car first if children play nearby or you could not see well.
- Check your mirrors and look over both shoulders.
- Use your backup camera and sensors as helpers, not as your only check.

### While You Back

1. Put your foot on the brake and shift to reverse.
2. Turn your body to the right and look back through the rear window. Put your right hand on the top of the passenger seat back if it helps you turn.
3. Steer with your left hand at the top of the wheel. Turn the wheel in the direction you want the back of the car to go: right to go right, left to go left.
4. Back slowly, at walking speed or less. Control the speed with the brake.
5. Glance to the front and sides now and then, because the front of the car swings out when you turn while backing.

### Backing Into the Street

Backing out of a driveway is a common cause of crashes. Back slowly, stop at the sidewalk to look for people walking, then check both directions for traffic. When you can, back into your driveway and drive out forward, where you see the street much better.

**Do not back up in a travel lane**, except to parallel park. Drivers behind you do not expect a car coming toward them in reverse. If you go past a driveway, a turn or a parking space, drive on and turn around where it is safe. On a freeway, never back up.

## 8.7 Pulling Away from the Curb

Drivers already on the road have the right of way, so you must wait for a safe gap.

1. **Start the car** and keep your foot on the brake. Release the parking brake.
2. **Signal** toward the lane you are entering.
3. **Check mirrors and look over your shoulder** toward the traffic lane. Your blind spot on that side is where a car or bike can hide.
4. **Wait for a gap** large enough to reach the speed of traffic without making anyone brake.
5. **Move out smoothly** and speed up to match the traffic. Cancel your signal.

From a parallel space, also look for people getting out of parked cars ahead and for cyclists coming up from behind.

### Leaving a Driveway or Alley

Coming out of a driveway, alley or parking lot, stop before you cross the sidewalk. Yield to people walking, then to traffic on the street.

## Quick Review

- After parking: park position, parking brake, engine off, look before you open the door.
- Hills: downhill with a curb toward it, uphill with a curb away from it, no curb toward the road edge.
- Never park in a crosswalk, an intersection, on a sidewalk or across a driveway; the box gives the distances.
- No Stopping is the strictest sign; No Parking still allows a brief stop to load.
- Back slowly while looking back, and never in a travel lane except to parallel park; pull out only after a signal, a head check and a safe gap.
