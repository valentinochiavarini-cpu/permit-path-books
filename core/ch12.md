# Chapter 12: Your Car, Passengers and Your Record

You can be a careful driver and still get hurt by a bald tire, a burned out brake light or a seat belt worn the wrong way. Your car is part of your safety plan, and so is the way every person in it is buckled in. So is your driving record: every ticket you collect follows you.

## 12.1 Before You Drive

Before you get in, walk around the car and look:

- **Tires:** do any look low or flat? Any bulges or deep cuts in the sidewalls?
- **Under the car:** fresh puddles of fluid can mean a leak.
- **Glass and lights:** are the windows, mirrors and light covers clean and unbroken?
- **Around the car:** children, pets, bikes or toys behind or beside you.

### Inside, Before You Start

1. Lock the doors.
2. Adjust the seat so you can press the brake pedal all the way down with your knee still slightly bent, and reach the top of the wheel with a slight bend in your elbows.
3. Adjust the head restraint so its middle is about level with the tops of your ears. Set too low, it cannot stop your head from snapping back in a rear end crash.
4. Set the inside mirror and both side mirrors (see 12.4).
5. Fasten your seat belt and make sure every passenger does the same.
6. Clear windows of frost, fog, snow and clutter.

## 12.2 Dashboard Warning Lights

A dashboard light that stays on after start up, or comes on while you drive, is telling you something. Your owner's manual shows every symbol.

| Color | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Red | Serious problem or danger | Stop safely soon and check |
| Yellow or amber | Something needs attention | Have it checked soon |
| Green or blue | A system is on | Nothing; it is information |

- **Oil pressure** (red, shaped like an oil can): the engine may not be getting oil. Pull over and turn off the engine. Driving on can destroy it.
- **Engine temperature** (red, a thermometer in liquid): the engine is overheating. Pull over and let it cool. Never open a hot radiator cap.
- **Brake system** (red, often the word BRAKE or an exclamation point in a circle): the parking brake is on, or the brake system has a problem.
- **Battery or charging** (red, a battery shape): the charging system is failing and the car may soon stall.
- **Check engine** (yellow, an engine outline): have the engine checked soon. A flashing check engine light is more urgent.
- **Tire pressure** (yellow, a horseshoe shape with an exclamation point): one or more tires are low.
- **ABS** (yellow): the anti-lock brakes have a fault. Normal braking still works.
- A **blue** light shaped like a headlight means your high beams are on.

## 12.3 Tires, Brakes and Steering

- **Tire pressure.** Use the pressure printed on the sticker inside the driver's door frame or in the owner's manual. The number on the tire's sidewall is the maximum the tire can hold, not the right pressure for your car. Check pressure when the tires are cold, at least once a month.
- **Tread.** Tread grooves push water out from under the tire, so worn tread raises your risk of skidding and hydroplaning. Slide a penny into a groove with Lincoln's head upside down and facing you. If you can see the top of his head, the grooves are too shallow and it is time for a new tire. Most tires also have small bars molded across the grooves; when the tread wears down level with them, replace the tire.
- **Brakes.** A brake pedal that feels soft or sinks toward the floor, a car that pulls to one side when you brake, or grinding and squealing sounds all mean the brakes need a mechanic soon.
- **Steering.** The wheel should not have much free play, and the car should go straight on a level road without pulling.

## 12.4 Lights, Glass and Mirrors

Check that your headlights (low and high beams), taillights, brake lights, turn signals and license plate light all work. A turn signal that blinks much faster than usual often means one of its bulbs has burned out.

Set the **inside mirror** to frame the whole rear window. Set each **side mirror** so you just barely see the side of your own car at the inner edge; this cuts down the blind spot. Every car still has **blind spots** that no mirror shows, so always turn your head and look over your shoulder before you change lanes or pull out. The passenger side mirror on most cars is convex: vehicles in it are closer than they appear.

Loose items fly in a crash or a hard stop, and a water bottle that rolls under the brake pedal can keep you from stopping. Keep the floor near the pedals clear and put heavy items in the trunk. Never let a passenger ride in the cargo area of a pickup or the back of an SUV.

## 12.6 Seat Belts

Seat belts are the single best way to survive a crash. They keep you in your seat, spread the force across the strongest parts of your body, and keep you from being thrown out of the car, which is almost always deadly. Your arms cannot brace you: in a crash at 30 mph, your body keeps moving at 30 mph after the car stops.

- The **lap belt** goes low and snug across your hips, not your stomach.
- The **shoulder belt** crosses the middle of your chest and your shoulder, away from your neck.
- Never put the shoulder belt behind your back or under your arm. In a crash, that can cause serious internal injuries.
- If you are pregnant, keep wearing the belt: shoulder belt between the breasts and off to the side of the belly, lap belt below the belly across the hips.

## 12.7 Airbags

**Airbags** inflate in a fraction of a second in a moderate or severe crash. They are built to work with seat belts, not instead of them. An unbelted person can be thrown forward into an airbag as it bursts open, and that can cause serious injury or death.

- Sit with at least 10 inches between your chest and the airbag cover in the steering wheel.
- Hold the wheel with your hands on either side, not across the top.
- Do not lean your head against the door or window, where side and curtain airbags open.
- A rear-facing child seat must never ride in a seat with an active front airbag.

## 12.8 Children and Passengers

Keep children 12 and under in the back seat, away from the force of a front airbag. Keep each child in each stage below as long as the seat's height and weight limits allow, rather than moving up early.

| Stage | Who | Key rule |
|---|---|---|
| Rear-facing seat | Infants and toddlers | Back seat; never facing an active airbag |
| Forward-facing seat with harness | Children who outgrow rear-facing | Use the harness and the top tether |
| Booster seat | Children who outgrow the harness | Until the adult belt fits right |
| Seat belt | Children the belt fits | Lap belt on upper thighs, shoulder belt on chest |

A child is ready for the adult belt alone when they can sit all the way back with knees bent at the seat edge, the lap belt lies across the upper thighs (not the stomach), and the shoulder belt crosses the chest and shoulder (not the neck or face). For many children that happens between 8 and 12 years old.

Install every car seat exactly as its instructions and your car's manual say. A seat that moves more than an inch side to side at the belt path is too loose.

### Never Leave a Child Alone in the Car

A closed car heats up fast, and a child's body heats up three to five times faster than an adult's. More than half of child hot car deaths happen because a caregiver forgot the child was there.

- Never leave a child alone in a car, not even for a minute. The same goes for pets.
- Look in the back seat every time before you lock the car and walk away.
- Lock your car when it is parked so children cannot climb in and get trapped.
- If you see a child alone in a hot car, call 911 right away.

## 12.9 License, Registration and Insurance

When you drive, keep these with you or in the car. A police officer can ask to see all three at any traffic stop.

- Your **driver's license** or learner's permit.
- The vehicle's **registration**, which shows the car is registered with the state.
- **Proof of insurance**, on a card or, where the law allows, on your phone.

**Liability insurance** pays for injuries and damage you cause to other people. It does not pay to fix your own car; that takes collision coverage. The law sets the minimum amount of coverage you must carry (see 12.10).

Your license plates must be clean, lit at night and easy to read. Renew the registration before it expires.

> **Test trap:** The registration proves the car is registered with the state. It does not prove you are insured or licensed; those need their own documents.

## 12.10 Your Driving Record

Your **driving record** lists your traffic convictions, crashes and any suspensions. Insurance companies check it, and a poor record raises what you pay.

- **Points.** Each moving violation you are convicted of goes on your record, usually counted in points. Serious ones, such as speeding far over the limit or passing a stopped school bus, count more.
- **Action as they add up.** First a warning letter, then a required exam, driving course or hearing, then a suspension of your license.
- **Points come off.** They drop off after a set time with a clean record, and a course can sometimes remove some sooner.
- **Insurance.** Keep the required liability insurance on any car you drive or own, and carry proof. Driving uninsured can cost you your registration and your license.

New and young drivers often face action sooner (Chapter 1). Driving while your license is suspended adds more suspension time and heavy fines.

## Quick Review

- Walk around, then set seat, head restraint, mirrors and belt before every drive.
- Red dashboard lights: stop soon and check. Yellow: have it checked soon.
- Use the tire pressure on the door sticker, not the number on the tire.
- Lap belt low on the hips, shoulder belt across the chest; airbags work with belts, 10 inches back.
- Children 12 and under in back; never leave a child alone in a car; carry license, registration and insurance.
- Tickets add points to your record; as they add up you face a warning, a course or exam, then a suspension.
